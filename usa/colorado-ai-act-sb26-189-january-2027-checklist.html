Colorado AI Act after SB 26-189: what applies on 1 January 2027

Checked 13 Oct 2026 against the official sources listed below. Plain-English checklist, not legal advice.

Short answer: SB 24-205 was never enforced (a court ruled in April 2026 that it could not yet be enforced), and Colorado enacted SB 26-189 (signed 14 May 2026), which repeals and replaces it with an automated decision-making technology (ADMT) law effective 1 January 2027. It covers ADMT used in consequential decisions such as employment, housing, lending, insurance, health care, education and essential government services. Attorney General rules are due by 1 January 2027.

Checklist for 1 Jan 2027

  • Inventory: which of your tools make or substantially shape consequential decisions about Colorado residents?
  • Role: are you the developer (you build it) or deployer (you use it)? Obligations differ.
  • Scope: ADMT = technology that processes personal data and generates predictions, recommendations, classifications, rankings or scores used to make, guide or assist a decision about a person. Consequential decision = access to, eligibility for, or compensation related to education, employment, housing, financial or lending services, insurance, health care, or essential government services (official bill summary).
  • Developers of covered ADMT (from 1 Jan 2027): give deployers technical documentation on intended uses, categories of training data, known limitations, and instructions for appropriate use and human review; notify deployers of material updates.
  • Developers and deployers: keep records showing compliance for at least 3 years.
  • Deployers: clear and conspicuous notice at the point of interaction; within 30 days after an adverse outcome, a plain-language description of the ADMT's role; honour requests for personal data, correction, and meaningful human review and reconsideration.
  • Contracts: get the developer documentation written into vendor agreements now, not at renewal.
  • Watch the Attorney General rulemaking.

Enforcement

Enforced by the Attorney General under the Colorado Consumer Protection Act (a violation is a deceptive trade practice). Before 1 January 2030 the AG must give 60 days' notice and a chance to cure where a cure is possible. No new private right of action. The AG must adopt rules on the post-adverse-outcome disclosures by 1 January 2027 (official bill summary).
