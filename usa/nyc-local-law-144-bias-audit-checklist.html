NYC Local Law 144 checklist: AI hiring tools, bias audits and candidate notice (2026)

Checked 13 Oct 2026 against the official sources listed below. Plain-English checklist, not legal advice.

Short answer: if a tool scores, ranks or recommends candidates and substantially assists a hiring or promotion decision for New York City roles, the employer or employment agency needs an independent bias audit within the past year, a public summary of results, and notice to candidates before use. The duty sits with the employer, not the vendor.

Checklist

  • List every tool that produces a score, rank, classification or recommendation about candidates.
  • For each: is it used for NYC roles? Who is the employer of record?
  • Independent bias audit within the last 12 months (selection or scoring rates and impact ratios by sex, race/ethnicity and intersections).
  • Public summary on the careers site: audit date, data source, counts, impact ratios, tool distribution date. Keep it up at least 6 months after last use.
  • Candidate notice in advance of use (the DCWP rules set the notice requirements).
  • Vendors: publish your own audit so customers can link to it; it does not move their duty, but it removes friction in every deal.

What happens if you skip it

The DCWP FAQ penalty schedule runs from $375 to $1,500 per violation (Gibson Dunn summary). A New York State Comptroller audit published in 2026 found DCWP enforcement had gaps and reviewed public company websites for compliance, so public pages are what gets checked.
