Short answer: since 9 October 2025, banks and other payment service providers in euro-area Member States must check, before you authorise a euro credit transfer, whether the payee's name you typed matches the account (Article 5c(1)(a) and 5c(9) of Regulation (EU) No 260/2012, inserted by Regulation (EU) 2024/886). Banks in Member States outside the euro area must do this from 9 July 2027. The check is free (Article 5b(2)). If the bank warns you and you pay anyway, the bank is not liable for a wrong account number (Article 5c(8), PSD2 Article 88). If the bank fails to run the check and that causes a wrong payment, it must refund you without delay (Article 5c(8), second subparagraph). Business customers may switch the check off for bulk payment files (Article 5c(6)): then the risk is theirs.

What to tell a client in one minute

"When you type a supplier's name and IBAN, your bank now checks them against each other and shows match, close match or no match before you confirm. Treat a no match as a stop sign: call the supplier on a number you already have. If you pay after a warning, the bank does not have to give the money back. If the bank skipped the check, it does. And if you send bulk payment files and switched the check off, you carry the risk yourself, so do not do that for new or changed suppliers."

Why AI matters here

Fake invoices and "our bank details have changed" emails are now easy to write with AI tools, and voice messages can be faked. The payee check helps because a fraudster's account is usually held in a different name: if you type the name from your own supplier record, the bank should show NO MATCH. Our reading: the check only compares the name (or for companies, where the bank offers it, a tax number, EUID or LEI: Article 5c(1)(b)) with the account. It does not tell you whether the invoice is genuine. That is why the sheet still holds payments where bank details changed or a request was unusual until someone confirms by phone. If your AI invoice tool or a payment initiation service fills in the name and IBAN for you, Article 5c(2) puts the duty to get the payee information right on the payment initiation service provider; an AI tool that is not one is simply your input.

The rules the sheet uses

  • The check (Article 5c(1)(a)): where you insert the account identifier and the payee's name, your bank must match them, immediately after you enter them and before you can authorise. If they do not match, it must tell you and warn that authorising "might lead to transferring the funds to a payment account not held by the payee". If they almost match, it must show you the name held for that account. Recital 21 gives the reasons for a close match: diacritics, transliteration, everyday names versus formal names.
  • Name of the payee: for a person, name and surname; for a company, the commercial or legal name (new Article 2, point (1d)).
  • Companies (Article 5c(1)(b)): where the bank offers it, you can give a fiscal number, a European unique identifier (EUID) or an LEI instead of the name, and the payee's bank checks that.
  • Every channel (Article 5c(1)): online banking, app, branch; for paper orders when you are present (Article 5c(4)). The check must not stop you from authorising (Article 5c(5)).
  • Free (Article 5b(2)): the payee check must be free for all customers (euro area from 9 January 2025, others from 9 January 2027: Article 5b(3)).
  • Bulk opt-out (Article 5c(6) and (7)): customers who are not consumers can opt out of the check when they submit multiple payment orders as a package, and can opt back in at any time. When they opt out, the bank must warn them about the risk.
  • Liability (Article 5c(8)): a bank that met the rules is not liable for a payment to an unintended payee based on a wrong unique identifier (PSD2 Article 88). If the bank (or a payment initiation service provider under Article 5c(2)) failed to comply and that results in a defectively executed payment, the payer's bank must refund without delay. Banks must tell customers what ignoring a warning means for liability and refunds (Article 5c(7)).
  • Wrong account number (PSD2 Article 88): a payment executed according to the unique identifier counts as correctly executed; the bank must still make reasonable efforts to recover the funds and, if that fails, give you on written request the information you need for a legal claim. It may charge for recovery if the framework contract says so.
  • Time limit (PSD2 Article 71(1)): tell your bank without undue delay, and at the latest 13 months after the debit date. For business customers the contract may set other time limits and exclude Article 89 (PSD2 Article 61(1)); Member States may treat microenterprises like consumers (Article 61(3)).
  • Dates (Article 5c(9)): euro-area banks from 9 October 2025; banks in Member States outside the euro area from 9 July 2027. The same Regulation makes banks offer instant euro payments: sending from 9 October 2025 in the euro area (Article 5a(8)).

Checklist before the next payment run

  • Payee names typed from your own supplier records, not copied from the invoice or email.
  • Every NO MATCH stopped and checked by phone with a known contact.
  • Every CLOSE MATCH compared with the name the bank shows.
  • New or changed bank details confirmed by phone before the first payment.
  • Bulk opt-out switched off, or new and changed payees paid singly.
  • Bank's written answer on the check and on contract terms filed (letter above).
  • Wrong payments reported to the bank the same day; 13-month limit noted.

Limits and our reading

  • Read: the provisions listed at the top, in the 2024 Official Journal text of Regulation (EU) 2024/886 and the 2015 text of PSD2. Not read: a consolidated text of Regulation (EU) No 260/2012 and its Annex (which defines the account identifier), the European Payments Council verification of payee rulebook and its exact result labels, EBA or national guidance, any later EU payment services law that may replace PSD2 articles, national penalty rules (Article 11(1a)), and your bank's contract.
  • Our reading: the sheet treats a payment to the wrong account after NO CHECK, where the check was due and you had not opted out, as a refund case under Article 5c(8); in practice the bank may argue that the missing check did not cause the loss. It treats payment after a NO MATCH or CLOSE MATCH warning as your risk. The phone confirmation rules in columns G to I are our suggestion, not law. Columns B to J are your answers, not checked.

Questions

What is verification of payee in the EU?

A free check your bank must run before you authorise a euro credit transfer: it compares the payee's name you typed with the account and shows a match, an almost match (with the account holder's name) or a no match warning. Article 5c of Regulation (EU) No 260/2012, inserted by Regulation (EU) 2024/886.

From when must banks run the check?

Banks in euro-area Member States from 9 October 2025; banks in Member States outside the euro area from 9 July 2027 (Article 5c(9)).

Who pays if I ignore a no match warning?

You do, in our reading. A bank that met the rules is not liable for a payment to an unintended payee based on a wrong account number (Article 5c(8), Directive (EU) 2015/2366 Article 88). It must still make reasonable efforts to recover the funds (Article 88(3)).

What if my bank did not run the check?

If the bank failed to comply and that results in a defectively executed payment, it must refund you without delay and restore your account (Article 5c(8), second subparagraph).

Can a business switch the check off?

Only for multiple payment orders submitted as a package, and only customers who are not consumers. They can opt back in at any time (Article 5c(6)). The bank must warn them of the risk (Article 5c(7)).

Does a match mean the invoice is genuine?

No. The check only compares the name with the account. A fake invoice that names the fraudster's own company would match. Type names from your own supplier record and confirm changed bank details by phone.
