A customer wants their data exported from your AI tools? A GDPR Article 20 data portability request sheet for small businesses

Updated 25 Oct 2026 · Disclosure: the EURO AI Register mentioned below is an Unadhi product with a free edition. This guide is for information only, not legal advice.

What you get: a finished portability request sheet: one row per request, a formula that tells you whether Article 20 applies (consent or contract, and automated means), a column for the data to include from each AI tool, a column for what to leave out and why (AI scores and other inferred data, other people's data), the export format and route, a reply-date formula, a status formula and two made-up example rows.

Short answer: if a customer or user asks for "all my data" to take to another provider, and you hold it on the basis of their consent or a contract in software, you must give them what they provided (including what they typed into your chatbot and their usage history) in a file a machine can read, free of charge and within one month. AI scores and profiles you created are generally not part of the export, but the person can still see them through an access request.

Step 1: check whether Article 20 applies

  • Two tests, both needed: the lawful basis is consent or contract, and the processing is automated (Art. 20(1)). Look the basis up in your records; do not decide it per request.
  • Typical yes: a SaaS or app customer leaving for a competitor, an online shop customer wanting their order history, a client of a coaching or booking service wanting their chatbot conversations and uploaded files.
  • Typical no: data you keep because the law requires it (invoices for tax), data held on legitimate interests (for example a cold-prospect list), paper files. Handle those as access requests.

Step 2: the portability request sheet (one row per request)

ColumnWhat to writeSource

Put the column letters A to L in row 1 and the first request in row 2; the formulas above assume that layout and the exact words consent, contract and Yes in D and E (a drop-down list avoids typing errors). Sort by column J so "Reply due within 7 days" comes first.

Step 3: getting the export out of AI tools

  • Check each tool's own export: many chatbot, CRM and helpdesk tools have a per-contact export to CSV or JSON. Write down in your records which button or menu does it, so the next request takes minutes.
  • Where a supplier holds the data for you as processor, your processor agreement should oblige them to help you answer data subject requests (Art. 28(3)(e)). Ask early; the one-month clock is yours.
  • Add a one-page field list (column name, meaning) so the file is usable. Recital 68 encourages interoperable formats but says you are not obliged to adopt technically compatible systems.
  • Remove other people's personal data that would adversely affect them before sending (Art. 20(4)), and send by a secure link rather than as a plain email attachment.

Do this week

  • Add a "lawful basis" column to your list of AI tools if it is missing, so column D is a copy-paste
  • For each AI tool, find and note the per-person export function and the formats it offers
  • Decide which AI-generated fields count as inferred (scores, summaries, segments) and write the list down once
  • Check that your processor agreements cover help with data subject requests
