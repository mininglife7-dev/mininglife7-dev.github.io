Common charger check sheet for EU web shops selling phones, earbuds, speakers, tablets and laptops: USB-C and USB PD, the no-charger option, and the charger pictogram and power label close to the price under Directive (EU) 2022/2380, before Black Friday, for accountants, bookkeepers and web agencies

What you get: a finished common charger check sheet for EU web shops that sell phones, earbuds, headphones, speakers, tablets, cameras, e-readers, keyboards, mice, sat navs, handheld consoles and laptops. One row per product. You fill short answers from the product page and the manufacturer's data sheet, and the formulas return the Annex Ia point and start date, a build check (USB-C receptacle, USB Power Delivery above 15 W), a charger offer check (a version without a charger must also be offered), what the product page must show close to the price (the charger pictogram and the power label, with the watts filled in), a to-do list with the Article behind each line and a result (OK, FIX PAGE, FIX OFFER, STOP SELLING, CHECK BY HAND, NOT IN SCOPE or FIX INPUT). Short answer: since 28 December 2024 (laptops since 28 April 2026) the covered devices must have a USB-C receptacle, and devices charging above 5 V, 3 A or 15 W must support USB Power Delivery (Annex Ia Part I to Directive 2014/53/EU). A shop that sells a device with a charger must also offer it without one (Article 3a(1)). When selling online, the shop must show a pictogram saying whether a charger is included and the power label (minimum and maximum watts, “USB PD”) close to the price (Articles 3a(2) and 13(2)). Black Friday and Christmas listings for phones and earbuds are a good moment to check.

Why accountants, bookkeepers and web agencies should look at this now

  • Peak season listings. Phones, earbuds, speakers and consoles are top Black Friday and Christmas gifts. New bundles (“with fast charger”) often go live without a no-charger option or without the pictogram near the price.
  • The page duty sits with the seller. Article 3a(2) applies to every economic operator, and the new subparagraph in Article 13(2) makes distributors ensure the label is “displayed in a visible and legible manner and, in the case of distance selling, close to the price indication”. Web agencies build those price blocks.
  • Laptops joined in April 2026. Stock lists bought before and after 28 April 2026 can mix compliant and older models; the placing-on-the-market date sits in the purchase files the bookkeeper keeps.
  • Market surveillance can act on missing labels. Article 43(1) now lists a pictogram or label not drawn up correctly, missing, or not displayed as required, and a breach of Article 3a(1), as formal non-compliance.
  • It pairs with our energy label sheet for web shops (smartphones and tablets carry an energy label too) and our battery sheet.

Limits and our reading

  • National law decides penalties and enforcement. Member States had to apply the rules from the dates above (Article 2(1) of Directive 2022/2380). We did not read national transposition laws, Commission guidance or FAQs, or the harmonised standards.
  • Our reading, flagged: a product without any radio function is not radio equipment, so Article 3(4) does not apply to it; a device that charges only wirelessly is marked CHECK BY HAND because points 2 and 3 apply only “in so far as” it can be charged by wire; stock placed on the market before the start date is CHECK BY HAND because we did not read a rule on it; we read “close to the price indication” as the same visible area as the price, not a separate tab. The pictogram and label drawings are images in the Official Journal; copy them from there or from the manufacturer, keeping the side “a” at 7 mm or more (Parts III and IV).
  • Not covered: other Radio Equipment Directive rules (CE marking, declaration of conformity, cybersecurity), chargers sold on their own, cables, wireless charging standards still to come (Article 3(4)), the energy label and ecodesign rules for phones and tablets, and battery rules.
  • The scraper gives dated product data only. It does not decide whether a product complies.

FAQ

Does the common charger rule apply to my web shop or only to manufacturers?

Both. The USB-C build rule (Annex Ia Part I) is on the product itself, so a shop should not sell a non-compliant model. The pictogram showing whether a charger is included is an obligation of every economic operator, and for distance selling it must be shown close to the price (Article 3a(2) of Directive 2014/53/EU). Distributors must make sure the power label is displayed close to the price when selling at a distance (Article 13(2), added by Directive (EU) 2022/2380).

Which products are covered and from when?

Handheld mobile phones, tablets, digital cameras, headphones, headsets, handheld videogame consoles, portable speakers, e-readers, keyboards, mice, portable navigation systems and earbuds from 28 December 2024, and laptops from 28 April 2026 (Article 2(1) of Directive (EU) 2022/2380), in so far as they are radio equipment and can be recharged by wired charging.

Must I sell phones without a charger?

No. If you offer a covered product together with a charger, you must also offer it without any charger (Article 3a(1)). You may still sell bundles. The Commission must report by 28 December 2026 on whether to propose mandatory unbundling (Article 47(3)); that is a report, not a new rule for shops.

What is the power label?

A label set out in Annex Ia Part IV that shows the minimum power the device needs to charge and the maximum power needed for its maximum charging speed, in watts, plus "USB PD" if it supports USB Power Delivery. The figures come from the manufacturer; the shop shows the label close to the price.

When is USB Power Delivery required?

When the device can be charged by wire at more than 5 volts, more than 3 amperes or more than 15 watts, it must incorporate USB Power Delivery and any other fast-charging protocol must allow USB PD's full functionality (Annex Ia Part I point 3).

Does the sheet cover old stock placed on the market before the dates?

It flags it as CHECK BY HAND. The Directive sets the application dates but we did not read a provision or guidance on stock placed on the market earlier, so we do not give an answer for it. Ask your national market surveillance authority.
