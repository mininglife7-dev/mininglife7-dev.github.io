Your AI tool got a customer's details wrong: a GDPR Article 16 correction request sheet for small EU businesses

Updated 22 Oct 2026 · Disclosure: the EURO AI Register mentioned below is an Unadhi product with a free edition. This guide is for information only, not legal advice.

What you get: a finished correction request sheet for the day a customer, client or job applicant says your AI tool holds something wrong about them: an 11-line request log (deadline, identity, what is wrong, the pause while you check, what you changed, who you told), a search list of 8 places AI tools keep wrong facts, the message to send your AI supplier, and three replies to copy (corrected, more time needed, not changed).

The short answer

Under GDPR Article 16 a person can have inaccurate personal data about them corrected without undue delay, and incomplete data completed, including by adding their own statement. It does not matter that an AI tool wrote the wrong fact: once you keep or use it, the accuracy principle applies to you (Article 5(1)(d): every reasonable step must be taken to erase or rectify inaccurate data without delay). Answer within one month (Article 12(3)), pause use of the disputed data while you check if they ask (Article 18(1)(a)), and tell the suppliers and others you shared it with (Article 19).

A disclaimer is not a fix. The EDPB ChatGPT Taskforce report (23 May 2024) said that telling users output may be biased or made up helps with transparency but is not sufficient to comply with the data accuracy principle. Keep the “AI can make mistakes” line on your chatbot, and still correct wrong facts when you are told.

Message to your AI supplier

Subject: Correction request under GDPR Article 16, [our reference]
We have received a correction request from a person whose data you process for us under our agreement. Please (1) tell us where you hold data about [identifier, e.g. email address or customer ID] in [tool name], including logs and stored outputs, (2) apply this correction: [old] → [new], or delete the wrong record if it cannot be edited, and (3) confirm in writing by [date]. Please also tell us whether any stored output will be regenerated from the old data.

Three replies to copy

A. Corrected. Thank you for telling us. We have corrected [the fact] to [the correct fact] in [our customer records / our call notes / our assistant’s saved answers] on [date], and asked [supplier] to do the same. We have also told [recipients]. If you see the old information again, please reply to this email.

B. More time needed. We received your request on [date]. Because [reason, e.g. we need to check records held by two suppliers], we need up to two more months and will reply by [date]. Until then we have stopped using the disputed information except to store it.

C. Not changed. We checked [the fact] against [source, e.g. the signed order of 3 March] and it is accurate, so we have not changed it. We have added your statement to the record so that anyone who reads it sees your view. You can complain to [supervisory authority] and you can go to court if you disagree.

Do this week

  • Name one person who handles correction requests and put the request log in a shared folder
  • For each AI tool, write down where its outputs about people are saved (use the search list)
  • Check whether each tool lets you edit or delete a stored output, and how to ask the supplier if not
  • Check your supplier contracts include help with data subject requests (Article 28(3)(e))
  • Add the “AI can make mistakes, tell us and we will correct it” line and a contact address to your chatbot and privacy notice
