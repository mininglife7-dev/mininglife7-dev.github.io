Short answer: before a consumer is bound by an online order, Article 6(1)(c) requires "the geographical address at which the trader is established as well as the trader's telephone number and email address", plus details of any other online channel that keeps a dated written record, and all channels must let the consumer "contact the trader quickly and communicate with him efficiently". Article 21 says a consumer calling the trader's line about a concluded contract "is not bound to pay more than the basic rate", which the Court of Justice read as no more than a standard geographic landline or mobile call (C-568/15). The burden of proof for this information is on the trader (Article 6(9)).

Why accountants, bookkeepers and web agencies should look at this now

  • Premium-rate hotlines are a clear-cut fix. In C-568/15 a German trader's support line for existing customers began with 0180 and, per the order for reference, cost EUR 0.14 a minute from a landline and EUR 0.42 from a mobile, more than a standard call (para 9). The ruling is short: calls about a concluded contract may not cost more than a standard geographic or mobile call.
  • The wording changed. Directive (EU) 2019/2161 replaced point (c) and removed the fax number (recital 46). The old text said phone and fax "where they are available"; the new text names "the trader's telephone number and email address". Older shop templates and terms often still follow the old wording.
  • The withdrawal instructions are a common gap. If the shop uses the model instructions, note 2 of Annex I(A) asks for name, geographical address, telephone number and email address, and C-266/19 says a phone number shown on the website must go in there too.
  • It is a quick, billable check. About five minutes per shop once the scraper has run: one look at the checkout, the terms and the withdrawal page, six answers, and the sheet writes the fix list.

Step 3: the five-minute checklist per shop

  1. Open the checkout up to the last step before paying (stop there) and the terms linked from it. Is the trader's geographic address there? (Column E.) A contact page alone may not be enough if it is not part of what the consumer sees before ordering; check that the terms or checkout carry it.
  2. Are the telephone number and email address there too? (F and G.) Compare with what the scraper found in C and D.
  3. Is there a chat, contact form or messenger? Does the consumer get a copy with date and time (for example an email copy of the message)? (H.)
  4. Which number do existing customers call? Look it up on the phone bill or with the provider and pick its type. (I.)
  5. Open the withdrawal instructions. If they follow the EU model text, is the phone number there next to the address and email? (J.)
  6. Take dated screenshots of each page (Article 6(9)).

Frequently asked questions

Does an EU web shop have to show a telephone number before the order?

Article 6(1)(c) of Directive 2011/83/EU, as replaced by Directive (EU) 2019/2161 (applied from 28 May 2022), lists 'the geographical address at which the trader is established as well as the trader's telephone number and email address'. Before that change the text said 'where available', and in C-649/17 (10 July 2019) the Court of Justice held that traders did not have to set up a new telephone line. Whether that reasoning still holds under the new wording is not answered by the texts we read; check national law.

Can a customer hotline be a premium-rate number?

Not for calls about a contract already concluded. Article 21 says the consumer contacting the trader by telephone in relation to the contract 'is not bound to pay more than the basic rate', and the Court of Justice held that such calls may not exceed the cost of a call to a standard geographic landline or mobile telephone line; whether the trader makes a profit is irrelevant if that limit is kept (C-568/15, 2 March 2017).

Must the phone number also appear in the withdrawal instructions?

If the trader uses the model instructions in Annex I(A), note 2 says to insert name, geographical address, telephone number and email address. In C-266/19 (14 May 2020) the Court held that a phone number shown on the website so that an average consumer would think it is used for consumer contact is 'available' and must also appear in those instructions.

Is a contact form or chat enough instead of phone and email?

Article 6(1)(c) says that where the trader provides other means of online communication that let the consumer keep the written correspondence with date and time on a durable medium, details of those means must be given too, and that all means must let the consumer contact the trader quickly and communicate efficiently. Our reading: a form or chat is an addition, not a replacement for the telephone number and email address the text lists.
