Short answer: since 3 December 2018 (Article 11(1)), Regulation (EU) 2018/302 says a trader must not, for reasons related to a customer's nationality, place of residence or place of establishment: block or limit access to its website or app (Article 3(1)); redirect the customer to a country version without explicit consent, and after a consented redirect the original version must stay easily accessible (Article 3(2)); apply different general conditions of access, including net prices, when the customer buys goods delivered to (or collected in) a Member State the trader already offers, buys electronically supplied services, or receives services at a physical place where the trader operates (Article 4(1)); or apply different payment conditions within the means of payment it accepts (Article 5(1)). It does not oblige a shop to deliver to new countries (recital 28), and it does not apply to purely internal situations within one Member State (Article 1(2)).

Why accountants, bookkeepers and web agencies should look at this now

  • Many small shops block or redirect by IP address without knowing it. A plugin, a CDN rule or a shop-builder setting sends visitors from other countries away or hides the checkout. Recital 18 names IP-address and satellite-location tracking as examples of the technological measures Article 3(1) is about.
  • "German customers only" is not the same as "delivery to Germany only". A shop may keep its delivery area. What it may not do is refuse a customer from another EU country who wants the goods delivered inside that area or collected where the shop offers collection (Article 4(1)(a)).
  • Card refusals are an easy fix. Shops are free to choose which means of payment they accept (recital 32), but once they accept a card brand and category, a card issued in another EU country must be treated the same (Article 5(1)).
  • It is a quick, billable check. About 15 minutes per shop: one test visit, one test checkout to the payment step, and the sheet writes the fix list for the client or their web designer.

  2. Optional: run the Contact Details Scraper on the same homepages to get each client shop's role mailbox (info@, shop@) for sending the fix list. Personal addresses are dropped by default.

Step 3: test-visit checklist (about 10 minutes per shop)

  • Open the shop as a visitor from another EU country (a colleague abroad, or a network location in another Member State). Write H: does the shop open, block, hide the checkout or jump to another country version?
  • If it jumps: was there a choice first ("Stay on example.de / Go to example.fr")? Can you get back to the version you opened? Write I and J.
  • If it blocks: does a notice name the law that requires the block, in the page language? Write K. If yes, the result is CHECK BY HAND: confirm that the law exists and applies (Art 3(3)).
  • Go to checkout with a billing address in another EU country and a delivery address in a country the shop serves. Can you order? Do price, shipping and terms stay the same? Write L and M.
  • At the payment step, is a card of an accepted brand and category, or a SEPA debit, from another EU country accepted? Write N. Stop before paying.

Copy-ready country-version banner (redirect with consent)

It looks like you are visiting from [France].
[Go to example.fr]   [Stay on example.de]
You can switch country version at any time at the bottom of every page.

Article 3(2) asks for explicit consent before a redirect; recital 20 says the consent can be remembered for later visits (for example in an account preference) and can be withdrawn at any time, and all versions should stay easily accessible.

Copy-ready "why we block" notice (only where a law requires it)

We cannot show [product group] to visitors in [Member State].
Reason: [name and article of the Union or national law that requires this].
This notice is shown in the language of the page you opened.

Article 3(3) allows a block, limit or redirect only where it is necessary to comply with a legal requirement in Union law or in national law in line with Union law, and the explanation must be "clear and specific", in the language of the interface the customer first opened. Do not use this notice for commercial reasons.

Frequently asked questions

Does a web shop have to deliver to every EU country?

No. Recital 28 of Regulation (EU) 2018/302 says the Regulation does not impose an obligation to deliver goods cross-border where the trader would not otherwise offer delivery. Under Article 4(1)(a), the shop must sell on the same terms to a customer from another EU country who wants the goods delivered to a Member State the shop already delivers to, or collected at an agreed place the shop offers.

Can a shop have different prices in different countries?

Yes, within limits. Article 4(2) allows general conditions of access, including net sale prices, that differ between Member States and are offered on a non-discriminatory basis to customers in a specific territory or group. What Article 4(1) forbids is applying different conditions because of the customer's nationality, residence or establishment, for example charging a French resident more on the German version for the same delivery.

Is redirecting visitors to a country version allowed?

Only with the customer's explicit consent, and the version first opened must remain easily accessible (Article 3(2)). Recital 20 says the consent can be remembered for later visits and withdrawn at any time. Without consent, a redirect is allowed only where a legal requirement makes it necessary, with a clear and specific explanation (Article 3(3)).

Does the Regulation apply to sales to businesses?

Yes, where the business buys for the sole purpose of end use (Article 2(13)). Purchases for resale are outside the definition of customer. Purely internal situations, where all elements are in one Member State, are also outside the Regulation (Article 1(2)).
