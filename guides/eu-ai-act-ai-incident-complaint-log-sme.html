AI incident and complaint log for small EU businesses: one sheet for AI mistakes, customer complaints and serious incidents (EU AI Act, 2026)

Updated 20 Oct 2026 · Disclosure: the EURO AI Register mentioned below is an Unadhi product with a free edition. This guide is for information only, not legal advice.

What you get: a finished AI incident and complaint log you can paste into Google Sheets or Excel: one row for every AI mistake, customer complaint or "why did the AI decide this?" request, with what happened, who was affected, a triage level (A to D), who you told and when, your reply, and the date it was closed. Plus a four-level triage table, a who-to-tell-by-when table taken from Articles 26(5), 73, 85 and 86 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, and short reply wording you can reuse.

Why a small business should keep one now

  • Anyone can complain to the authority. Under Article 85, any person who thinks the AI Act has been broken may complain to the relevant market surveillance authority. The Commission's AI Act Service Desk FAQ says national enforcement powers started on 2 August 2026 for the rules already in force, such as the prohibited practices and the Article 50 transparency rules (chatbot notices, deepfake labels).
  • Serious-incident duties are coming for high-risk use. If you deploy a high-risk AI system (for example AI that screens job applicants or scores credit, see the Annex III check), Article 26(5) tells you to monitor it, suspend it and tell the provider and the authority when it presents a risk, and to report serious incidents. After the Digital Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744), the high-risk rules for Annex III systems apply from 2 December 2027, and from 2 August 2028 for high-risk AI built into products under Annex I.
  • A log is how you prove you reacted. When a customer, staff member or authority asks "what did you do when the chatbot gave wrong advice?", a dated row with the action and the closing date is a far better answer than memory.

Step 1: sort every report into one of four levels

LevelWhat it looks likeWhat you do
A. Everyday errorChatbot gives a wrong opening time; AI writing tool invents a product feature; AI translation garbles an email. Nobody harmed.Fix it, note the fix, tell the tool's provider if it repeats. Close within your normal service time.
B. Complaint about how you use AI"I didn't know I was talking to a bot." "This ad photo is fake." "Why was my application rejected by a computer?"Reply to the person, check your chatbot notice or AI content labels, fix the gap, record the reply date. If it is an explanation request about a high-risk decision, see Article 86 below.
C. The tool may present a riskA high-risk system used as instructed keeps producing results that could harm health, safety or people's fundamental rights, for example rejecting every applicant over a certain age.For high-risk systems: suspend use, and without undue delay tell the provider or distributor and the market surveillance authority (Article 26(5)). For other tools: stop using it for that task and tell the provider.
D. Possible serious incidentAn AI malfunction that directly or indirectly led to death or serious harm to health, a serious and irreversible disruption of critical infrastructure, a breach of EU law protecting fundamental rights, or serious harm to property or the environment (Article 3(49)).Immediately tell the provider first, then the importer or distributor and the market surveillance authority (Article 26(5)). Keep the evidence and the system logs. Get legal advice.

Most small businesses will only ever log level A and B rows. That is fine: the point is that you notice, act and can show it.

Step 2: who to tell, and by when

Not all facts known yetReporterAn incomplete initial report may be sent first, followed by a complete one (Article 73(5))

The Commission also published a draft serious-incident reporting template with that consultation. If you ever reach level D, ask the provider which authority and form to use in your country; national market surveillance authorities differ, so check the official source for yours.

Give every row a number and never delete a row; mark it closed instead. Store screenshots, emails and the AI tool's own logs next to the row. For high-risk systems, Article 26(6) asks deployers to keep the logs the system generates, to the extent they control them, for at least six months unless other law says otherwise, so do not let the tool's logs expire while a row is still open.

Ready-to-use reply wording

SituationShort reply
Level A or B complaint"Thank you for telling us. Our AI assistant gave you wrong information about [topic]. The correct position is [answer]. We have corrected the assistant and logged your report (ref. [no.])."
"Was that a bot?""Yes, that chat was with an AI assistant. You can reach a person at [email / phone / hours]. We have made the notice clearer at the start of the chat."
Explanation request (Article 86)"A [tool type] helped us with [step]. The decision was made by [role] on [date]. The main elements were [criteria]. A person has reviewed your file again on [date]. You can ask further questions at [contact]."
Telling the provider (level C or D)"We are a deployer of [system, version]. On [date] we observed [facts]. We have suspended use from [date]. Please confirm receipt, tell us whether you will report this to the market surveillance authority, and send the reference."

Checklist

  • Every AI tool you use is listed in your AI inventory, so a log row can name it exactly (SME AI inventory template)
  • One named owner for the log, and staff know where to report an AI mistake (part of your staff AI use policy)
  • Customers have a way to reach a person and to complain; the chatbot says it is AI
  • For each high-risk tool: the provider's contact for incidents, and your market surveillance authority, written down in advance (AI vendor questions)
  • Human oversight and system logs kept for high-risk tools (oversight log · record-keeping guide)
  • Personal data in a complaint handled under your data-protection rules; a data breach may also need its own GDPR notification

This log is a starting point, not a legal opinion or proof of compliance. Other reporting duties (data protection, product safety, sector rules) can apply to the same event, with their own deadlines. Dates and duties above were checked against the AI Act text, the Commission's AI Act Service Desk and the Digital Omnibus on 20 October 2026; check the official source before acting on a real incident. At your next review, ask "has a customer, employee or applicant complained about an AI tool you use this year, and where did you write it down?", note the answer with a date, and hand them this log.
