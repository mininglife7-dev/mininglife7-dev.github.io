Short answer: since 27 September 2026 EU Member States must apply the national rules transposing Directive (EU) 2024/825 (Article 4(1)). It adds four green practices to the list of commercial practices that are unfair "in all circumstances" in Annex I to the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive 2005/29/EC: a sustainability label not based on a certification scheme or not established by public authorities (point 2a); a generic environmental claim such as "eco-friendly" or "green" where the trader cannot demonstrate recognised excellent environmental performance relevant to the claim (point 4a); a claim about the whole product or business when it concerns only one aspect (point 4b); and a claim, based on offsetting greenhouse gas emissions, that a product has a neutral, reduced or positive impact on the environment in terms of emissions (point 4c). Future claims such as "net zero by 2030" need a detailed, realistic, regularly verified plan (new Article 6(2)(d)). Check your national law for the exact wording and penalties.

Frequently asked questions

What counts as a "generic environmental claim"?

Under Article 2(p) of Directive 2005/29/EC, as inserted by Directive (EU) 2024/825, it is an environmental claim, in written or oral form, that is not on a sustainability label and whose specification is not given in clear and prominent terms on the same medium. Recital 9 gives examples: "environmentally friendly", "eco-friendly", "green", "nature's friend", "ecological", "environmentally correct", "climate friendly", "gentle on the environment", "carbon friendly", "energy efficient", "biodegradable" and "biobased". It also says "climate-friendly packaging" is generic, while "100 % of energy used to produce this packaging comes from renewable sources" is specific.

When can a shop still say "eco-friendly"?

When it can demonstrate recognised excellent environmental performance relevant to the claim (Annex I point 4a): performance under the EU Ecolabel Regulation (EC) No 66/2010, a national or regional EN ISO 14024 type I ecolabel officially recognised in a Member State, or top performance under other EU law (Article 2(s)). Recital 10 adds that "energy efficient" could rest on class A under Regulation (EU) 2017/1369, and that "sustainable", "conscious" or "responsible" should not rest only on environmental performance because they also cover social characteristics.

Can we still say "climate neutral" because we buy carbon credits?

No for the product: Annex I point 4c bans claiming, based on offsetting greenhouse gas emissions, that a product has a neutral, reduced or positive impact in terms of emissions. Recital 12 says such claims are allowed only when based on the actual lifecycle impact of the product, and that companies may still advertise investments in environmental initiatives, including carbon credit projects, if the information is not misleading.

Does this apply to shops selling only to businesses?

Directive (EU) 2024/825 amends Directive 2005/29/EC, which concerns unfair business-to-consumer commercial practices. A shop selling only to businesses should check its national law, which may go further.
