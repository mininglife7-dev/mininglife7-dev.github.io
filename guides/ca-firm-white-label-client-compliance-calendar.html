What belongs on a white-label client compliance calendar for CA firms (GST, TDS, MCA)

Updated 13 Oct 2026 · Disclosure: the white-label calendar linked below is an Unadhi product (₹999/year).

Short answer: put only the dates small clients ask about, mark extensions in plain words, and put your firm name (and contact line) on the page. Skip niche forms that never apply. A one-pager clients can pin or forward beats a 40-row spreadsheet they ignore.

The dates that drive most client calls

BlockWhat to showWhy clients ask
GSTGSTR-1 / IFF, GSTR-3B (monthly 20th; QRMP 22nd or 24th by state), PMT-06 / CMP-08 where relevant, GSTR-7 if you deduct TDS under GST"When is 3B due?" is the most common call before the 20th
TDS / TCSMonthly deposit due dates, quarterly statement due dates, and certificate issue dates (new 2025 Act form numbers: 138, 140, 144, 143, 131, 133)Clients miss deposits more often than returns

How to show extensions without confusing anyone

  • Keep the original statutory date, then add one line: "Extended to DD Mon YYYY (CBDT Circular … / CBIC …)".
  • Example still live in Oct 2026: CBDT Circular 07/2026 moved the tax-audit report to 21 Oct 2026 and the audit-case ITR to 21 Nov 2026 for non-transfer-pricing cases.
  • Link or name the official source on that row so your staff can re-check in ten seconds.

What "white-label" should mean on the page

  • Header: your firm name exactly as clients know it, plus a contact line (phone, email or website).
  • Body: the quarter's dates in large, scannable type — not a wall of statute text.
  • Formats clients use: a one-page PDF to email, an editable DOCX if staff need to add a client-specific row, and a WhatsApp-sized image for the practice group chat.
  • Importable .ics: so clients who want reminders can add the national dates to Google Calendar, Outlook or their phone in one step.

You can build all of this from the GST portal and CBDT notices. The work is the checking and the formatting, four times a year.
