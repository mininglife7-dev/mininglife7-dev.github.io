Short answer: Directive (EU) 2023/2673 inserted Article 11a into the Consumer Rights Directive 2011/83/EU. Member States had to adopt the rules by 19 December 2025 and apply them from 19 June 2026 (Article 2(1) of Directive 2023/2673). Since then, for distance contracts concluded through an online interface such as a website or app, the trader must let the consumer withdraw through a withdrawal function labelled "withdraw from contract here" (or an unambiguous corresponding wording), continuously available during the withdrawal period, prominently displayed and easily accessible. It leads to an online statement and a "confirm withdrawal" button, and the trader must send an acknowledgement on a durable medium with the content, date and time without undue delay. Recital 37 says this applies to all distance contracts with a right of withdrawal, not only financial services.

Why accountants, bookkeepers and web agencies should look at this now

  • It is new and easy to miss. The amending directive is titled "financial services contracts concluded at a distance", so many shop owners never read it. Article 11a still reaches an ordinary clothes or furniture shop.
  • A missing withdrawal notice is expensive. If the shop does not give the withdrawal information required by Article 6(1)(h), the 14-day period is extended by 12 months (Article 10(1)), and the consumer is not liable for diminished value of the goods (Article 14(2)).
  • Phone and email are both required. Since Directive (EU) 2019/2161, Article 6(1)(c) asks for the geographical address, the telephone number and the email address. A shop with only a contact form fails this line.
  • It is a quick, billable check. One hour per client shop, with a clear fix list for the shop builder.

Step 3: tab 2, products (one row per product from the product scraper)

If an exception applies, the shop must say so before the order (Article 6(1)(k)). Text for the shop's withdrawal instructions

Annex I(A) of Directive 2011/83/EU, instruction 3 as replaced by Directive (EU) 2023/2673, gives this sentence for traders that must provide the function: "You can also exercise your right of withdrawal online at [insert internet address or another appropriate explanation of where the withdrawal function is available]. If you use this online feature, we will send you an acknowledgement of receipt of the withdrawal on a durable medium (e.g. by email), including its content and the date and time of its submission, without undue delay." Use your national language version of the model instructions.

National law and penalties

Member States set the penalties (Article 24 of Directive 2011/83/EU as replaced by Directive (EU) 2019/2161). They must be effective, proportionate and dissuasive. For widespread infringements handled in coordinated action under Regulation (EU) 2017/2394, the maximum fine must be at least 4% of the trader's annual turnover in the Member States concerned, or at least EUR 2 million where turnover information is not available. National rules, the exact national button wording and whether your country transposed on time all differ: check your national law.

Frequently asked questions

Does the withdrawal button apply to ordinary web shops, not only banks?

Yes. Article 11a covers distance contracts concluded by means of an online interface, and Recital 37 of Directive (EU) 2023/2673 says the obligation should apply not only to distance contracts for financial services but to all distance contracts subject to the right of withdrawal under Directive 2011/83/EU.

From when does it apply?

Member States had to adopt and publish the measures by 19 December 2025 and apply them from 19 June 2026 (Article 2(1) of Directive (EU) 2023/2673).

Is an email address for withdrawals enough?

Not for contracts concluded through an online interface. The consumer can still use the model form or any other unequivocal statement (Article 11(1)), but Article 11a(1) says the trader must ensure the consumer can also withdraw by using a withdrawal function.

Can the shop let customers withdraw from only part of an order?

Recital 37 says that where several goods or services are ordered in the same distance contract, the trader can offer the possibility to withdraw from a part rather than the whole contract.
