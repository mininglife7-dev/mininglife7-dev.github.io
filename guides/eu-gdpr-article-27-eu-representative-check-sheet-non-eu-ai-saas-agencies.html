Selling to EU customers from outside the EU? A GDPR Article 27 check sheet: do you need an EU representative?

Updated 24 Oct 2026 · Disclosure: the EURO AI Register mentioned below is an Unadhi product with a free edition. This guide is for information only, not legal advice.

What you get: a finished EU representative check sheet for businesses based outside the EU: an eight-signal targeting test, the three-part exemption test, a 10-column sheet with one row per service and a result formula, three filled example rows, a written mandate checklist and the privacy notice line to add.

Short answer: if you have no office in the EU but offer goods or services to people in the EU, or track their behaviour there, GDPR applies to that processing (Article 3(2)) and you must name an EU representative in writing (Article 27(1)), unless your processing is occasional, involves no large-scale health or criminal data, and is unlikely to create a risk. A website people in the EU can open is not enough on its own.

Step 1: are you offering services to people in the EU?

Recital 23 and the EDPB guidelines list signs of intent. One sign alone may not be enough; weigh them together. Mere accessibility of your site, an email address, or a language generally used in your own country does not count.

Read it this way: no Y at all means Article 3(2) is probably not met; write down why. Several Y, or signal 8 Y, means GDPR applies to that service and you go to Step 2.

Step 2: the exemption test (all three must be yes)

  • Occasional: not carried out regularly and outside the regular course of your business (EDPB). A live SaaS product or a monthly retainer for EU clients is regular.
  • No large-scale special category or criminal data: no large-scale health, biometric or other Article 9(1) data and no Article 10 data
  • Unlikely to result in a risk to people's rights and freedoms, given the nature, context, scope and purposes

Public authorities and bodies are also exempt (Article 27(2)(b)).

Step 4: if you need one, the written mandate checklist

  • Designate the representative in writing (Article 27(1); Recital 80 calls it a written mandate) and keep the signed copy
  • Pick a person or company established in a Member State where your EU users are (Article 27(3)); where many are in one country, the EDPB recommends that country
  • Mandate them to be addressed in addition to or instead of you by supervisory authorities and data subjects on all processing issues (Article 27(4))
  • Give them your records of processing and keep them updated; they must keep and produce the record (Article 30; EDPB section 4)
  • Make sure they can communicate in the languages of the authorities and people concerned (EDPB section 4)
  • Do not use your external EU DPO as representative; the EDPB sees the roles as incompatible
  • Remember it does not shift liability: legal actions can still be brought against you (Article 27(5))

Step 5: the privacy notice line

Our representative in the European Union under Article 27 GDPR is [name], [postal address in Member State], [email]. You and supervisory authorities can contact them, in addition to or instead of us, on any question about how we process personal data.

Articles 13(1)(a) and 14(1)(a) require the representative's identity and contact details in your notice, and Article 30(1)(a) and 30(2)(a) put them in your records. See the Article 13 AI wording sheet and Article 30 records sheet.
