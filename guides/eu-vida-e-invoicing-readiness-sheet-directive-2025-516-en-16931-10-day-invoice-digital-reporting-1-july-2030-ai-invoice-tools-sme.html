Short answer: Council Directive (EU) 2025/516 ("VAT in the Digital Age", ViDA) changes the VAT Directive in stages. Since 14 April 2025 a Member State may require businesses established there to issue electronic invoices for domestic supplies without the buyer's agreement (new Articles 218 and 232, second paragraphs). From 1 July 2030, invoices are by default electronic invoices in a structured format that complies with the European standard EN 16931, intra-EU B2B supplies and reverse-charge supplies must be invoiced within 10 days, and the data from each invoice is reported transaction by transaction (new Articles 217, 218, 222, 262 to 264). A PDF is not an electronic invoice under the new definition, whether it was typed by a person or read by an AI tool.

Why accountants and bookkeepers should look at this now

  • Software contracts signed now will still run in 2030. A client choosing invoicing or bookkeeping software this year should ask whether it can issue and receive EN 16931 invoices in one of the two official syntaxes (UBL or UN/CEFACT CII).
  • Some deadlines come earlier. From 1 July 2028 the 15th-of-next-month invoice deadline in Article 222 also covers domestic reverse-charge supplies under Article 194; call-off stock dispatches stop at 30 June 2028 and Article 17a ends on 30 June 2029; and the deemed-supplier rule for short-term accommodation and road passenger transport sold through online marketplaces applies from a national date between 1 July 2028 and 1 January 2030.
  • Your own Member State may move first. The Directive lets Member States require domestic e-invoices before 2030. Those national dates are not in the Directive: put them in column M of the client tab from your national tax authority.
  • AI invoice reading will change role. Many small firms now use AI or OCR tools to read PDF purchase invoices. Once suppliers must send structured e-invoices, the data arrives machine-readable. Plan to receive the structured file into the bookkeeping software, and keep the AI tool for checking, not for creating the legal record.

The seller reports "at the time when the invoice is issued or should have been issued" (new Art. 263(1)); where the buyer issues the invoice for the seller (self-billing), the limit is 5 days. Member States may exempt buyers from reporting acquisitions (new Art. 262(4)). Before 1 July 2028, domestic Article 194 supplies follow national invoice deadlines. Counting "10 days following" as event date plus 10 calendar days is our working reading: check how your Member State counts days.

What else changes on 1 July 2030 (for the client letter)

  • No buyer consent for EN 16931 e-invoices. Issuing a standard-compliant e-invoice to a business or non-taxable legal person "shall not be subject to acceptance by the recipient" (new Art. 232). Invoices in other formats still need acceptance unless national rules say otherwise.
  • Losing the zero rate. The Article 138 exemption does not apply where the supplier has not reported the transaction data or the data is wrong, unless the supplier "can duly justify any shortcomings" (new Art. 138(1a)).
  • Deduction may depend on the e-invoice. For transactions under domestic digital reporting, a Member State may make the buyer's deduction conditional on holding a compliant electronic invoice (new Art. 168, second paragraph).
  • Third parties can do it. The client or "a third party acting in the name and on account of" the client may issue compliant e-invoices; Member States may also allow a public portal (new Art. 218(5)).
  • Hybrid invoices. An invoice that combines structured data and a readable image counts if all the reportable data is in the structured part (Recital 9).
  • Later domestic alignment. Member States that already had domestic real-time reporting in place on 1 January 2024 have until 1 January 2035 to align their domestic systems (Art. 6(5)).

What to tell a client in one paragraph

"From 1 July 2030, invoices for your sales to businesses in other EU countries must be structured electronic invoices to the European standard, issued within 10 days, and their data goes to the tax authority invoice by invoice. A PDF will not be enough. Before you renew or buy invoicing software, get the supplier to confirm in writing that it issues and receives EN 16931 invoices in UBL or CII, shows your bank account and quotes the original number on credit notes. If you use an AI tool to read supplier PDFs, plan to receive the structured file instead. Your own country may require e-invoices earlier, so we will check national law each year." Penalties and national start dates are set by each Member State: check the national VAT law before advising.
