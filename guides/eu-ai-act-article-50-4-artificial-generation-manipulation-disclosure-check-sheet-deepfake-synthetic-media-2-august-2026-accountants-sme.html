Short answer: from 2 August 2026 (AI Act Article 113), a business that deploys an AI system to generate or manipulate image, audio or video that is a deep fake must disclose that the content was artificially generated or manipulated (Article 50(4)). The same article also covers AI-generated or manipulated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest. The disclosure must be clear and distinguishable at the latest at first exposure (Article 50(5)). Do not confuse this with the Digital Omnibus four-month transition to 2 December 2026: that transition (new Article 111(4) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, added by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744) is only for Article 50(2) machine-readable marking by providers of systems placed before 2 August 2026. It does not move the Article 50(4) date.

  • SMEs publish AI ads, social posts, voiceovers and client videos every week. Many treat "AI-generated" as a branding choice; Article 50(4) makes disclosure a legal duty for deep fakes and for public-interest text.

Step 3: checklist

  1. Did we use an AI system that generated or manipulated this piece (deployer under Article 3(4))?
  2. Is the piece image, audio or video that could be a deep fake under Article 3(60), or text published to inform the public on a matter of public interest (Article 50(4))?
  3. Is any law-enforcement authorisation exception claimed, and do we have the basis on file?
  4. If it is an evidently artistic, creative, satirical or fictional work, have we still disclosed the existence of generated or manipulated content in an appropriate way (Article 50(4); recital 134)?
  5. For public-interest text, was there human review or editorial control, and who holds editorial responsibility?
  6. Is the disclosure clear, distinguishable and shown at the latest at first exposure, and does it meet accessibility requirements (Article 50(5))?
  7. Have we kept a screenshot or copy of how it appeared?
  8. Are we mixing this up with Article 50(2) marking (provider duty, Omnibus transition to 2 December 2026)? Keep those on the separate marking sheet.
  9. Have we told the client that Article 50(4) has applied since 2 August 2026?
  10. Who decided, on what date, and when is the next review?

Frequently asked questions

What does Article 50(4) of the AI Act require?

Deployers of an AI system that generates or manipulates image, audio or video content constituting a deep fake must disclose that the content has been artificially generated or manipulated. Deployers that generate or manipulate text published to inform the public on matters of public interest must disclose that the text has been artificially generated or manipulated. The information must be clear and distinguishable at the latest at first interaction or exposure and must meet accessibility requirements (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 50(4) and 50(5)).

What is a deep fake under the AI Act?

Article 3(60) defines a deep fake as AI-generated or manipulated image, audio or video content that resembles existing persons, objects, places, entities or events and would falsely appear to a person to be authentic or truthful. Recital 134 uses the same idea. Purely cartoon or obviously synthetic imagery that would not falsely appear authentic sits outside that definition on our reading of those words.

Does the Digital Omnibus 2 December 2026 date apply to Article 50(4)?

No. Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 added Article 111(4) only for providers of generative systems that must comply with Article 50(2) machine-readable marking: systems placed on the market before 2 August 2026 must take the necessary steps to comply with Article 50(2) by 2 December 2026. We searched the Omnibus text for other Article 50 changes affecting 50(4) and found none. Article 50(4) deployer disclosure follows the general application date of 2 August 2026 in Article 113.

Are there exceptions to Article 50(4)?

Yes. The deep-fake disclosure does not apply where the use is authorised by law to detect, prevent, investigate or prosecute criminal offences. Where the content forms part of an evidently artistic, creative, satirical, fictional or analogous work or programme, the duty is limited to disclosing the existence of such generated or manipulated content in an appropriate manner that does not hamper the display or enjoyment of the work. For public-interest text, the duty does not apply where the use is so authorised, or where the AI-generated content has undergone human review or editorial control and a natural or legal person holds editorial responsibility for the publication (Article 50(4)).
