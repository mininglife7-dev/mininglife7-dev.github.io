Short answer: Directive (EU) 2019/1937 requires private-sector legal entities with 50 or more workers to set up internal reporting channels and follow-up procedures (Article 8(1) and (3)). The 50-worker threshold does not apply to entities within the scope of the Union acts in Parts I.B and II of its Annex, which include financial services and anti-money-laundering rules (Article 8(4)). The channel must protect the reporter's identity, acknowledge a report within seven days and give feedback within three months (Article 9(1)). Reports may be made in writing or orally, or both (Article 9(2)). Under the EU AI Act, the same Directive applies to reports of infringements of the AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 87), so AI tools used in a channel need the same care as the rest of it.

Why clients ask their accountant this question

  • Headcount crosses 50. You see the payroll first. The obligation for entities with 50 to 249 workers had to be in national law by 17 December 2023 (Article 26(2)); for others the transposition date was 17 December 2021 (Article 26(1)).
  • Bank, insurer or investor questionnaires ask whether the company has a whistleblowing channel.
  • Clients want an AI tool to do it: a chatbot for intake, automatic call transcripts, or a general AI assistant to summarise reports. The Directive's confidentiality and record-keeping rules (Articles 16 to 18) still apply.
  • Some small firms are in scope regardless of size because they fall under the financial services or anti-money-laundering acts listed in the Annex (Article 8(4)).

Checklist: the procedure (Article 9 and Article 18)

  • ☐ Channel designed and run securely so the reporter's identity and any third party named stay confidential, with no access for non-authorised staff (Art 9(1)(a)).
  • ☐ Acknowledgement of receipt within seven days (Art 9(1)(b)).
  • ☐ Impartial person or department named to follow up, keep in touch with the reporter, ask for more information and give feedback (Art 9(1)(c)); it may be the same person who receives reports.
  • ☐ Diligent follow-up (Art 9(1)(d)); for anonymous reports, where national law provides for it (Art 9(1)(e), Art 6(2)).
  • ☐ Feedback within three months (Art 9(1)(f)).
  • ☐ Clear, easily accessible information on reporting externally to the competent authorities (Art 9(1)(g), Art 7(3)).
  • ☐ Written and/or oral reporting; oral by phone or voice messaging, and a physical meeting on request within a reasonable time (Art 9(2)).
  • ☐ Recorded phone line: recording or a complete and accurate transcript only with the reporter's consent, and the reporter can check, rectify and sign the transcript (Art 18(2)). Unrecorded line: accurate minutes, which the reporter can check, rectify and sign (Art 18(3)). Meetings: records only with consent (Art 18(4)).
  • ☐ Reports stored no longer than necessary and proportionate (Art 18(1)); personal data clearly not relevant to a report not collected, or deleted without undue delay (Art 17).
  • ☐ Third-party provider running the channel applies the same Article 9(1) safeguards (Art 8(5)).
  • ☐ Shared receipt and investigation only for entities with 50 to 249 workers, and each entity still keeps confidentiality, gives feedback and addresses the breach (Art 8(6)).
  • ☐ No contract, policy or arbitration clause waives or limits the rights (Art 24).

When AI tools touch the channel

  • Call transcription: Article 18(2) allows a recording or a "complete and accurate transcript" prepared by the staff members responsible, and only with consent. If an AI tool drafts the transcript, a responsible staff member should check it, and the reporter must be offered the chance to check, rectify and sign it. See our AI meeting note-taker setup sheet.
  • Chatbot intake: the identity must not reach anyone beyond authorised staff (Article 16(1)), and data clearly irrelevant to the report should not be collected (Article 17). The chatbot's provider must design it so people know they are interacting with an AI system unless that is obvious (AI Act Article 50(1)); see our Article 50 chatbot disclosure checklist.
  • General AI assistants: pasting a report into a general-purpose AI tool shares its content, and possibly the reporter's identity, with a further service. Check this against Articles 9(1)(a) and 16(1) before anyone does it, and check the supplier contract under the GDPR (Article 17 of the Directive points to Regulation (EU) 2016/679); see our processor agreement check sheet and retention schedule for chat logs and transcripts.
  • Reports about AI itself: the Directive applies to reports of infringements of the AI Act (AI Act Article 87), so a worker who reports a suspected AI Act infringement is protected under the same rules, and the client's channel should be ready to receive such reports.

For managers: what counts as retaliation (Article 19)

Retaliation, threats of it and attempts at it are prohibited, including: suspension, dismissal or equivalent; demotion or withheld promotion; transfer of duties, change of location, reduced wages or changed hours; withheld training; negative performance assessment or reference; disciplinary measures or financial penalties; coercion, intimidation, harassment or ostracism; discrimination or unfair treatment; not converting or not renewing a temporary contract, or ending it early; harm to reputation (including on social media) or financial loss; blacklisting; early termination of a contract for goods or services; cancellation of a licence or permit; psychiatric or medical referrals (Article 19(a)-(o)). In court, a detriment after a report is presumed to be retaliation unless the employer proves duly justified grounds (Article 21(5)). Protection extends to facilitators, colleagues and relatives who could suffer retaliation (Article 4(4)).

Limits and open points

This is a Directive: companies follow their national transposing law, which can be stricter (Article 25(1)), can require channels below 50 workers (Article 8(7)), decides whether anonymous reports must be accepted (Article 6(2)), and sets penalties for hindering reports, retaliation and breaches of confidentiality (Article 23). The Directive covers breaches of the Union law areas in Article 2 and its Annex; Member States may extend protection to other areas (Article 2(2)). Where sector acts in Part II of the Annex have their own reporting rules, those apply first (Article 3(1)). The Annex is a dynamic list, so check the current consolidated version on EUR-Lex. We read the Directive as published in OJ L 305 of 26.11.2019. For the national rules, get advice and check the national authority's guidance.

Frequently asked questions

Which companies must have a whistleblowing channel?

Private-sector legal entities with 50 or more workers (Article 8(3)), and, regardless of size, entities within the scope of the Union acts in Parts I.B and II of the Annex, such as financial services and anti-money-laundering rules (Article 8(4)). Member States may require it for smaller entities after a risk assessment (Article 8(7)).

What are the deadlines for handling a report?

Acknowledge receipt within seven days and give feedback within a reasonable timeframe of no more than three months from the acknowledgement, or from the end of the seven-day period if no acknowledgement was sent (Article 9(1)(b) and (f)).

Can a small company use an outside provider or share a channel?

Yes. A third party may operate the channel if it applies the Article 9(1) safeguards (Article 8(5)). Entities with 50 to 249 workers may share resources for receiving and investigating reports, but each keeps its duties of confidentiality, feedback and addressing the breach (Article 8(6)).

Can we use AI to transcribe whistleblower calls?

Article 18(2) allows a recording or a complete and accurate transcript only with the reporter's consent, prepared by the staff members responsible, and the reporter must be able to check, rectify and sign it. Treat an AI transcript as a draft that a responsible staff member checks.
