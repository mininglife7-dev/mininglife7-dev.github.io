Short answer (checked 23 Oct 2026): the Code on Wages, 2019 came into force on 21 November 2025 (S.O. 5322(E), sections 1 to 41 and 69) and replaced the Payment of Bonus Act, 1965 (section 69). Under section 39(1) of the Code, bonus must be paid by credit to the employee's bank account within eight months from the close of the accounting year. The accounting year is the year starting 1 April (section 2(a)), so bonus for FY 2025-26 (year ended Tuesday 31 March 2026) is due by Monday 30 November 2026, unless the government has extended the period on the employer's application (total extension never more than two years). For establishments where the Central Government is the appropriate Government, the Ministry of Labour fixed the eligibility ceiling at ₹21,000 a month (S.O. 4711(E)) and the calculation ceiling at ₹7,000 a month or the Central minimum wage, whichever is higher (S.O. 4710(E)), both dated 25 August 2026 and deemed in force from 21 November 2025. For all other establishments the State Government is the appropriate Government (section 2(d)), so use your State's notification. This is a starting point, not legal or tax advice.

Why CA firms should own this date

  • The tax side: section 43B(c) of the Income-tax Act, 1961 allows bonus to employees (section 36(1)(ii)) only in the year it is actually paid, unless it is paid on or before the client's section 139(1) due date for that year and the evidence of payment is furnished with the return (section 43B, Income Tax Department). For audit cases that due date for AY 2026-27 is 21 November 2026 (CBDT Circular No. 07/2026), nine days before the labour-law limit. A client who pays on 30 November is compliant under the Code but loses the FY 2025-26 deduction; it moves to the year of payment.
  • The provision in the accounts: bonus provided for FY 2025-26 and not paid shows up in your tax audit and in the return's section 43B schedule. One sheet keeps the provision, the payment date and the return date together.
  • The penalty side: under section 54(1)(a) of the Code, an employer who pays an employee less than the amount due can be fined up to ₹50,000; a repeat within five years can mean imprisonment up to three months or a fine up to ₹1 lakh, or both (section 54(1)(b)).

Who is covered (section 41 and section 26)

TestRule in the Code
SizeThe chapter applies to an establishment in which 20 or more persons are employed or were employed on any day during the accounting year (section 41(2)).
Excluded employersSection 41(1) lists exclusions, including the LIC, RBI, Indian Red Cross and similar institutions, universities and other educational institutions, and institutions including hospitals, chambers of commerce and social welfare institutions established not for profit; also establishments under a government department or local authority, and any establishment the appropriate Government exempts by notification.
New establishmentsFor the first five accounting years after the employer first sells goods or renders services, bonus is payable only for a year in which the employer derives profit from that establishment (section 26(6)).
EmployeeDrawing wages not above the notified ceiling and has put in at least 30 days' work in the accounting year (section 26(1)). Apprentices under the Apprentices Act, 1961 are not "employees" (section 2(k)).
Days counted as workedLay-off under an agreement or standing orders, leave with wages, temporary disablement from an accident at work, and paid maternity leave (section 28).
DisqualifiedAn employee dismissed for fraud; riotous or violent behaviour on the premises; theft, misappropriation or sabotage of the establishment's property; or conviction for sexual harassment (section 29).
AmountMinimum 8.33% of wages earned or ₹100, whichever is higher, whether or not there is allocable surplus (section 26(1)); up to a maximum of 20% where allocable surplus allows (section 26(3)), and any productivity bonus by agreement must keep the total within 20% (section 26(5)).
Already paid?Puja or other customary bonus, or part of the bonus paid in advance, in the same accounting year can be deducted from the bonus due (section 37). Loss caused by the employee's proven misconduct in that year can also be deducted (section 38).

"Wages" changed. Under section 2(y) wages are basic pay, dearness allowance and retaining allowance, and exclude items such as HRA, conveyance, employer PF contribution, overtime and commission. But if the excluded items (clauses (a) to (i)) are more than one-half of total remuneration, the excess over one-half is added back into wages. So a salary structure with a small basic can now produce a larger bonus base than last year's payroll formula. Run the add-back before you trust the payroll figure.

FY 2025-26 straddles 21 November 2025

The Payment of Bonus Act, 1965 applied until 20 November 2025 and the Code from 21 November 2025; the two Central notifications are deemed in force from that date. The eight-month limit gives the same 30 November 2026 date under the Code. How to split the year (for example, which definition of wages applies to April to November 2025) is a judgement for the client's labour adviser: the sheet works for either answer if you fill column F on the basis you settle on, and keep a note of it.

Notes

  • Check whether your client's State has notified its own bonus ceilings under section 26 before you fill columns F and G; we do not state any State figure here.
  • Where a bonus dispute is pending, section 39(2) gives one month from the award or settlement, but the employer must still pay 8.33% within the eight months.
  • Allocable surplus is 67% of available surplus (60% for a banking company), section 31(1); the gross-profit method is to be prescribed by the Central Government (section 32).
  • The sheet holds employee pay data: share it only inside your firm and with the client.
