EU tyre label check sheet for web shops selling car, van and truck tyres: the label close to the price or nested (Article 6(7)), the product information sheet, marketplace duties (Article 8), severe snow and ice grip under Regulation (EU) 2020/740, before the winter and Black Friday sales, for accountants, bookkeepers and web agencies

Legal facts come from the Official Journal text we read today on the EU Publications Office site (EUR-Lex returns a bot check): Regulation (EU) 2020/740 on the labelling of tyres (OJ L 177, 5.6.2020) and its three corrigenda. The Publications Office lists no act amending it. We did not read national laws, penalties, national winter tyre rules, Commission guidance or FAQs. This guide is for information only, not legal advice.

What you get: a finished EU tyre label check sheet for web shops that sell car, van and truck tyres (classes C1, C2 and C3). One row per tyre listing. You fill short answers from the product page and the supplier's label, and the formulas return a scope check (Article 2 exclusions: studded, below 80 km/h, rim size, second-hand, re-treaded), a label check (shown close to the price, or nested, and legible), an information sheet check, a page text check, a snow and ice check (ice grip statement, “winter” titles without the severe snow pictogram), a to-do list with the Article behind each line and a result (OK, FIX LABEL, FIX SHEET, FIX TEXT, ADD ICE NOTE, CHECK BY HAND, NOT IN SCOPE or FIX INPUT). Also included: a supplier request and a client email. We tested the formulas in LibreOffice on twenty made-up listings, before and after fixes, before publishing.

Short answer: since 1 May 2021 (Article 18), a web shop that resells tyres must show the EU tyre label close to the price and make the product information sheet reachable (Article 6(7)); own-brand sellers and importers have the same duty under Article 4(2). The label may be nested (one click from the price) but must be clearly visible, legible and proportionate to 75 × 110 mm. Marketplaces must enable the label and sheet near the price and tell sellers of the duty (Article 8). Where relevant, websites must carry the ice grip statement (Annex IV point 4). Winter season and Black Friday listings are a good moment to check.

Why accountants, bookkeepers and web agencies should look at this now

  • Winter tyre season. November is when shops add winter and all-season ranges, often by bulk import from a supplier feed. Labels and sheet links are easy to drop in an import.
  • Black Friday price blocks. New sale templates move the price; the label must stay “close to the price indication” (Articles 4(2), 6(7)). Web agencies build those templates.
  • Inspections are routine. Member States must run routine and ad hoc inspections of points of sale (Article 11(4)) and set penalties (Article 11(5)). Penalty amounts are national; we did not read them.
  • Second-hand and imports. Used tyres are out of scope unless imported from a third country (Article 2(2)(h)); the purchase files the bookkeeper keeps show where stock came from.
  • It pairs with our energy label sheet for web shops (the tyre label uses the same EU product database, Article 3(10)) and our product safety page sheet.

FAQ

Does a web shop that resells tyres have to show the EU tyre label?

Yes. For tyres sold or offered for sale on the internet, distributors must ensure that the tyre label is displayed close to the price indication and that the product information sheet can be accessed (Article 6(7) of Regulation (EU) 2020/740). Suppliers (EU manufacturers, importers and authorised representatives) have the same duty for distance selling under Article 4(2).

Can the label sit behind a click?

Yes, for a specific tyre type sold on the internet the label may be made available in a nested display (Articles 4(2) and 6(7)). The label shown must be clearly visible and legible and proportionate to the size in Annex II (minimum 75 mm wide and 110 mm high). We read a label hidden in a separate tab away from the price as not close to the price; the sheet marks it MOVE.

Which tyres are outside the rules?

Article 2(2) excludes off-road professional tyres, tyres only for vehicles first registered before 1 October 1990, T-type temporary-use spares, tyres with a speed rating below 80 km/h, tyres with a nominal rim diameter not over 254 mm or of 635 mm or more, studded tyres, racing-only tyres, and second-hand tyres unless imported from a third country. Requirements for re-treaded tyres apply once a suitable test method is available (Article 2(1)).

What must a marketplace do?

A hosting service provider that allows tyres to be sold through its site must enable the display of the tyre label and the product information sheet provided by the supplier close to the price, and must inform the distributor of the obligation to display them (Article 8).

What is the product information sheet?

A standard document with the supplier's trade name, tyre type identifier, size, load index and speed symbol, fuel efficiency, wet grip and rolling noise classes, the noise value in dB, whether it is a severe snow or ice grip tyre, and the start and end weeks of production (Annex III). Suppliers enter the label and the sheet's parameters into the public part of the EU product database (Article 5, Annex VII point 1).

Has Regulation 2020/740 changed since 2021?

The EU Publications Office metadata we read today lists three corrigenda (Annex I Part C and the Annex II label format) and no amending act; the consolidated version is still the original text. A Commission proposal of 24 June 2026 (52026PC0565) on digital options for energy and tyre labelling exists. We did not read it, and a proposal is not law.
