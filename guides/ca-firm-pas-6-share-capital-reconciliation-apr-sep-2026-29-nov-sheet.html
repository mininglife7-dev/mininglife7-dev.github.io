PAS-6 for April to September 2026 by 29 November: a share capital reconciliation sheet for CA firms

What you get: a ready-to-copy Google Sheet layout for the PAS-6 half-yearly share capital reconciliation for the half year 1 April to 30 September 2026. Tab 1 has one row per company client with a must-file test (unlisted public company, or private company that is not a small company), a due-date formula (29 November 2026), certifier, SRN and a status formula. Tab 2 has one row per ISIN: shares in the register of members against shares held with NSDL, CDSL and in physical form, with a difference formula that flags any gap you must explain in the form. Tab 3 filters the clients still unfiled. You also get a made-up example, a plan for 16 to 29 November and a WhatsApp message for clients.

Short answer (checked 26 Oct 2026): rule 9A(8) of the Companies (Prospectus and Allotment of Securities) Rules, 2014 asks an unlisted public company to file PAS-6, signed off by a practising company secretary or chartered accountant, within 60 days from the end of each half year. Rule 9B(5) applies the same sub-rules to private companies that must dematerialise their shares, which means every private company that is not a small company (government companies are out). Sixty days from 30 September 2026 is Sunday 29 November 2026, so aim to file by Friday 27 November. Since 1 December 2025 a small company is one with paid-up capital up to ₹10 crore and turnover up to ₹100 crore (G.S.R. 880(E)); holding or subsidiary companies and section 8 companies cannot be small companies.

Who files and who does not

  • Unlisted public companies: file under rule 9A, unless the company is a Nidhi, a government company or a wholly-owned subsidiary.
  • Private companies: file under rule 9B unless they are a small company or a government company. Test the small-company limits on the figures section 2(85) uses (paid-up capital, and turnover for the immediately preceding financial year).
  • Companies that became small after the December 2025 change: some clients that were above ₹4 crore / ₹40 crore are now small companies. We did not find an MCA clarification on whether they stop filing PAS-6 at once, so mark them "Check" in the sheet and agree the position with the client's company secretary before you skip a half year.
  • One form per ISIN: a company with equity and preference ISINs files once for each.

Tab "Unfiled": who still has to file

Use =FILTER('Clients'!A2:N,'Clients'!H2:H="Yes",'Clients'!L2:L=""), then sort by number of ISINs so the multi-ISIN clients get the first call. Add columns for the date the RTA promised the reconciliation and the date the signing professional will sign.

Made-up example (not a real client)

"Sample Tools Private Limited" has paid-up capital of ₹12 crore and FY 2025-26 turnover of ₹85 crore. Its capital is above ₹10 crore, so it is not a small company and column H says Yes. It has one equity ISIN: 1,20,00,000 shares in the register, 1,05,00,000 with NSDL, 14,50,000 with CDSL and 50,000 physical. The difference is zero, so column I says MATCH. A second client, "Sample Foods Private Limited", has ₹3 crore capital and ₹60 crore turnover and is a subsidiary of a listed company: it cannot be a small company, so it files too. A third, ₹6 crore capital and ₹70 crore turnover with no holding company, was above the old limits but is now a small company: mark it "Check".

Plan for 16 to 29 November

  • Mon 16 Nov: run the must-file test for every company client and ask each RTA for the 30 September holding position for every ISIN.
  • Fri 20 Nov: fill the ISINs tab; chase any gap until there is a written reason.
  • Wed 25 Nov: the signing professional reviews and signs; file on the MCA V3 portal.
  • Fri 27 Nov: target day. Fill the date filed and SRN columns.
  • Sun 29 Nov: legal last day. Anything unfiled moves to the Unfiled tab as LATE.
