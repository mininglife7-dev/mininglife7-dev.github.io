EU AI Act record-keeping for small business AI users: what to log and what to keep (2026)

Updated 14 Oct 2026 · Disclosure: the EURO AI Register mentioned below is an Unadhi product with a free edition. This guide is for information only, not legal advice.

Short answer: if your business only uses AI tools such as ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot or a vendor's website chatbot, and none of them is used for a high-risk purpose, the EU AI Act does not require you to keep technical AI logs. The deployer log duty in Article 26(6), keeping the logs a system generates automatically for at least six months, covers high-risk AI systems only, and the high-risk rules for Annex III uses (such as hiring, education and credit scoring) apply from 2 December 2027 after the Digital Omnibus on AI. What you can be asked about today is narrower: the AI literacy measures you took (Article 4), that you do not use banned practices (Article 5), and Article 50 transparency where it applies. A short, dated register is the easiest way to show all three.

What the Act actually says about logs

  • Providers build the logging in. Article 12 requires high-risk AI systems to technically allow automatic recording of events (logs) over their lifetime. That is a design duty for the company that makes the system, not for you.
  • Deployers of high-risk systems keep the logs. Article 26(6) says deployers of high-risk AI systems keep the logs automatically generated by that system, to the extent the logs are under their control, for a period appropriate to the intended purpose and of at least six months, unless other EU or national law (in particular data protection law) provides otherwise.
  • No log duty for ordinary AI use. The Act sets no general duty to log prompts or outputs for tools that are not high-risk. Keeping full chat histories that contain personal data can create GDPR problems of its own, so do not log "just in case".

Dates and what moved: EU AI Act in 2026: what applies now. Official texts: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 and the amending Regulation (EU) 2026/1744.

Quick check: is any of your tools in high-risk territory?

  • Screening CVs, ranking candidates, or deciding on promotion, dismissal or task allocation for staff
  • Assessing creditworthiness or setting life or health insurance prices for individuals
  • Deciding admission to, or grading in, education and vocational training
  • Biometric identification or categorisation, or emotion recognition
  • Access to essential public services or benefits
  • AI as a safety component of a regulated product (machinery, medical devices, toys, lifts)

If nothing is ticked, the high-risk duties (including the six-month log rule) do not reach you through that tool. If something is ticked, run that tool through the free risk-tier checker and get specialist advice. Article 6(3) can take some Annex III uses out of high-risk, for example narrow preparatory tasks, so a tick is a reason to check, not a verdict.

If a tool is (or may become) high-risk: what Article 26 adds

From the applicable date (2 December 2027 for Annex III uses), deployers of high-risk systems must, among other things:

  • use the system in line with the provider's instructions for use (Art. 26(1))
  • assign human oversight to people with the competence, training and authority to do it (Art. 26(2))
  • make sure input data they control is relevant and sufficiently representative (Art. 26(4))
  • monitor the system, inform the provider of risks, and report serious incidents (Art. 26(5))
  • keep the automatically generated logs under their control for at least six months (Art. 26(6))
  • inform workers' representatives and affected workers before using it at work (Art. 26(7)), and tell people when an Annex III system is used to make or help make decisions about them (Art. 26(11))
  • for public bodies, private providers of public services, and credit and life or health insurance uses: carry out a fundamental rights impact assessment (Art. 27)

Practical step now: ask that vendor whether you can export the logs, where they are stored and how long they are kept. If the logs sit only on the vendor's side, write down who holds them.
