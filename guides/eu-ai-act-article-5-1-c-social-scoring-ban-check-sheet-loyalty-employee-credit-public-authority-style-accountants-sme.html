Short answer: from 2 February 2025 (AI Act Article 113), the Union prohibits placing on the market, putting into service or using AI systems that evaluate or classify natural persons or groups over a certain period of time based on social behaviour or known, inferred or predicted personal or personality characteristics, where the social score leads to detrimental treatment in unrelated social contexts and/or to treatment that is unjustified or disproportionate (Article 5(1)(c); recital 31). Recital 31 also says the ban should not affect lawful evaluation practices carried out for a specific purpose under Union and national law. The Digital Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744) added intimate-image and CSAM bans as Article 5(1)(ba)/(bb) from 2 December 2026; it did not amend Article 5(1)(c) (our Omnibus check). Fines for Article 5 breaches sit under Article 99(3): up to EUR 35 000 000 or 7% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher (for SMEs, the lower of those two caps — Article 99(6)).

Why this lands on the accountant's, tax adviser's or payroll bureau's desk

  • Clients reuse loyalty, HR and credit scores across contexts without noticing. Article 5(1)(c)(i) targets exactly that cross-context detriment.
  • Payroll and HR tools often ship "attitude", "reliability" or "engagement" scores that can spill into housing, credit or shift cuts — the (i) and (ii) triggers.
  • The ban has already applied since 2 February 2025. Anything still live that fits the test is overdue, not a future deadline.
  • Article 5 fines are the top tier (Article 99(3)). Accountants are asked "is our scoring tool illegal?" — one row per practice answers with the article behind it.

Step 3: checklist

  1. Is an AI system evaluating or classifying natural persons or groups (Articles 3(1), 5(1)(c))?
  2. Is it placed, put into service or used in the EU, or is its output used in the EU (Article 2)?
  3. Does the score build over a certain period of time across contexts (Article 5(1)(c); recital 31)?
  4. Are the inputs social behaviour or known, inferred or predicted personal or personality characteristics?
  5. Does the score lead to detrimental treatment in an unrelated social context (Article 5(1)(c)(i))?
  6. Or to treatment that is unjustified or disproportionate to the behaviour or its gravity (Article 5(1)(c)(ii))?
  7. If a lawful specific-purpose evaluation is claimed, what Union or national law and purpose (recital 31)? Keep it in writing.
  8. If the row is STOP or IN SCOPE, has the practice been switched off? The ban has applied since 2 February 2025 (Article 113).
  9. Have we told the client that Article 5 fines are the top tier (Article 99(3)/(6))?
  10. Who decided, on what date, and when is the next review?

Limits and open points

Read for this guide (3 Nov 2026, EUR-Lex): AI Act (EU) 2024/1689 Article 2 (scope), Article 3(1) (AI system), Article 3(3)-(4) and (9)-(11) as needed for provider/deployer/placing/putting into service, Article 5(1)(c) and the surrounding Article 5(1) list, Article 99(3) and 99(6) as they touch Article 5, Article 113 (Chapters I and II from 2 February 2025), recital 31 on social scoring; Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (Digital Omnibus on AI) Article 5 amendment inserting (ba)/(bb) only — we searched the Omnibus text for social scoring / Article 5(1)(c) changes and found none (our check).

Not read for this guide: Commission guidelines on prohibited AI practices, any Commission FAQ on Article 5, national laws that add or interpret scoring bans, national penalty rules under Article 99(1)-(2), and case law on what counts as "unrelated" contexts or "disproportionate" treatment.

Our reading, not the law's words: the column list and grades; treating STOP as "in scope and still live"; treating a recital 31 claim as CHECK BY HAND rather than automatic out-of-scope; same-shop loyalty with no (i)/(ii) detriment as out of scope; the sample client wording; and the note that Omnibus did not move Article 5(1)(c).

Frequently asked questions

What does Article 5(1)(c) of the AI Act ban?

It prohibits placing on the market, putting into service or using AI systems for the evaluation or classification of natural persons or groups of persons over a certain period of time based on their social behaviour or known, inferred or predicted personal or personality characteristics, where the social score leads to detrimental or unfavourable treatment in unrelated social contexts, or to treatment that is unjustified or disproportionate to their social behaviour or its gravity (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 5(1)(c)).

When did the social scoring ban start to apply?

Chapters I and II of the AI Act, which include Article 5, apply from 2 February 2025 (Article 113). Non-compliance with Article 5 can attract administrative fines of up to EUR 35 000 000 or 7% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher; for SMEs the lower of those two caps applies (Article 99(3) and 99(6)).

Does a shop loyalty score always fall under the ban?

No. Recital 31 says the prohibition should not affect lawful evaluation practices carried out for a specific purpose in accordance with Union and national law. A loyalty score used only for the same-shop reward it was collected for, with no unrelated or disproportionate detriment, sits outside Article 5(1)(c) on that reading. Reusing the same score to refuse credit, housing or another unrelated service is the classic (i) trigger.

Did the Digital Omnibus change Article 5(1)(c)?

No. Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 amended Article 5 by inserting points (ba) and (bb) on non-consensual intimate material and child sexual abuse material (from 2 December 2026). We searched the Omnibus text: it does not amend Article 5(1)(c) social scoring.

Is an ordinary credit score for lending banned?

Not automatically. Recital 31 preserves lawful evaluation for a specific purpose under Union and national law. A score used only for the credit decision it was built for may sit outside the ban; reusing it in an unrelated context (for example hiring) or in a disproportionate way is different. The sheet marks a claimed recital 31 carve-out as CHECK BY HAND so you keep the legal basis.
