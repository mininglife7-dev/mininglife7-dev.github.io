We did not read national laws, penalties, harmonised standards or Commission guidance. This guide is for information only, not legal advice.

What you get: a finished toy warnings check sheet for EU web shops, one row per toy listing (one row per warning if a toy needs more than one). You fill short Y/N answers from the product page and the box, and the formulas return a scope check (toy for children under 14, Article 2 exclusions), an age warning check, a category warning check with the exact Annex V wording to paste, a conflicting warning check (a baby toy marked “not suitable for children under 36 months”), a language check, a to-do list with the Article behind each line and a result (OK, ADD AGE, ADD WARNING, FIX WORDING, REMOVE WARNING, TRANSLATE, NOT IN SCOPE or FIX INPUT). Also included: a manufacturer request and a client email. We tested the formulas in LibreOffice on twenty made-up listings, before and after fixes, before publishing.

Short answer: under Article 11(2) of Directive 2009/48/EC, warnings that decide the purchase, such as the minimum or maximum user age and the other Annex V warnings, must be clearly visible to the consumer before the purchase, including when the purchase is made online. A photo of the box is easy to miss; put the words on the page. The warnings in Annex V Part B points 2 to 10 (for example “Only for domestic use” or “Protective equipment should be worn. Not to be used in traffic”) must be used as worded (Article 11(1)), and a toy must not carry a warning that conflicts with its intended use. The new Regulation (EU) 2025/2509 keeps the online rule (Article 6(3)) and applies from 1 August 2030 (Article 59).

Why accountants, bookkeepers and web agencies should look at this now

  • Christmas is the toy season. Shops add gift ranges in November, often by bulk import from a supplier feed. The feed carries the title, price and photos; the warning text is easy to lose.
  • Distributors must check before selling. Before making a toy available, a distributor must verify the conformity marking and that the toy comes with instructions and safety information in a language consumers in that country easily understand (Article 7(2)).
  • Importers count as the manufacturer's stand-in. A client who buys toys from outside the EU and puts them on the EU market is an importer (Article 3(5)), with its own duties (Article 6). The purchase files a bookkeeper keeps show who imported the stock.
  • It pairs with our product safety page sheet (GPSR Article 19), which covers the manufacturer name, address and product identifier on the page.

What changes in 2030

  • Regulation (EU) 2025/2509 repeals the Directive with effect from 1 August 2030 (Article 56) and applies from that date (Article 59); Articles 28 to 44 and 49 to 55 already apply from 1 January 2026.
  • The online rule stays: warnings on user limits and the Annex III category warnings must be clearly visible to the consumer before purchase, including in distance sales (Article 6(3)).
  • New: each toy needs a digital product passport (Article 19(1)); its data carrier must be clearly visible before any purchase, including in distance sales (Article 19(7)).
  • Toys placed on the market in conformity with the Directive before 1 August 2030 can still be sold after that date (Article 57(1)).

Limits and our reading

  • National law decides language rules and penalties. A Member State may set the language of warnings and safety instructions (Articles 4(7), 11(3)); we did not read national rules or penalties.
  • Our reading, flagged: a warning that appears only inside a photo of the box is ADD WARNING, because it is easy to miss and not text a buyer can read on every screen; every toy row asks for an age limit, because Annex V Part A says the user limits include at least the minimum or maximum age; the Directive puts the marking duty on the manufacturer (Article 11(2)) and the check duty on the distributor (Article 7(2)), and we treat the web shop that shows the page as the one who must make the words visible online; for point 1 the sheet accepts any equivalent wording, as the text says “such as”. Whether a toy is “manifestly unsuitable” for under-3s is your call (Annex V point 1).
  • Not covered: the safety requirements (Article 10, Annex II), chemicals limits, CE marking and conformity assessment, the GPSR online duties for web shops and marketplaces (see our GPSR sheet), and the Annex I list in detail.
  • The scraper gives dated product data only. It does not decide whether a page complies.

FAQ

Must a web shop show toy warnings online?

Yes. Warnings which determine the decision to purchase the toy, such as the minimum and maximum ages for users and the other applicable Annex V warnings, must appear on the consumer packaging or be otherwise clearly visible to the consumer before the purchase, including where the purchase is made online (Article 11(2) of Directive 2009/48/EC).

Can we change the wording of a warning?

Not for Annex V Part B points 2 to 10: those warnings must be used as worded (Article 11(1)). For point 1 the text gives examples such as 'Not suitable for children under 36 months' or 'Not suitable for children under three years', or the graphic.

Can a baby toy say 'Not suitable for children under 36 months'?

No. A toy must not bear an Annex V Part B warning that conflicts with its intended use, as determined by its function, dimensions and characteristics (Article 11(1)).

What does a reseller have to check?

Before making a toy available, a distributor must verify that it bears the required conformity marking and comes with the required documents and with instructions and safety information in a language consumers in that Member State easily understand, and that the manufacturer and importer details and identification are there (Article 7(2)).

When does the new Toy Safety Regulation apply?

Regulation (EU) 2025/2509 applies from 1 August 2030, with Articles 28 to 44 and 49 to 55 applying from 1 January 2026 (Article 59). It keeps the rule that warnings must be visible before purchase in distance sales (Article 6(3)) and adds a digital product passport (Article 19). Toys placed on the market under the Directive before 1 August 2030 may still be sold (Article 57(1)).
