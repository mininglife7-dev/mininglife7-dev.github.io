Short answer: the Digital Omnibus, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, inserts a new prohibited practice into Article 5(1) of the AI Act, point (ba): AI systems that generate or manipulate realistic images, videos, audio or similar material of an identifiable person's intimate parts, or of that person engaged in sexually explicit activities, without that person's freely-given, specific, informed, unambiguous and explicit consent. It applies from 2 December 2026 (Article 113(a) as amended). For providers it bites when that output is the intended purpose, or a reasonably foreseeable and reproducible outcome with no reasonable and adequate safeguards (new Article 5(1a)(a)). For deployers it bites only when they use the system for that purpose (Article 5(1a)(b)). Edits that do not increase exposure of intimate parts or change the nature of a depicted sexual activity are not "manipulation" (Article 5(1b)). The same amendment adds point (bb) on child sexual abuse material under Directive 2011/93/EU; that point is not graded by this sheet and any such case goes straight to the police and a lawyer. Fines for Article 5 breaches are the top tier (Article 99(3), lower cap for SMEs under Article 99(6)).

Why this lands on the accountant's or agency's desk

  • 23 days left (as of 9 Nov 2026). Clients that sell or embed image, avatar or "photo enhance" features into EU products need a written answer before 2 December 2026.
  • Software houses and app studios are usually the provider. Their question is whether their safeguards are documented and tested, not whether the feature exists.
  • Agencies, studios and ordinary SMEs are usually deployers. Their exposure is a staff member misusing a tool, so a written use policy matters.
  • It sits next to the Article 50(4) deepfake disclosure sheet and the Article 50(2) marking sheet, which share the 2 December 2026 date for older generators. Labelling content never makes a banned output lawful.

Step 3: checklist

  1. Is an AI system involved (Article 3(1)), and is it placed, put into service or used in the EU, or its output used there (Article 2)?
  2. Can it produce realistic images, video, audio or similar material of an identifiable person's intimate parts or sexually explicit activity? Recital 12 says "realistic" means a credible real-life depiction of face, voice or body and excludes cartoonish or physically impossible depictions.
  3. Is the client the provider or a deployer of the tool (role check sheet)?
  4. Provider: is that output the intended purpose, or a foreseeable and reproducible outcome? If foreseeable, which safeguards prevent it, how were they tested, and how is reported misuse corrected (Article 5(1a)(a))?
  5. Deployer: is there a written rule that forbids using any tool for this purpose (staff AI use policy template)?
  6. Where consent is relied on, is there proof that each depicted person gave freely-given, specific, informed, unambiguous and explicit consent for that generation (Article 5(1)(ba)), handled under the GDPR?
  7. Is the fix done before 2 December 2026, and who signed it off and when?

Frequently asked questions

What does Article 5(1)(ba) of the AI Act ban?

It bans placing on the market, putting into service or using an AI system that generates or manipulates realistic images, videos, audio or similar material of an identifiable natural person's intimate parts, or of that person engaged in sexually explicit activities, without that person's freely-given, specific, informed, unambiguous and explicit consent for that generation or manipulation. The point was inserted into Article 5(1) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 by the Digital Omnibus, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744.

When does the intimate deepfake ban apply?

From 2 December 2026. Article 113(a), as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, keeps 2 February 2025 for Chapters I and II but makes Article 5(1) points (ba) and (bb) and Article 5(1a) and (1b) apply from 2 December 2026.

Is a general image generator banned just because misuse is possible?

No, not on that ground alone. For providers the ban covers systems whose intended purpose is that output, or where it is a reasonably foreseeable and reproducible outcome without significant technical modification and the system lacks reasonable and adequate safeguards to reliably prevent it and to correct observed or reported misuse (Article 5(1a)(a)). Recital 12 lists examples of safeguards such as refusal training, runtime prompt guardrails, output filters, usage restrictions, abuse detection and notice-and-action mechanisms.

Is a business that only uses an image tool caught?

A deployer is caught only where it uses the system for the purpose of generating or manipulating such material (Article 5(1a)(b)). Recital 12 says lawful use for other purposes and accidental generation are not covered.

What are the fines?

Breaches of Article 5 can attract fines of up to EUR 35 000 000 or 7% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher; for SMEs the lower of the two applies (Article 99(3) and 99(6)).
