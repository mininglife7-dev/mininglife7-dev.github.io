FY 2025-26 missing ITC supplier chase sheet for CA firms: every invoice not in GSTR-2B that suppliers must report by 11/13 November 2026 (section 16(4))

What you get: a ready-to-copy Google Sheet layout with one row per FY 2025-26 purchase invoice or debit note that is in a client's books but not in their GSTR-2B. By formula it tells you, row by row, the date the supplier must report it by, the GSTR-3B the client claims it in, "Chase supplier", "Claim in Oct GSTR-3B", "Claimed" or "Window closed", and the days left. Plus a supplier-wise tab, a made-up example row, a plan to 30 November and a ready message for suppliers.

Short answer (checked 23 Oct 2026): under section 16(4) of the CGST Act, input tax credit on an invoice or debit note of FY 2025-26 cannot be taken after 30 November 2026, or after the client files the FY 2025-26 annual return, whichever is earlier. Under section 16(2)(aa) the credit is available only once the supplier has reported the invoice in their GSTR-1 or IFF so that it reaches the client's GSTR-2B. So for every missing invoice the supplier has to report it in their October 2026 GSTR-1 (due 11 November) or IFF (due 13 November), and the client claims it in the October 2026 GSTR-3B (due 20 November), filed no later than 30 November.

What the law says

  • Section 16(4). A registered person "shall not be entitled to take input tax credit in respect of any invoice or debit note ... after the thirtieth day of November following the end of financial year to which such invoice or debit note pertains or furnishing of the relevant annual return, whichever is earlier" (CBIC, section 16).
  • Section 16(2)(aa), in force since 1 January 2022: credit only where the details of the invoice or debit note have been furnished by the supplier in the statement of outward supplies and communicated to the recipient.
  • The supplier's side has the same date. Under the proviso to section 37(3), a supplier cannot add a missed FY 2025-26 invoice to GSTR-1 after 30 November 2026 or after filing their own annual return, whichever is earlier. If the supplier files their GSTR-9 early, their window closes early too.

Message to a supplier

"Dear [supplier], the invoices below, issued by you to [client name, GSTIN] between April 2025 and March 2026, do not appear in our GSTR-2B: [invoice numbers, dates, tax]. Under the GST law they can still be reported in your October 2026 GSTR-1 (due 11 November) or IFF (due 13 November), and not after 30 November 2026. Please add them and reply with the filing date. Thank you."
