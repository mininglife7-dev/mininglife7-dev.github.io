Short answer: Article 5(3) of the ePrivacy Directive (Directive 2002/58/EC, as amended by Directive 2009/136/EC) allows storing information on, or reading information from, a visitor's device only with consent after clear and comprehensive information about the purposes, unless it is for the sole purpose of transmitting a communication or strictly necessary for a service the visitor explicitly requested. The European Data Protection Board says this is not only about cookies: local storage, tracking pixels, tracking links, unique identifiers and some IP-based tracking can be covered too (Guidelines 2/2023, version 2.0, adopted 7 October 2024). An AI chat widget, analytics script or ad pixel added to a client site is therefore a consent question before it is a GDPR question.

Why accountants and bookkeepers should look at this now

  • AI chat widgets are the new tracker. Many clients add an AI chatbot to their site with one line of code. Many widgets store a visitor ID in a cookie or in local storage as soon as the page loads, before anyone opens the chat. Check what the client's own widget does.
  • "It is not personal data" is not an answer. The Court of Justice ruled in Planet49 (C-673/17, 1 October 2019) that Article 5(3) applies whether or not the stored or accessed information is personal data.
  • Pre-ticked boxes do not count. The same judgment says consent is not valid through a pre-checked box the user must untick, and that the information given must include how long the cookies last and whether third parties can access them.
  • It is a quick win in a client review. One page of this sheet per client website turns a vague "is our cookie banner fine?" into a list of named scripts with a fix next to each.

The order of tests and the result words are our working rule, not the law's wording. "MAY BE EXEMPT" means only that the item could fit one of the two exemptions in Article 5(3). The EDPB guidelines say whether an exemption applies must be assessed case by case, with the national transposition and national authority guidance (Guidelines 2/2023, paragraph 4). Write the reason in column D and check your country's guidance.

AI chat widgets: four questions to ask the supplier

  • Which cookies or local-storage keys does the widget set, and does it set any before the visitor opens the chat?
  • Can loading be delayed until the visitor clicks "chat" or gives consent?
  • Does the supplier use the stored IDs or chats for its own purposes (analytics, product improvement, model training)? If yes, that is "any other purpose" in column H.
  • Which other companies can access the stored information (column J)?

National law

Article 5(3) is in a directive, so each country applies it through its own law, and the national data protection or telecoms authority enforces it and sets the penalties. Rules on first-party statistics, consent lifetimes and banner design differ between countries. Check your national law and your authority's cookie guidance. This page covers the EU texts in force as we read them today.

Frequently asked questions

Does the cookie rule cover local storage and pixels, not only cookies?

Yes, according to the EDPB. Its Guidelines 2/2023 on the technical scope of Article 5(3) cover tracking pixels and tracking links (including in emails), information processed locally and then sent to a server, unique identifiers, IP-based tracking where the IP address comes from the user's device, and IoT reporting.

Does it matter whether the information is personal data?

No. In Planet49 (C-673/17) the Court of Justice ruled that Article 5(3) is not interpreted differently according to whether the stored or accessed information is personal data.

Can analytics run without consent as "strictly necessary"?

WP29 Opinion 04/2012 says analytics cookies are not strictly necessary to provide a function explicitly requested by the user, so they do not fall under the exemption. It noted that first-party aggregated statistics carry less privacy risk. Some national authorities have since issued their own guidance on audience measurement, so check your country.
