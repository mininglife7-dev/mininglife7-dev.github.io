What you get: a finished, tested suspicious activity alert log for accounting, bookkeeping, payroll and tax firms. One row per alert, whether it came from an AI screening tool, a member of staff or a bank query. Formulas tell you, row by row, whether the assessment record is on file, whether a report to the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) is due now, whether a pending transaction must be held, whether anything was disclosed to the client, the 5-working-day deadline for an FIU information request, and the date until which records must be kept. Each row ends in one result: REPORT NOW, FIX, CHECK BY HAND, HOLD, REPLY TO FIU, OPEN or OK.

Short answer: From 10 July 2027 (AMLR Article 90; Settings!B3 counts the days, 243 on 9 Nov 2026), external accountants, auditors and tax advisers in every EU country follow one directly applicable rulebook. When they know, suspect or have reasonable grounds to suspect that funds or activities are the proceeds of crime, they must report to the FIU "promptly", "regardless of the amount involved" (Article 69(1)(a)). They must write down every assessment, "whether or not such assessment results in a suspicious transaction report" (Article 77(1)(b)), answer FIU requests "within 5 working days" (Article 69(1)), hold suspicious transactions until reported (Article 71) and never tip off the client (Article 73). Until 10 July 2027 your national law under the current directive applies; check it.

Why this matters for firms that use AI screening tools

  • An AI alert is not an assessment. Article 69(2) asks the firm to assess transactions "on the basis of and against any relevant fact and information known to them". A screening tool flags; a person decides. The sheet treats an unreviewed alert as FIX (our suggestion, see the limits below).
  • The "no suspicion" decision needs a record too. Article 77(1)(b) requires "a record of the assessment ... including the information and circumstances considered and the results", whether or not a report follows. Firms that clear AI false hits verbally have no record.
  • Automated decisions need a person. Article 76(5) lets firms use automated processes and AI systems, but any decision to take on, refuse or keep a client, or to change due diligence, must be "subject to meaningful human intervention". The client may get an explanation and challenge such a decision, "except in relation to a report as referred to in Article 69".
  • Tipping off is easy by accident. A junior asking the client "why did our system flag this payment?" can be a disclosure under Article 73(1).

The rules the sheet checks

RuleWhat the text says
Report (Art 69(1)(a))Report to the FIU "on their own initiative" where they know, suspect or have reasonable grounds to suspect proceeds of criminal activity or links to terrorist financing. "All suspicious transactions, including attempted transactions and suspicions arising from the inability to conduct customer due diligence" are reported.
Who sends it (Art 69(6), 70(1))The compliance officer transmits it to the FIU of the Member State where the firm is established. Member States may let accountants and tax advisers send it to a designated self-regulatory body, which forwards it "promptly and unfiltered".
FIU requests (Art 69(1))Reply "within 5 working days". In "justified and urgent cases" the FIU may shorten that, "including to less than 24 hours", or extend it.
Legal-position exemption (Art 70(2))Accountants and tax advisers are exempt from reporting information received while "ascertaining the legal position" of a client or defending or representing the client in judicial proceedings, unless they take part in laundering, give advice for laundering, or know the client seeks advice for that purpose. Member States may narrow the exemption (Art 70(3)).
Hold the transaction (Art 71)Refrain from transactions known or suspected to be related to criminal proceeds until reported. The firm may proceed, after assessing the risks, if no contrary FIU instruction comes "within 3 working days of submitting the report". If refraining is not possible, inform the FIU "immediately after".
Protection (Art 72)Good-faith disclosure to the FIU is not a breach of contractual or legal secrecy and brings no liability "of any kind".
No tipping off (Art 73)Do not disclose to the client or third persons that activities are being assessed, that a report was or will be made, or that an analysis may be carried out. Exceptions: authorities (73(2)), the same group (73(3)), the same network or partnership of accountants (73(4)), the same transaction with another obliged entity in the EU (73(5)). Trying to dissuade a client from illegal activity is not disclosure (73(6)).
Records (Art 77)Keep the assessment record and any report, unredacted, for 5 years from the end of the client relationship (or the occasional transaction, or the refusal), then delete the personal data, unless other law or a competent authority requires longer (up to 5 more years).

Staff checklist

  • Every AI or staff alert gets a row and a named reviewer.
  • Write down the facts you looked at and your decision, even when the answer is "no suspicion".
  • Suspicion: the compliance officer reports to the FIU promptly; the amount does not matter.
  • Do not process a suspicious payment until it is reported, and then wait 3 working days for FIU instructions.
  • Do not ask the client about the alert in a way that reveals it. Route questions through the compliance officer.
  • Answer FIU requests within 5 working days, or the deadline the FIU set.
  • Keep the log and reports, unredacted, for 5 years after the client relationship ends.

Limits and our reading

  • Dates. The AMLR applies from 10 July 2027 (Article 90). Until then, national laws under the current anti-money-laundering directive apply, with their own deadlines and forms. AMLA implementing standards on the report format (Art 69(3)) and guidelines on indicators (Art 69(5)) were not read.
  • Our reading, flagged: requiring a person to review every AI alert (the AMLR requires meaningful human intervention for the decisions in Art 76(5)(b), not explicitly for every alert); counting working days with WORKDAY, which skips weekends but not public holidays (add your national holidays as the third argument); "5 working days" counted from the day after receipt; treating a missing FIU instruction as "none received".
  • Not covered: national transposition and self-regulatory body rules (Art 70(1) and (3)), the Directive (EU) 2024/1640 FIU powers, high-value goods reports (Art 74), information-sharing partnerships (Art 75), GDPR duties for this log, and penalties.
  • Source note. The official OJ text we read prints the AI Act reference in Article 76(5) as "Regulation (EU) 2024/xxx"; it means Regulation (EU) 2024/1689.

FAQ

When do the EU AMLR reporting rules apply to accountants and tax advisers?

Regulation (EU) 2024/1624 applies from 10 July 2027 (Article 90). External accountants, auditors and tax advisers are obliged entities (Article 3(3)(a)). Until then, national laws under the current anti-money-laundering directive apply.

Do we have to report small amounts?

Yes. Article 69(1)(a) requires a report where the firm knows, suspects or has reasonable grounds to suspect criminal proceeds 'regardless of the amount involved', including attempted transactions and suspicions arising from the inability to do customer due diligence.

How fast must we answer an FIU request?

Within 5 working days (Article 69(1)). In justified and urgent cases the FIU may shorten the deadline, including to less than 24 hours, or extend it.

Can we process a payment after reporting a suspicion?

Article 71(1): refrain until the report is submitted and any FIU instructions are followed. If no contrary instruction comes within 3 working days of the report, the firm may proceed after assessing the risks. If refraining is not possible, inform the FIU immediately after (Article 71(2)).

Can an AI screening tool decide whether to report?

The AMLR asks the firm to assess against all relevant facts (Article 69(2)) and requires meaningful human intervention for automated decisions on taking on, refusing or keeping clients (Article 76(5)(b)). We suggest a trained person reviews every alert and records the result (Article 77(1)(b)).

Can we tell the client their payment was flagged?

No. Article 73(1) prohibits disclosing that activities are being assessed or reported. Exceptions cover authorities, the same group or network, and the same transaction with another EU obliged entity (Article 73(2)-(5)); trying to dissuade a client from illegal activity is not disclosure (Article 73(6)).
