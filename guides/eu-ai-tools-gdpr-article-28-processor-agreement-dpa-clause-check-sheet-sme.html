Does your AI supplier's data processing agreement cover GDPR Article 28? A clause check sheet for small businesses using AI tools

Updated 24 Oct 2026 · Disclosure: the EURO AI Register mentioned below is an Unadhi product with a free edition. This guide is for information only, not legal advice.

What you get: a finished Article 28 clause check sheet: one row per AI tool or supplier, one column per Article 28(3)(a) to (h) point plus the sub-processor terms in Article 28(2) and 28(4), a result formula that counts missing clauses, three made-up example rows, and a short what to do if a clause is missing list. Open each supplier's data processing agreement (DPA), mark Y, Partial or N per column, and the sheet tells you where the gaps are.

Short answer: when an AI tool processes personal data on your behalf (client emails, call recordings, customer chats, CVs), the supplier is your processor and Article 28 requires a written contract that covers specific points. Many suppliers publish a DPA; your job is to check that it covers every point and to keep the version you accepted.

Step 1: is the supplier your processor?

  • Does the tool handle personal data that you put in or collect (names, emails, voices, chat logs, CVs)? If no personal data goes in, this sheet is not needed for that tool; note why.
  • Does the supplier process it on your behalf, for your purpose? Then it is your processor and the row goes on the sheet.
  • Do the terms let the supplier use the data for its own purposes? Article 28(10) says a processor that determines purposes and means is considered a controller for that processing. Flag it in column C and decide whether to switch that use off.
  • Choose only processors with sufficient guarantees (Article 28(1)); an approved code of conduct or certification can be one element of that (Article 28(5)).

What to do if a clause is missing

  • Look again first: the DPA is often a separate page or annex incorporated by reference in the terms of service or order form. Save the exact version you accepted (Article 28(9): in writing, including electronic form).
  • Ask for an addendum: write to the supplier naming the missing points, for example "Article 28(3)(g): please confirm deletion or return at our choice after the service ends". Article 28(6) to (8) allow the contract to be based on standard contractual clauses adopted by the Commission or a supervisory authority, and the EDPB says relying on them may help rebalance negotiating positions.
  • Do not accept it just because you are small: the EDPB says the imbalance in contractual power between a small controller and a big provider does not justify accepting non-compliant terms; if you use the service, you have accepted responsibility.
  • Sub-processors: ask to be actively told of each new or replaced sub-processor with time to object (Article 28(2)); a list on a website without notice is not enough, according to the EDPB. Check where they are with the international transfer check sheet.
  • Audits: a clause that makes audits cost so much they would never happen is a Partial: the EDPB says clearly disproportionate or excessive cost clauses should not be inserted.
  • If you cannot fix it: stop sending personal data to that tool, or replace it, and record the decision in column N. Under Article 82, a controller involved in processing is liable for damage caused by processing that infringes the GDPR, while a processor is liable only where it breached processor-specific obligations or acted outside your lawful instructions, so the written terms matter.
