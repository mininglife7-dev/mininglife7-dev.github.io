Short answer: from 2 February 2025 (AI Act Article 113), the Union prohibits the placing on the market, the putting into service for this specific purpose, or the use of AI systems that create or expand facial recognition databases through the untargeted scraping of facial images from the internet or CCTV footage (Article 5(1)(e); recital 43). Recital 43 states that the practice should be prohibited because it adds to the feeling of mass surveillance and can lead to gross violations of fundamental rights, including the right to privacy. Facial images are an example of biometric data under Article 3(34). The Digital Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744) added intimate-image and CSAM bans as Article 5(1)(ba)/(bb) from 2 December 2026; it did not amend Article 5(1)(e) (our Omnibus check). Fines for Article 5 breaches sit under Article 99(3): up to EUR 35 000 000 or 7% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher (for SMEs, the lower of those two caps — Article 99(6)).

Why this lands on the accountant's, tax adviser's or agency's desk

  • HR photo and face-gallery tools sold as "match every staff photo from the public web" sit on the Article 5(1)(e) path when they create or expand a facial recognition database through untargeted scraping.
  • Marketing face databases built by scraping internet faces, and shop CCTV analytics vendors that expand an FR database from untargeted CCTV face scrape, are the classic client questions after a complaint or a regulator letter.
  • The ban has already applied since 2 February 2025. Anything still live that fits the test is overdue, not a future deadline.
  • Article 5 fines are the top tier (Article 99(3)). Accountants are asked "is our face-scrape / CCTV FR database illegal?" — one row per practice answers with the article behind it.

Step 3: checklist

  1. Is an AI system involved (Articles 3(1), 5(1)(e))?
  2. Is it placed, put into service or used in the EU, or is its output used in the EU (Article 2)?
  3. Does it create or expand a facial recognition database (Article 5(1)(e); recital 43)? Facial images are biometric data (Article 3(34)).
  4. Does that create/expand happen through the untargeted scraping of facial images (Article 5(1)(e); recital 43)?
  5. Is the source the internet, CCTV footage, or both?
  6. If a targeted-only claim is made, what evidence shows it is not untargeted scraping?
  7. If a not-an-FR-database claim is made, what evidence shows there is no facial recognition matching database?
  8. If the row is STOP or IN SCOPE, has the practice been switched off? The ban has applied since 2 February 2025 (Article 113).
  9. Have we told the client that Article 5 fines are the top tier (Article 99(3)/(6))?
  10. Who decided, on what date, and when is the next review?

Frequently asked questions

What does Article 5(1)(e) of the AI Act ban?

It prohibits the placing on the market, the putting into service for this specific purpose, or the use of AI systems that create or expand facial recognition databases through the untargeted scraping of facial images from the internet or CCTV footage (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 5(1)(e)). Recital 43 states that the practice should be prohibited because it adds to the feeling of mass surveillance and can lead to gross violations of fundamental rights, including the right to privacy. Facial images are an example of biometric data under Article 3(34).

When did the untargeted facial-image scraping ban start to apply?

Chapters I and II of the AI Act, which include Article 5, apply from 2 February 2025 (Article 113). Non-compliance with Article 5 can attract administrative fines of up to EUR 35 000 000 or 7% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher; for SMEs the lower of those two caps applies (Article 99(3) and 99(6)).

Does every staff photo library or shop CCTV system trigger the ban?

No. Article 5(1)(e) needs an AI system that creates or expands a facial recognition database through the untargeted scraping of facial images from the internet or CCTV footage. A paper photo album, consented staff enrollment for door access that is not untargeted scraping, or ordinary CCTV without creating or expanding an FR database sits outside this ban on the answers given. Targeted-only or not-an-FR-database claims are marked CHECK BY HAND on the sheet.

Did the Digital Omnibus change Article 5(1)(e)?

No. Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 amended Article 5 by inserting points (ba) and (bb) on non-consensual intimate material and child sexual abuse material (from 2 December 2026). We searched the Omnibus text: it does not amend Article 5(1)(e) untargeted facial-image scraping.

How is this different from Article 5(1)(g) biometric categorisation and Article 5(1)(h) real-time remote biometric identification?

Article 5(1)(e) bans creating or expanding facial recognition databases through untargeted scraping of facial images from the internet or CCTV. Article 5(1)(g) bans biometric categorisation that deduces or infers listed sensitive attributes. Article 5(1)(h) restricts real-time remote biometric identification in publicly accessible spaces for law enforcement. One tool can fail one sheet and pass another; run the matching sheet for the practice you see.
