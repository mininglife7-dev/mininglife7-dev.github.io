Before you paste customer data into an AI tool: a GDPR pseudonymisation check sheet (direct identifiers, quasi-identifiers, the key kept apart) for small businesses

Updated 27 Oct 2026 · Disclosure: the EURO AI Register mentioned below is an Unadhi product with a free edition. This guide is for information only, not legal advice.

What you get: a finished pseudonymisation check sheet in three tabs: a field list where you mark every column or text field of the data set you want to put into an AI tool as a direct identifier, a quasi-identifier or other data, with the action for each; a key and separation tab that records where the lookup table or secret key is kept, who can open it and who must not; and a result tab with a formula that tells you whether the file is ready, needs more work, or is still plainly identifying, plus four made-up example rows.

Short answer: replacing names with codes does not take data out of the GDPR. Recital 26 says personal data which have undergone pseudonymisation and "could be attributed to a natural person by the use of additional information should be considered to be information on an identifiable natural person". Pseudonymisation is still worth doing: it "can reduce the risks to the data subjects concerned" (Recital 28). For it to count as pseudonymisation under Article 4(5), the additional information (your code list or key) must be "kept separately" and protected by technical and organisational measures.

Why this matters before you use AI tools

Small businesses paste support tickets, CRM exports, call notes and staff spreadsheets into chatbots and AI analysis tools every day. Taking out names first is a good habit, but it is often done halfway: the email address stays in the signature, the order number stays in the subject line, or the town plus job title plus age still point to one person. The EDPB guidelines say the legal definition "requires a look at all parts of the personal data, not only the pseudonyms" (Guidelines 01/2025, para. 7). This sheet makes you look at every field.

Step 1: tab 1, field list (one row per column or free-text field)

ColumnWhat to writeSource
A. Data set and AI toolFor example "Helpdesk export Jan to Sep 2026 → AI summary tool"
B. FieldEach column name, and each free-text field (message body, notes, call transcript) as its own rowEDPB Guidelines 01/2025 para. 7 ("all parts of the personal data")
C. TypeDirect (name, email, phone, customer or order number, national ID, IP address), Quasi (age, gender, job title, town, income, length of service and similar attributes that together may point to a person), or OtherArt. 4(1) GDPR (identifiers); Guidelines 01/2025 paras. 83, 101
D. ActionRemove, Replace with code, Generalise (age 37 → 30-39, postcode → region), or KeepGuidelines 01/2025 paras. 83, 102
E. Needed for the AI task?Yes or No. If No, the action is RemoveArt. 5(1)(c) data minimisation
F. Check=IF(AND(C2="Direct",D2="Keep"),"FIX: direct identifier left in",IF(AND(E2="No",D2<>"Remove"),"FIX: not needed, remove it",IF(AND(C2="Quasi",D2="Keep"),"REVIEW: quasi-identifier kept","OK")))Formula is our working rule

Free-text fields need a second look: names, phone numbers and addresses often sit inside message bodies and email signatures, not only in their own columns. Search the text for "@", digit runs and greeting lines before you mark a free-text row OK.

Step 2: tab 2, key and separation

Article 4(5) asks for three things, which the EDPB describes as three actions: transform the data, keep the additional information separately, and apply measures so the data are not attributed to people (Guidelines 01/2025, para. 5).

ColumnWhat to writeSource
A. MethodLookup table (a list matching each code to the real name or ID) or keyed function (a code calculated with a secret key)Guidelines 01/2025 paras. 88, 92
B. Where the table or key is keptFor example "separate encrypted folder on the office drive, not in the AI tool, not in the shared export folder"Art. 4(5) "kept separately"; Recital 29
C. Who may open itRoles, not names, if you share the sheetRecital 29: "indicate the authorised persons"
D. Who must not see itThe people and tools that will work on the coded data (the AI tool and its vendor, the staff using the output)Guidelines 01/2025 paras. 20, 35 (the "pseudonymisation domain")
E. Codes random or guessable?Random, or Guessable (initials, date of birth, a plain hash of the email address). Guessable means FIXGuidelines 01/2025 paras. 88, 89 (a secret with "sufficient entropy"; keyed one-way functions such as MACs); "Guessable" list is our working rule
F. Public sources checked?Could someone match the coded rows with public posts, reviews or social media? Yes, No or Not checkedGuidelines 01/2025 para. 21

The best result the sheet can give is "pseudonymised", never "anonymous". The EDPB says that even if all the additional information has been erased, pseudonymised data "becomes anonymous only if the conditions for anonymity are met" (Guidelines 01/2025, para. 22), and Recital 26 asks you to consider "all the means reasonably likely to be used", taking into account cost, time and available technology. Anonymisation needs its own assessment.
