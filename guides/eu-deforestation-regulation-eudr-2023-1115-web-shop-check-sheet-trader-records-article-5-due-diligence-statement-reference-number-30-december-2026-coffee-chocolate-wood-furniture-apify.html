Short answer: the main obligations of Regulation (EU) 2023/1115 apply from 30 December 2026 (Article 38(2), as replaced by Regulation (EU) 2025/2650), 50 days from 10 November 2026. A web shop that buys covered products from an EU supplier is a trader: it may sell them “only if” it holds the supplier's name, postal address, email and, where the supplier is the operator, “the reference numbers of the due diligence statements” (Article 5(1) and 5(3)(a)), and keeps them for at least five years (Article 5(4)). A shop that imports, or buys from a seller outside the EU, is the operator (Article 7) and must submit a due diligence statement before selling (Article 4(2)).

Why accountants, bookkeepers and web agencies should look at this now

  • The trader rules are light but strict. No statement to file, but no sale without the supplier details on file (Article 5(1)). Most of these details already sit in the purchase ledger; the missing piece is usually the statement reference number, which operators must pass down the chain (Article 4(7)).
  • Company size decides two things. Non-SME traders must register before selling (Article 5(2)); micro and small operators established by 31 December 2024 get until 30 June 2027 (Article 38(3)). You hold the balance sheet, turnover and headcount figures that decide the size (Directive 2013/34/EU, Article 3).
  • Imports from outside the EU change the role. A small shop importing teak furniture or coffee beans becomes the operator, with full due diligence (Articles 4 and 8 to 11).
  • Fines are serious. For legal persons, the maximum fine set by Member States must be at least 4% of total annual Union-wide turnover (Article 25(2)(a)).

Limits and our reading

  • National law decides penalties and enforcement. We did not read national laws, Commission guidance, FAQs or the information system manuals. Check the Annex I CN codes against the Combined Nomenclature for each product.
  • Our reading, flagged: Article 38(3) names only operators, so the sheet gives traders and downstream operators of every size 30 December 2026; the sheet treats used or second-hand items as CHECK BY HAND because only some Annex I entries exclude them; for products added from 30 December 2027 the sheet shows that date for all roles; and it asks larger shops to verify due diligence on a substantiated concern (Article 5(6)) as a reminder, not a finding.
  • Not covered: the content of due diligence (Articles 9 to 11) and the statement fields (Annex II); the simplified declaration for micro or small primary operators (Article 4a), which concerns growers in low-risk countries; authorised representatives (Article 6); exports; products produced before 29 June 2023 (Article 1(2)) and the timber transition in Article 37; and customs steps (Article 26).

FAQ

When does the EU Deforestation Regulation apply to web shops?

From 30 December 2026. Regulation (EU) 2025/2650 replaced Article 38: Articles 3 to 13, 16 to 24, 26, 31 and 32 of Regulation (EU) 2023/1115 apply from 30 December 2026 (Article 38(2)). Operators that are natural persons or micro or small undertakings established as such by 31 December 2024 get 30 June 2027, except for products covered by the Annex to the old Timber Regulation (EU) No 995/2010 (Article 38(3)).

Is a web shop that buys chocolate or coffee from an EU wholesaler an operator?

Usually not. A shop that only makes products available that someone else first placed on the EU market is a trader (Article 2(17)). Since the 2025 amendment, traders do not file due diligence statements. They must hold supplier and business buyer details before selling (Article 5(1) and (3)), keep them for at least five years (Article 5(4)) and, if they are not SMEs, register in the EU information system (Article 5(2)).

When is the web shop the operator?

When it is the first to make the product available on the EU market, for example by importing it, or by buying from a seller established outside the EU that places it on the EU market: Article 7 deems the first person established in the Union who makes such products available to be the operator. Operators must exercise due diligence (Articles 8 to 11) and submit a due diligence statement before selling (Article 4(2)).

Which shop products are covered?

Only products listed in Annex I that contain or are made using cattle, cocoa, coffee, oil palm, rubber, soya or wood, for example coffee (0901), chocolate (1806), cocoa powder (1805), new tyres (4011), rubber gloves (4015), many wood articles (4401 to 4421), paper and paperboard articles (ex 48) and wooden furniture (ex 9403). Bamboo and rattan products, many used or second-hand products and printed books (deleted by Regulation (EU) 2025/2650) are out.

What changed in September 2026?

Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/2102 (OJ 17.9.2026) amended Annex I. It adds, from 30 December 2027, instant coffee (2101 11 00), soaps made using oil palm (3401 11 00, 3401 20) and several palm oil chemicals, and frozen cattle tongues. It removes cattle hides and leather (4101, 4104, 4107), rubber belts (4010) and other vulcanised rubber articles (4016), and clarifies that packing used to carry another product and used or second-hand products under many entries are not covered.

Does the scraper check deforestation compliance?

No. The product scraper lists products, titles and prices from a shop so you can find the covered lines quickly. Supplier records, statement reference numbers and the company size come from the client's purchase files and accounts.
