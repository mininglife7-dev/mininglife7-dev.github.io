Article 6(3) "not high-risk" check sheet for accountants: the four conditions, the profiling rule, the Article 6(4) assessment record and EU database registration (AI Act Articles 6 and 49, Digital Omnibus 2026/1744) for business clients

What you get: a finished Article 6(3) check sheet for accountants, tax advisers and bookkeepers to use with business clients that build, sell or buy AI tools in the EU AI Act's high-risk areas (hiring, staff management, credit, education and the other Annex III areas). One row per AI system: enter the client's role and nine short answers, and the sheet sorts the system (HIGH-RISK (PROFILING), HIGH-RISK, ART 6(3) MAY APPLY, CHECK or NOT IN ANNEX III), gives a result (OK, FIX, CHECK BY HAND or the duties that follow) and a to-do list with the Article behind each item. Also included: an assessment and registration log with a status formula, a fill-in assessment record (Article 6(4)), the EU database fields for these systems as cut by the Digital Omnibus (Annex VIII Section B), questions for suppliers, and a client email. Made-up example rows show how it fills in.

Short answer: an AI system listed in Annex III of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 is high-risk (Article 6(2)), unless it does not pose a significant risk of harm to health, safety or fundamental rights, including by not materially influencing the outcome of decision-making. That exception applies where the system is meant for (a) a narrow procedural task, (b) improving the result of a human activity already completed, (c) detecting decision-making patterns or deviations without replacing or influencing the human assessment without proper human review, or (d) a preparatory task (Article 6(3)). A system that profiles natural persons is always high-risk. A provider that uses the exception must document its assessment before the system is placed on the market and register itself and the system in the EU database (Articles 6(4) and 49(2)). Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (Digital Omnibus on AI) kept that registration and cut the fields to fill in, and moved the high-risk rules for Annex III systems to 2 December 2027.

Why clients ask their accountant this question

  • A software client sells into an Annex III area, for example an HR tool that reads CVs or a lending tool that sorts documents, and wants to know if it is "high-risk" or can use the exception.
  • A client buys such a tool and the supplier says "not high-risk under Article 6(3)". The client needs to know what to ask and keep.
  • Investor, bank or customer questionnaires ask for the AI Act classification of a product.
  • The dates moved. After Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, Chapter III Sections 1 to 3 (classification, requirements, provider and deployer duties) apply to Annex III systems from 2 December 2027 (Article 113, point (c), as amended). See our deadline sheet after the Digital Omnibus.

1. Intended purpose: [what the system is meant to do, for whom, in which process]

2. Annex III point: [for example 4(a)] and why it is listed there.

4. Condition relied on: [(a) narrow procedural task / (b) improves completed human activity / (c) detects patterns or deviations / (d) preparatory task], with a description of what the system does and does not do.

5. No material influence on the outcome: [why the output does not affect the substance of the decision; who takes the decision; what human review exists].

6. No significant risk of harm to health, safety or fundamental rights: [reasoning].

7. Placed on the market / put into service on: [date, after this assessment] · EU database registration: [date, reference].

8. Review: [re-assess when the intended purpose or functions change] · Kept for production to national competent authorities on request (Article 6(4)).

Checklist: the EU database fields for an Article 6(3) system

Annex VIII Section B lists what the provider enters (Article 71(2)). Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 deleted points 7 and 9 (Article 1, point (42)), so the remaining fields are:

  • ☐ Name, address and contact details of the provider (point 1).
  • ☐ Name, address and contact details of anyone submitting on the provider's behalf (point 2).
  • ☐ Name, address and contact details of the authorised representative, where applicable (point 3); non-EU providers, see our authorised representative guide.
  • ☐ Trade name and any unambiguous reference for identification and traceability (point 4).
  • ☐ Description of the intended purpose (point 5).
  • ☐ The Article 6(3) condition or conditions relied on (point 6).
  • ☐ Status: on the market or in service, no longer placed on the market or in service, recalled (point 8).

The information is kept up to date (Annex VIII Section B) and, outside the restricted law enforcement and migration sections, is publicly available (Article 71(4)). Market surveillance authorities may use it for checks (Article 80(8)).

Questions for a supplier that says "not high-risk"

  • Which Annex III point did you consider, and which Article 6(3) condition do you rely on?
  • Does the system profile natural persons in any feature we use?
  • What is your EU database entry (Article 49(2)), or when will you register?
  • Will you tell us if a market surveillance authority evaluates the classification (Article 80) or if a change makes the system high-risk?
  • How should we use it so we stay within the intended purpose you assessed?

More supplier questions: our AI vendor questions for deployers. If the system is high-risk, see our Article 26 deployer duties sheet.

If an authority disagrees

A market surveillance authority that has sufficient reason to consider a system classified under Article 6(3) is in fact high-risk evaluates it, and can require the provider to bring it into compliance and take corrective action within a set period (Article 80(1)-(2)). A provider that does not comply in time, or that misclassified the system to get around the Chapter III Section 2 requirements, is subject to fines under Article 99 (Article 80(4) and (7)). Member States lay down the penalty rules, taking into account the interests of SMEs (Article 99(1)).

Limits and open points

Dates: Article 6 sits in Chapter III Section 1, which applies to Annex III systems from 2 December 2027; Article 49 (registration) sits in Section 5, which Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 did not move, and the Act's general date of application is 2 August 2026 (Article 113). The text does not say in so many words when Article 49(2) registrations are expected for systems assessed before 2 December 2027. Our working plan: document the assessment now and register before launch or at the latest by 2 December 2027, and follow the EU database's and your authority's instructions. Guidelines: the Commission was to publish guidelines and practical examples on Article 6 (Article 6(5)); we did not read them for this guide, so check them alongside this sheet. The Commission may also add, change or delete conditions by delegated act (Article 6(6)-(7)). National law: penalties and the competent authorities are set nationally (Article 99(1)). Annex III point 5(b) excludes AI systems used to detect financial fraud. We read Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 as published (OJ L, 12.7.2024) and the amendments in Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (OJ L, 24.7.2026); check the consolidated version on EUR-Lex.

Frequently asked questions

When is an Annex III AI system not high-risk?

When it does not pose a significant risk of harm to health, safety or fundamental rights, including by not materially influencing the outcome of decision-making, and it is meant for a narrow procedural task, to improve a completed human activity, to detect decision patterns or deviations without replacing the human assessment without proper human review, or for a preparatory task (Article 6(3)). A system that profiles natural persons is always high-risk.

Does a provider using the exception still have to register?

Yes. The provider documents its assessment before placing the system on the market or putting it into service and registers itself and the system in the EU database (Articles 6(4) and 49(2)). Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 kept the registration and deleted two of the Annex VIII Section B fields (the summary of grounds and the list of Member States).

What should a business that buys the tool do?

Ask the supplier which Article 6(3) condition it relies on and for its EU database entry, which is public (Article 71(4)), use the tool within the intended purpose assessed, and keep the reply with its AI system record.

When do the high-risk rules apply to Annex III systems?

From 2 December 2027 for Chapter III Sections 1 to 3, and from 2 August 2028 for AI systems that are high-risk under Article 6(1) and Annex I (Article 113, point (c), as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744).
