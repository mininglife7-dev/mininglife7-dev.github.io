Staff AI use policy template for small EU businesses (EU AI Act and GDPR starting point)

Updated 14 Oct 2026 · Disclosure: the EURO AI Register mentioned below is an Unadhi product with a free edition. This guide is for information only, not legal advice.

Short answer: the EU AI Act does not require a document called an "AI policy". But since 2 February 2025, Article 4 has required providers and deployers of AI systems to take measures so that staff who use AI on their behalf have sufficient AI literacy. For a small team, a one-page set of rules that people have read, plus a list of approved tools and a briefing date, is a simple, dated way to show what you did. Copy the template below, fill in the brackets, and review it every 6 months.

Why a one-page policy is worth 30 minutes now

  • AI literacy (Article 4) and the banned practices (Article 5) have applied since 2 February 2025. Rules for general-purpose AI model providers have applied since 2 August 2025; those mainly concern the companies that build the models, not the businesses that use them.
  • Transparency duties (Article 50) for chatbots and AI-generated content apply from 2 August 2026. Staff need to know when to tell people they are dealing with AI.
  • GDPR still applies. Pasting customer or employee personal data into an AI tool is processing personal data. If the vendor processes it for you, you need a data processing agreement (GDPR Article 28) and a lawful basis.

For the full timeline, including what the Digital Omnibus on AI moved, see EU AI Act in 2026: what applies now. Official text: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689.

Before you write it: 5 facts to collect

  • Which AI tools are paid for or switched on (SaaS invoices, Microsoft 365 / Google Workspace AI features, website chatbot)
  • Which staff use free personal AI accounts for work
  • Whether any tool touches hiring, credit, staff monitoring or biometrics (flag for a lawyer)
  • Which vendors have a data processing agreement on file
  • Who will own the policy (a named person, not "IT")

The template (copy, edit, sign)

[COMPANY NAME] - Staff AI use policy
Version [1.0] · Effective [DATE] · Owner [NAME, ROLE] · Next review [DATE + 6 months]

1. Purpose and scope
This policy covers every employee, contractor and freelancer who uses AI tools
(chat assistants, writing, image, transcription, coding or analytics tools) for
[COMPANY NAME] work, on company or personal devices.

2. Approved tools
Only these tools may be used for work:
- [Tool 1] - for [purpose] - data allowed: [public / internal / personal data with DPA]
- [Tool 2] - for [purpose] - data allowed: [...]
Want a new tool? Ask [OWNER] first. It is added to our AI register before use.

3. You may use approved AI tools to
- draft emails, posts and documents that you then check and edit
- summarise public or internal non-personal material
- brainstorm, translate and proofread
- [other approved uses]

4. You must not
- paste customer, employee or supplier personal data, passwords, bank details or
  confidential contracts into any tool not marked "personal data with DPA" above
- use AI to decide on hiring, firing, pay, credit or customer eligibility without
  a named person reviewing the decision; raise any such use with [OWNER] first
- use AI to manipulate people, exploit vulnerabilities, score people socially or
  read emotions at work (these uses can be banned under the EU AI Act)
- present AI output as checked fact, legal, tax or medical advice without review
- sign up for new AI tools with a company email without approval

5. Human check
A person reads and corrects every AI output before it goes to a customer, a
public channel, an authority or a contract. The person who sends it is
responsible for it.

6. Telling people about AI
- Our website or support chatbot tells users they are talking to an AI.
- Images, audio or video made or edited with AI that could look real are labelled.
- If unsure, ask [OWNER].

7. Mistakes and incidents
Report wrong, harmful or leaked AI output, or personal data pasted by mistake, to
[OWNER] the same day. No blame for honest reports.

8. Training (AI literacy)
Everyone who uses AI for work reads this policy and attends a short briefing on
how our tools work, their limits and these rules. Dates are recorded below.

9. Review
[OWNER] reviews this policy every 6 months and whenever we add an AI tool.

Read and understood:
Name ______________ Role ______________ Date ______________ Signature __________

How to roll it out in one week

  1. Day 1: fill in sections 2 and 3 from your tool list. Keep it to one page.
  2. Day 2: check each approved tool's data terms and note which ones have a data processing agreement.
  3. Day 3: run a 20-minute briefing: what each tool is for, where it goes wrong (made-up facts, outdated answers, bias), and the "must not" list.
  4. Day 4–5: collect signatures and record the briefing date per person.
  5. Ongoing: every new tool goes through section 2 before anyone uses it.
