Short answer: Most small businesses are deployers of limited-risk AI. What binds them today is Article 4 AI literacy (since 2 February 2025) and Article 50 transparency (from 2 August 2026); Annex III high-risk duties start 2 December 2027. Public price points for help: SetAIComply's pricing page says “a full-time compliance hire costs €60k–120k per year, and a Big-4 engagement €75k–150k”; Crux Digits' public comparison puts specialist advice at €80–€300 per hour and fixed-price checks at roughly €950–€5,000. Our $29 kit is the step before any of that: list your AI systems, screen them, write the literacy policy and plan 90 days — so if you do hire help, you pay for judgement, not for list-making.

Why this matters now (official dates)

  • Article 4 AI literacy has applied since 2 February 2025 to providers and deployers, whatever the risk level. As replaced by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, it asks organisations to take measures to support the AI literacy of their staff; it does not require them to guarantee a specific level for any individual. The European Commission's Q&A says there is no need for a certificate: an internal record of trainings or guidance is enough, and national authorities supervise and enforce from August 2026 (Commission AI literacy Q&A).
  • Article 50 transparency duties (for example, telling people they are talking to a chatbot) apply from 2 August 2026 (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689).
  • High-risk rules were moved by the Digital Omnibus on AI, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744: Annex III uses (such as recruitment and credit scoring) from 2 December 2027, and AI in regulated products (Annex I) from 2 August 2028.

When you should hire a professional instead of (or after) the kit

  • A system may be high-risk: hiring, credit scoring, education, biometrics, medical, critical infrastructure.
  • You build AI and place it on the EU market as a provider.
  • A classification is contested, or you need legal privilege.

What the kit does not do

It does not classify your systems for you, is not a conformity assessment, and gives no seal. It is the inventory and AI-literacy first step. Crux Digits' own page warns against buying a retainer before a compliance check; an inventory is what any check starts from.

Can a $29 kit replace an EU AI Act lawyer or consultant?

No. Use the kit for groundwork: listing your AI systems, screening them, writing the literacy policy and planning 90 days. Hire a qualified lawyer when a system may be banned or high-risk, when you build AI as a provider, or when a classification is contested or you need legal privilege.

Do the EU AI Act high-risk rules apply from August 2026?

No. Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 set Annex III high-risk obligations for 2 December 2027 and Annex I regulated products for 2 August 2028. Article 4 AI literacy has applied since 2 February 2025, and Article 50 transparency duties apply from 2 August 2026.
