Short answer: when an online order makes the consumer pay, Article 8(2) of Directive 2011/83/EU says the trader must show, "in a clear and prominent manner, and directly before the consumer places his order", the main characteristics, the total price with taxes and delivery charges, the contract duration and termination conditions, any minimum duration and, in the consolidated text, the harmonised durability guarantee label where it applies (Article 6(1), points (a), (e), (la), (o) and (p)). The order button must be labelled "only with the words 'order with obligation to pay' or a corresponding unambiguous formulation". If it is not, "the consumer shall not be bound by the contract or order." The Court of Justice ruled that only the words on the button count, not the rest of the page (C-249/21). Article 8(3) adds that trading websites must show delivery restrictions and accepted means of payment "at the latest at the beginning of the ordering process", and Article 22 says pre-ticked paid extras must be refunded.

Why accountants, bookkeepers and web agencies should look at this now

  • It is about getting paid. A vague button ("Continue", "Complete booking") can mean the consumer is not bound by the order (Article 8(2), second subparagraph). In C-249/21 a hotel claimed a EUR 2,240 cancellation fee after a booking made with a "complete booking" button, and the question was whether a contract existed at all.
  • The list changed on 27 September 2026. EUR-Lex's consolidated text dated 27 September 2026 adds point (la), the harmonised durability guarantee label, to what must appear directly before the order button. Shop templates built earlier do not have it.
  • Hidden charges cannot be collected. If delivery or other charges were not shown as Article 6(1)(e) requires, "the consumer shall not bear those charges or costs" (Article 6(6)).
  • The burden of proof is on the trader (Article 6(9)), so a dated screenshot of each checkout step is worth keeping.
  • It is a quick, billable check. About 15 minutes per shop: one test checkout to the last step, eight answers, and the sheet writes the fix list for the client or their web designer.

Copy the formulas into row 2 and fill down. TEXTJOIN works in Excel 2019 or later, Microsoft 365, Google Sheets and LibreOffice. The word lists are our suggestion; add the wording your clients use. Column E is only a hint that delivery and payment information exists somewhere in the scraped text; Article 8(3) asks where it appears (by the start of the ordering process), which only the test checkout shows. Column N is our reading: the Directive gives one example ("order with obligation to pay") and says any other wording must be a "corresponding unambiguous formulation". Words such as "buy now" or "pay now" name paying or buying, so the sheet marks them likely OK; words such as "continue", "confirm" or "complete booking" do not, so it marks them VAGUE. The Court left it to the national court to decide whether "complete booking" is unambiguous in German, and national courts may draw the line differently, so treat N as a sorting rule, not a ruling. "PayPal" is ignored, so "Continue to PayPal" is not read as naming payment.

Step 3: test-checkout checklist (about 10 minutes per shop)

  • Open the shop as a consumer. Before adding anything to the basket, can you see where the shop delivers and which means of payment it accepts? Write G (Article 8(3): "at the latest at the beginning of the ordering process").
  • Add one product and go to the last step before payment. On that screen, next to the button, are the main characteristics and the total price with taxes and delivery shown? Write H and I. A delivery charge that first appears after the button counts as No.
  • Subscription, membership or fixed-term contract? Is the duration, how to end it and any minimum term shown on the same screen? Write J, or n.a.
  • Does the producer offer a free durability guarantee over two years for this product? If yes, is the harmonised label shown before the button? Write K, or n.a. if no such guarantee.
  • Copy the button words exactly into L. Look at the button only, not the text around it (C-249/21).
  • Are any paid extras ticked by default? Write M. Take dated screenshots of each step and stop before paying.

Frequently asked questions

What happens if the order button only says 'Continue' or 'Complete booking'?

Article 8(2) of Directive 2011/83/EU says the button must be labelled only with the words 'order with obligation to pay' or a corresponding unambiguous formulation, and if the trader has not complied, the consumer is not bound by the contract or order. In C-249/21 the Court of Justice ruled that only the words on the button are taken into account, and left it to the national court to decide whether 'complete booking' is unambiguous.

What must be shown directly before the order button?

In the EUR-Lex consolidated text dated 27 September 2026: the main characteristics, the total price including taxes and delivery or other charges, the contract duration and termination conditions, any minimum duration of the consumer's obligations and, where the producer offers a free durability guarantee of more than two years, the harmonised label (Article 6(1), points (a), (e), (la), (o) and (p)).

Where must delivery restrictions and payment methods appear?

Article 8(3): trading websites must indicate clearly and legibly, at the latest at the beginning of the ordering process, whether any delivery restrictions apply and which means of payment are accepted.

Are pre-ticked paid extras allowed?

Article 22 requires the consumer's express consent to any extra payment. If the trader inferred consent through default options the consumer had to reject, the consumer is entitled to reimbursement of that payment.

Does this apply to shops selling only to businesses?

No. The Directive applies to contracts between a trader and a consumer (Article 3(1)). The sheet marks business-only shops OUT OF SCOPE.
