AML client due diligence sheet for accountants and tax advisers: beneficial owners at 25%, update dates and retention under the EU AML Regulation from 10 July 2027 (Regulation (EU) 2024/1624)

What you get: a finished client due diligence sheet for accounting, bookkeeping and tax firms, in three tabs: a clients tab with one row per client that lists the gaps in each file against the EU Anti-Money Laundering Regulation and gives the next date the client information must be updated; an ownership tab that works out who the beneficial owners of a client company are using the 25% test; and a retention tab that gives the date each closed file must be deleted. A trigger checklist and a letter you can send to small business clients asking for their documents follow. Made-up example rows show how it fills in.

Short answer: Regulation (EU) 2024/1624, the EU AML Regulation, applies from 10 July 2027 directly in every Member State (Article 90). Auditors, external accountants and tax advisers are "obliged entities" (Article 3(3)(a)). Before taking on a client they must identify and verify the client and its beneficial owners (people with 25% or more, or control by other means, Articles 51 to 53), understand the purpose of the work, check targeted financial sanctions and whether anyone is a politically exposed person (Article 20(1)). Client information must be updated at least every 5 years, or every year for higher-risk clients (Article 26(2)), and records are kept for 5 years after the relationship ends, then deleted (Article 77(3)). Until 10 July 2027, your national AML law applies.

Why firms and their small business clients should look at this now

  • Fixed lists replace national variations. The Regulation sets out the minimum identity details for people and companies (Article 22(1)) and for beneficial owners (Article 22(2), pointing to Article 62(1)(a)). Files built under today's national rules may miss items such as place of birth, all nationalities or the beneficial owner's identity document number.
  • Every file needs an update date. The time between updates depends on risk but may never exceed 1 year for higher-risk clients and 5 years for all others (Article 26(2)). Changes in a client's circumstances also trigger a review (Article 26(3)).
  • No beneficial owner found is not the end of the file. If no natural person can be identified after all possible means, the firm records that and identifies and verifies all senior managing officials (Article 22(2)).
  • If you cannot finish due diligence, you cannot take the work. The firm must refrain from the business relationship, end an existing one and consider a suspicious transaction report (Article 21(1)), with a limited exception for ascertaining a client's legal position or court work (Article 21(2)).
  • AI tools in client onboarding have conditions. Decisions from automated processes or AI systems are allowed only if they use due diligence data, a person meaningfully reviews any decision to accept, refuse or change the level of checks, and the client can get an explanation and challenge it (Article 76(5)).

Step 2: tab 2, ownership (one row per person behind a client company)

Ownership interest means 25% or more of shares, voting rights or other ownership interest, directly or indirectly. Indirect ownership is worked out by multiplying the percentages along each chain of companies and adding the chains together (Article 52(1)). Control by other means is checked separately, whatever the percentage (Article 51 and Article 53(3): majority of voting rights, right to appoint or remove most of the board, veto rights, decisions on profit distribution).

Checklist: when due diligence must be done (Article 19(1))

  • ☐ Taking on a new client (a business relationship)
  • ☐ A one-off transaction of EUR 10 000 or more, in one go or in linked parts
  • ☐ Helping to set up a company or other legal entity, or (for accountants and tax advisers) helping to transfer ownership of one, whatever the amount
  • ☐ Any suspicion of money laundering or terrorist financing, whatever the amount
  • ☐ Doubts about whether earlier identity data is true or enough
  • ☐ Doubts about whether the person you deal with is really the client or authorised to act for it

National law and open points

The Regulation is directly applicable, but supervision, penalties, any lower national cash limit (Article 80(2)) and, where a Member State allows it, reporting suspicions through a self-regulatory body (Article 70(1)) are national. The EU Anti-Money Laundering Authority (AMLA) issues guidelines on risk factors and ongoing monitoring (Articles 20(3) and 26(5)). EUR-Lex also lists a pending court action (case T-451/24) asking for partial annulment of parts of Articles 19, 20 and 21. Check your national rules and your professional body's guidance. Frequently asked questions

When does the EU AML Regulation apply to accountants and tax advisers?

From 10 July 2027 (Article 90 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1624). Auditors, external accountants and tax advisers are obliged entities under Article 3(3)(a).

Who is a beneficial owner of a client company?

Any natural person who owns, directly or indirectly, 25% or more of the shares, voting rights or other ownership interest, or who controls the company by other means, such as the right to appoint or remove most of the board (Articles 51 to 53).

How often must client information be updated?

Based on risk, but at least every year for higher-risk clients and at least every 5 years for all others, and also when the client's circumstances change (Article 26(2) and (3)).
