Short answer: the EU Online Dispute Resolution platform is gone. Regulation (EU) 2024/3228 stopped new complaints on 20 March 2025 and repealed Regulation (EU) No 524/2013 with effect from 20 July 2025 (Articles 1 and 2). The old duty in Article 14 of Regulation 524/2013 to put "an electronic link to the ODR platform" on every EU web shop therefore ended on that date. Many shop footers, imprint pages, terms and order emails still carry the old link and sentence, which now points consumers to a service that no longer exists. What did not end: Article 13 of the ADR Directive 2013/11/EU. A trader that is committed or obliged to use an ADR (out-of-court dispute) entity must still name it, with its website address, on its website and, if applicable, in its terms. Directive (EU) 2025/2647 tightens this wording ("clear, prominent") from 20 September 2028.

Why accountants, bookkeepers and web agencies should look at this now

  • It is on almost every older EU shop. From 2016 the link was required for every online trader established in the EU, so shop themes, legal text generators and imprint pages copied it everywhere.
  • Old text can now mislead. A sentence like "you can use the EU ODR platform at ec.europa.eu/consumers/odr" invites customers to a closed service. Removing it is a small, clear fix.
  • The ADR part is easy to drop by mistake. When someone deletes the ODR paragraph, the ADR entity name the shop is committed to often goes with it. Article 13 of Directive 2013/11/EU still asks for it.
  • It is a quick, billable check. Around 15 minutes per client website, with one fix list you can send to the web designer.

What the law says, in short

  • The old link duty. Article 14(1) of Regulation 524/2013 required EU traders selling online, and online marketplaces, to "provide on their websites an electronic link to the ODR platform", and to state their email addresses. Article 14(2) added the link to offer emails and terms for traders committed or obliged to use ADR. Regulation 2024/3228 repealed the whole Regulation with effect from 20 July 2025.
  • Why it was closed. Recital 4 of Regulation 2024/3228: only about 200 cases a year across the Union reached an ADR entity through the platform; Recital 6: the platform should be discontinued.
  • The ADR duty that stays. Article 13(1) of Directive 2013/11/EU: traders that commit to, or are obliged to use, ADR entities must inform consumers about them, including the website address. Article 13(2): on the website, where one exists, and, if applicable, in the general terms. Article 13(3) (current text): when a complaint made directly to the trader could not be settled, the trader gives that information on paper or another durable medium, saying whether it will use the ADR entity.
  • The email still matters. The email duty in the repealed Article 14(1) is gone, but Article 6(1)(c) of the Consumer Rights Directive 2011/83/EU still requires contact details before a distance contract, and Article 6(1)(t) requires, where applicable, information on the out-of-court complaint and redress mechanism the trader is subject to and how to access it.
  • Other laws still mentioning ODR. Recital 8 of Regulation 2024/3228 notes that references in Directives 2015/2302, 2019/2161 and 2020/1828 were left for a separate act; Directive 2025/2647 makes those amendments (Articles 2 to 4).

Copy-ready wording (our suggestions)

If the client is committed or obliged to use an ADR entity: "We are committed to use [name of ADR entity] ([website address]) to resolve disputes with consumers. You can contact it if we cannot settle your complaint together."

If the client does not use ADR: remove the ODR paragraph. Whether a statement such as "we are not willing or obliged to take part in dispute resolution proceedings" is required depends on national law; some countries require it. Check before deleting such a sentence.

Frequently asked questions

Do EU web shops still need the ODR platform link?

No. Regulation (EU) 2024/3228 repealed Regulation (EU) No 524/2013, which contained the link duty, with effect from 20 July 2025, and the platform was discontinued.

Can we delete the whole dispute resolution paragraph?

Only the ODR part, safely. If the trader is committed or obliged to use an ADR entity, Article 13 of Directive 2013/11/EU still requires its name and website address on the website and, where applicable, in the general terms. Some national laws also require a statement when the trader does not take part.

What changes in 2028?

Directive (EU) 2025/2647 must be applied from 20 September 2028. ADR information must then be clear and prominent on the website and in the general terms, and traders contacted by an ADR entity must say within 20 working days whether they will take part, unless participation is mandatory, outcomes do not need their consent, or they already committed contractually.
