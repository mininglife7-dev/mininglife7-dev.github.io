Short answer: before a consumer is bound by an online order, Article 6(1)(e) requires the total price inclusive of taxes (or the manner of calculation) and all additional freight, delivery, postal and other costs (or that such charges may be payable). For subscriptions and indeterminate contracts, the Omnibus amendment adds total costs per billing period and, for fixed-rate contracts, total monthly costs. Article 6(1)(ea) requires, where applicable, notice that the price was personalised on the basis of automated decision-making. Article 6(1)(i) covers return costs on withdrawal. If (e) or (i) information is missing, Article 6(6) says the consumer shall not bear those charges or costs. Article 8(2) also requires point (e) to be shown clearly and prominently directly before the order when the contract is concluded by electronic means.

Why accountants, bookkeepers and web agencies should look at this now

  • Undisclosed extras are a money rule. Article 6(6) is blunt: miss the Article 6(1)(e) or (i) charges information and the consumer does not bear those charges.
  • Subscription shops often show only “from EUR X / month”. After the Omnibus amendment, Article 6(1)(e) asks for total costs per billing period and, for fixed-rate contracts, total monthly costs.
  • Personalised pricing needs a short notice. Article 6(1)(ea) is easy to miss on Shopify apps that score customers; Omnibus recital 45 distinguishes that from plain dynamic market pricing.
  • It is a quick, billable catalogue check. Run the product scraper, open cart and checkout once per shop, answer the columns, and the sheet writes the fix list with Article cites. Use our separate delivery arrangements sheet for timing and green delivery, and the 30-day lowest-price log for Directive 98/6/EC Article 6a prior-price announcements (including Black Friday 27 Nov 2026).

Step 3: the five-minute checklist per shop

  1. Open a typical product page. Is the total price inclusive of taxes clear (or the manner of calculation if the price cannot be fixed in advance)? (Column H / Article 6(1)(e); also Article 8(2) prominence before the order button.)
  2. Add to cart and open the last checkout step before paying. Are freight, delivery, postal and other costs shown as an amount, as free, or as a clear “may be payable” note? (I / Article 6(1)(e) and 6(6).)
  3. If the offer is a subscription or indeterminate contract, are total costs per billing period (and monthly costs if fixed-rate) shown? (J–K / Omnibus amendment to Article 6(1)(e).)
  4. If the shop personalises prices with automated decision-making, is that stated? Dynamic market pricing alone is not enough (L / Article 6(1)(ea); Omnibus recital 45).
  5. For goods: does the returns page say who pays return costs on withdrawal? If not told, the consumer shall not bear them (M / Articles 6(1)(i) and 6(6)).
  6. Take dated screenshots of product, cart, checkout and returns (Article 6(9) burden of proof on the trader).

Frequently asked questions

Must an EU web shop show a tax-inclusive total price before the consumer orders?

Article 6(1)(e) of Directive 2011/83/EU requires, before the consumer is bound by a distance or off-premises contract, 'the total price of the goods or services inclusive of taxes, or where the nature of the goods or services is such that the price cannot reasonably be calculated in advance, the manner in which the price is to be calculated, as well as, where applicable, all additional freight, delivery or postal charges and any other costs or, where those charges cannot reasonably be calculated in advance, the fact that such additional charges may be payable'. Article 8(2) also requires the trader to make the consumer aware of Article 6(1)(e) (among other points) in a clear and prominent manner directly before the order when the contract is concluded by electronic means and places the consumer under an obligation to pay.

What if shipping or other extra charges were not told before the order?

Article 6(6) says: if the trader has not complied with the information requirements on additional charges or other costs as referred to in point (e) of paragraph 1, or on the costs of returning the goods as referred to in point (i) of paragraph 1, the consumer shall not bear those charges or costs.

What extra total-price rules apply to subscriptions?

The Omnibus Directive (EU) 2019/2161 amended Article 6(1)(e). For a contract of indeterminate duration or a contract containing a subscription, the total price shall include the total costs per billing period. Where such contracts are charged at a fixed rate, the total price shall also mean the total monthly costs. Where the total costs cannot be reasonably calculated in advance, the manner in which the price is to be calculated shall be provided.

When must a shop say the price was personalised?

Article 6(1)(ea), inserted by Directive (EU) 2019/2161, requires, where applicable, that the consumer is informed 'that the price was personalised on the basis of automated decision-making'. Omnibus recital 45 explains that this covers personalisation based on automated decision-making and profiling of consumer behaviour; it should not apply to techniques such as dynamic or real-time pricing that change the price in response to market demands when those techniques do not involve personalisation based on automated decision-making.

Must return costs on withdrawal be stated?

Article 6(1)(i) requires, where applicable, that the consumer is told they will have to bear the cost of returning the goods in case of withdrawal and, for distance contracts, if the goods cannot normally be returned by post, the cost of returning the goods. If that information is missing, Article 6(6) says the consumer shall not bear those charges or costs. Is this the same as the delivery or prior-price sheets?

No. The delivery arrangements sheet focuses on shipping timing, arrangements, green delivery and Article 18. The prior-price / 30-day lowest-price sheets cover Directive 98/6/EC Article 6a price-reduction announcements.
