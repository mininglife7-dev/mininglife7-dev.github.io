Short answer: eIDAS knows three levels. Any electronic signature (a typed name, a scanned image, a click) cannot be refused as evidence only because it is electronic (Article 25(1)), but national law decides its weight (recital 49). An advanced signature must meet the four tests of Article 26. Only a qualified signature, an advanced signature made with a qualified signature creation device and based on a qualified certificate (Article 3(12)), has the same legal effect as a handwritten signature (Article 25(2)) and is recognised in every Member State (Article 24a(1)). Which level a document needs is a question of national law or the contract, not of eIDAS (Article 2(3)).

What to tell a client in one minute

"Most e-sign tools give you a simple electronic signature. That is fine for many everyday documents, and a court cannot throw it out just because it is electronic. But where the law or the other side asks for a handwritten signature, only a qualified electronic signature counts the same, and that needs a qualified certificate from a provider on an official EU trusted list. Some documents must still be signed on paper under national law. So before we sign electronically, we check what this document needs and what the tool really gives."

Why AI matters here

E-sign tools now add AI features: contract summaries, clause suggestions, automatic field filling and AI-assisted identity checks. None of these changes the signature level. The level depends only on the four tests in Article 26 and, for qualified, on the certificate and the device (Article 3(12)). Our reading: if an AI assistant or a colleague can apply your signature from a shared account, the "sole control" test in Article 26(c) is hard to meet, so answer column G with N. Recitals 51 and 52 accept signing devices held or managed by a third party (remote signing) only with mechanisms that keep the signatory in sole control.

The rules the sheet uses

  • Electronic signature (Article 3(10)): data in electronic form attached to or logically associated with other data and used by the signatory to sign. The signatory is a natural person (Article 3(9)).
  • Legal effect (Article 25): an electronic signature shall not be denied legal effect and admissibility as evidence solely because it is electronic or not qualified (25(1)); a qualified electronic signature has the equivalent legal effect of a handwritten signature (25(2)). Recital 49: otherwise it is for national law to define the legal effect.
  • Advanced (Article 26(1)): uniquely linked to the signatory; capable of identifying the signatory; created using signature creation data the signatory can, with a high level of confidence, use under their sole control; linked to the signed data so any later change is detectable.
  • Qualified (Article 3(12)): an advanced signature created by a qualified signature creation device (Annex II) and based on a qualified certificate (Article 3(15), Annex I) issued by a qualified trust service provider. Member States publish trusted lists of these providers (Article 22).
  • Cross-border: qualified signatures and seals based on a qualified certificate issued in one Member State are recognised as qualified in all others (Article 24a(1), inserted by Regulation (EU) 2024/1183, which deleted Article 25(3) and Article 35(3)).
  • Seals (Articles 3(25), 35, 36): a company's electronic seal ensures origin and integrity; the same four tests apply (Article 36); a qualified seal enjoys a presumption of integrity and correct origin (Article 35(2)). Recital 58: where a qualified seal is required, a qualified signature of the company's authorised representative should be equally acceptable.
  • Form rules stay national (Article 2(3) as amended): eIDAS does not affect Union or national law on the conclusion and validity of contracts, other form rules or sector-specific form requirements.
  • Electronic documents (Article 46): an electronic document shall not be denied legal effect and admissibility as evidence solely because it is electronic.
  • Coming: European Digital Identity Wallets must let all natural persons sign with qualified electronic signatures by default and free of charge (Article 5a(5)(g), inserted by Regulation (EU) 2024/1183); Member States may limit the free use to non-professional purposes (recital 20). Wallets are due within 24 months of the implementing acts (Article 5a(1)); we did not check those dates.

Questions

Is a typed name or a clicked 'I agree' a valid electronic signature in the EU?

It is an electronic signature (Article 3(10) of Regulation (EU) No 910/2014). It cannot be refused as evidence only because it is electronic or not qualified (Article 25(1)), but national law decides how much weight it carries (recital 49). Only a qualified electronic signature has the same legal effect as a handwritten signature (Article 25(2)).

What makes an electronic signature 'advanced'?

Article 26: it is uniquely linked to the signatory, capable of identifying the signatory, created using signature creation data the signatory can, with a high level of confidence, use under their sole control, and linked to the signed data so that any later change is detectable.

What makes it 'qualified'?

An advanced electronic signature created by a qualified electronic signature creation device and based on a qualified certificate for electronic signatures (Article 3(12)). Qualified certificates are issued by qualified trust service providers, which appear on national trusted lists (Article 22).

Is a qualified signature from another Member State accepted?

Yes. Qualified electronic signatures based on a qualified certificate issued in one Member State are recognised as qualified in all other Member States (Article 24a(1), inserted by Regulation (EU) 2024/1183, which deleted the old Article 25(3)).

Does eIDAS decide which documents may be signed electronically?

No. The Regulation does not affect Union or national law on the conclusion and validity of contracts, other legal or procedural form rules, or sector-specific form requirements (Article 2(3) as amended by Regulation (EU) 2024/1183). Check national law for each document type.

What is an electronic seal and when does a company use one?

An electronic seal is data attached to other data to ensure its origin and integrity, created by a legal person (Article 3(24), (25)). Recital 59 says seals serve as evidence that a document was issued by a legal person. A qualified seal enjoys a presumption of integrity and correct origin (Article 35(2)).
