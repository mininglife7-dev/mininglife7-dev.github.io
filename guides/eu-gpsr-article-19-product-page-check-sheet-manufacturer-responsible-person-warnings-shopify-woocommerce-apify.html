Short answer: since 13 December 2024 (GPSR Article 52), when you sell consumer products online to people in the EU, the offer "shall clearly and visibly indicate at least" four things (Article 19): (a) the manufacturer's name, registered trade name or trade mark and the "postal and electronic address at which they can be contacted"; (b) if the manufacturer is not established in the EU, the name, postal and electronic address of the EU responsible person; (c) information identifying the product, "including a picture of it, its type and any other product identifier"; and (d) any warning or safety information, in a language consumers can easily understand as set by the Member State where you sell. The Commission's guidelines repeat the same list for "a product offer on your e-shop" (C/2025/6233, Official Journal 21 November 2025).

Note on (a) and (b): if the manufacturer is outside the EU, the page needs both the manufacturer's details and the responsible person's. In a September 2024 reply to Toy Industries of Europe, Commission staff wrote that "the requirements under Article 19 (a) and (b) are to be read as cumulative" (a service-level reply, not an official interpretation; see Sources). The formula already treats them that way.

Are you selling to EU consumers at all? An online offer counts if it is "targeted at consumers in the Union" (Article 4). The Commission lists factors for that case-by-case test: where you dispatch to, the languages used, the payment methods, and use of a Member State's currency or domain name (Guidelines, section on sales channels). Shops outside the EU that ship to the EU are covered.

Frequently asked questions

Since when does Article 19 apply?

The GPSR applies from 13 December 2024 (Article 52).

Can I show only the EU responsible person for a non-EU brand?

No. Article 19(a) asks for the manufacturer's details in every case, and 19(b) adds the responsible person when the manufacturer is outside the EU. Commission staff called the two "cumulative" in a 2024 reply (not an official interpretation).

Does this cover marketplaces?

If you sell on a marketplace, Article 22(9) requires the marketplace to design its interface so that traders can enter the same information and to show it on the product listing. This sheet is built for your own web shop pages.

Is passing this sheet enough for GPSR?

No. Article 19 is one duty. The GPSR also covers safety by design and risk analysis (Article 9), the responsible person's checks (Article 16) and accident notification (Article 20), among others.

Not legal advice. The result formula only checks whether information is shown, not whether it is correct or whether the product is safe. Product-specific EU laws (toys, electrical goods, cosmetics and others) can add more labelling rules. When in doubt, ask your national market surveillance authority or a lawyer. Spotted an error?
