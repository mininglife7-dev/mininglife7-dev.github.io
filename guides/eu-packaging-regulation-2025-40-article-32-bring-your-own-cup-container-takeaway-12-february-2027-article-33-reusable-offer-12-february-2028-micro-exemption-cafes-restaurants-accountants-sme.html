Short answer: by 12 February 2027 (94 days from 10 Nov 2026), every business in the accommodation and food service sector that sells hot or cold drinks or ready-prepared food in take-away packaging in an EU country must let customers bring their own container to be filled, at no higher price and under no less favourable conditions, and must say so on a clearly visible sign at the point of sale (Regulation (EU) 2025/40, Article 32). This applies to micro-enterprises too. By 12 February 2028 the same businesses must also offer take-away in reusable packaging within a re-use system, again with a sign and at no higher price; micro-enterprises are exempt from that second step (Article 33).

What to tell a client in one minute

  • Small does not mean exempt. A one-person coffee cart selling take-away coffee is covered by the own-container rule from 12 February 2027. Only the reusable packaging offer (Article 33) has a micro-enterprise exemption.
  • Same price or cheaper. No surcharge for washing or handling the customer's cup. A discount is fine.
  • Put up the sign. A clearly visible, legible sign at the counter: you can bring your own cup or box.
  • Write the rules for refill. Which containers you accept, the hygiene standard and the customer's responsibility. Display them or hand them out. You may refuse a dirty or unsuitable container, and you are not liable for hygiene problems caused by it (Article 28).
  • Plan the reusable offer. Businesses with 10 or more staff, or with more than EUR 2 million turnover and balance sheet, need a reusable cup or box within a re-use system (for example a deposit system) by 12 February 2028.
  • Eat-in plastic ends in 2030. From 1 January 2030, single-use plastic plates, cups, trays and boxes for food and drinks eaten on the premises, and single-use plastic portion packs of sauces, sugar and creamer, may not be placed on the market (Article 25, Annex V points 3 and 4).

Checklist before 12 February 2027

  • List every client outlet that sells take-away drinks or food, including kiosks, food trucks, hotel bars and canteens.
  • Check the till, kiosk and delivery app prices: the own-container price must not be higher than the single-use price.
  • Put up the counter sign and display the rules for refill.
  • Train staff to accept clean containers and to refuse unsuitable ones politely, under the written rules.
  • Fix each client's micro status from the latest accounts, including partner and linked enterprises.
  • For clients that are not micro: choose a re-use system (for example a deposit cup scheme) in time for 12 February 2028.
  • Budget now for replacing single-use plastic eat-in tableware and portion packs before 1 January 2030.

Limits and our reading

  • Not read, flagged: national penalty rules (Member States set them by 12 February 2027, Article 68(1)); national re-use targets or extra rules under Article 51 and Article 33(6); national measures kept under Article 70(4); the NACE Rev. 2 classification itself (we used the definition's wording "Accommodation and Food Service Activities"); EEA incorporation of Regulation 2025/40; the Commission FAQs that accompany guidance C/2026/3084; the Commission's Annex V guidelines due by 12 February 2027 (Article 25(6)); the Single-Use Plastics Directive 2019/904 and national laws based on it beyond what guidance C/2026/3084 says; food hygiene law. The EU Publications Office search we ran on 10 Nov 2026 found no act amending Articles 28, 32 or 33 (it found Delegated Decision (EU) 2026/429 on pallet wrappings and a Commission proposal on authorised representatives for EPR).
  • Our reading: Article 32 has no size exemption, so the sheet applies it to micro-enterprises; a business without take-away sales is treated as outside Articles 32 and 33; "provide a system" is checked as: accepts own containers, same or lower price and conditions, and a sign; the micro test uses fewer than 10 staff and turnover or balance sheet up to EUR 2 million and does not add partner and linked enterprises for you; the dates in Articles 32 and 33 ("by") are read as the last day; the Annex V check is a planning flag only and does not test the 5 % plastic threshold or the drinking-water and micro options.

Questions

Do cafés and takeaways have to accept customers' own cups in the EU?

Yes, from 12 February 2027. Businesses in the accommodation and food service sector that sell hot or cold drinks or ready-prepared food in take-away packaging must provide a system for consumers to bring their own container to be filled (Regulation (EU) 2025/40, Article 32(1)). There is no micro-enterprise exemption in Article 32.

Can we charge more when a customer brings their own cup?

No. The product must be offered at no higher cost and under no less favourable conditions than the same product sold in single-use packaging (Article 32(2)). A discount is allowed; a surcharge is not.

Do we need a sign?

Yes. Article 32(2) requires clearly visible and legible information boards or signs at the point of sale telling consumers they can get the product in a container they bring. Article 33(2) adds a second sign once you offer reusable packaging.

Can we refuse a dirty container?

Yes. If you offer refill you must tell customers which containers can be used, the hygiene standards and their own responsibility (Article 28(1)). You may refuse a container that does not follow those rules, in particular if it is unhygienic or unsuitable, and you bear no liability for hygiene or food safety issues from containers provided by the customer (Article 28(4)).

What changes on 12 February 2028?

From that date the same businesses must also give consumers the option of take-away in reusable packaging within a re-use system, with a sign and at no higher price (Article 33(1) to (3)). Micro-enterprises (fewer than 10 staff and turnover or balance sheet up to EUR 2 million) are exempt from Article 33 (Article 33(4)), but not from Article 32.

Does this apply in Northern Ireland?

No. The Commission notice on the Windsor Framework (C/2025/946) lists Articles 29 to 33 of Regulation (EU) 2025/40 among the provisions that do not apply to and in the United Kingdom in respect of Northern Ireland.
