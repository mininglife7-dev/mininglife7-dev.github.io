One month to answer: a GDPR Article 12 deadline tracker for customer data requests that come in through AI chatbots and tools

Updated 27 Oct 2026 · Disclosure: the EURO AI Register mentioned below is an Unadhi product with a free edition. This guide is for information only, not legal advice.

What you get: a finished request deadline tracker in three tabs: a request log with one row per request and a reply-by date formula that handles short months and weekends, an extension and refusal tab with the reasons you must give, and a holidays tab for your country, plus AI tool examples and two made-up example rows.

Short answer: under Article 12(3) GDPR you must tell the person what you did about an access, correction, erasure, restriction, portability or objection request without undue delay and within one month of receiving it. You can add two further months only where needed because of the complexity and number of requests, and you must tell the person about the extension, with reasons, inside the first month. If you will not act, Article 12(4) says you must explain why, and mention the right to complain and go to court, also within one month. Replies are free of charge unless a request is manifestly unfounded or excessive (Article 12(5)).

Why AI tools make this harder

Requests now arrive through chatbots, AI email assistants and support widgets, and the data sits in several AI suppliers at once: chat transcripts, call recordings, meeting notes, lead lists. A request that lands in a chatbot transcript on a Friday can be missed for days. Article 12(2) says you must make it easy to use these rights, so a request counts from the day it reaches you, whichever channel it came through.

Step 3: tab 3, holidays

List your country's public holidays for 2026 and 2027 in column A, one date per row, and name the range Holidays. The reply-by formulas skip these days and weekends. If you handle requests for several EU countries, keep one column per country and point each row at the right one.

Checks before you send a reply

  • Did you search every AI tool listed in column D, including supplier-held logs?
  • Is the reply in clear and plain language (Article 12(1))?
  • Did you tell every recipient about a correction, erasure or restriction? See the Article 19 recipient notices sheet
