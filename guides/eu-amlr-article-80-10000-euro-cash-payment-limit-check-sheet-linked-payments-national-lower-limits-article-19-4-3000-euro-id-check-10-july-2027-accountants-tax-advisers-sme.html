Short answer: From 10 July 2027 (AMLR Article 90; Settings!B3 counts the days, 242 on 10 Nov 2026) one EU-wide rule applies: "Persons trading in goods or providing services may accept or make a payment in cash only up to an amount of EUR 10 000 or the equivalent in national or foreign currency, whether the transaction is carried out in a single operation or in several operations which appear to be linked" (Article 80(1)). It covers paying as well as receiving. Countries may keep or set lower limits (Article 80(2) and (3)). Payments between private persons not acting professionally, and payments or deposits made at the premises of a bank, e-money institution or payment service provider, are outside the limit (Article 80(4)). Until 10 July 2027 only your national rules apply; check them.

What to tell a client in one minute

  • 10,000 euro is the ceiling, not the trigger. "Up to an amount of EUR 10 000": exactly 10,000 is allowed; 10,000.01 is not.
  • Splitting does not help. Several operations "which appear to be linked" count together. A linked transaction is "two or more transactions with either identical or similar origin, destination and purpose, or other relevant characteristics, over a specific period" (Article 2(1)(20)). Give linked payments the same Link ID; the sheet adds them up.
  • Foreign currency counts at its euro value. The limit is "EUR 10 000 or the equivalent in national or foreign currency". Enter the rate you used.
  • Cash is more than banknotes. AMLR uses the definition in Regulation (EU) 2018/1672: currency, bearer-negotiable instruments (such as traveller's cheques and bearer cheques), gold coins with at least 90% gold, gold bars with at least 99.5% gold, and prepaid cards (Article 2(1)(a) and Annex I).
  • Paying in at the bank is different. A payment or deposit made at the premises of a bank or payment institution is outside the limit; above the limit it "shall be reported to the FIU within the deadlines imposed by the FIU" (Article 80(4)(b)).
  • Penalties are national. Member States must take "appropriate measures, including the imposition of penalties" against businesses suspected of breaching the limit (Article 80(5)).

What we tested

Fourteen made-up payments in LibreOffice (xlsx → ods → csv), today fixed at 10 Nov 2026, in two runs. Run 1 (no national limit, not an obliged entity): 14/14 intended results and an empty row staying blank, no formula errors. 8,500 euro for a used car (OK); 12,000 for a machine in September 2027 (OVER LIMIT); 15,000 paid to a builder in January 2027 (PREPARE: the EU limit does not apply yet on that date); 6,000 plus 5,000 for the same furniture order two days apart (both OVER LIMIT, linked total 11,000); a private car sale between private persons (OUT OF SCOPE); 25,000 paid in at the supplier's bank branch (EXEMPT); 9,900 Swiss francs at 1.07 (OVER LIMIT, 10,593 euro); 9,000 euro paid in gold coins (OK); an unclear voucher (CHECK BY HAND); a cash loan repayment (CHECK BY HAND); a dollar amount without a rate (FIX); 4,000 from a walk-in buyer (OK); exactly 10,000 (OK). Run 2 (national limit 5,000, business is an obliged entity): 14/14 again, with the 4,000 walk-in buyer giving IDENTIFY CUSTOMER (Art 19(4)) and every row above 5,000 giving OVER LIMIT.

Client checklist

  • List every cash payment in or out of 3,000 euro or more, and every deal paid in parts.
  • From 10 July 2027, take or pay anything above 10,000 euro (or your lower national limit) by bank transfer or card.
  • Do not split a payment to stay under the limit: linked operations count together.
  • Convert foreign cash at the rate of the day and keep the rate.
  • Remember gold coins, gold bars, bearer cheques and prepaid cards count as cash.
  • Change price lists, contracts and payment plans that allow large cash payments after 10 July 2027.
  • Jewellers, dealers in high-value goods and other obliged entities: identify the customer for one-off cash payments from 3,000 euro.

Limits and our reading

  • Dates. Article 80 and Article 19 apply from 10 July 2027 (Article 90). Until then, only national rules apply, including any national cash limits already in force.
  • Not read, flagged: national cash limits and their exact wording (enter your country's limit in Settings!B5 and check whether "up to" or "below" is used); national penalties; AMLA regulatory technical standards on linked transactions and lower thresholds (Article 19(9)); later amendments to Annex I of Regulation (EU) 2018/1672 (prepaid cards); VAT and tax-law rules on cash receipts.
  • Our reading, flagged: treating column D OTHER payments (loans, capital, wages, refunds) as CHECK BY HAND rather than in or out of scope; the reading that the FIU report for bank-premises payments in Article 80(4)(b) is made by the bank (the sentence does not name who reports); summing linked payments across both directions and dates by Link ID; keeping the bank receipt is our suggestion.

FAQ

When does the EU 10,000 euro cash limit apply?

From 10 July 2027, when Regulation (EU) 2024/1624 applies (Article 90). Until then only national rules apply, including national cash limits already in force.

Is exactly 10,000 euro in cash allowed?

Yes. Article 80(1) allows cash payments 'only up to an amount of EUR 10 000'. Anything above 10,000 euro is not allowed, unless an exception in Article 80(4) applies. A lower national limit may apply.

Can a client split a large payment into several cash payments?

No. The limit applies 'whether the transaction is carried out in a single operation or in several operations which appear to be linked' (Article 80(1)). Linked transactions have identical or similar origin, destination and purpose, or other relevant characteristics, over a specific period (Article 2(1)(20)).

Does the limit apply when a business pays cash, not only when it receives it?

Yes. Persons trading in goods or providing services may 'accept or make' a cash payment only up to 10,000 euro (Article 80(1)).

Which cash payments are outside the limit?

Payments between natural persons who are not acting in a professional capacity, and payments or deposits made at the premises of credit institutions, e-money issuers and payment service providers (Article 80(4)). The second group above the limit is reported to the FIU.

Do gold coins and prepaid cards count as cash?

Yes. AMLR uses the definition of cash in Regulation (EU) 2018/1672: currency, bearer-negotiable instruments, gold coins with at least 90% gold and gold bars with at least 99.5% gold, and prepaid cards (Article 2(1)(a) and Annex I).
