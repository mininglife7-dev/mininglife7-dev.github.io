Short answer: since 28 May 2022 (Directive (EU) 2019/2161, Article 7), three EU rules apply to every web shop that shows consumer reviews or star ratings. Article 7(6) of the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive 2005/29/EC: if a trader provides access to consumer reviews, information about whether and how it ensures the reviews come from consumers who actually used or purchased the product is "material". Leaving it out can be a misleading omission. Annex I point 23b: saying the reviews come from such consumers without "reasonable and proportionate steps" to check is always unfair. Annex I point 23c: submitting or commissioning fake reviews, or misrepresenting reviews (for example publishing only the positive ones), is always unfair. National fines can reach at least 4% of annual turnover in coordinated cross-border cases (Article 13(3)).

Why accountants, bookkeepers and web agencies should look at this now

  • Almost every shop theme shows stars. Shopify and WooCommerce review apps print labels such as "Verified buyer" by default. The label is a statement under point 23b, so the client needs a real check behind it.
  • The notice is usually missing. Many small shops show reviews with no word on how they are collected, whether negative ones are published or how the average score is worked out. The Commission's guidance says this information belongs next to the reviews (section 4.2.4).
  • Review incentives are common. "10% off for a review" emails, free samples and influencer seeding create rewarded reviews. Recital 47 of Directive 2019/2161 asks traders to say whether reviews have been sponsored or influenced by a contractual relationship.
  • It is a quick, billable check. About 10 minutes per shop for three product pages, with one fix list you can send to the client or their web designer.

Step 4: tab 3, check steps (what counts as "reasonable and proportionate")

Section 4.2.4 of the Commission's guidance (2021/C 526/01) lists these as possible steps. What is enough depends on the business model, its size and the level of risk. Small shops are not expected to match large review sites and marketplaces, and checks must not make honest reviewing too hard.

Practices to stop (Annex I 23c, Recital 49, guidance 4.2.4): buying reviews or likes, or asking staff, friends or paid people to write them; publishing only positive reviews and deleting negative ones; linking a customer's endorsement to different content than they meant; pre-filled positive review templates; contacting customers during moderation to get them to change or withdraw a negative review; and average ratings built on undisclosed or opaque criteria. Removing fake negative reviews as part of a real check remains allowed.

What the law says, in short

  • Article 7(6) UCPD (inserted by Article 3(4)(c) of Directive 2019/2161): "Where a trader provides access to consumer reviews of products, information about whether and how the trader ensures that the published reviews originate from consumers who have actually used or purchased the product shall be regarded as material."
  • Recital 47 of Directive 2019/2161: traders should say whether processes are in place, how the checks are made, and how reviews are processed, for example whether all reviews, positive or negative, are posted and whether reviews have been sponsored or influenced by a contractual relationship with a trader.
  • Commission guidance, section 4.2.4: the duty applies to any trader giving access to reviews, also through a third-party review tool shown on its site. The information must be clear and intelligible and available on the same interface where the reviews are shown, including through clearly identified, prominent links. It covers how reviews are sourced, whether all are published and how average scores are calculated. "Reviews" includes ratings.
  • Annex I points 23b and 23c (inserted by Article 3(7)(b) of Directive 2019/2161) are practices that are unfair "in all circumstances". No test of the effect on the consumer is needed.
  • Penalties, Article 13 (replaced by Article 3(6)): effective, proportionate and dissuasive. In coordinated actions under Article 21 of Regulation (EU) 2017/2394, the maximum fine must be at least 4% of the trader's annual turnover in the Member States concerned, or at least EUR 2 million where turnover information is not available. National amounts and procedures are set by each Member State.
  • Dates: Member States had to adopt the rules by 28 November 2021 and apply them from 28 May 2022 (Directive 2019/2161, Article 7(1)).

Copy-ready "how we check reviews" notice (our suggestion)

Put it directly above or below the reviews, or link to it there with a visible link such as "How we check reviews". Change it to match what the client really does. Never claim a check that does not happen:

"How we check reviews. We invite reviews only from customers who have placed an order, by an email sent [X] days after delivery. Each review is linked to an order number. We publish all reviews, positive and negative, except those that break our review rules (abusive language, personal data, or not about the product). The star score is the average of all published reviews for this product over [all time / the last X months]. Customers who received [a discount / a free product] for a review are marked as such. Questions about a review: [role email]."

If the client does not check reviews: remove "verified" and "customer" labels, and say plainly: "We do not check whether reviewers have bought the product."

Frequently asked questions

Does a small web shop have to explain how it checks reviews?

Yes, if it shows consumer reviews or ratings. Article 7(6) of Directive 2005/29/EC treats information on whether and how the trader ensures reviews come from real buyers or users as material. If the shop does not check, it should say so and must not present the reviews as coming from buyers.

Can a shop label reviews as "verified"?

Only if it takes reasonable and proportionate steps to check that they come from consumers who actually used or purchased the product, for example by linking reviews to orders. Otherwise it is a banned practice under Annex I point 23b.

Can a shop delete negative reviews?

Publishing only positive reviews and deleting negative ones misrepresents reviews and is banned under Annex I point 23c (Recital 49 of Directive 2019/2161). Removing fake negative reviews as part of a genuine check of where reviews come from remains allowed (Commission guidance, section 4.2.4).
