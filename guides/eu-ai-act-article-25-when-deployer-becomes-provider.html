Article 25 of the EU AI Act: three ways a deployer turns into a provider (and takes on provider duties)

Last updated 21 Oct 2026 · India

Short answer. Under Article 25(1) of the EU AI Act (Regulation 2024/1689), a deployer, distributor or importer is treated as the provider of a high-risk AI system, with the provider obligations of Article 16, if it: (a) puts its name or trademark on a high-risk system already on the market; (b) makes a substantial modification to a high-risk system that stays high-risk; or (c) changes the intended purpose of an AI system (including a general-purpose one) so that it becomes high-risk. The original provider must then cooperate and hand over the information needed (Article 25(2)).

Three triggers, with SME examples

TriggerExample
(a) Your brand on itA recruitment agency white-labels a CV-screening tool as its own product
(b) Substantial modificationYou retrain a supplier's credit-scoring model on your data and change how it decides
(c) New, high-risk purposeYou use a general chatbot to rank job applicants or decide on staff promotions

What changes if you become the provider

  • Risk management, data governance and technical documentation (Articles 9–11)
  • Logging, transparency to deployers, human oversight, accuracy and robustness (Articles 12–15)
  • Quality management system, conformity assessment, CE marking and EU database registration

How to stay a deployer

  • Use high-risk tools only for the purpose the provider states
  • Do not rebrand them as your own
  • Get written confirmation from the vendor before retraining or changing logic
  • Record each AI system, its purpose and your role, and review when use changes

Common mistakes

  • Assuming "we only use it" always means deployer. Purpose changes can flip your role.
  • Using general AI for HR or credit decisions without checking Annex III.
  • No record of the intended purpose agreed with the vendor.

Frequently asked questions

When does a deployer become a provider under the AI Act?

Under Article 25(1): when it puts its name or trademark on a high-risk AI system, substantially modifies one that remains high-risk, or changes an AI system's intended purpose so it becomes high-risk.

Can using ChatGPT make my company a provider?

It can, if you change a general-purpose system's intended purpose to a high-risk use listed in Annex III, such as ranking job applicants (Article 25(1)(c)).

What happens to the original provider?

It is no longer the provider of that specific system for the AI Act, but must cooperate and provide the information needed (Article 25(2)).

What duties does a new provider take on?

The provider obligations in Article 16, including risk management, documentation, conformity assessment and registration.
