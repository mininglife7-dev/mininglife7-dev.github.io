Short answer: since 18 August 2025 the waste battery chapter of Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 applies (Article 96(2)(c)). A shop selling batteries with delivery must offer free take-back “at the point of delivery to the end-user or at a local collection point”, and “the end-user shall be informed when ordering a battery of the take back arrangements” (Article 62(5)). From 18 February 2027 (101 days from 9 November 2026), products with portable batteries need removal and replacement instructions “made available permanently online, on a publicly available website” (Articles 11(1) and 96(2)(a)).

Why accountants, bookkeepers and web agencies should look at this now

  • The rules hit small online sellers directly. A shop that imports torches, toys or e-bike packs from outside the country, or ships batteries to buyers in another Member State, can be a “producer” under Article 3(47), with registration in each country (Article 55(2)). Registration and producer fees are costs your client may not have booked.
  • Selling into another country needs a local representative. A seller shipping batteries directly to end-users in a Member State where it is not established must appoint an authorised representative there by written mandate (Article 56(3)).
  • The take-back line is easy to miss. Many checkouts say nothing about old batteries. One sentence and a link usually fix it.
  • It is a quick, billable check that pairs with our product safety page sheet and our reparability and spare parts sheet.

Product page and checkout wording (template)

Old batteries: we take them back free of charge, whatever the brand and
whether or not you buy from us. Hand them to our driver at delivery or
drop them at [collection point / list of collection points]. Our
collection points: [link]. Why separate collection matters, safety tips
for lithium batteries and what the symbols mean: [producer or scheme
information page]. Included producer recycling cost: [amount, if the
producer gives one].

Adapt the delivery promise to what the shop really offers; Article 62(5) allows either the point of delivery or a local collection point.

Questions for the supplier or brand

1. In which Member States are you registered in the producer register for
   these batteries, and what are the registration numbers (Article 55)?
2. Which compliance scheme (producer responsibility organisation) do you
   use in each country (Article 57)?
3. Where is your waste battery information page for end-users
   (Article 74(1)(a)-(f)), in the local languages?
4. What producer cost per battery should we show at the point of sale

Limits and our reading

  • National law and registers decide the details. Registration steps, collection schemes, fees, languages and penalties are set nationally; we did not read them. We read the Regulation as published in 2023 and did not check later amending acts, so confirm the dates on EUR-Lex before you rely on them.
  • Our reading, flagged: that a web shop selling products with batteries should show the Article 74 information too; treating Article 11 as applying to whoever places the product on the market (brand or importer), so a reseller asks the brand; keeping a supplier's registration number on file (our suggestion, not a legal duty for a plain distributor); and treating a missing producer cost line as CHECK rather than FIX, because the amount comes from the producer.
  • Not covered: battery labels (Article 13) and the CE marking, spare-part availability for five years (Article 11(7)), carbon footprint and recycled content, due diligence, the battery passport, the take-back of products with batteries under the rules on waste electrical and electronic equipment, and online marketplace duties under Article 62(6).

FAQ

Do web shops have to take back old batteries?

Yes, for the categories of batteries they sell. Article 62(1) obliges distributors to take back waste batteries free of charge and without requiring a purchase, and Article 62(4) applies this to distributors selling at a distance to end-users. For sales with delivery, the shop must offer free take-back at the point of delivery or at a local collection point, and the buyer must be told the take-back arrangements when ordering a battery (Article 62(5)). These rules are in Chapter VIII, which applies from 18 August 2025 (Article 96(2)(c)).

Does take-back cover phones, toys or torches with a battery inside?

Not under Article 62: the take-back obligation 'shall not apply to waste products containing batteries' (Article 62(2)(a)). Those products are usually handled under the national rules for waste electrical and electronic equipment, which this guide does not cover.

What changes on 18 February 2027?

Article 11 applies (Article 96(2)(a)). Anyone placing on the market products with portable batteries must make the batteries readily removable and replaceable by the end-user, and the instructions and safety information on use, removal and replacement must be 'made available permanently online, on a publicly available website' (Article 11(1)). Some products are exempt (Article 11(2) and (3)).

Is a web shop a battery producer?

It can be. Article 3(47) counts as producer anyone who sells under its own name, first supplies batteries in a Member State from another country, or sells by distance contract directly to end-users in a Member State while established in another country. Producers must register in each Member State where they first make batteries available (Article 55(2)), and a seller of the last type must appoint an authorised representative there by written mandate (Article 56(3)).

What information must the shop show about waste batteries?

Distributors must provide the producer's information on waste prevention, separate collection, collection points, safety, the meaning of the labels and the impact of hazardous substances (Article 74(1)(a) to (f)), plus how to return waste batteries free of charge (Article 74(4)), also when selling through online marketplaces. Article 74(5) adds that the producer's costs listed in Article 56(4)(a) to (d) are shown separately to the end-user at the point of sale of a new battery.

No. The product scraper lists products, titles and prices so you can find the battery lines quickly. Whether a page shows take-back and waste battery information is checked by hand, with a dated screenshot.
