Short answer: Article 5(1) of the e-Commerce Directive (2000/31/EC) says a business with a website or web shop must make "easily, directly and permanently accessible" at least: (a) its name; (b) the geographic address where it is established; (c) contact details "including his electronic mail address" that allow it to be contacted "rapidly" and "in a direct and effective manner"; (d) its trade register and registration number, if registered; (e) the supervisory authority, if the activity needs an authorisation; (f) for regulated professions (accountants, tax advisers, lawyers and others), the professional body, title and Member State, and a reference to the professional rules; and (g) the VAT identification number, if the activity is subject to VAT. If you sell to consumers at a distance, Article 6(1)(c) of the Consumer Rights Directive (2011/83/EU, as amended from 28 May 2022) adds the telephone number and email address before the consumer is bound.

Frequently asked questions

Does every website need a phone number?

Under the e-Commerce Directive, not necessarily. In deutsche internet versicherung (C-298/07, 16 October 2008) the Court of Justice held that, besides the email address, the provider must give another means of rapid, direct and effective contact, which "does not necessarily have to be a telephone number"; an online enquiry form answered by email can be enough (in that case replies came within 30 to 60 minutes), but a non-electronic means must be offered on request to someone without network access. For distance sales to consumers, Article 6(1)(c) of the Consumer Rights Directive, as amended by Directive (EU) 2019/2161 from 28 May 2022, lists the telephone number and email address as information to give.

Is the VAT number always required on the website?

Article 5(1)(g) asks for it "where the service provider undertakes an activity that is subject to VAT". A small business that is not subject to VAT has nothing to show under (g); write N in column J.

Are these all the rules for a legal notice?

No. These are the EU minimums. Article 5(1) starts "at least", and each Member State writes its own national law with its own wording and sometimes extra items, and other EU laws add more (for example price, withdrawal and complaint information for consumer sales in Article 6 of the Consumer Rights Directive). Check your country's rule before you tell a client their site is complete.

What happens if details are missing?

Both directives leave penalties to the Member States, which must be "effective, proportionate and dissuasive" (e-Commerce Directive Article 20; Consumer Rights Directive Article 24).
