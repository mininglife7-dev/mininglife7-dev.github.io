Short answer: For one or two AI tools, none of them high-risk, a spreadsheet with one row per system, its classification, the article and the date you decided is a fair record — even a paid competitor, CompliPath, says so on its own page, and Crux Digits calls a spreadsheet “a legitimate starting point”. A spreadsheet starts to break when the law changes under it (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 replaced Article 4 and moved the high-risk dates), when you need evidence linked to each duty, or when a customer asks you to show rather than tell. Why this matters now (dates (source: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689))

  • Article 4 AI literacy has applied since 2 February 2025 to providers and deployers, whatever the risk level. As replaced by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, it asks organisations to take measures to support the AI literacy of their staff; it does not require them to guarantee a specific level for any individual. The European Commission's Q&A says there is no need for a certificate: an internal record of trainings or guidance is enough, and national authorities supervise and enforce from August 2026 (Commission AI literacy Q&A).
  • Article 50 transparency duties (for example, telling people they are talking to a chatbot) apply from 2 August 2026 (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689).
  • High-risk rules were moved by the Digital Omnibus on AI, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744: Annex III uses (such as recruitment and credit scoring) from 2 December 2027, and AI in regulated products (Annex I) from 2 August 2028.

Rules of thumb: one row per system as used (the same tool for two purposes can be two rows), record who decided and when, and re-check every row when the law changes — as it did on 27 July 2026, when Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 entered into force.

Signs you have outgrown the spreadsheet

  • More than a handful of AI tools across teams, and nobody is sure the sheet is current.
  • A customer or supervisor asks for the evidence behind a row, not just the row.
  • A system might touch hiring, credit, education or biometrics (Annex III) — then also talk to a lawyer.

Frequently asked questions

Is a spreadsheet enough for an EU AI Act AI inventory?

For one or two AI systems that are not high-risk, yes: a spreadsheet with the classification, the article relied on and the decision date is a fair record. Paid vendor CompliPath and consultancy Crux Digits both say this on their own public pages, read on 14 Oct 2026.

What columns should an AI inventory have for the EU AI Act?

The Act sets no single template for deployers. Useful columns: system name, vendor, purpose, owner, your role, the Article 5 banned-practice check, Annex III and Annex I checks, Article 50 transparency trigger, decision and date, who decided, literacy measures taken, evidence link and next review date.

When have I outgrown a spreadsheet for AI Act records?

Signs include more than a handful of AI tools across teams with nobody sure the sheet is current, a customer or supervisor asking for the evidence behind a row, or a system that may touch hiring, credit, education or biometrics under Annex III. In that last case, also talk to a lawyer.

Do I need a certificate to prove Article 4 AI literacy?

No. The European Commission's AI literacy Q&A says there is no need for a certificate; an internal record of trainings or guidance is enough. As replaced by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, Article 4 asks organisations to take measures to support staff AI literacy, not to guarantee a specific level for each person.
