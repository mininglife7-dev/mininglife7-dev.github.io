Someone disputes what your AI tool says about them? A GDPR Article 18 restriction request sheet for small businesses

Updated 25 Oct 2026 · Disclosure: the EURO AI Register mentioned below is an Unadhi product with a free edition. This guide is for information only, not legal advice.

What you get: a finished restriction request sheet: one row per request, a column to pick which of the four Article 18 grounds applies, a reply-date formula (one month, or three with an extension), a list of every AI tool and export holding the data with how you froze it, the suppliers you told under Article 19, a status formula, the notice before lifting, and two made-up example rows.

Short answer: if someone says what your AI tool recorded about them is wrong, or objects to how you use it, they can ask you to stop using that data while you check. You then keep it but do not use it, except with their consent or for legal claims and a few other narrow reasons. Reply within one month, tell the suppliers you shared it with, and tell the person before you lift the restriction.

Step 1: spot the request

  • Typical AI cases: "your call summary says I agreed to something I never agreed to", "your chatbot logged the wrong complaint", "stop using that AI lead score until you check it", "keep my data, I need it for my dispute with you".
  • Log it on the day it arrives. The one-month clock in Article 12(3) starts on receipt, not when someone reads the inbox.
  • If you have reasonable doubts about who is asking, you may ask for information to confirm identity (Art. 12(6)); note it in column C.

Step 2: the restriction request sheet (one row per request)

ColumnWhat to writeSource
A. ReceivedDate the request arrived and how (email, form, phone, chat). Any wording counts; the person does not have to say "Article 18"Art. 12(2), 12(3)
B. Reply due=EDATE(A2,1). If the request is complex and you extend, the new date is =EDATE(A2,3) and you must tell the person, with reasons, before B2Art. 12(3)
C. Person and dataWho asked (identity checked how), and which data: an AI call summary, a chatbot transcript, an AI lead score, an AI-written note on their fileArt. 12(6)
D. GroundPick one: 1 accuracy contested · 2 unlawful, wants restriction not erasure · 3 you no longer need it, they need it for a legal claim · 4 objection under Art. 21(1) being checked. Ground 1 usually comes with a rectification request; ground 4 with an objectionArt. 18(1)(a) to (d)
E. AI tools holding itEvery place the data sits: the AI tool itself, its exports, your CRM, shared drives, the supplier's history or training settings. Copy the list from your Article 30 recordsArt. 30(1); Recital 67
F. How you froze itOne line per place in E: moved to a locked folder, hidden from users, export deleted, automations and AI re-scoring switched off for that person, label "RESTRICTED, do not use" addedRecital 67
G. Allowed uses while restrictedStorage only, unless one of the exceptions applies: the person's consent, legal claims, another person's rights, important public interest. Write which one and why, or "storage only"Art. 18(2)
H. Recipients toldEach supplier, client or partner you disclosed the data to, date told, or why it was impossible or disproportionate. Note if the person asked for the list and when you sent itArt. 19
I. Status=IF(L2<>"","Lifted",IF(TODAY()>B2-7,"Reply due within 7 days","Restricted")). The 7-day warning is your own working rule, not a legal oneArt. 12(3)
J. Check resultGround 1: accurate or corrected (date). Ground 4: whether your legitimate grounds override theirs. Grounds 2 and 3: when the reason endsArt. 18(1)
K. Told before liftingDate you told the person the restriction will be lifted, and the reason. Do this before you lift itArt. 18(3)
L. LiftedDate the restriction was removed in every place in E, and who checkedArt. 5(2)

Sort by column I so "Reply due within 7 days" comes first. In a spreadsheet, put the column letters A to L in row 1 and the first request in row 2; the formulas above assume that layout.

Step 3: freezing data inside AI tools

  • Recital 67 says that in automated systems the restriction should in principle be ensured by technical means, so the data is not processed further and cannot be changed, and the restriction should be clearly marked in the system.
  • In practice for small teams: export the disputed record to a locked folder, remove the person from AI scoring, follow-up automations and mailing lists, and add a visible "restricted" tag in your CRM so nobody acts on the AI output.
  • Check the supplier side too: chat history, saved prompts, and whether the tool may use your data for training. Our privacy by default settings sheet lists where these switches usually are.
