Human oversight log for high-risk AI: a one-page weekly sheet for small EU deployers (2026)

Updated 19 Oct 2026 · Disclosure: the EURO AI Register mentioned below is an Unadhi product with a free edition. This guide is for information only, not legal advice.

What you get: a finished one-page weekly human oversight log you can print or keep in a sheet: who oversaw which high-risk AI tool, what they reviewed, any override or stop, and what to escalate. Fill one row per oversight action (or one summary row per day). File it with the tool's instructions for use. Aimed at Article 26 deployer duties in Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 — especially Article 26(2) (assign competent people) and the monitoring / inform duties in Article 26(5).

Most high-risk deployer obligations apply from 2 December 2027. Building the habit now is cheap; scrambling later is not.

What Article 26 asks of you (plain English)

  • Art. 26(1): use the system according to the provider's instructions for use.
  • Art. 26(2): assign oversight to named people who have the competence, training, authority and support to do it.
  • Art. 26(4): if you control the input data, keep it relevant and representative for the intended purpose.
  • Art. 26(5): monitor operation; if the use may present a risk under Art. 79(1), inform the provider/distributor and the market surveillance authority and suspend use; if you identify a serious incident, inform the provider first, then importer/distributor and authorities (Art. 73 applies if you cannot reach the provider).
  • Art. 26(6): keep automatic logs under your control for at least six months (or longer if the purpose needs it).

This weekly sheet does not replace the automatic logs or the instructions for use. It shows that a real person was on the job.

Print it, keep it in a shared folder, or paste the table into Google Sheets / Excel. One sheet per high-risk tool. Keep filled sheets for at least as long as you keep the related automatic logs (six months minimum under Art. 26(6), longer if your purpose or national rules need it).

How to fill each column without fluff

  1. Date — the day oversight happened, not the day you wrote it up.
  2. What was reviewed — a concrete sample: "all shortlists for role X", "every alert over threshold Y", "random 20 outputs".
  3. Volume or period — enough that someone else can see you did not only check one case.
  4. OK / override / stop — pick one. Override means a human changed or rejected the AI output. Stop means you paused or switched the tool off.
  5. Notes — one line on what looked wrong (wrong category, odd score, missing data, possible bias). Empty notes on an "OK" day are fine.
  6. Escalated to — named role or "provider email on [date]". If you think Art. 79 risk or a serious incident, follow Art. 26(5) timelines, not just this sheet.

Checklist before you rely on the log

  • The tool is in your AI inventory (SME AI inventory template)
  • Annex III result written down with a date (Annex III check)
  • Provider's instructions for use on file; oversight measures from those instructions named (12 vendor questions)
  • Named overseer has training and authority to override or stop (Art. 26(2)); AI-literacy duty under Art. 4 applies since 2 February 2025
  • Automatic logs under your control are being kept (≥ 6 months): record-keeping guide
  • If the tool touches staff or applicants, the notice and workers' representatives steps are done: staff / applicant notice template
