Short answer: under DAC7 (Council Directive (EU) 2021/514, applied from 1 January 2023), platforms such as online marketplaces and rental sites must report sellers who rent out property, provide personal services, sell goods or rent out transport to the tax authority by 31 January of the following year, and give the seller the same figures by the same date. For sales of goods only, a seller is left out if it made fewer than 30 sales and received no more than EUR 2 000 in the year. For property rental, personal services and transport rental there is no such small-seller exemption. The report includes the seller's name, address, tax number, VAT number where available, bank account, and the money received and fees charged per quarter.

Why accountants and bookkeepers should look at this now

  • The 2026 statements arrive by 31 January 2027. The platform must give each reportable seller the reported figures no later than 31 January of the following year (Annex V, Section III, A(5)). Those figures land at the tax authority at the same time, so the client's 2026 return should agree with them.
  • Clients forget small side income. A dental practice owner renting a flat for a few weekends, a bookkeeping client selling crafts online, a freelancer booked through a services app: each can appear in a report the client never thinks about.
  • The figure is net of fees. "Consideration" is the amount paid to the seller net of fees, commissions or taxes withheld by the platform (Annex V, Section I, definition of "Consideration"), and the fees are reported separately. Your books may show gross sales and fees as an expense, so the difference column needs a fee check before you call it a mismatch.
  • Missing data can close the account. If a seller does not give the requested information after two reminders, and not before 60 days have passed, the platform must close the account or withhold payment (Annex V, Section IV, A(2)).

National law

Member States had to apply the DAC7 rules from 1 January 2023 (Article 2(1) of Directive (EU) 2021/514), each through its own law. Penalties, the exact form of the seller statement and how the tax authority uses the data differ by country. The report does not by itself decide whether the income is taxable or whether VAT is due. Check your national rules. This page covers the EU text as we read it today.

Frequently asked questions

When must a platform report 2026 seller income under DAC7?

No later than 31 January 2027, to the tax authority, and by the same date it must give the seller the information that relates to them (Annex V, Section III, A(1) and A(5)).

Is there a minimum below which a seller is not reported?

Only for sales of goods: a seller with fewer than 30 sales and total consideration of no more than EUR 2 000 in the year is an excluded seller. Property rental, personal services and transport rental have no such threshold.

Is the reported amount before or after platform fees?

After. Consideration is defined net of fees, commissions or taxes withheld or charged by the platform, and those fees are reported separately for each quarter.
