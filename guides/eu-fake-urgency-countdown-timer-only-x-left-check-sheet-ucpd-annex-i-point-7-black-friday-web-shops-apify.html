Short answer: a countdown timer or “only X left” message is fine when it is true. UCPD Annex I point 7 bans, in all circumstances, “falsely stating that a product will only be available for a very limited time, or that it will only be available on particular terms for a very limited time, in order to elicit an immediate decision and deprive consumers of sufficient opportunity or time to make an informed choice.” The Commission Guidance says this “includes fake timers and limited stock claims on websites”. False information about availability is also a misleading action under Article 6(1)(b).

Why accountants, bookkeepers and web agencies should look at this before Black Friday

  • Black Friday is 27 November 2026. Timers and “last chance” banners go up in the next two weeks. A timer that resets on reload, or a “sale ends Sunday” banner that is still there on Monday, is the classic fake timer.
  • No case-by-case test. Annex I practices are unfair “in all circumstances” (Article 5(5)). The authority does not need to prove the effect on consumers for a point 7 breach.
  • Proof is on the shop. Under Article 12 an authority or court can ask the trader to prove a factual claim and treat it as inaccurate if the proof is missing. Column L makes sure proof exists.
  • It is a quick, billable catalogue check. Run the product scraper, open the sale pages, answer the columns, and the sheet writes the fix list with Article cites. Five-minute checklist (per sale page)

  1. Is the shop selling to consumers? B2B-only shops are outside the UCPD (Article 3(1)) — column C.
  2. Copy every urgency or scarcity line on the page: timers, “ends tonight”, “last chance”, “only X left”, “X people viewing / bought” (E–F).
  3. Timers and deadlines: will the price or terms really end at the stated time? Reload the page; check again after the end date. If the same deal is still there, or the timer restarts, it is a false time limit (Annex I point 7).
  4. Stock: compare the number shown with real stock on the same day. Showing fewer than you have is false scarcity (Annex I point 7 per the Guidance; Article 6(1)(b)).
  5. Demand: is the “people viewing / bought” number taken from real live data? If it is random or fixed, remove it (Article 6(1); Annex I point 18).
  6. Also check the advertised price is available in reasonable quantities (bait advertising, Annex I point 5) and that the advertised item is not refused or swapped (Annex I point 6).
  7. Keep a dated screenshot plus the promotion end setting or stock export for every claim (Article 12).

Safe banner wording

Black Friday price: EUR [price] until [day date, time, time zone].
From [next day] the price returns to EUR [price].
[Only show a count if it comes from live stock:] [n] in stock now.

Remove: timers that restart on reload; “ends today” banners that run for days; fixed or random “only 2 left” or “people viewing” numbers.

Frequently asked questions

Are countdown timers banned in the EU?

Not as such. A countdown timer is fine when it is true. UCPD Annex I point 7 bans, in all circumstances, falsely stating that a product will only be available for a very limited time, or only on particular terms for a very limited time, in order to elicit an immediate decision and deprive consumers of sufficient opportunity or time to make an informed choice. The Commission Guidance (2021/C 526/01, section 4.2.7) says this includes fake timers and limited stock claims on websites.

Is 'only 3 left' allowed?

Yes, if it is true. If the page shows fewer items left than the shop really has, the claim is false scarcity: the Commission Guidance lists limited stock claims on websites under Annex I point 7, and Article 6(1)(b) UCPD treats false information about a product's availability as a misleading action.

What about '12 people are viewing this' messages?

The UCPD has no point naming them, so the sheet tests them under Article 6(1) (false information that deceives the average consumer) and flags Annex I point 18 (materially inaccurate information on market conditions or on the possibility of finding the product). If the number is not taken from real live data, the sheet grades FIX.

Does a shop need intent for this to be unfair?

Annex I practices are unfair in all circumstances, with no case-by-case test of their effect. Point 7 itself speaks of the aim 'to elicit an immediate decision'. The Commission Guidance says the UCPD does not require intention for the deployment of a dark pattern. Our sheet does not try to prove intent; it checks whether the claim is true.

What fines apply?

Article 13 UCPD leaves penalties to Member States. For coordinated cross-border actions under Article 21 of Regulation (EU) 2017/2394, the maximum fine must be at least 4% of the trader's annual turnover in the Member States concerned, or at least EUR 2 million where turnover information is not available. Check national law for the exact amounts.

Why keep dated proof?

Article 12 UCPD lets courts and authorities require the trader to furnish evidence as to the accuracy of factual claims and to consider the claims inaccurate if the evidence is not furnished or is insufficient. A dated screenshot plus the promotion end setting or a stock export is the simplest proof.
