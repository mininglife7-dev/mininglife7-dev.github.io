Customer data leaked through an AI tool: a GDPR 72-hour personal data breach sheet for small EU businesses (Articles 33 and 34)

Updated 21 Oct 2026 · Disclosure: the EURO AI Register mentioned below is an Unadhi product with a free edition. This guide is for information only, not legal advice.

What you get: a finished breach sheet to fill once per incident: a clock line for the 72 hours, a "is it a breach?" table with six common AI tool cases, a risk table for the notify / don't notify call, the four items the authority must be told, a note to the people affected when the risk is high, and a breach log row you keep for every case.

The short answer

If personal data in an AI tool is disclosed, accessed, changed, lost or destroyed by accident or without authorisation, that is a personal data breach (Article 4(12) GDPR). You must notify your data protection authority without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours of becoming aware, unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to people (Article 33(1)). If the risk to people is high, you must also tell them without undue delay (Article 34(1)). Whatever you decide, record the breach (Article 33(5)).

The three types come from the EDPB guidelines: confidentiality (unauthorised or accidental disclosure or access), integrity (unauthorised or accidental alteration) and availability (accidental or unauthorised loss of access or destruction). A case can be more than one type. Staff pasting customer data into an AI tool your business has not approved can also raise questions; record what was shared, with which service, and assess it the same way. If you are unsure whether it is a breach, write down why you decided either way.

The EDPB advises that if in doubt, the controller should err on the side of caution and notify. Its separate Guidelines 01/2021 give worked examples, including ransomware and data sent to the wrong person.

If you do not have everything yet, you may give the information in phases without undue further delay (Article 33(4)). Use your authority's own breach form; most publish one online. Notify the authority competent under Article 55 (with cross-border processing, normally your lead authority under Article 56).

Step 5: tell the people affected when the risk is high (Article 34)

[Name], on [date] we found that [what happened, in plain words] in [the AI tool]. It involved your [data types]. This may mean [likely consequences]. We have [what you did] and we suggest you [for example: watch for unexpected emails, change a password]. Questions: [contact point]. [Your name, business]

The message must be in clear and plain language and give at least the contact point, likely consequences and measures (Article 34(2)). You do not need to send it if the data was protected so it is unintelligible to anyone not authorised (for example encryption), if later measures mean the high risk is no longer likely, or if it would involve disproportionate effort, in which case a public communication or similar measure is needed instead (Article 34(3)).

Article 33(5) requires you to document any personal data breach, including the facts, its effects and the remedial action, so the authority can check compliance. That includes breaches you decided not to report.

Do this week

  • For each AI tool, note the supplier's security contact and how it will tell you about incidents
  • Check each AI supplier contract includes the processor's duty to help you with breaches (Article 28(3)(f))
  • Find your data protection authority's breach form and save the link with this sheet
  • Agree who in the business can make the risk call at a weekend

Law changes under discussion: the European Commission's Digital Omnibus proposal (COM(2025) 837) would change Article 33 so that only breaches likely to result in a high risk are notified, within 96 hours. As of 21 October 2026 it has not been adopted; the 72-hour rule above is the law in force. Check the current text before relying on any change.
