Short answer: under Article 16(1) of the EU Textile Labelling Regulation (EU) No 1007/2011, in force since 8 May 2012, the fibre composition "shall be clearly visible to the consumer before the purchase, including in cases where the purchase is made by electronic means". So a web shop selling clothing, bed linen, towels, curtains or other textile products must show it on the product page. A sewn-in label is not enough. The composition must use only the fibre names in Annex I (Article 5), list every fibre with its percentage by weight in descending order (Article 9(1)), use "100%", "pure" or "all" only for products made of one fibre (Article 7), and say "Contains non-textile parts of animal origin" where there are, for example, leather or fur trims (Article 12).

Why accountants, bookkeepers and web agencies should look at this now

  • Small fashion and home shops often miss it. Shop themes show title, price and photos. The composition is often only on the physical label or in a photo of it, or it is missing for some variants.
  • Marketing words get in the way. "Bamboo", "spandex" and "microfibre" are not fibre names in Annex I. Trade marks such as Lycra or Tencel may appear only immediately before or after the composition (Article 16(2)), not instead of it.
  • The shop is responsible too. A distributor must make sure the products it sells carry the required labelling (Article 15(3)). A shop that sells under its own brand, attaches the label itself or changes the label counts as the manufacturer (Article 15(2)).
  • It is a quick, billable check. About 10 to 15 minutes per shop for ten product pages, with one fix list you can send to the client or their web designer.

Step 3: tab 2, scope list (products that need no fibre label)

Annex V lists 42 kinds of textile product for which labelling or marking is not mandatory (Article 17(2)). Ones often sold in small web shops include:

  • toys (item 22) · textile parts of footwear (23) · animal clothing (41) · watch straps (2) · travel goods (17) · fancy goods and saddlery (16) · make-up cases (27) · oven gloves and cloths (25) · tea and coffee cosies (5, 6) · phone covers up to 160 cm² (30) · flags and banners (42) · disposable products, except wadding (35) · old made-up textile products explicitly stated to be such (13)

Two exceptions: if a trade mark or company name on these products contains a fibre name from Annex I (or one that could be confused with it), Articles 11, 14, 15 and 16 apply anyway (Article 17(2)). Annex VI lists products where one inclusive label is enough, for example cleaning cloths, ribbons, belts, handkerchiefs, laces and sewing yarns sold to consumers (Article 17(3)). The composition must still reach each buyer, including the consumer (Article 17(5)).

Also treated as textile products (Article 2(2)): products with at least 80% textile fibres by weight; furniture, umbrella and sunshade coverings with at least 80% textile components; and the textile part of mattress coverings, camping goods coverings and the upper layer of multi-layer floor coverings, where it is at least 80% of that layer or covering.

What the common marketing words map to is a question for the supplier's specification, not the sheet. For example, a fibre sold as "bamboo" must be described by the Annex I name whose fibre description it meets (Annex I defines viscose, for instance, as "regenerated cellulose fibre obtained by the viscose process"). What the law says, in short

  • Article 16(1): the fibre composition descriptions must be shown in catalogues and trade literature, on packaging, labels and markings, "easily legible, visible, clear and in print which is uniform as regards its size, style and font", and "clearly visible to the consumer before the purchase, including in cases where the purchase is made by electronic means".
  • Article 16(2): trade marks or the company name may be given immediately before or after the composition. "Other information shall be always displayed separately."
  • Article 16(3): labelling or marking in the official language or languages of the Member State where the products are made available to the consumer, unless that Member State provides otherwise.
  • Article 9: name and percentage by weight of all fibres in descending order. A fibre of up to 5%, or fibres totalling up to 15%, may be shown as "other fibres" with their total percentage where they cannot easily be stated at the time of manufacture (9(2)).
  • Article 7: "100%", "pure" or "all" only for products made exclusively of the same fibre. Up to 2% extraneous fibres (5% after carding) is allowed if technically unavoidable and not added as a matter of routine.
  • Article 11: products with components of different fibre content must state the content of each component. Not compulsory for components that are not main linings and are under 30% of the total weight.
  • Article 12: the phrase "Contains non-textile parts of animal origin" whenever such products are made available on the market, not misleading and easy to understand.
  • Article 20(3): a manufacturing tolerance of 3% between the stated composition and the result of laboratory analysis.
  • Enforcement: market surveillance authorities check that the fibre composition matches the information supplied (Article 18). Penalties are set in national law.
  • Review: the Commission's review page (read 29 Oct 2026) still names the second quarter of 2026 for a planned proposal to revise the Regulation. The EUR-Lex page for the Regulation lists no amending act after Regulation (EU) 2018/122. Until a new act is adopted, the rules above apply.

Copy-ready composition line (our suggestion)

Put it next to the price or directly under the product title, in a visible text block (not only in a photo), once for each variant whose composition differs:

"Material: 92% cotton, 8% elastane." · "Material: Shell: 100% polyamide. Lining: 100% polyester. Contains non-textile parts of animal origin." · "Material: 95% lyocell (Tencel™), 5% elastane." Use the official language of each country the shop sells to, and take the figures from the supplier's specification or the physical label, never from a guess.

Frequently asked questions

Does a web shop have to show the fibre composition on the product page?

Yes. Article 16(1) of Regulation (EU) No 1007/2011 says the fibre composition must be clearly visible to the consumer before the purchase, including when the purchase is made by electronic means. A sewn-in label that the buyer sees only after delivery is not enough.

Can a shop describe a product as "bamboo" or "spandex"?

Not as the fibre composition. Only the names in Annex I may be used (Article 5), and neither word is on that list (elastane is). Trade marks such as Lycra may be shown only immediately before or after the composition (Article 16(2)). Ask the supplier for the correct Annex I name.

Do toys, shoes and pet clothing need a fibre label?

No. Toys, textile parts of footwear and animal clothing are in Annex V, the list of products for which fibre labelling is not mandatory (Article 17(2)). This changes if a trade mark or company name on the product contains a fibre name.
