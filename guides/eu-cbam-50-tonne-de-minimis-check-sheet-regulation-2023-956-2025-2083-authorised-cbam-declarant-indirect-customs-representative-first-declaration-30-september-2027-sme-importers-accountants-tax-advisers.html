What you get: a finished, tested CBAM 50-tonne check sheet for accountants, tax advisers and bookkeepers whose SME clients import steel, aluminium, fertilisers or cement from outside the EU: metal traders, builders, machine shops, workshops, garden and farm suppliers, web shops selling screws, brackets or aluminium profiles. One row per customs declaration. Formulas read the CN code against Annex I, drop out-of-scope origins, add up each importer's tonnes per calendar year, compare them with the 50-tonne single mass-based threshold, check the authorised CBAM declarant status (including for an indirect customs representative) and show the 30 September declaration date with the days left. Each row ends in one result: EXEMPT UNDER 50 T, WATCH: NEAR 50 T, APPLY NOW, DECLARE BY [date], PROVISIONAL, PENALTY RISK, OVERDUE, DONE, FIX, FIX REPRESENTATIVE, CHECK BY HAND, NOT CBAM GOODS or OUT OF SCOPE.

Short answer: since 1 January 2026, CBAM goods may be imported "only by an authorised CBAM declarant" (Article 4, applying from 1 January 2026 under Article 36(2)(b)). The exception: an importer whose net mass of CBAM goods in a calendar year "does not cumulatively exceed" 50 tonnes, all CN codes added together, is exempt for that year and declares the exemption in the customs declaration (Article 2a(1), Annex VII point 1). Go above 50 tonnes and every obligation applies to all goods imported that year (Article 2a(2)). The first CBAM declaration and surrender of certificates, for 2026 imports, is due by 30 September 2027 (Articles 6(1) and 22(1)): 324 days from 10 November 2026.

What to tell a client in one minute

  • It is a yearly total, not a per-shipment rule. The 50 tonnes "shall apply to the total net mass of goods under all CN codes aggregated per importer and per calendar year" (Article 2a(1)). Ten 6-tonne pallets of steel brackets are 60 tonnes.
  • Crossing 50 tonnes pulls in the whole year. Above the threshold the importer "shall be subject to all obligations ... in respect of all emissions embedded in all goods imported in that calendar year" (Article 2a(2)).
  • Apply before you cross it. An importer that expects to exceed the threshold submits the application via the CBAM registry (Article 5(1b), 5(3)); recital 10 of Regulation (EU) 2025/2083 says the status should be obtained "before the single mass-based threshold is exceeded". Importers that applied by 31 March 2026 may provisionally keep importing until the authority decides (Article 17(7a)).
  • Brokers need the status too. An indirect customs representative must be an authorised CBAM declarant before importing CBAM goods, "irrespective of whether the importer is exempted" (Article 5(1a)).
  • The authorities see the numbers. Customs pass the CN code, quantity and origin of each import to the Commission (Article 25(2)), which shares a list of importers above 90% of the threshold with the national authorities (Article 25a(1)). Splitting imports to stay under 50 tonnes is circumvention (Article 27(2)(b)).
  • Penalties are high. Importing above the threshold without the status: three to five times the Article 26(1) penalty per certificate not surrendered, on all emissions of the year (Article 26(2) and (2a)). The authority may reduce it where the threshold was exceeded by no more than 10% (Article 26(2a)).

Client checklist

  • List every 2026 import under the Annex I CN codes, with net mass and origin, per importer.
  • Add up the net mass per calendar year. Above 50 tonnes? All of that year's CBAM imports are covered.
  • Near 50 tonnes with more orders planned? Apply via the CBAM registry before the threshold is crossed.
  • Below 50 tonnes? Make sure the broker declares the exemption in each customs declaration.
  • Using an indirect customs representative? Confirm in writing that it is an authorised CBAM declarant.
  • Covered for 2026? Ask suppliers for embedded emissions data now, or plan for default values, and diarise 1 February 2027 (certificates on sale) and 30 September 2027 (declaration and surrender).
  • Keep the records until the end of the fourth year after the declaration year.

Limits and our reading

  • Dates. Articles 2a, 4, 6 to 9, 21, 22(1) and 23 to 27 apply from 1 January 2026; Article 22(2) from 1 January 2027; Article 20(1) from 1 February 2027 (Article 36(2)).
  • Not read, flagged: Commission implementing and delegated acts (application format, default values, declaration format, certificate sale rules), any delegated act changing the 50 tonnes after 2026, Commission CBAM guidance and the CBAM registry manuals, national penalty and procedure rules, Directive 2003/87/EC Article 16 (the size of the Article 26(1) penalty), and the Commission's 2025 proposal to extend CBAM to downstream goods (not adopted when we checked). Check the CN code with the customs declaration or the broker; the Combined Nomenclature itself was not read by us.
  • Our reading, flagged: treating returned goods, inward and outward processing and ex 2507 00 80 as CHECK BY HAND; counting the importer's tonnes by import date within the calendar year; showing NEAR from 90% (the Article 25a(1) monitoring list), which is our warning level, not a legal limit; asking for the status for indirect representatives on every row they declare.

FAQ

Who is exempt under the CBAM 50-tonne rule?

An importer whose imports of CBAM goods (iron and steel, aluminium, fertilisers, cement) do not cumulatively exceed 50 tonnes of net mass in a calendar year, all CN codes added together, is exempt from the obligations of Regulation (EU) 2023/956 for that year and declares the exemption in the customs declaration (Article 2a(1), Annex VII point 1).

What happens if a client goes above 50 tonnes during the year?

All obligations then apply to all emissions embedded in all goods imported in that calendar year, not only the tonnes above 50 (Article 2a(2)). Imports may only be made by an authorised CBAM declarant (Article 4); an importer above the threshold without that status faces a penalty of three to five times the Article 26(1) penalty per certificate not surrendered (Article 26(2) and (2a)).

Does the 50 tonnes count per shipment or per year?

Per importer and per calendar year, adding up the net mass under all CN codes (Article 2a(1)). Splitting imports artificially to stay below the threshold is treated as circumvention (Article 27(2)(b)) and non-genuine arrangements are disregarded (Article 25a(4)).

Does the exemption apply to hydrogen and electricity?

No. Article 2a does not apply to imports of electricity or hydrogen (Article 2a(4)), so those imports need an authorised CBAM declarant whatever the quantity.

When is the first CBAM declaration due?

By 30 September 2027 for goods imported in 2026, and by 30 September every year after that; CBAM certificates for the same emissions are surrendered by the same date (Articles 6(1) and 22(1)). Certificates go on sale from 1 February 2027 (Article 20(1)).

Our client uses a customs broker. Who needs the status?

An indirect customs representative must hold the status of authorised CBAM declarant before importing CBAM goods, even if the importer is exempt (Article 5(1a)). Where it acts as authorised CBAM declarant, it carries the importer's obligations for those goods (Article 5(2a)). With a direct representative, on our reading of Article 3(15) the client itself is the importer.
