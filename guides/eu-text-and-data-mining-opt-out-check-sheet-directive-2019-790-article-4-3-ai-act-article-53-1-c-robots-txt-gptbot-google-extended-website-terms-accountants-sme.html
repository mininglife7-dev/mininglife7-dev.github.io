Short answer: In the EU, anyone may copy lawfully accessible content for text and data mining unless the rightholder has expressly reserved that use "in an appropriate manner, such as machine-readable means in the case of content made publicly available online" (Directive 2019/790, Article 4(3)). For online content, recital 18 says the reservation should be made "by the use of machine-readable means, including metadata and terms and conditions of a website or a service". Since 2 August 2025, providers of general-purpose AI models placed on the EU market must have a policy to "identify and comply with, including through state-of-the-art technologies" such reservations (AI Act Article 53(1)(c); Article 113(b)). Models placed on the market before that date must comply by 2 August 2027, 266 days from 9 November 2026 (Article 111(3)).

Why accountants, tax advisers and web agencies should look at this now

  • Clients ask "can we stop AI firms using our website?" The EU answer is a reservation under Article 4(3). Without one, the general text and data mining exception applies to lawfully accessible content.
  • The reservation has to be readable by machines for online content (recital 18). A sentence buried in a PDF brochure is weak evidence. A robots.txt group plus a clear sentence in the website terms is a simple, dated way to show it.
  • AI model providers must now look for it. Article 53(1)(c) makes respecting Article 4(3) reservations part of their legal duties, and recital 106 says this applies "regardless of the jurisdiction in which the copyright-relevant acts underpinning the training" take place.
  • Getting it wrong costs visibility. Blocking the wrong bot can take a client out of AI search answers. The operators document their training crawlers and their search bots separately, so the sheet checks both.
  • It is a short, billable job for any firm that already looks after client websites or content.

Step 1: decide, client by client

  1. Ask each client: do you want your website texts, photos and other content kept out of AI model training? Record Y, N or Undecided in column C. This is the client's decision as rightholder, not yours.
  2. Ask whether any content is licensed to an AI company by contract (column N). Recital 18 says that in other cases rights can also be reserved "by other means, such as contractual agreements or a unilateral declaration".
  3. Ask whether the client wants to appear in AI search answers (column L).

Step 2: check /robots.txt

  1. Open https://client-site.example/robots.txt. The rules must be in a file named "/robots.txt" at the top level of the site (RFC 9309, Section 2.3). If the address returns a 4xx error, the file counts as unavailable and "the crawler MAY access any resources on the server" (Section 2.3.1.3). Enter Y in column D only if the file loads.
  2. For each token below, tick Y if there is a group with that User-agent line and Disallow: / (or User-agent: * with Disallow: /, which blocks every crawler, search engines included). Crawlers must match the user-agent name case-insensitively (Section 2.2.1).
  3. Log the date in column E. Crawlers may cache robots.txt but "SHOULD NOT use the cached version for more than 24 hours" (Section 2.4). The sheet asks you to re-check every 90 days (Settings!B4, our choice), because sites get rebuilt and robots.txt files get overwritten.

Text and data mining: [Company] expressly reserves the rights to make
reproductions and extractions of the content of this website for text
and data mining, including for training AI models, under Article 4(3)
of Directive (EU) 2019/790. This does not affect text and data mining
for scientific research under Article 3 of that Directive.

The research carve-out is in the law: a reservation does not affect Article 3 text and data mining by research organisations and cultural heritage institutions (Article 4(4)), and contract terms contrary to Article 3 are unenforceable (Article 7(1)).

Client checklist

  • Decide: keep website content out of AI training, yes or no.
  • Check /robots.txt loads at the top level of the site.
  • Add a Disallow: / group for GPTBot, Google-Extended, ClaudeBot, Applebot-Extended and CCBot.
  • Leave Googlebot and other search engine groups as they are; Google-Extended does not affect Google Search.
  • Decide separately about OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot and PerplexityBot (AI search answers).
  • Add the reservation sentence to the website terms.
  • Keep dated copies of robots.txt and the terms.
  • Re-check after every website rebuild and at least every 90 days.

Limits and our reading

  • National law decides the details. Article 4 is in a directive; each Member State's copyright law applies it. We did not read any national law or court ruling.
  • Our reading, flagged: treating a robots.txt group per AI crawler as a "machine-readable means" under Article 4(3) and recital 18 (the Directive does not name robots.txt); treating the website terms as a second, human-readable layer; the 90-day re-check interval; and the choice of the five training tokens, which come from the operators' own pages, not from EU law. Other AI developers use other crawler names; add columns if you need them.
  • A reservation looks forward. Anthropic's page speaks of "future materials". Nothing in robots.txt removes content already collected.
  • Not covered: the scientific research exception in detail (Article 3), databases and software rights, the training-content summary under Article 53(1)(d), and the AI Office template and code of practice for model providers.

FAQ

Can a business stop AI firms from training on its website under EU law?

Directive (EU) 2019/790, Article 4(3) lets the rightholder expressly reserve text and data mining 'in an appropriate manner, such as machine-readable means in the case of content made publicly available online'. Without a reservation, the general text and data mining exception applies to lawfully accessible content. A reservation does not affect text and data mining for scientific research under Article 3 (Article 4(4)).

Is robots.txt a valid reservation?

The Directive does not name robots.txt. Recital 18 says that for online content the reservation should be made 'by the use of machine-readable means, including metadata and terms and conditions of a website or a service'. Our reading is that a robots.txt group for each AI crawler is a machine-readable means; we pair it with a sentence in the website terms and flag this as our reading.

What does the AI Act add?

Article 53(1)(c) requires providers of general-purpose AI models to put in place a policy to comply with Union copyright law and to 'identify and comply with, including through state-of-the-art technologies, a reservation of rights expressed pursuant to Article 4(3) of Directive (EU) 2019/790'. Chapter V applies from 2 August 2025 (Article 113(b)); models placed on the market before that date must comply by 2 August 2027 (Article 111(3)).

Which crawler names does the sheet check?

Five tokens that the operators themselves describe as controlling AI training use: GPTBot (OpenAI), Google-Extended (Google), ClaudeBot (Anthropic), Applebot-Extended (Apple) and CCBot (Common Crawl). AI search bots (OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot, PerplexityBot) are checked in a separate column because their operators document them separately from training.

Will blocking Google-Extended remove the site from Google Search?

Google's crawler page says Google-Extended 'does not impact a site's inclusion in Google Search nor is it used as a ranking signal in Google Search'. Do not block Googlebot itself unless the client wants to leave Google Search.

Does robots.txt physically stop crawlers?

No. RFC 9309 says 'These rules are not a form of access authorization.' It is a signal that compliant crawlers follow and a dated record of the reservation. Keep dated copies of robots.txt and the website terms.
