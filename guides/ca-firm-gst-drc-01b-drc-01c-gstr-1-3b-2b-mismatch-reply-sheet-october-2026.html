DRC-01B and DRC-01C sheet for CA firms: catch GSTR-1 vs GSTR-3B and GSTR-2B vs GSTR-3B gaps for October 2026 before GSTR-1 is blocked

Updated 24 Oct 2026 · Disclosure: the white-label calendar linked below is an Unadhi product (₹999/year). This guide is for information only, not professional advice.

What you get: a ready-to-copy Google Sheet layout that catches GST return mismatches before they turn into a blocked GSTR-1. Tab 1 has one row per client per tax period and compares tax in GSTR-1 (as amended in GSTR-1A, or IFF) with tax in GSTR-3B. Tab 2 compares ITC available in GSTR-2B with ITC claimed in GSTR-3B. Tab 3 tracks every DRC-01B and DRC-01C intimation with a 7-day reply-by date, days left and a "GSTR-1 blocked?" flag. You also get a made-up example, a plan from GSTR-3B for October (20 November) to GSTR-1 for November (11 December) and a WhatsApp message for clients.

Short answer (checked 24 Oct 2026): if tax in GSTR-1/IFF is higher than tax in GSTR-3B beyond a limit set on the portal, the client gets Form GST DRC-01B (rule 88C). If ITC claimed in GSTR-3B is higher than ITC in GSTR-2B beyond a limit, the client gets Form GST DRC-01C (rule 88D). Either way the client must pay through DRC-03 with interest, or explain, in Part B within seven days. Until Part B is filed, GSTR-1/IFF for the next period cannot be filed (rule 59(6)(d) and (e)). The rules leave the amount and percentage to the GST Council, and the portal calls them "pre-defined" and "configurable"; we did not find the figures in an official source, so the sheet uses your own alert levels.

What the rules say

  • Output tax gap, rule 88C(1). Where tax payable as per GSTR-1, "as amended in FORM GSTR-1A if any", or IFF for a tax period exceeds tax payable as per GSTR-3B for that period "by such amount and such percentage, as may be recommended by the Council", the person is told of the difference in Part A of DRC-01B on the portal and by email, and directed to pay the difference with section 50 interest through DRC-03, or explain it, within seven days. Rule 88C was inserted by Notification 26/2022-CT; the GSTR-1A words came in through Notification 12/2024-CT.
  • Reply, rule 88C(2). Pay fully or partly through DRC-03 and give the details in Part B of DRC-01B, or give reasons in Part B for any part left unpaid.
  • If nothing happens, rule 88C(3). If the amount stays unpaid and there is no explanation, or the explanation is not accepted by the proper officer, it is recoverable under section 79.
  • ITC gap, rule 88D(1)-(2). Where ITC availed in GSTR-3B for a tax period or periods exceeds ITC available in GSTR-2B by such amount and percentage as the Council recommends, the person gets Part A of DRC-01C and must pay the excess with section 50 interest through DRC-03, or explain, in Part B within seven days. Inserted by Notification 38/2023-CT.
  • If nothing happens, rule 88D(3). The amount can be demanded under section 73 or 74, or section 74A (words inserted with effect from 1 November 2024 by Notification 20/2024-CT).
  • The block, rule 59(6)(d) and (e). A person who received a rule 88C or 88D intimation "shall not be allowed to furnish" GSTR-1 or IFF for a subsequent tax period unless they have paid the amount or filed a reply explaining what remains unpaid.
  • Portal FAQs. The intimation comes by email and SMS with a Reference Number, and is also under Services > Returns > Return Compliance > Liability Mismatch (DRC-01B) or ITC Mismatch (DRC-01C). For QRMP filers it is generated after the quarterly GSTR-3B; for monthly filers after each monthly GSTR-3B. After Part B is filed, GSTR-1/IFF can be filed straight away (log out and in again if it does not unlock).
  • Part B details from the portal manuals. Part B has two sub-parts: the ARN of a DRC-03 payment, and a reason with an explanation of up to 500 characters (four listed reasons plus "other"). You can use either or both; an ARN is not compulsory. The DRC-03 must be for the same GSTIN, filed on or after the date of Part A, for the same period (for quarterly filers, at least one month of the quarter), with cause of payment "Liability mismatch - GSTR-1 to GSTR-3B" (DRC-01B) or "ITC mismatch - GSTR-2A/2B to GSTR-3B" (DRC-01C). Filing is with EVC or DSC.
  • Who gets them. The FAQs list regular taxpayers (including SEZ units and developers) and casual taxpayers, plus taxpayers who opted in or out of composition (DRC-01B) or opted out of composition (DRC-01C).
  • The limits. Neither rule states a rupee amount or percentage. We did not find an official notification or portal page giving the current figures, so check before you quote any number to a client. Figures circulating online are not used here.

Made-up example (not a real client)

Example only: "Sharada Traders" (made-up name), a monthly filer. October 2026 GSTR-1 shows tax of ₹4,20,000. Its GSTR-3B for October shows ₹3,60,000, because two invoices worth ₹60,000 of tax were shown in GSTR-1 but missed in 3B. Tab 1: gap ₹60,000, gap 14.3%, so "Explain or pay before filing" with your example alert levels of ₹50,000 / 5%. Tab 2: GSTR-2B ITC ₹2,10,000, 3B ITC ₹2,40,000 because ₹30,000 of IGST on an import of goods is not in 2B; excess ₹30,000, 14.3%, so "Explain or reverse before filing", with the bill of entry linked as working paper.
