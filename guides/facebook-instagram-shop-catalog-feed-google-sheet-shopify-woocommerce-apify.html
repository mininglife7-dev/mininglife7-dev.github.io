Facebook and Instagram shop catalogue feed from your own Shopify or WooCommerce store: one Google Sheet Meta Commerce Manager reads on a schedule, with a check for rows Meta will drop

Updated 22 Oct 2026 · Disclosure: the product data scraper linked below is an Unadhi product on the Apify Store. This guide is free, and the sheet also works with the free product export from your own Shopify or WooCommerce admin. Current Apify pricing is only on the Store page. This page has no affiliate links and nobody paid to be mentioned. Unadhi is not connected to Meta.

What you get: one Google Sheet that Meta Commerce Manager can read as a scheduled catalogue feed for your Facebook and Instagram shop and catalogue ads. The feed tab has one row per product from your own web shop with the exact column names Meta asks for: id, title, description, availability, condition, price, sale_price, link, image_link, brand, gtin and google_product_category. A separate checks tab flags, in plain English, the rows Meta is likely to drop or show badly: duplicate id (Meta ignores every copy), no image or an image that is not JPEG or PNG, no description, description same as title, title over 65 characters, price missing, and no category. Re-import your products before Black Friday and the feed updates on Meta's next scheduled fetch. Formulas are below. The example rows are made up.

Who this is for, and when you need it

Small brands selling on their own Shopify or WooCommerce shop who want their products in a Meta catalogue for an Instagram or Facebook shop or for catalogue ads. Shopify's Facebook & Instagram sales channel and the Facebook for WooCommerce plugin can sync products for you, and if that sync works for you, use it. This sheet is for when it doesn't: the plugin keeps failing, you want to choose exactly which products go in, you want to fix titles and categories for Meta without changing your shop, or you sell from a shop the plugins don't cover. Only use it for your own products: Meta requires each link to be a product page on your business's own website.

Step 1: get your products into a tab named scrape

With the product data scraper

Open the E-commerce Product Data Scraper (Shopify, WooCommerce, any shop) on the Apify Store and use this input with your own shop address:

{
  "startUrls": [{ "url": "https://your-shop.example" }],
  "maxProductsPerShop": 2000,
  "includeDescription": true
}
  • maxProductsPerShop: set it above your number of products (the default is 100). You pay only for products delivered.
  • includeDescription: keep it on. Meta requires a description, and the scraper gives it as plain text, which is what Meta's description field wants.
  • The scraper follows robots.txt and reads your shop's terms page. If your own terms forbid automated access (Shopify's default terms do), it skips the shop and charges nothing. Check the run's Storage → Key-value store → SHOPS_SUMMARY: your shop should show ok. If it shows blocked-by-terms, use your admin export below.

In the run, export the dataset as CSV. In your Google Sheet use File → Import → Upload, "Insert new sheet", and name the tab scrape. Do not rename headers. You get one row per product with columns such as url, productId, sku, gtin, title, brand, description, price, compareAtPrice, currency, inStock, category and images/0. For Shopify shops, brand is the product's vendor, category is the product type, and price, compareAtPrice, sku and gtin (the barcode) come from the cheapest variant. For WooCommerce shops, compareAtPrice is the regular price, category is the product's categories joined by commas, and brand is usually empty, so the sheet uses the brand you type in settings.

Free, from your own shop admin

  • Shopify: Products → Export gives one row per variant with columns such as Handle, Title, Body (HTML), Vendor, Type, Variant SKU, Variant Price, Variant Compare At Price, Variant Barcode and Image Src. Build the link yourself as your shop address + /products/ + Handle. Body (HTML) contains HTML; Meta wants plain text in description.
  • WooCommerce: the built-in product CSV exporter (Products → Export) gives name, SKU, description, categories, regular price, sale price, stock and image columns.

The formulas below look columns up by header name, so with an admin export you change only the header names in quotes.

Step 3: build the feed tab

Add a tab named feed and drag it to be the first tab, so it is what opens when Meta fetches the link. Row 1 holds the header names exactly as shown (lower case, US English); row 2 holds the formulas, which you copy down one row per row of scrape. Every formula uses the same lookup, shown in full for title:

=IFERROR(INDEX(scrape!$A2:$ZZ2, MATCH("title", scrape!$A$1:$ZZ$1, 0))&"", "")

Below, S("name") is short for that formula with "name" in place of "title"; type the full formula in the sheet.

ColHeaderFormula (row 2)
Aid=IF(S("title")="", "", IF(S("sku")<>"", S("sku"), S("productId")))
Btitle=LEFT(S("title"), 200)
Cdescription=LEFT(S("description"), 9999)
Davailability=IF(A2="", "", IF(UPPER(S("inStock"))="TRUE", "in stock", "out of stock"))
Econdition=IF(A2="", "", settings!$B$3)
Fprice=IF(A2="", "", LET(p, N(VALUE("0"&S("price"))), c, N(VALUE("0"&S("compareAtPrice"))), cur, IF(S("currency")="", settings!$B$2, S("currency")), IF(MAX(p, c)=0, "", TEXT(MAX(p, c), "0.00")&" "&cur)))
Gsale_price=IF(A2="", "", LET(p, N(VALUE("0"&S("price"))), c, N(VALUE("0"&S("compareAtPrice"))), cur, IF(S("currency")="", settings!$B$2, S("currency")), IF(AND(p>0, c>p), TEXT(p, "0.00")&" "&cur, "")))
Hlink=S("url")
Iimage_link=S("images/0")
Jbrand=IF(A2="", "", IF(S("brand")="", settings!$B$1, LEFT(S("brand"), 100)))
Kgtin=REGEXREPLACE(S("gtin"), "[^0-9]", "")
Lgoogle_product_category=IFERROR(VLOOKUP(S("category"), categories!$A:$B, 2, FALSE), "")

Notes on the formulas:

  • id: Meta asks for a unique content ID and suggests the SKU. Products without a SKU fall back to the shop's product ID. If you run a Meta Pixel with catalogue ads, the id must match the content ID your pixel sends, so check which one your shop's pixel uses before you switch feeds.
  • price and sale_price: Meta wants a number with a full stop as the decimal point, a space, then the ISO currency code, with no currency symbol, for example 24.00 GBP. price takes the larger of price and compare-at price; sale_price is filled only when the product is really on sale, because Meta requires the sale price to be lower than the full price. If your sale ends, re-import the scrape tab so the sale price disappears.
  • Products with variants: the scraper gives one row per product, priced at its cheapest variant. If your sizes or colours have different prices, or your shop sells clothing or shoes (Meta asks for size, color and item_group_id there), build the feed from your admin export instead, one row per variant, with the parent product's handle in an extra item_group_id column.
  • gtin: Meta asks you to send a GTIN only if you are sure it is correct, with no dashes or spaces. Leave the column empty if your barcodes are your own internal codes.
  • Keep the feed tab clean: only the Meta columns go on it. Notes, checks and helper columns go on other tabs.

Step 4: the checks tab

Add a tab named checks. In A2 put =feed!A2; in B2 the problem list; in C2 the verdict. Copy both down as far as the feed goes.

B2: =IF(A2="", "", TEXTJOIN(" · ", TRUE,
  IF(COUNTIF(feed!$A:$A, A2)>1, "Duplicate id", ""),
  IF(feed!I2="", "No image", IF(REGEXMATCH(LOWER(feed!I2), "\.(jpe?g|png)(\?|$)"), "", "Image not JPEG or PNG")),
  IF(feed!C2="", "No description", IF(LOWER(TRIM(feed!C2))=LOWER(TRIM(feed!B2)), "Description same as title", "")),
  IF(EXACT(feed!B2, UPPER(feed!B2))*REGEXMATCH(feed!B2, "[A-Z]"), "Title in capital letters", ""),
  IF(LEN(feed!B2)>65, "Title over 65 characters", ""),
  IF(feed!F2="", "Price missing", ""),
  IF(feed!L2="", "No category", "")))
C2: =IF(A2="", "", IF(B2="", "Yes", IF(B2="Title over 65 characters", "OK (title may be cut)", "Fix")))
  • Duplicate id: Meta says that if the same ID appears more than once, it ignores every instance, so both products vanish. Give one of them its own SKU in your shop.
  • Image not JPEG or PNG: Meta's image rules ask for JPEG or PNG, at least 500 × 500 pixels. Many shop themes serve WebP; upload a JPEG or PNG as the product's first image. The check reads only the file ending, so also open one image per product type to confirm the size.
  • Title over 65 characters: allowed (the limit is 200), but Meta recommends 65 or fewer so the title is not cut off.
  • No category: add the shop category to the categories tab.

Fix problems in your shop (or in the scrape tab, if you don't want to change your shop), then re-import. Add a filter on the checks tab and work through every "Fix" row before the first upload.

Step 5: connect the sheet to Commerce Manager

  • In Google Sheets, Share → General access → Anyone with the link (Viewer). Meta's help says the link must not be set to Restricted. Anyone with the link can see your product data, so keep cost prices and private notes out of this spreadsheet.
  • In Commerce Manager, open your catalogue → Catalogue → Data sources → Add items → Data feed, choose Google Sheets and paste the sheet's link.
  • Choose a schedule (hourly, daily or weekly). The default replace setting deletes from the catalogue any product you removed from the sheet; update only adds and changes products.
  • After the first fetch, open the data source's issues list and compare it with your checks tab. Meta's own diagnostics are the final word.
  • Before each sale or season, re-import the scrape tab. The feed and checks recalculate and Meta picks up the change on the next scheduled fetch.
