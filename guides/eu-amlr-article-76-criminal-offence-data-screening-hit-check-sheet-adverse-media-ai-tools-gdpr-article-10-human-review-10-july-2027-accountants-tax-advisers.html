Short answer: news of a fraud probe, a court record or a screening-tool alert about a client is personal data relating to criminal convictions and offences under GDPR Article 10, which may only be processed when Union or national law authorises it. From 10 July 2027, Article 76 of the AML Regulation (EU) 2024/1624 is that law for obliged entities, which include external accountants and tax advisors. It only covers data linked to money laundering, its predicate offences or terrorist financing, from reliable, accurate and up-to-date sources, with allegations kept apart from convictions, the client told in advance, high security, and no commercial reuse. If an AI or automated tool is involved, a person must meaningfully review any accept, refuse or due-diligence-level decision, the tool may only use customer due diligence data, and the client can ask for an explanation and challenge the decision (Article 76(5)). Delete 5 years after the relationship ends (Article 77(3)).

Why this lands on the accountant's desk

  • 243 days left to 10 July 2027, when the AMLR applies (Article 90). Screening files built now will still be in use then.
  • AI adverse-media tools return allegations, name matches and old news alongside convictions. Article 76(3)(b) requires a procedure that keeps them apart.
  • Article 30(5) GDPR: the under-250-staff exemption from the record of processing does not apply when processing includes criminal-offence data, so even a two-partner firm needs this in its record.

Step 3: checklist

  1. List every screening hit that mentions a crime, from any source: court registers, regulators, news, AI adverse-media tools, emails from third parties.
  2. Label each one as allegation, investigation, proceedings or conviction in the client file, never just "flagged".
  3. Check the offence is money laundering, a predicate offence or terrorist financing. If not, do not use it for due diligence and delete it unless another law authorises keeping it.
  4. Check the source: who published it, when, and whether it was later corrected.
  5. Put a short AML data paragraph in the engagement letter or privacy notice (template below) (privacy notice wording sheet).
  6. If an AI tool scores or filters clients, make sure a named person reviews every accept, refuse or due-diligence-level decision, the tool only uses customer due diligence data, and the client can get an explanation and challenge it (GDPR Article 22 decision sheet).
  7. Restrict access to the screening file and keep it out of marketing, pricing and CRM lists (access security sheet).
  8. Add the processing to the record of processing (Article 30 sheet) and, if screening is large scale, run a DPIA, since Article 35(3)(b) GDPR names large-scale processing of criminal-offence data (DPIA screening sheet).
  9. Diary the delete-by date: 5 years after the relationship ends or is refused (retention schedule).

Step 4: what to tell the client (engagement letter template)

As an obliged entity under anti-money-laundering law, we carry out customer due diligence on you and your beneficial owners. For that purpose only, we may process information about criminal convictions, offences, investigations or allegations relating to money laundering, its predicate offences or terrorist financing, obtained from reliable sources [including screening tools from (supplier)]. We record allegations separately from convictions, we do not use this information for marketing or any other commercial purpose, and access is restricted. Where a screening tool is used, a member of our staff reviews every decision to take on, keep or refuse a client or to change the level of checks, and you may ask for an explanation and challenge that decision. We keep due diligence records for 5 years after our relationship ends and then delete them, unless an authority requires longer. This is a regulatory summary, not legal advice.

Frequently asked questions

Can an accountant keep criminal-offence data found in AML screening?

Article 10 GDPR allows processing of personal data relating to criminal convictions and offences only under the control of official authority or when Union or Member State law authorises it with appropriate safeguards. Article 76(1) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1624 (AMLR) is such a Union law for obliged entities, which include auditors, external accountants and tax advisors (Article 3, point (3)(a)), but only to the extent strictly necessary to prevent money laundering and terrorist financing and only with the safeguards in Article 76(2) and (3).

Which conditions does Article 76 set for criminal-offence data?

The client must be told such data may be processed for AML compliance, the data must come from reliable sources and be accurate and up to date, decisions must not lead to biased and discriminatory outcomes, and security must be high (Article 76(2)). In addition the data must relate to money laundering, its predicate offences or terrorist financing, and procedures must keep allegations, investigations, proceedings and convictions apart, respecting the presumption of innocence (Article 76(3)).

Can an AI screening tool decide to refuse a client?

Article 76(5) allows decisions from automated processes, profiling or AI systems only if the data used is limited to data obtained under Chapter III (customer due diligence), any decision to enter, refuse, maintain or change the level of due diligence is subject to meaningful human intervention, and the customer can obtain an explanation and challenge the decision, except for a suspicious transaction report under Article 69.

Can we reuse screening results for marketing or pricing?

No. Article 76(4) says personal data processed on the basis of the AMLR may be used only to prevent money laundering and terrorist financing, must not be further processed in an incompatible way, and processing for commercial purposes is prohibited.

How long can screening results be kept?

Article 77(3) sets 5 years from the end of the business relationship, the occasional transaction or the refusal, and then personal data must be deleted, unless a competent authority requires further retention case by case (up to 5 more years) or another law sets a period.

When does this apply?

The AMLR applies from 10 July 2027 (Article 90). Until then the national law that transposes the current AML directive applies, together with GDPR Article 10. The sheet lets a firm check its screening files now so they meet the Article 76 conditions on day one.
