Statutory auditor term and rotation: section 139 and rule 5 in plain words

Last updated 27 Oct 2026 · India

Short answer. Under section 139(1) of the Companies Act 2013, every company appoints an auditor at its first AGM to hold office until the sixth AGM, a five-year term. Section 139(2) adds rotation for listed companies and the classes in rule 5 of the Companies (Audit and Auditors) Rules 2014: an individual auditor can serve one term of five consecutive years and an audit firm two terms of five years, followed by a five-year cooling-off. Rule 5 covers unlisted public companies with paid-up capital of ₹10 crore or more, private companies with paid-up capital of ₹50 crore or more, and companies with public borrowings of ₹50 crore or more. One person companies and small companies are outside rotation. The company files ADT-1 within 15 days of the meeting.

Checklist

  • Year of first appointment of the current auditor noted
  • Rotation class checked against rule 5 thresholds
  • Count of terms already served (including the firm's network rules)
  • Board and AGM resolutions for the new term
  • ADT-1 filed within 15 days

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting the term already served before the 2013 Act took effect, which counts toward rotation.
  • Re-appointing the same firm after two terms without a cooling-off.
  • Not refiling ADT-1 at reappointment.

Frequently asked questions

How long is a statutory auditor's term?

Five years, from the end of one AGM to the end of the sixth AGM.

Which companies must rotate auditors?

Listed companies and the classes in rule 5: unlisted public with paid-up ₹10 crore+, private with paid-up ₹50 crore+, and companies with public borrowings of ₹50 crore+.

What is the cooling-off period?

Five years before the same individual or firm can be appointed again.
