Leaving a cloud or AI tool? An EU Data Act switching sheet (Article 25 notice, transition and retrieval periods; switching charges end 12 January 2027) for small businesses and their accountants

Updated 28 Oct 2026 · Disclosure: the EURO AI Register mentioned below is an Unadhi product with a free edition. This guide is for information only, not legal advice.

What you get: a finished cloud and AI software switching sheet in three tabs: a services tab with one row per cloud or AI tool your business pays for, a gap formula that lists what the contract is missing under Article 25 of the EU Data Act, and a result that says OK TO PLAN, ASK SUPPLIER or OUT OF SCOPE; a switch dates tab that works out the notice, transition, retrieval and reply dates from the day you send your request; and a switching request template you can paste into an email to the supplier. Four made-up example rows show how it fills in.

Short answer: since 12 September 2025 the Data Act (Regulation (EU) 2023/2854) requires providers of "data processing services", which include cloud software sold as Software as a Service (Recital 81), to set out switching rights in a written contract: a notice period that "shall not exceed two months", a transitional period of at most "30 calendar days", data retrieval of "at least 30 calendar days" afterwards, and full erasure after that (Article 25(2)(a), (d), (g) and (h)). From 12 January 2027 providers "shall not impose any switching charges" (Article 29(1)). Until then they may charge only reduced switching charges that "shall not exceed the costs incurred" and are "directly linked to the switching process" (Article 29(2) and (3)).

Why accountants and their clients should look at this now

Small businesses now run their books, chat support, meeting notes, CRM and file storage on cloud and AI tools, often on annual plans that renew in January. The Data Act was written so that customers can "switch from one data processing service to another while maintaining a minimum functionality of service and without downtime", and it says that "customers benefiting from free-tier offerings should also benefit" (Recital 78). Article 23 bans "pre-commercial, commercial, technical, contractual and organisational obstacles" that stop a customer from terminating, signing with a new provider or "porting the customer's exportable data and digital assets ... including after having benefited from a free-tier offering" (Article 23(a) to (c)).

Two dates matter for advice given this quarter. First, switching charges, including "data egress charges" (Article 2(35) and (36)), must fall to zero on 12 January 2027. A client planning to leave a tool early next year may save money by timing the switch, and an invoice line for "migration" or "egress" dated after that day deserves a question. Second, Article 50 says the Regulation "shall apply from 12 September 2025"; its later dates for older contracts (12 September 2027) are written for Chapter IV on unfair contractual terms, not for the switching chapter (Chapter VI).

What the Data Act does not do: it does not cover a tool that is not a "data processing service" as defined in Article 2(8) ("ubiquitous and on-demand network access to a shared pool of configurable, scalable and elastic computing resources"), so check each tool. It also does not replace GDPR rights over personal data, such as data portability. And the core switching duties do not apply to "a non-production version for testing and evaluation purposes and for a limited period of time" (Article 31(2)); some duties, including the switching-charge rule, do not apply to services whose "majority of main features has been custom-built" for one customer and which are not offered at broad commercial scale (Article 31(1)).

Step 3: tab 3, switching request template

Article 25(3) lets the customer notify the provider that, at the end of the notice period, it will switch to a different provider, switch to its own on-premises systems, or erase its data. Paste this into an email and fill the brackets:

Subject: Switching request under Article 25 of the EU Data Act (Regulation (EU) 2023/2854), [customer name], account [number]

We give notice that, at the end of the notice period in our contract, we will [switch to [destination provider name and contact] / move to our own on-premises systems / erase our exportable data and digital assets]. Please confirm: (1) the date the notice period ends and the transitional period starts; (2) the export methods and formats available to us and the link to your online register of data formats (Article 26); (3) the full list of data and digital assets we can port (Article 25(2)(e)); (4) any switching or data egress charges and how they relate to your direct costs (Article 29(3)), or that none apply from 12 January 2027 (Article 29(1)); (5) the retrieval period after the transition and the date of full erasure (Article 25(2)(g) and (h)). If the 30-day transitional period is technically unfeasible, please tell us within 14 working days with reasons (Article 25(4)).

What to tell a client in one paragraph

"If you plan to leave a cloud or AI tool, check the contract against the Data Act first. The notice period cannot be longer than two months, the supplier must keep the service running for up to 30 days while you move, you get at least 30 more days to download your data, and from 12 January 2027 the supplier cannot charge you for switching. Before that date, any switching charge must be reduced and must not exceed the supplier's direct costs. Send the request in writing and keep the reply." Stay inside your professional rules and refer contract disputes to a lawyer.
