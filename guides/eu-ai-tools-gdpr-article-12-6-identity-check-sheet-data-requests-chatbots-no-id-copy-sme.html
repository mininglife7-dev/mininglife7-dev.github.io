Is it really them? A GDPR Article 12(6) identity check sheet for data requests that arrive through AI chatbots, without asking for ID copies

Updated 27 Oct 2026 · Disclosure: the EURO AI Register mentioned below is an Unadhi product with a free edition. This guide is for information only, not legal advice.

What you get: a finished identity check sheet in three tabs: a check log with one row per request and a formula that picks the lightest check that fits, a method table with what to ask for and what never to ask for, and a pause tracker that works out the new reply-by date while you wait for the person, plus AI tool examples and three made-up example rows.

Short answer: under Article 12(6) GDPR you may ask for extra information only when you have reasonable doubts about who is asking, and only what is necessary to confirm their identity. The EDPB says a copy of an ID card is generally not an appropriate way to check identity, and is disproportionate when the person is already known to you, for example through a login or the email address they gave you. If you ask for information without undue delay, the one-month clock can be paused until you get what you need (EDPB Guidelines 01/2022, para. 159).

Why AI tools make this harder

A request typed into a website chatbot, an AI phone agent or a support widget often comes with nothing but a first name. The data itself may sit in a chatbot transcript, a call recording or a meeting note-taker, and sending that to the wrong person is a personal data breach (EDPB para. 80). So you need a check, but a light one: asking everyone for a passport scan collects more data than you need and makes the right harder to use, which Article 12(2) forbids.

The law sets "without undue delay" as the target and one month as the outer limit, so answer as soon as the identity is confirmed rather than waiting for the new date. The Holidays range is the same one used in the Article 12 deadline tracker.
