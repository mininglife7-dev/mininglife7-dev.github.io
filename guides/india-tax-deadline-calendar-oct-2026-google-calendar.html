Why put tax dates in your calendar

Most late fees are not caused by missing money. They happen because a due date was in a PDF nobody opened. GST late fees, TDS late-filing fees and interest run per day, and a due date that falls on a Sunday does not move by itself. A calendar puts the date where you already look every morning, and a reminder three days earlier leaves time to collect invoices and challans or send them to your CA.

Common mistakes

  • Trusting a calendar over a notification. Governments extend dates by notification, sometimes on the last day. Every event says "Check official portal for extensions"; do that a few days before.
  • Waiting for a Sunday date to move. It doesn't, unless a notification says so. 18 Oct 2026 (CMP-08) and 15 Nov 2026 (Form 131) are Sundays. File early.
  • Picking the wrong quarterly GSTR-3B date. QRMP filers in Telangana, AP, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Gujarat, MP, Chhattisgarh and Goa use the 22nd; most northern and eastern states and Delhi use the 24th. Delete the event that isn't yours.
  • Still looking for Form 26Q. From tax year 2026-27 the statements are Forms 138, 140, 144 and 143. The TCS statement is now due on the 31st too, not the 15th.
  • Importing twice. Importing the file again creates duplicate events. Use the subscribe link if you want updates.
