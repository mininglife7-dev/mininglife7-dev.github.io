Short answer: the AMLR applies from 10 July 2027 (Article 90). As part of customer due diligence, an accountant or tax adviser must determine whether the client, its beneficial owner and any person the work is done for is a politically exposed person, a family member or a person known to be a close associate (Article 20(1)(g)). If so, on top of normal due diligence: senior management approval, adequate measures to establish source of wealth and source of funds, and enhanced ongoing monitoring (Article 42(1)). When a PEP leaves the function, risk-based measures continue until the risk is gone, and for at least 12 months (Article 45(2)). The same applies to family members and close associates (Article 46).

What to tell a client in one minute

"From July 2027 the EU anti-money laundering rules ask me, as your accountant, to check whether you, your owners or anyone we act for holds or held a prominent public role, such as minister, member of parliament, judge of a top court, mayor of a city of 50,000 or more, or board member of a state-owned company, or is a close family member or business partner of such a person. This is a standard check, not an accusation: the law says that refusing someone only because they are a PEP is against its spirit. If the answer is yes, I need a short note on where your wealth and the money in our work come from, and a sign-off from my firm's senior management."

Why AI matters here

Many firms run names through screening tools, often AI or fuzzy-matching software, to find PEPs. The AMLR does not change the test because a tool is used: the firm must still determine the status (Article 20(1)(g)) using the definitions in Article 2(1)(34) to (36) and the official lists of prominent public functions (Article 43). Treat a tool hit as a lead to confirm and record, and a "no hit" as an answer only for the names and lists the tool actually checked. Column C of the sheet is your confirmed answer, not the tool's.

The rules the sheet uses

  • Who is a PEP (Article 2(1)(34)): a person who is or has been entrusted with a prominent public function. In a Member State: heads of state and government, ministers and deputy or assistant ministers; members of parliament or similar bodies; members of governing bodies of political parties with seats in national bodies, or in regional or local bodies for constituencies of at least 50,000 inhabitants; members of supreme, constitutional or other top courts; members of courts of auditors or central bank boards; ambassadors, chargés d'affaires and high-ranking armed forces officers; members of the management or supervisory bodies of state-controlled enterprises, and of medium or large enterprises controlled by regional or local authorities; heads of regional and local authorities with at least 50,000 inhabitants; other functions set by Member States. Also top officials of international organisations, equivalent EU-level functions, and equivalent functions in third countries. Member States may set lower thresholds (Article 2(3), (4)).
  • Family members (Article 2(1)(35)): spouse or registered or similar partner; children and their spouses or partners; parents; and siblings only for head of state, head of government and minister-level functions or equivalents. Member States may designate siblings more broadly (Article 2(5)).
  • Close associates (Article 2(1)(36)): a person known to have joint beneficial ownership of legal entities or arrangements, or other close business relations, with a PEP; or the sole beneficial owner of an entity or arrangement known to have been set up for the de facto benefit of a PEP.
  • Measures (Article 42(1)): senior management approval for the occasional transaction or for starting or continuing the relationship; adequate measures to establish source of wealth and source of funds; enhanced ongoing monitoring. Recital 82 says senior management approval need not always come from the board.
  • Former PEPs (Article 45): take the continuing risk into account; apply one or more Article 34(4) measures until the risk no longer exists, and in any case for not less than 12 months after the person left the function; the same applies to new clients who held a function in the past.
  • Family and associates (Article 46): Articles 42, 44 and 45 apply to them as well.
  • Not a reason to refuse: recital 98 says refusing a relationship simply because someone is a PEP, family member or close associate is contrary to the letter and spirit of the Regulation.
  • Coming guidance: by 10 July 2027 AMLA must issue guidelines on identifying close associates and on the risk level of categories of PEPs, including after they leave office (Article 42(2)); the Commission will publish a single list of prominent public functions in the Official Journal (Article 43(5)).

Questions

When do the AMLR PEP rules apply to accountants and tax advisers?

From 10 July 2027 (Article 90 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1624). Auditors, external accountants and tax advisers are obliged entities (Article 3(3)(a)). Until then, national laws based on Directive (EU) 2015/849 apply.

Who counts as a politically exposed person under the AMLR?

A person who is or has been entrusted with a prominent public function listed in Article 2(1)(34), such as ministers, members of parliament, top judges, central bank board members, ambassadors, board members of state-controlled enterprises, and heads of regional or local authorities with at least 50,000 inhabitants, plus equivalent EU, international and third-country functions.

Are siblings of a PEP family members?

Only for head of state, head of government, minister, deputy or assistant minister functions and their EU-level or third-country equivalents (Article 2(1)(35)(d)). Member States may designate siblings more broadly (Article 2(5)).

What must I do if a client is a PEP?

On top of normal due diligence: get senior management approval, take adequate measures to establish source of wealth and source of funds, and run enhanced ongoing monitoring (Article 42(1)). The same applies to family members and close associates (Article 46).

How long do measures last after a PEP leaves office?

Until the risk from the former function no longer exists, and in any case for at least 12 months after the person left the function (Article 45(2)).

Can I refuse a client because they are a PEP?

Recital 98 says refusing a business relationship simply because someone is a PEP, family member or close associate is contrary to the letter and spirit of the Regulation.
