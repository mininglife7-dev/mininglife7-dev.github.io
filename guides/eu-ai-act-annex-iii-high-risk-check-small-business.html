Is our AI tool high-risk under the EU AI Act? An Annex III check for small businesses (2026)

Updated 14 Oct 2026 · Disclosure: the EURO AI Register mentioned below is an Unadhi product with a free edition. This guide is for information only, not legal advice.

Short answer: for most small businesses, no. Under the EU AI Act a tool is high-risk because of what you use it for, not because it is "AI". Writing emails, summarising documents, translating, bookkeeping help or a customer-service chatbot are not on the high-risk list. The list that matters for most firms is Annex III: eight areas such as hiring and staff management, credit scoring and insurance pricing for individuals, and education. If one of your tools is used in those areas, the high-risk rules reach you from 2 December 2027 (moved from 2 August 2026 by the Digital Omnibus on AI). Check each tool once, write down why, and you are done until its use changes.

The 10-minute check, one tool at a time

  1. Write the real use in one sentence. Not "we use Copilot" but "HR uses Copilot to shortlist CVs for sales jobs". The same tool can be minimal-risk in one team and high-risk in another.
  2. Is that use in one of the eight areas above? If not, it is not high-risk under Annex III. Note "no Annex III use" with the date.
  3. Is it a safety component of a regulated product (medical devices, machinery, toys, lifts and the other Annex I laws)? That is the separate Article 6(1) route, with rules from 2 August 2028.
  4. If it is in an Annex III area, does Article 6(3) take it out? It can, if the system poses no significant risk of harm and only:
    • performs a narrow procedural task (for example turning unstructured documents into structured data);
    • improves the result of work a person already finished (for example polishing the wording of a letter already written);
    • detects decision patterns or deviations from earlier decisions, without replacing or influencing the human assessment unless a person reviews it; or
    • does a preparatory task for an assessment (for example indexing or translating files before a person decides).
  5. Profiling override. If the system profiles natural persons (automated evaluation of things like work performance, reliability or behaviour), it is always high-risk, whatever step 4 says.
  6. Write down the result and why. Date, person, outcome, and which step decided it. If you rely on step 4, keep the vendor's own assessment: a provider that decides its Annex III system is not high-risk has to document that assessment (Art. 6(4)).

If a tool is high-risk: what changes for you as the user

If you only use a high-risk tool (you are the deployer), most of the heavy work, such as technical documentation and conformity assessment, is the vendor's. From 2 December 2027 Article 26 asks you to use it as instructed, give trained people human oversight, check the input data you control, monitor it and report serious incidents, keep its automatic logs for at least six months, and inform workers' representatives and affected workers before using it at work. Details: record-keeping for SME deployers and 12 questions to ask your AI vendor.

If you built the tool, or sell it under your own name, you are the provider and the provider duties apply. Get specialist advice.

What applies today, whatever the result

  • AI literacy measures for staff who use AI (Art. 4, since 2 February 2025): Article 4 for small business
  • No banned practices (Art. 5, since 2 February 2025): the Article 5 list
  • Transparency for chatbots and AI-generated content (Art. 50, since 2 August 2026): Article 50 checklist
