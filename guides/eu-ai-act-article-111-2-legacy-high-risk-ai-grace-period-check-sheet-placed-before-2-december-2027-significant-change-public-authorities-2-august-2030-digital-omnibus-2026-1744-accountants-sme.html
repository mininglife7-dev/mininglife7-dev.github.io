Short answer: high-risk AI systems already on the EU market get a grace period. Under Article 111(2) of the AI Act, as replaced by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (Digital Omnibus on AI), the Regulation applies to operators of high-risk AI systems placed on the market or put into service before the date of application of Chapter III only if, from that date, those systems undergo significant changes in their designs. After the Omnibus, Chapter III Sections 1 to 3 apply from 2 December 2027 for Annex III systems and 2 August 2028 for Annex I systems (Article 113(c) as amended). Recital 39 of the Omnibus adds that the grace period works per type and model: if the first unit was lawfully placed before the date, later units of the unchanged design are covered too. Two limits: Article 5 bans always apply, and high-risk systems intended to be used by public authorities must comply by 2 August 2030 in any case.

Why this lands on the accountant's desk

  • 388 days left to 2 December 2027 (as of 9 Nov 2026). Whether a client's product counts as "already on the market" before that date changes its compliance budget by a lot, so it belongs in planning and in valuations.
  • Proof of the first placing date (contract, invoice, go-live record) is a bookkeeping record. It is the evidence the grace period depends on.
  • A planned upgrade can end the grace period. Product road maps and capitalised development costs should be checked against "significant change" before the date passes.
  • A deployer that makes a substantial modification becomes the provider (Article 25 guide).

Step 3: checklist

  1. Is the system high-risk, and by which route: an Annex III use case (Article 6(2)) or a safety component of an Annex I product (Article 6(1))? See the Annex III check and the Article 6(3) exception sheet.
  2. Could any use be a prohibited practice? Article 111(2) is without prejudice to Article 5 (Article 5 list).
  3. When was the first unit of this type and model placed on the EU market (first making available, Article 3(9)) or put into service (first use, Article 3(11))? Where is the dated proof?
  4. Is the system intended to be used by public authorities? If so, plan full compliance by 2 August 2030.
  5. List every planned change to design or intended purpose and test each one against "substantial modification" (Article 3(23); recital 177). A yes means a new conformity assessment (Article 43(4)).
  6. Who is the provider and who is the deployer (role check sheet)? Put the change-control rule in the supplier contract (contract clauses).
  7. Record who decided, why and the date.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Article 111(2) grace period?

Article 111(2) of the AI Act, as replaced by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, says the Regulation applies to operators of high-risk AI systems (other than Annex X large-scale IT system components) that were placed on the market or put into service before the date of application of Chapter III referred to in Article 113 only if, as from that date, those systems are subject to significant changes in their designs. It is without prejudice to Article 5, so the bans still apply.

Which dates count after the Digital Omnibus?

Article 113, third paragraph, point (c), as amended, makes Chapter III Sections 1, 2 and 3 apply from 2 December 2027 for AI systems classified as high-risk under Article 6(2) and Annex III, and from 2 August 2028 for those classified under Article 6(1) and Annex I. Our sheet reads the Article 111(2) date per route on that basis; that is our reading and is flagged below.

Does the grace period cover every unit sold later?

Recital 39 of Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 says the grace period applies to the type and model: if at least one unit was lawfully placed on the market or put into service before the date, other units of the same type and model may continue to be placed on the market, made available or put into service without additional obligations, as long as the design stays unchanged. The decisive factor is when the first unit of that type and model was first placed on the Union market or put into service.

What counts as a significant change?

Recital 177 of the AI Act says significant change should be understood as equivalent in substance to substantial modification. Article 3(23) defines that as a change not foreseen or planned in the initial conformity assessment that affects compliance with the Chapter III Section 2 requirements or modifies the intended purpose. Recital 39 of Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 says a significant design change after the date triggers full compliance, including conformity assessment.

Are public bodies treated differently?

Yes. Providers and deployers of high-risk AI systems intended to be used by public authorities must take the necessary steps to comply by 2 August 2030 in any case (Article 111(2), second sentence).

What about general-purpose AI models and older generators?

Separate rules: providers of general-purpose AI models placed on the market before 2 August 2025 must comply by 2 August 2027 (Article 111(3)), and providers of generative AI systems placed on the market before 2 August 2026 must comply with Article 50(2) by 2 December 2026 (new Article 111(4)). This sheet does not grade those.
