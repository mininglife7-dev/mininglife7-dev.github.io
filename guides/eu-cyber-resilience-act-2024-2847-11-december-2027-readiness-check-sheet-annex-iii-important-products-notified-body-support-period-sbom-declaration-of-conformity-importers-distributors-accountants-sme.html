Short answer: the Cyber Resilience Act applies from 11 December 2027 (395 days from 11 Nov 2026). From that day, every hardware or software product with a data connection that is placed on the EU market needs a documented cybersecurity risk assessment, the Annex I security and vulnerability handling requirements, a support period of at least five years (or the expected use time if shorter), technical documentation, the right conformity procedure, an EU declaration of conformity and the CE marking (Articles 6, 13 and 71(2)). Two parts already apply: the notified body rules since 11 June 2026 and the Article 14 reporting duty since 11 September 2026, which covers products placed on the market earlier as well (Article 69(3)). Products placed on the market before 11 December 2027 otherwise stay outside the Regulation unless they are substantially modified from that date (Article 69(2)).

What to tell a client in one minute

  • Software counts. A "product with digital elements" is a software or hardware product and its remote data processing solutions, including components sold separately (Article 3(1)). A desktop app, a mobile app sold or given away in a commercial activity, firmware and a connected device are all in; a website that does not support a product's function is not.
  • The date is per unit or version. Placing on the market is the first making available in the EU (Article 3(21)). Stock placed before 11 December 2027 can still be sold under Article 69(2); new units and new versions from that day need the full paperwork.
  • Know the category. Products whose core functionality is in Annex III (for example password managers, VPNs, routers, operating systems, smart locks, security cameras, baby monitors, connected toys, children's wearables) are "important". Class I can use internal control only if harmonised standards, common specifications or an EU certificate were applied in full; class II needs a notified body or an EU certificate (Articles 7 and 32).
  • Five years of security updates. The support period is at least five years unless the product is expected to be used for less; the end month and year must be shown at the time of purchase (Article 13(8) and (19)).
  • Reporting already applies. Actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents must be notified to the coordinating CSIRT and ENISA within 24 hours (early warning) and 72 hours, with a final report later (Article 14). See our Article 14 reporting sheet.
  • Rebranding or changing it makes you the manufacturer. An importer or distributor that sells under its own name or makes a substantial modification takes on the manufacturer's duties (Articles 21 and 22).

Why AI matters here

AI products are software, so a client's AI app, chatbot module or AI-enabled device sold in the EU is also a product with digital elements. Article 12 links the two laws: a product that is a high-risk AI system under the AI Act and meets the CRA's Annex I requirements is deemed to meet the AI Act's Article 15 cybersecurity requirement if this is shown in the EU declaration of conformity, and the AI Act's conformity procedure (its Article 43) is used, except that Annex III and IV products under AI Act internal control go through the CRA procedures for the cybersecurity requirements (Article 12(3)). Checklist before 11 December 2027

  • List each client product and software version, with the date the first unit or version was or will be placed on the EU market; keep invoices or release notes that prove dates before 11 December 2027.
  • Check Article 14 reporting now: who in the client spots actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents, and who files within 24 hours.
  • Match each product's core functionality against Annexes III and IV (and the technical descriptions in Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2392); book notified bodies early for class I products without full standards, class II and Annex IV products.
  • Write the cybersecurity risk assessment and the software bill of materials; set and publish the support period end date.
  • Publish a vulnerability contact and a coordinated vulnerability disclosure policy; set up free, separate security updates kept available for 10 years.
  • Prepare the EU declaration of conformity (Annex V or simplified Annex VI), CE marking and Annex II user information.
  • Importers and distributors: ask suppliers now for the declaration, CE marking, Annex II information and support end date for deliveries after 11 December 2027.
  • For AI products, check whether they are high-risk under the AI Act and plan one declaration covering both laws (Article 28(3)).

Questions

When does the Cyber Resilience Act apply?

From 11 December 2027 for most duties. Article 14 reporting of actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents has applied since 11 September 2026, and the notified body rules since 11 June 2026 (Article 71(2) of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847).

Does the CRA apply to software, apps and AI tools?

Yes, if they are products with digital elements made available in a commercial activity with a data connection to a device or network (Articles 2(1), 3(1) and 3(22)). Software and its remote data processing solutions are covered; websites that do not support a product's function are not.

Can a client keep selling stock placed on the market before 11 December 2027?

Yes. Products placed on the market before that date are subject to the Regulation only if they are substantially modified from that date, but Article 14 reporting applies to them anyway (Article 69(2) and (3)).

When is a notified body needed?

For Annex III class I products where harmonised standards, common specifications or an EU certificate were not applied in full, for class II products (unless an EU certificate is used) and for Annex IV critical products (Articles 8(1) and 32(2) to (4)). Open-source Annex III products may use internal control if the technical documentation is public (Article 32(5)).

How long is the support period?

At least five years, or the expected use time if the product is expected to be used for less than five years. The end date, at least month and year, must be shown at the time of purchase (Article 13(8) and (19)).

What are the fines?

Up to EUR 15 million or 2.5% of worldwide annual turnover for breaches of Annex I and Articles 13 and 14, up to EUR 10 million or 2% for other listed duties and up to EUR 5 million or 1% for misleading information (Article 64). Micro and small manufacturers are not fined for missing the 24-hour early warning deadlines (Article 64(10) as corrected).
