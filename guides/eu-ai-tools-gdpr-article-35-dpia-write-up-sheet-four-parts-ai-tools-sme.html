Your AI tool needs a DPIA: a GDPR Article 35 write-up sheet for small businesses (the four required parts, risk scores, sign-off and when to ask the authority)

Updated 24 Oct 2026 · Disclosure: the EURO AI Register mentioned below is an Unadhi product with a free edition. This guide is for information only, not legal advice.

What you get: a finished DPIA write-up sheet: one row per AI tool that screened in, one column for each of the four parts Article 35(7) GDPR requires, likelihood and severity scores before and after your measures, columns for DPO advice, people's views and the EU AI Act inputs, a decision formula that tells you when to consult your data protection authority (Article 36), a sign-off and review date, and two made-up example rows.

Short answer: if your DPIA screening says an AI tool needs a DPIA, you must write it before you start using the tool. It must at least describe the processing and purposes, explain why it is necessary and proportionate, assess the risks to people, and list the measures that address those risks. If a high risk remains that your own measures cannot reduce, you must consult your data protection authority before you start.

Step 1: before you write

  • Run the DPIA screening sheet first. This write-up sheet is for the tools that screened in.
  • One DPIA can cover a set of similar processing operations with similar high risks (Art. 35(1)), so a firm using the same AI transcription tool in three offices can write one row.
  • Collect what you already have: the tool's entry in your Article 30 records, the supplier's processor agreement, your legitimate interest assessment if you rely on it, and for high-risk AI systems the provider's instructions for use.

Keep the risk lines (E to H) on rows 2 to 9 under each tool, or on a second tab with the tool name in the first column. Sort by column L so "Consult authority before start" comes first. The 1 to 3 scales and the thresholds are your own working numbers; the GDPR asks you to assess likelihood and severity (Recital 90) but does not set a scale.

Step 3: if column L says "Consult authority before start"

  • Do not start the processing. Article 36(1) requires prior consultation where the DPIA indicates a high risk in the absence of measures you take to mitigate it.
  • Send the authority: the responsibilities of controller, joint controllers and processors where applicable; the purposes and means; the measures and safeguards; the DPO's contact details where applicable; the DPIA itself; and anything else it asks for (Art. 36(3)).
  • Put the dates in your calendar: written advice within up to eight weeks of receipt, extendable by six weeks for complex processing; the authority tells you of an extension within one month, and the periods can be paused while it waits for information it asked for (Art. 36(2)).

Do this week

  • List the AI tools that screened in and give each one row
  • Fill C and D in plain words; most of it is already in your Article 30 records
  • Write at least three risk lines per tool and score them before and after measures
  • Check the measures against the privacy by default settings sheet and the access security sheet
  • For high-risk AI systems, also check the Article 27 fundamental rights impact assessment and the human oversight log
  • Sign off, file it, and set the review date
