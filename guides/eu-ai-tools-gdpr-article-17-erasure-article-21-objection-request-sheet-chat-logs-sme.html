A customer asks you to delete their data or stop using it in your AI tools: a GDPR erasure (Article 17) and objection (Article 21) request sheet for small EU businesses

Updated 22 Oct 2026 · Disclosure: the EURO AI Register mentioned below is an Unadhi product with a free edition. This guide is for information only, not legal advice.

What you get: a finished request sheet with one row per request: a request log (date, deadline, identity check, ground, exception, extension, reply, proof), a grounds table with AI-tool examples for each ground in Article 17(1) and Article 21, a search list of the places AI tools keep personal data, a supplier line for Article 19, and three reply texts to copy: done, need more time, and refused.

The short answer

When someone asks you to delete their data (Article 17) or to stop using it (Article 21), reply without undue delay and at the latest within one month (Article 12(3)). Check which ground applies and whether an exception in Article 17(3) lets you keep some data. Search every AI tool, transcript, recording and supplier log, tell each recipient of the data (Article 19), and keep a short written record of what you decided and why. An objection to direct marketing must always be followed (Article 21(3)).

The EDPB's report on its 2025 coordinated enforcement action on the right to erasure (adopted 10 February 2026; 32 authorities, 764 controllers) found that many controllers, especially small ones that rarely get requests, had no internal procedure. It recommends a documented procedure with clear deadlines and steps, and writing down the reasons when you rely on an exception.

Table 2: which ground applies

ArticleGroundAI-tool example
17(1)(a)The data are no longer necessary for the purpose you collected them forOld chatbot transcripts kept 'just in case' after the enquiry is closed
17(1)(b)They withdraw consent and there is no other legal groundRecording of a sales call made on consent; AI newsletter personalisation based on consent
17(1)(c) with 21(1)They object to processing based on legitimate interests (Article 6(1)(f)) and you have no overriding legitimate groundsAI scoring of support tickets or leads that you run on legitimate interests
17(1)(c) with 21(2)They object to direct marketing; this objection is absolute (Article 21(3))AI-written marketing e-mails, AI audience segments for ads
17(1)(d)The data were processed unlawfullyPossibly: staff pasted customer data into a personal AI account with no legal basis
17(1)(e)EU or national law requires erasureCheck your national rules
17(1)(f)Data of a child collected for an online service under Article 8(1)Children's accounts on an app with AI features

Exceptions (Article 17(3)), the ones a small business most often meets: (b) you must keep the data to comply with a legal obligation under EU or national law, for example invoices kept for tax rules; (e) you need the data to establish, exercise or defend legal claims. Apply them only to the data they really cover and delete the rest. If you keep data under an exception, the EDPB report recommends safeguards such as restriction (Article 18) or keeping the data separate.

Objection (Article 21(1)): where you rely on legitimate interests, you must stop unless you can show compelling legitimate grounds that override the person's interests, rights and freedoms, or the data is needed for legal claims. Direct marketing (Article 21(2)-(3)): no balancing; stop using the data for marketing, including AI profiling linked to it. You must tell people about the right to object clearly and separately at the latest in your first communication with them (Article 21(4)).

The EDPB found controllers treating deletion of a user account as erasure while data stayed in internal systems, and some relying on weak anonymisation, such as partial masking, instead of deletion. If you replace data instead of deleting it, make sure the person can no longer be identified.

Step 4: tell the supplier and other recipients (Article 19)

Communicate the erasure to each recipient the data was disclosed to, unless this proves impossible or involves disproportionate effort, and tell the person who those recipients are if they ask (Article 19). Your AI supplier, as processor, must help you answer rights requests where possible (Article 28(3)(e)).

Please delete all personal data relating to [name / e-mail / customer ID] held in [tool] for our account, including conversation history, uploaded files, outputs and logs, and confirm in writing when this is done and when copies in your back-ups will be removed. Our reference: [R-001].

Step 5: three replies to copy

Done:

Thank you for your request of [date]. We have deleted your personal data from [list of tools and records] and asked [supplier] to delete its copies. [We keep your invoices for [period] because [law] requires it; they are not used for anything else.] If you have questions, reply to this e-mail.

Need more time (send within the first month):

We received your request on [date]. Because [reason, for example several systems and suppliers are involved], we need up to two more months and will reply by [date]. [While we check your objection, we have restricted the use of your data.]

Refused, fully or partly (within one month):

We cannot delete [data] because [reason and Article 17(3) point, for example a legal duty to keep invoices under [law] until [date]]. We have deleted [the rest]. You can lodge a complaint with a data protection supervisory authority, for example [your authority], and seek a judicial remedy.
