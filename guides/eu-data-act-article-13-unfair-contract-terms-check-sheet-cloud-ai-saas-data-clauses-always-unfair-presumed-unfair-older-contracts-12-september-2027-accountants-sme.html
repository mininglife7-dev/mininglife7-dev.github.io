Short answer: under Article 13 of the EU Data Act (Regulation (EU) 2023/2854), a data term that a supplier imposed on your business on a take-it-or-leave-it basis is not binding on you if it is unfair. Three kinds of term are always unfair (Article 13(4)), seven are presumed unfair (Article 13(5)), and the rest face a general test (Article 13(3)). It covers terms about access to and use of data and liability and remedies for data obligations, not the price or the main subject matter. It applies to contracts concluded after 12 September 2025 now, and from 12 September 2027 to older contracts that are open-ended or run to at least 11 January 2034 (Article 50).

Why this lands on the accountant's desk

  • 307 days left (from 9 Nov 2026) to 12 September 2027, when older open-ended cloud, AI tool and data contracts come into scope.
  • Small firms rarely negotiate supplier terms. Article 13 only bites if you tried and could not change the term (Article 13(6)), so the evidence of asking matters.
  • AI tool terms often let the supplier reuse your data or refuse an export. Those match the presumed-unfair types in Article 13(5)(b), (c) and (e).

Step 3: checklist

  1. List every contract under which a supplier holds, processes or provides data for the business: cloud storage, accounting and payroll software, AI tools, CRM, connected devices, data feeds.
  2. Note the date each contract was concluded and whether it is open-ended or has a fixed end date.
  3. Pull out only the data terms: who may access and use the data, exports and copies, termination, liability and remedies.
  4. For each, record whether you tried to change it. If you never asked, Article 13 may not apply; ask now in writing for terms you can still renegotiate.
  5. Code each term with the key above and read the status.
  6. For UNFAIR and PRESUMED UNFAIR terms, write to the supplier (template below).
  7. Check the data processing agreement and sub-processor terms separately under GDPR (Article 28 DPA sheet).
  8. If you plan to leave a cloud or SaaS supplier, use the switching rules too (Data Act switching sheet).
  9. Diary 12 September 2027 for older open-ended or long contracts.

Frequently asked questions

Does the Data Act protect a small firm against one-sided cloud or AI tool terms?

Yes, for data terms. Article 13(1) of Regulation (EU) 2023/2854 says a contractual term concerning access to and use of data, or liability and remedies for the breach or termination of data related obligations, that one enterprise has unilaterally imposed on another is not binding on the latter if it is unfair. It protects any enterprise on the receiving end, not only SMEs, and the parties cannot exclude it or vary its effects (Article 13(9)).

When is a term unilaterally imposed?

Article 13(6): when one party supplied it and the other could not influence its content despite an attempt to negotiate it. The party that supplied the term must prove it was not imposed, and it cannot itself argue that its own term is unfair. Recital 59 adds that a term simply provided and accepted, or negotiated and agreed in amended form, is not unilaterally imposed. So keep the emails or redlines that show you asked.

What is the difference between the two lists?

Terms in Article 13(4) are always unfair, for example excluding liability for intentional acts or gross negligence. Terms in Article 13(5) are presumed unfair, for example stopping you getting a copy of your data or from terminating within a reasonable period; recital 62 says the supplier can rebut the presumption by showing the term is not unfair in the specific case. Anything else is tested under the general rule in Article 13(3): a gross deviation from good commercial practice in data access and use, contrary to good faith and fair dealing.

Does it apply to contracts we signed before September 2025?

Article 50: Chapter IV applies to contracts concluded after 12 September 2025. For contracts concluded on or before 12 September 2025 it applies from 12 September 2027, but only if they are of indefinite duration or due to expire at least 10 years from 11 January 2024 (that is, on or after 11 January 2034, our reading of the date). Shorter older contracts are not covered.

Can we challenge the price this way?

No. Article 13(8) excludes terms defining the main subject matter of the contract and the adequacy of the price, as against the data supplied in exchange. A price-change clause can still fall under Article 13(5)(g) if there is no valid reason and no right to terminate.

What happens to the rest of the contract?

Article 13(7): where the unfair term can be severed, the remaining terms stay binding. The sheet's to-do column reminds you to ask for a corrected clause rather than to walk away.
