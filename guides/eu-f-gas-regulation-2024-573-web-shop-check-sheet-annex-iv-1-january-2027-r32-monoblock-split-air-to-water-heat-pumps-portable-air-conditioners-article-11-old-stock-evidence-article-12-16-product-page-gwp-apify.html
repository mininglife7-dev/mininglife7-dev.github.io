Short answer: from 1 January 2027, 52 days from 10 November 2026, Annex IV of Regulation (EU) 2024/573 bans placing on the EU market plug-in, monoblock and other self-contained air conditioners and heat pumps up to 12 kW (point 8(b)) and split air-to-water heat pumps up to 12 kW (point 9(b)) that contain fluorinated greenhouse gases with a GWP of 150 or more. R-32 has a GWP of 675 (Annex I), so R-32 monoblock and split air-to-water units are caught unless a safety exception applies. Stock already placed on the market before that date may still be sold, but from 1 January 2028 only “if evidence is provided” that it was placed on the market before the date (Article 11(1)). Product pages for units with a GWP of 150 or more must already give the F-gas label information (Article 12(16)).

Why accountants, bookkeepers and web agencies should look at this now

  • Stock bought in December can become hard to sell. The ban follows the date a unit was first placed on the EU market (Article 3(6)), not the date the shop sells it. Units the importer releases in January 2027 cannot be sold, so a shop needs the supplier's dates for its stock.
  • The evidence rule hits old stock one year later. From 1 January 2028 the shop must be able to show that each R-32 monoblock it sells was placed on the market before 1 January 2027 (Article 11(1)). Purchase invoices and supplier confirmations sit with the bookkeeper.
  • Product pages are part of it. Article 12(16) puts the label information (contains fluorinated greenhouse gases, gas designation, kg and CO2 equivalent, GWP) into “descriptions used for advertising” for GWP 150 or more. Web agencies maintain those pages.
  • Split systems need an installer. Non-hermetically sealed equipment may only be sold to an end user with evidence that the installer holds an F-gas certificate under Article 10 (our paraphrase) and will install it (Article 11(7)).

FAQ

What changes for heat pumps and air conditioners on 1 January 2027?

Under Annex IV of Regulation (EU) 2024/573, from 1 January 2027 these may no longer be placed on the EU market if they contain fluorinated greenhouse gases with a GWP of 150 or more: plug-in, monoblock and other self-contained air conditioners and heat pumps up to and including 12 kW (point 8(b)), self-contained units above 12 kW up to 50 kW (point 8(d)), split air-to-water systems up to 12 kW (point 9(b)) and chillers up to 12 kW (point 7(b)). Chillers above 12 kW with a GWP of 750 or more follow on the same date (point 7(d)). Each point has an exception where safety requirements at the site of operation require it.

Can a web shop still sell R-32 units it already has in stock?

Yes, if the units were placed on the EU market before the prohibition date. The ban is on placing on the market, which Article 3(6) defines as the customs release for free circulation or supplying or making available in the Union for the first time. Article 11(1) adds that from one year after the date in Annex IV, units placed on the market before it may be supplied only if evidence is provided that they were placed lawfully before that date. For the 1 January 2027 bans that means from 1 January 2028. Units placed on the market unlawfully after the date may not be supplied at all.

What must the product page say?

Article 12(16) says the label information must also be included in descriptions used for advertising for products and equipment containing fluorinated greenhouse gases with a GWP of 150 or more. Under Article 12(3) that is an indication that the product contains fluorinated greenhouse gases, the industry designation of the gas, the quantity in weight and in CO2 equivalent, and the GWP. We read a web shop product description as a description used for advertising; the Regulation does not mention web shops by name.

What is the GWP of R-32, and how do I work out t CO2e?

Annex I gives HFC-32 a GWP of 675 and HFC-134a 1430. For mixtures, Annex VI gives the method: a weighted average of the weight shares times the GWP of each substance. Tonnes of CO2 equivalent are the weight of gas in tonnes times its GWP (Article 3(3)), so 1.5 kg of R-32 is 0.0015 x 675 = 1.013 t CO2e.

Does a split air conditioner need an installer with an F-gas certificate?

Article 11(7) says non-hermetically sealed equipment charged with fluorinated greenhouse gases may only be sold to an end user where evidence is provided that the installation is to be carried out by an undertaking holding a certificate under Article 10 (our paraphrase of the wording). Most split systems are joined on site, so the shop should collect that evidence before the sale, for example with an installer field at checkout.

Are propane (R-290) heat pumps affected?

Propane is not a fluorinated greenhouse gas: Annex VI lists it with a GWP of 0.02 as a non-fluorinated substance. Annex IV and the F-gas label rules in Article 12 do not apply to it. Other rules (product safety, energy labels, installation standards) still do.
