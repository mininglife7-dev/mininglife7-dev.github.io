Do your AI tools mean you need a data protection officer? A GDPR Article 37 check sheet for small businesses

Updated 24 Oct 2026 · Disclosure: the EURO AI Register mentioned below is an Unadhi product with a free edition. This guide is for information only, not legal advice.

What you get: a finished DPO check sheet: a six-question test from Article 37 GDPR and the official DPO guidelines, an 8-column sheet with one row per AI tool and a result formula, a German add-on for Section 38 BDSG, three filled example rows, and a one-paragraph decision record to keep with your records. Copy the tables into a spreadsheet and you are done in about 20 minutes.

Short answer: using an AI tool does not by itself mean you need a data protection officer. You need one when a core activity monitors people regularly and systematically on a large scale, or processes health or criminal-offence data on a large scale, or when national law (for example Germany's) says so.

Step 1: the six-question test

QuestionSourceAnswer
1. Are you a public authority or body (courts acting in their judicial capacity excepted)?Art. 37(1)(a)Y / N
2. Is the AI tool part of a core activity: a key operation you need to reach your business goals, or processing that is an inextricable part of it? Payroll, standard IT support and office productivity are usually ancillary.Art. 37(1)(b)–(c); Recital 97; WP243 section 2.1.2Y / N
3. Does that activity involve regular and systematic monitoring of people? Examples in the guidelines: email retargeting, data-driven marketing, profiling and scoring for risk assessment (credit scoring, insurance premiums, fraud prevention, anti-money-laundering), location tracking by apps, loyalty programmes, behavioural advertising, wellness and health data from wearables, CCTV, connected devices.Art. 37(1)(b); WP243 section 2.1.4Y / N
4. Does it involve health or other special category data (Article 9) or data on criminal convictions and offences (Article 10)?Art. 37(1)(c)Y / N
5. Is it on a large scale? Weigh the number of people (or share of the relevant population), the volume and range of data, how long it runs, and the geographic reach. Not large scale: patient data held by an individual physician, conviction data held by an individual lawyer.WP243 section 2.1.3; Recital 91Y / N
6. Does national law add a case? In Germany: at least 20 persons constantly dealing with automated processing, or any processing that needs a DPIA, or commercial processing for transfer or market or opinion research.Art. 37(4); Section 38(1) BDSGY / N

Read it this way: Q1 yes, or Q2 and Q3 and Q5 yes, or Q2 and Q4 and Q5 yes, or Q6 yes means a DPO is required. Anything else means not required by law, and you write down why.

If the answer is yes

  • Choose someone with expert knowledge of data protection law and practice; a staff member or an outside DPO on a service contract both work (Art. 37(5) and (6))
  • Publish the DPO's contact details and send them to your supervisory authority (Art. 37(7))
  • Involve the DPO early, give resources, take no instructions on their tasks, let them report to top management, and avoid conflicts of interest, for example the owner acting as their own DPO (Art. 38(1)–(3) and (6))
  • Their minimum tasks: inform and advise, monitor compliance and staff training, advise on DPIAs, and act as contact point for the supervisory authority (Art. 39)

If you appoint a DPO voluntarily, the guidelines say the same Article 37 to 39 rules apply to that person. If you would rather not, you can still name a privacy contact without calling them a DPO.
