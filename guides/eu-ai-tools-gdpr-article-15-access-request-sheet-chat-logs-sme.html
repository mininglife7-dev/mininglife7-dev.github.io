A customer asks what your AI tools hold about them: a GDPR Article 15 access-request sheet for small EU businesses (prompts, chat logs, transcripts, scores)

What you get: a finished access-request sheet to fill once per request: a deadline line, a where-to-search table for six common kinds of AI tool, the eight items Article 15 says you must tell the person, a section for automated scores and decisions, a check for other people's data in transcripts, and a short reply you can adapt.

The short answer

Under Article 15 GDPR, a person can ask whether you process their personal data and, if you do, get a copy of it plus eight pieces of information. Data inside AI tools counts like any other data you control: prompts, uploads, chat logs, transcripts, outputs about them and scores. You must answer without undue delay and within one month (Article 12(3)), and the first copy is free (Article 15(3)).

Where data goes to a country outside the EU, also tell them the safeguards used for that transfer (Article 15(2)), for example standard contractual clauses.

Step 4: scores and automated decisions

A score or label about the person is their personal data, so include it in the copy. If the tool makes or nearly makes a decision with legal or similarly significant effect, item (h) applies. In case C-203/22 (Dun & Bradstreet Austria, 27 February 2025) the Court of Justice said the explanation must describe the procedure and principles actually applied, in a way the person can understand, so they can see which of their data was used and how. If you believe trade secrets are involved, you cannot simply refuse; the Court said the information must then go to the authority or court, which weighs the interests. Ask your supplier now for a plain-language description of how its score works. See the Article 22 decision sheet.

Step 5: other people in the data

Transcripts and chat logs often contain other people. The right to a copy must not adversely affect the rights and freedoms of others (Article 15(4)). Remove or mask other people's details where giving them would harm those people, but do not withhold the person's own data for that reason. The Court of Justice held in case C-487/21 (4 May 2023) that the copy must be a faithful and intelligible reproduction of the data, which can mean extracts of documents or whole documents where that is needed to understand them.
