You answer short Y/N questions from the product page, and the formulas return the rule set (new wine rules, old stock, other alcohol or normal food rules), an ingredients and nutrition check, an allergens check, a strength and quantity check, a language and e-label check, a to-do list with Article cites and a one-word result. Short answer: since 8 December 2023, wine and aromatised wine (such as mulled wine and vermouth) must carry a list of ingredients and a nutrition declaration; the bottle may show only the energy value and give the rest by an e-label (QR code), but allergens such as sulphites must stay on the label with the word “contains” (Regulation (EU) No 1308/2013, Article 119(1)(h)–(i), (4)–(5), as amended by Regulation (EU) 2021/2117). A web shop selling prepacked drinks must make the mandatory food information available before the purchase is concluded, at no extra cost (Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011, Article 14(1)(a)). Wine produced and labelled before 8 December 2023 can be sold until stocks run out (Regulation (EU) 2021/2117, Article 5(8)).

Why accountants, bookkeepers and web agencies should look at this now

  • December is the wine shop's biggest month. Gift boxes, magnums and mulled wine go online in November, often copied from a supplier feed that carries the price and photo but not the ingredients.
  • The old-stock rule is narrow. Only wine produced and labelled before 8 December 2023 may sell through without the new particulars (Article 5(8) of Regulation (EU) 2021/2117). Every bottle labelled since then needs them.
  • The page counts, not just the bottle. Article 14(1)(a) is about the material supporting the distance sale. A QR code printed on the bottle is no help to a buyer who has not received it yet.
  • It pairs with our online food shop check sheet (Article 14), which covers food in general.

Limits and our reading

  • Our reading, flagged: Article 14(1)(a) covers “mandatory food information”, which Article 2(2)(c) defines as particulars required by Union provisions; we read that as including the wine particulars added to Article 119 of Regulation (EU) No 1308/2013. A clearly named free link to the producer's e-label before the buy button is treated as “other appropriate means clearly identified”. We apply the e-label rules (no sales content, no tracking) to the e-label page the shop links to, because the rules attach to the electronic means identified on the label.
  • Not read today: the consolidated texts, Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2019/33 and later delegated acts on wine labelling and ingredient names, any Commission guidance or Q&A, Annex V exemptions in detail, national rules (language, excise, age checks for alcohol sales) and penalties. The other compulsory wine particulars in Article 119(1)(a)–(g) (category, PDO/PGI, provenance, bottler, importer, sugar content of sparkling wine) are not checked by this sheet.
  • The scraper gives dated product data only. It does not decide whether a page complies.

FAQ

Must an online wine shop show the ingredients list and nutrition declaration before purchase?

For wine produced and labelled from 8 December 2023, the list of ingredients and the nutrition declaration became compulsory particulars (Article 119(1)(h) and (i) of Regulation (EU) No 1308/2013, as amended by Regulation (EU) 2021/2117). Article 14(1)(a) of Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011 says mandatory food information for prepacked food sold at a distance must be available before the purchase is concluded, on the page or by other clearly identified means, at no extra cost. Our reading: a clearly identified free link to the e-label before the buy button meets that; nothing on the page does not.

What about wine bottled before 8 December 2023?

Wine that met the old labelling rules and was produced and labelled before 8 December 2023 may continue to be placed on the market until stocks are exhausted (Article 5(8) of Regulation (EU) 2021/2117). The sheet marks such rows OK (OLD STOCK) for ingredients and nutrition. Allergens and the other particulars still apply. The vintage year alone does not tell you the labelling date; ask the producer.

Does this apply to mulled wine (Glühwein) and vermouth?

Yes. Aromatised wine products got the same nutrition declaration and ingredients list duty in a new Article 6a of Regulation (EU) No 251/2014, applying from 8 December 2023, with the same old-stock rule.

Do spirits, beer and cider need an ingredients list online?

Under Article 16(4) of Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011 the ingredients list and the nutrition declaration are not mandatory for drinks over 1.2% alcohol by volume, unless other Union rules require them (as they now do for wine and aromatised wine). Allergens (Article 9(1)(c)), the alcoholic strength (Article 9(1)(k)) and the net quantity still apply and must be available before purchase online (Article 14(1)(a)).

Can the e-label page show offers?

No. Where the full nutrition declaration or the ingredients list is given by electronic means, it must not be displayed with other information intended for sales or marketing purposes and no user data may be collected or tracked (Article 119(4) and (5) of Regulation (EU) No 1308/2013; Article 6a(2) and (3) of Regulation (EU) No 251/2014). Allergens must still appear on the label itself with the word 'contains'.
