GST returns barred after three years: a sheet for CA firms to catch every unfiled GSTR-1, GSTR-3B and GSTR-9 before the portal locks it (next: July–September 2023 GSTR-3BQ this week)

Updated 21 Oct 2026 · Disclosure: the white-label calendar linked below is an Unadhi product (₹999/year).

Short answer (checked 21 Oct 2026): a GST return cannot be filed after three years from its due date (sections 37(5), 39(11), 44(2) and 52(15) of the CGST Act, inserted by the Finance Act, 2023 and in force from 1 October 2023). It covers GSTR-1, IFF, GSTR-1A, GSTR-1Q, GSTR-3B, GSTR-3BQ, GSTR-4, GSTR-5, GSTR-6, GSTR-7, GSTR-8 and GSTR-9/9C. Once a period is barred, the GST portal locks the tile; the only route left is an application for unbarring to the tax officer, and if it is approved you get 30 days to file. For your clients this means: the quarterly GSTR-3BQ for July–September 2023 (due 22 or 24 October 2023) reaches three years this week, on Thursday 22 or Saturday 24 October 2026, and the October 2023 monthly returns follow in November. The FY 2022-23 GSTR-9/9C (due 31 December 2023) reaches three years on 31 December 2026. This is a starting point, not legal advice. Check the GST portal before you tell a client a date.

What you get from this guide: a finished three-year bar sheet for your firm: one row per client GSTIN per unfiled return, with the due date, the last day before the bar, days left and a flag that says File this month, Plan or Barred: unbarring application, all by Google Sheets formula. It comes with the list of periods that reach three years between now and January 2027, the unbarring steps from the GST portal's own FAQ, and three client messages. Nothing here needs software.

The portal may lock a few days later, but do not count on it. When GSTN switched the lock on, it barred returns in a batch: its advisory of 29 October 2025 listed October 2022 monthly returns, July–September 2022 quarterly returns, FY 2021-22 GSTR-4 and FY 2020-21 GSTR-9/9C as barred with effect from 1 December 2025. The law counts from the due date, not from the portal lock. Treat the "three years end on" column as the deadline and aim to file a week earlier.

Who is caught: GSTR-9 was optional for FY 2022-23 if aggregate turnover was up to ₹2 crore, and GSTR-9C was needed only above ₹5 crore (as reported for Notification 32/2023 – Central Tax; check the official source). Nil returns count too: a client with no business still has a GSTR-3B to file for every period while registered.

If a return is already barred: the unbarring steps

From the GST portal's FAQ on barring and unbarring of returns (fetched 21 Oct 2026):

  1. Open Services > Returns > Application for Unbarring Returns (or Quick Links > Application for Unbarring Returns).
  2. Explain the reason for the delay to the concerned tax officer. Keep the reason factual and keep proof (illness, records with a former accountant, portal error tickets).
  3. You get an email and SMS when the officer approves or rejects, and the order shows on the taxpayer's dashboard.
  4. If approved, all the barred returns must be filed within 30 days from the date of approval. Anything not filed in those 30 days is barred again.
  5. If rejected, you can reapply only twice with the same ARN. Only one request can be open at a time, and you cannot raise a new one after approval until all unbarred returns are filed.

Before you apply, have every barred return ready to file, because the 30 days start on approval. Start with the oldest period: the portal usually expects returns in period order. Being unable to file a return does not by itself settle the tax, interest or any notice for that period; talk to the client about that separately and check the official source.

Three client messages

After 24 October 2026 the GST portal will not accept it, three years after its due date. Please send the sales and purchase details for that quarter today so we can file it this week."

2. FY 2022-23 annual return: "Your GST annual return (GSTR-9) for 2022-23 must be filed by 31 December 2026. After that the portal will not take it. We need your books for 2022-23 by 30 November to reconcile and file in time."
