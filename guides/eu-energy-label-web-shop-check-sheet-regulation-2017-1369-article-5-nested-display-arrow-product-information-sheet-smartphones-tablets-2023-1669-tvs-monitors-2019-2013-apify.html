Short answer: under Article 5(1)(a) of the EU Energy Labelling Regulation (EU) 2017/1369, a dealer must display the energy label "in a visible manner, including for online distance selling", and must make the product information sheet available (Article 5(1)(b)). The product rules say how. For smartphones and tablets (Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/1669, applies from 20 June 2025) and for TVs and monitors (Delegated Regulation (EU) 2019/2013), Annex VIII requires the label "on the display mechanism in proximity to the price". A shop may instead show a coloured arrow with the class letter and the range of classes next to the price, which opens the full label on the first click, roll-over or tap. The product information sheet must also be near the price, or behind a link that reads "Product information sheet".

Why accountants, bookkeepers and web agencies should look at this now

  • Smartphones and tablets are new. Their label has applied since 20 June 2025. Many small phone, refurbished-device and accessories shops have not added it to product pages yet.
  • The arrow rules are exact. A grey badge saying "Energy class B" is not the arrow. Annex VIII asks for the arrow in the colour of the class, the letter in white in a font size equivalent to that of the price, the range of classes in black, and the label opening on the first click.
  • The shop is the dealer. The duty to show the label and the sheet online is on the dealer (Article 5 of Regulation 2017/1369 and Article 4 of each product regulation). If the shop has no label, it must ask the supplier, who must send it within five working days (Article 3(2)), or download it from the EU product database where that is possible (Article 5(3)).
  • It is a quick, billable check. About 10 to 15 minutes per shop for ten product pages, with one fix list you can send to the client or their web designer.

Step 3: tab 2, labelled product groups

The European Commission's product list (read 29 Oct 2026) marks these groups with "Energy Label". Products of these groups need a label; for the rest, write No label in G.

  • Cooking: domestic ovens · range hoods
  • Cleaning and drying: dishwashers · washing machines · washer dryers · tumble dryers
  • Refrigeration: fridges and freezers · commercial refrigerators · professional refrigerated storage cabinets
  • Heating, cooling and ventilation: air conditioners and comfort fans · local space heaters · ventilation units · solid fuel boilers · space heaters · water heaters
  • Electronic products: electronic displays (TVs, monitors) · smartphones and tablets
  • Light sources · tyres (tyres follow their own labelling regulation, which this sheet does not cover)

On the same list, vacuum cleaners, hobs, computers and external power supplies are marked "Ecodesign" only, with no energy label. Scope limits apply within groups. For example, the display act does not cover screens of 100 cm² or less, projectors or digital photo frames (Article 1(2) of Regulation 2019/2013), and the smartphone act does not cover rollable-screen devices or smartphones for high security communication (Article 1 of Regulation 2023/1669). A smartphone has a 4.0 to under 7.0 inch screen and a slate tablet 7.0 to under 17.4 inches (Article 2).

Step 4: tab 3, page check list (nested display)

Where the shop uses an arrow instead of the full label, Annex VIII point 3 sets this order. Tick each line on one product page per template:

  • The arrow is shown on the product page "in proximity to the price" (point 3(a)).
  • Its colour matches the class on the label, the letter is in 100% white, Calibri Bold, in a font size equivalent to that of the price, and the range of classes is in 100% black, with a black border (point 2).
  • The arrow links to the full label (point 3(b)), which opens after a mouse click, mouse roll-over or tap (point 3(c)), "on the first" one (point 1).
  • The label opens by pop-up, new tab, new page or inset screen (point 3(d)), and closes with a close option or other standard closing mechanism (point 3(f)).
  • On touch screens, the device's normal zoom works on the label (point 3(e)).
  • The alt text of the image is the class of the product, in a font size equivalent to that of the price (point 3(g)).
  • The label used is the one the supplier made available for this exact model, clearly visible and legible, and proportionate to the size in Annex III (point 1).
  • The product information sheet is near the price. If it sits behind a link or points to the EU product database, the link clearly reads "Product information sheet" (point 4).
  • Banners, newsletters and social ads for one specific model show the class and the range of classes (Annex VII, point 1 and point 4; Article 6(a) of Regulation 2017/1369).
  • No other label, mark or symbol on the page could mislead or confuse buyers about energy use (Article 6(c)).

What the law says, in short

  • Regulation (EU) 2017/1369, Article 2(13) and (15): a "dealer" is a retailer or other person who offers for sale, hire or hire purchase, or displays products to customers. "Distance selling" includes the internet and any other method where the customer "cannot be expected to see the product displayed".
  • Article 3(2): the supplier delivers printed labels and product information sheets to the dealer free of charge, "promptly and in any event within five working days" of the dealer's request.
  • Article 5: the dealer displays the label "in a visible manner, including for online distance selling", and makes the product information sheet available. A dealer without a label asks the supplier. A dealer without a sheet asks the supplier or prints or downloads one from the product database, where that function exists.
  • Article 6: visual ads and technical promotional material for a specific model refer to the class and the range of classes (6(a)). No other labels, marks or symbols that are likely to mislead or confuse customers (6(c)), and no labels that mimic the energy label on other products (6(d), (e)).
  • Article 7(4): penalties are set by each Member State and must be effective, proportionate and dissuasive. We did not read national penalty rules.
  • Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/1669 (smartphones and slate tablets): Article 4 dealers' duties, including "in the event of distance selling, the label and product information sheet are provided in accordance with Annexes VII and VIII". Applies from 20 June 2025 (Article 8). Recital 9 says marketplaces should let traders show the label and the product information sheet.
  • Delegated Regulation (EU) 2019/2013 (electronic displays): Article 4(c) to (e), same structure. Applies from 1 March 2021 (Article 11). Its Annex VIII text is the same in substance and also allows a right-pointing arrow (Figure 2).

Open points, not read: EUR-Lex lists Delegated Regulation (EU) 2021/340 as amending parts of the display act and a 2026 Commission proposal (52026PC0565) to amend Regulation 2017/1369. We read the display act as first published, not its consolidated text, and we did not read the 2026 proposal or the Court of Justice case files on Article 6(a) that EUR-Lex lists. EUR-Lex shows no amendment to Annex VIII of either product act. Check the current consolidated text before relying on a detail.

Frequently asked questions

Does a web shop have to show the energy label on the product page?

Yes, for products in a labelled group. Article 5(1)(a) of Regulation (EU) 2017/1369 requires the dealer to display the label in a visible manner, including for online distance selling. For TVs, monitors, smartphones and tablets, Annex VIII of each product act says the label goes near the price, or a coloured class arrow that opens the label on the first click.

Do smartphones and tablets need an energy label in web shops?

Yes, since 20 June 2025 (Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/1669, Article 8). The dealer must show the label and the product information sheet in line with Annexes VII and VIII (Article 4(b)). Rollable-screen devices and smartphones for high security communication are excluded.

What if the shop has no label file from the supplier?

Ask the supplier. Article 3(2) of Regulation 2017/1369 says the supplier must deliver labels and product information sheets free of charge within five working days of the dealer's request. A missing product information sheet can also be printed or downloaded from the EU product database where that function exists (Article 5(3)).
