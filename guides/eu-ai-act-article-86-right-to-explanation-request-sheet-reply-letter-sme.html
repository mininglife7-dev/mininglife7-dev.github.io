"Why did your AI decide this?" An EU AI Act Article 86 explanation request sheet and reply letter for small businesses

Updated 23 Oct 2026 · Disclosure: the EURO AI Register mentioned below is an Unadhi product with a free edition. This guide is for information only, not legal advice.

What you get: a finished explanation request sheet: a 12-column log, one row per request (decision, AI system, role of the AI output, main elements of the decision, person responsible, logs checked, GDPR route, reply due, outcome), a five-question check on whether Article 86 of the EU AI Act applies, and a reply letter you can fill in and send. Made for small businesses that use AI to screen job applicants, score credit, price insurance or assess learners, and for the accountants and advisers who help them.

The short answer

Article 86 of the EU AI Act lets a person affected by a decision you took on the basis of a high-risk AI system's output (Annex III, except critical infrastructure) ask you for clear and meaningful explanations of the role of the AI system and the main elements of the decision, where the decision has legal or similarly significant effects that they consider adverse to their health, safety or fundamental rights. It applies only where other Union law, such as GDPR, does not already provide that right. Under GDPR, decisions based solely on automated processing already carry rights today: meaningful information about the logic involved (Article 15(1)(h)) and human intervention, a chance to give their view and to contest (Article 22(3)). So the practical step is one log that handles both routes for every request.

Dates: Article 86 is in Chapter IX, covered by the general application date of 2 August 2026 (Article 113). The Annex III high-risk rules in Chapter III, Sections 1 to 3, apply from 2 December 2027 after the Digital Omnibus, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (Article 113(c)(i) as amended). The text does not say how those two dates interact for Article 86, so take advice on your case. From 2 December 2027, Article 26(11) also requires deployers of Annex III systems that make or help make decisions about people to tell those people that they are subject to the use of the system. A no to any of 1 to 4: Article 86 does not apply, but check the GDPR route in column J and consider explaining anyway; an unanswered person can complain to the market surveillance authority (Article 85) or, for personal data, to the data protection authority.

[Name of role] then [how the output was used, e.g. reviewed the 20 highest-ranked applications]. What you can do. If you think a fact is wrong or missing, reply to this letter by [date] and a member of staff who was not involved will review the decision.
