Short answer: before a consumer is bound by an online order, Article 6(1)(e) requires the total price and all freight, delivery or postal charges (or a clear statement that such charges may be payable). If those charges were not disclosed, Article 6(6) says the consumer shall not bear them. Article 6(1)(g) requires the arrangements for delivery (including environmentally friendly options where available, from 27 September 2026), performance, and the time by which the trader undertakes to deliver. Article 18 then sets a default: unless otherwise agreed, deliver without undue delay and not later than 30 days from the contract. The burden of proof for pre-contract information is on the trader (Article 6(9)).

Why accountants, bookkeepers and web agencies should look at this now

  • Undisclosed shipping is a money rule, not a soft tip. Article 6(6) is blunt: miss the Article 6(1)(e) charges information and the consumer does not bear those charges.
  • Green delivery wording is live. Directive (EU) 2024/825 replaced Article 6(1)(g) so that delivery arrangements include "environmentally friendly delivery options where available", and Member States apply those measures from 27 September 2026. Many Shopify and Woo themes still hide the option behind a carrier picker the customer never sees before the order button.
  • "Shipping calculated at checkout" is a common fail. If the consumer cannot see the amount (or that extra charges may be payable) before being bound, column P grades FIX under Article 6(1)(e) and 6(6).
  • It is a quick, billable check across a catalogue. Run the product scraper on a sample of product URLs, open cart and checkout once per shop, answer the columns, and the sheet writes the fix list with Article cites.

Step 3: the five-minute checklist per shop

  1. Open a typical product page. Are the main characteristics clear from the title and description? (Column K / Article 6(1)(a).)
  2. Add to cart and open the cart and the last checkout step before paying. Is the shipping or delivery charge shown as an amount, as free, or as a clear "may be payable" note? (G / Article 6(1)(e).) "Calculated later" with no amount and no "may be payable" note is a fail under Article 6(6).
  3. Is a delivery time or date stated (for example "2–3 working days" or a calendar date)? (H / Article 6(1)(g).)
  4. Are the arrangements for delivery described (carrier, method, pick-up point, click and collect)? (I.)
  5. If the shop offers a greener delivery option (bike courier, parcel locker, consolidated slow shipping), is it visible with the other options before the order? (J / Article 6(1)(g) as amended from 27 September 2026.)
  6. For goods, note any agreed day-count in M. If a customer order form lets buyers mark a date as essential, use N.
  7. Take dated screenshots of product, cart, checkout and footer (Article 6(9)).

Frequently asked questions

Must an EU web shop show shipping costs before the consumer clicks buy?

Article 6(1)(e) of Directive 2011/83/EU requires, before the consumer is bound by a distance or off-premises contract, 'the total price of the goods or services inclusive of taxes' and, where applicable, 'all additional freight, delivery or postal charges and any other costs' or, where those charges cannot reasonably be calculated in advance, 'the fact that such additional charges may be payable'. Article 6(6) adds that if the trader has not complied with those information requirements on additional charges, 'the consumer shall not bear those charges or costs'.

What if the shop never states a delivery time?

Article 6(1)(g) still requires the trader to give 'the time by which the trader undertakes to deliver the goods' before the consumer is bound. Separately, Article 18(1) says that unless the parties have agreed otherwise on the time of delivery, the trader shall deliver by transferring possession or control 'without undue delay, but not later than 30 days from the conclusion of the contract'.

Can the customer cancel if a delivery is late?

Under Article 18(2), where the trader misses the agreed time or the Article 18(1) limit, the consumer shall call upon the trader to deliver within an additional period appropriate to the circumstances; if that fails, the consumer may terminate. That extra-period step does not apply where the trader refused to deliver, where delivery within the agreed period is essential in the circumstances, or where the consumer informed the trader before the contract that delivery by or on a specified date is essential — then the consumer may terminate immediately (Article 18(2), second subparagraph). On termination the trader reimburses all sums paid without undue delay (Article 18(3)).

Do shops have to offer a green delivery option?

Article 6(1)(g), as replaced by Directive (EU) 2024/825 (applied from 27 September 2026), requires information on 'the arrangements for payment, delivery, including environmentally friendly delivery options where available'. Our reading of the words 'where available': if the shop offers such an option, it must be shown with the other delivery arrangements; the text we read does not create a duty to create a green option that does not exist. Check national transposition if you need a firm answer.
