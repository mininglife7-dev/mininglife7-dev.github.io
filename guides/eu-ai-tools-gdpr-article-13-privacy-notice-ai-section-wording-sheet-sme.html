The AI section of your privacy notice: a GDPR Article 13 and 14 wording sheet for small businesses

What you get: a finished AI section for your privacy notice: 10 fill-in lines per AI tool (what it does, what data goes in, purpose and legal basis, legitimate interest, supplier, transfers, retention, rights, whether people must use it, automated decisions), each tied to the GDPR article that requires it, plus an eight-line gap check you can tick before you publish. Made for small shops, agencies, clinics, trades and service businesses that added a chatbot, transcription tool or writing assistant.

The short answer

When you collect personal data, Article 13 GDPR requires you to tell people the purposes and legal basis, recipients, transfers outside the EU, retention, their rights and any automated decision-making covered by Article 22. If an AI tool processes that data, each of those facts applies to the tool, so the simplest fix is one short block per tool in your notice. Where the data did not come from the person, Article 14 adds the categories of data and the source. Article 12(1) requires all of it in clear and plain language and easy to reach.

Example, filled in: We use a website chatbot to answer questions about orders. It processes what you type to it. We do this to answer your enquiry, on the basis of our legitimate interest in answering quickly outside office hours. The chatbot is provided by a software supplier that processes it on our instructions. We keep chats for 90 days, then delete them. You do not have to use the chatbot; you can email us instead. The chatbot does not make decisions about you.

Do this week

  • List every AI tool that touches customer, client or staff data and write one block each
  • Read each supplier's terms for location, retention and use of your inputs, and fill lines 5 to 7 from them
  • Link the notice from the chatbot window, booking form or call script that collects the data
  • If you plan a new purpose for data you already hold, update the notice before you start (Article 13(3))
  • Pair the chatbot block with the AI Act chatbot disclosure, which is a separate duty
