AI clauses for your supplier contract: a fill-in clause sheet for small EU businesses buying AI tools (EU AI Act and GDPR)

Updated 20 Oct 2026 · Disclosure: the EURO AI Register mentioned below is an Unadhi product with a free edition. This guide is for information only, not legal advice.

What you get: a finished AI supplier clause sheet: twelve fill-in clauses you can paste into an email, an order form or a contract annex when you buy or renew an AI tool, each with the EU AI Act or GDPR article it supports and the kind of tool it is for. Plus a contract check sheet to record what each supplier agreed, and a short list of what to do this week. Built from the text of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 as consolidated after the Digital Omnibus on the Commission's AI Act Service Desk, Article 28 GDPR, and the Commission-hosted EU model contractual AI clauses.

The short version

  • No general AI Act rule says a small business must sign an AI contract. The written-agreement duty in Article 25(4) is between the provider of a high-risk AI system and the companies that supply models, tools or components for it.
  • But your own duties depend on the supplier. As a deployer of a high-risk system you must use it according to its instructions (Art. 26(1)), keep the logs under your control for at least six months (Art. 26(6)) and report risks and serious incidents (Art. 26(5)). You cannot do any of that without documents, log access and notices from the supplier.
  • If the tool sees personal data, GDPR already requires terms. Article 28(3) GDPR lists what a processor contract must contain.
  • Start with your questions, then lock the answers in. Ask the AI vendor questions first; this sheet turns the answers into commitments.

Most AI tools for small businesses are sold on standard online terms, and a supplier may refuse to change them. That is still useful: a written "no" to clause 3, 6 or 11 is a fact to record in your AI register, and it tells you whether to keep the tool for that use.

What the official model clauses are, and are not

The Procurement of AI community of practice on the European Commission's Public Buyers Community published updated EU model contractual AI clauses (MCC-AI) on 5 March 2025, in all EU languages: a full version for high-risk AI aligned with the AI Act, a light version for other AI, and a commentary. The commentary says they are written for public bodies, contain only AI-specific provisions, and "do not constitute a full contractual agreement": there are no terms on intellectual property, payment, delivery, applicable law or liability, so they are meant to be annexed to a contract that already covers those points. A private business can borrow from them clause by clause; the twelve rows above are a shorter, plain-language starting point in the same spirit.

Article 25(4) of the AI Act, as amended, also lets the AI Office develop and recommend voluntary model terms for contracts between providers of high-risk AI systems and their suppliers, published free of charge. Check the official source for whether they have been published before you rely on this sheet alone.

Do this week

  • List every AI tool you pay for or use for free, and who supplies it (SME AI inventory template)
  • Mark which tools touch personal data, talk to customers, generate content, or help with decisions about people
  • Check your role for each tool, especially if you resell or rebrand one (provider or deployer role check)
  • Send the vendor questions plus the clause rows that fit, and diarise the renewal date
  • Record every answer, including refusals, in the contract check sheet and keep the emails
  • Recheck tools that may be high-risk well before 2 December 2027 (Annex III check)
