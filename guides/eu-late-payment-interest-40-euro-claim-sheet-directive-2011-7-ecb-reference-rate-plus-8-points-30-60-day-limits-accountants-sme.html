Short answer: under the Late Payment Directive (Directive 2011/7/EU), a business that is paid late by another business or by a public authority is entitled, without sending a reminder, to statutory interest of the reference rate plus at least eight percentage points and to a fixed sum of at least EUR 40 for its recovery costs. In the euro area the reference rate is the ECB main refinancing rate in force on 1 January (for January to June) or 1 July (for July to December). For July to December 2026 that is 2.40%, so the minimum statutory rate is 10.40% a year (simple interest). Interest is charged on the amount due including VAT.

Why accountants and bookkeepers should look at this now

  • Year-end debtor reviews. Many clients close their books on 31 December. A list of late invoices with the interest and EUR 40 next to each one turns a vague "chase the debtors" into a number the client can decide on.
  • The rate changes on 1 January. The ECB main refinancing rate was raised to 2.65% from 16 September 2026, after the 1 July date. If it is still 2.65% on 1 January 2027, the minimum statutory rate for January to June 2027 becomes 10.65%. Add that row to the rates tab once the 1 January rate is known.
  • Clients often do not know the rules. No reminder is needed before interest starts (Article 3(1) and 4(1)), the EUR 40 is due as soon as interest becomes payable (Article 6(1) and (2)), and a contract term that excludes interest is grossly unfair (Article 7(2)).
  • A proposal to replace the directive is still open. EUR-Lex lists the Commission's proposal for a regulation on combating late payment (COM(2023) 533, procedure 2023/0323/COD) as "Ongoing" today. Until something new is adopted and applies, the directive as transposed in each country is the rule.

Check formula for Q2:

=IF(H2="","",TRIM(IF(AND(C2="Business",G2<>"",G2-D2>60),"CHECK: CONTRACT TERM OVER 60 DAYS ","")&IF(AND(C2="Public authority",G2<>"",G2-MAX(E2,F2)>30),"CHECK: PUBLIC BUYER TERM OVER 30 DAYS ","")&IF(E2="","INVOICE RECEIPT DATE UNCERTAIN: DUE DATE FROM GOODS OR SERVICES ","")&IF(AND(I2="",M2>0),"STILL UNPAID: INTEREST RUNS TO TODAY","")))

Counting the payment day as a late day, using 365 days, measuring the contract term from the invoice date and the flag wording are our working rules, not the directive's. The directive says interest runs "from the day following" the due date and is simple interest; national law may set the day count, how the period ends and how the contract term is measured. A "CHECK" flag does not make a term invalid: a term over 60 days between businesses is allowed if expressly agreed and not grossly unfair (Article 3(5)), and a public buyer may agree up to 60 days if objectively justified (Article 4(6)). Your country may also allow 60 days for public undertakings and health bodies (Article 4(4)). Consumers are outside the directive.

National law

The Late Payment Directive had to be transposed by 16 March 2013 (Article 12(1)), so each country applies it through its own law, which may be more favourable to the creditor (Article 12(3)) and may exclude debts in insolvency proceedings (Article 1(3)). Rates, day counts, recovery cost rules and court procedures differ. Check your national law and the statutory rate your country publishes. For unchallenged debts the directive requires that an enforceable title can normally be obtained within 90 calendar days of lodging the claim (Article 10(1)). Frequently asked questions

What is the EU statutory late payment interest rate for the second half of 2026?

In the euro area, at least 10.40% a year: the ECB main refinancing rate in force on 1 July 2026 (2.40%) plus eight percentage points. Your country may publish a higher rate, and countries outside the euro area use their national central bank rate.

Do I have to send a reminder before charging interest?

No. The directive says the creditor is entitled to interest for late payment without the necessity of a reminder, if it has fulfilled its obligations and has not received the amount due on time, unless the debtor is not responsible for the delay (Articles 3(1) and 4(1)).

Is interest charged on the amount including VAT?

Yes. The "amount due" includes the applicable taxes, duties, levies or charges specified in the invoice (Article 2(8)).
