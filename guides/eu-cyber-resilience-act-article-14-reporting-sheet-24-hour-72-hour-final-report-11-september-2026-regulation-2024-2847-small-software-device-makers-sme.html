Cyber Resilience Act reporting sheet: the 24-hour, 72-hour and final-report clocks that apply since 11 September 2026 (Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 Article 14) for accountants and their small software and device clients

Updated 28 Oct 2026 · Disclosure: the EURO AI Register mentioned below is an Unadhi product with a free edition. This guide is for information only, not legal advice.

What you get: a finished Cyber Resilience Act reporting sheet in three tabs: a product scope tab (one row per product) with a formula that says whether the Article 14 reporting duty applies now; an event clock tab that turns "when did we become aware" into the 24-hour, 72-hour and final-report due dates and flags anything late; and a notification and user-notice template with the fields Article 14 asks for. Four made-up rows show how it fills in.

Short answer: Regulation (EU) 2024/2847, the Cyber Resilience Act (CRA), mostly applies from 11 December 2027, but its Article 14 reporting duty has applied since 11 September 2026 (Article 71(2)). It covers every manufacturer of a product with digital elements that is in scope, including products placed on the EU market before 11 December 2027 (Article 69(3)). If such a manufacturer becomes aware of an actively exploited vulnerability or a severe incident affecting the security of its product, it must send an early warning within 24 hours, a notification within 72 hours and a final report later, through ENISA's single reporting point (Article 16) to the CSIRT of its main establishment, and tell affected users (Article 14).

Why accountants and bookkeepers should look at this now

  • It is already live. A small client that sells an app, a connected device, firmware or a software component with a network connection may be a "manufacturer" today, even for products sold years ago.
  • Many clients will not know. The headline CRA date is December 2027, so most small firms think nothing applies yet. One line in their year-end letter can fix that.
  • The clock starts at awareness. The 24 and 72 hours run from when the manufacturer "becomes aware", which is why the sheet records a date and time, not just a date.
  • AI features count like any other software. A product with an AI feature is treated like any other product with digital elements for CRA reporting. A business that only uses AI tools bought from others is not a manufacturer of those tools.

The CSIRT that receives the notice may ask for an intermediate status report (Art. 14(6)). Vulnerabilities that are not actively exploited, near misses and threats can be reported voluntarily (Art. 15).

User notice template (Art. 14(8) requires telling impacted users, and where appropriate all users, where appropriate in a structured, machine-readable format). Wording is ours:

Subject: Security notice for [product, versions]
We found [an actively exploited vulnerability / a security incident] affecting [product, versions] on [date].
What it can do: [one plain sentence].
What we have done: [update or mitigation, with date].
What you should do now: [install version X / change setting Y / contact us].
Questions: [security contact address]. We will update this notice when [the fix is out / the investigation closes].

Step 4: where the notice goes

  • Through the single reporting point run by ENISA under Article 16, using the electronic end-point of the CSIRT designated as coordinator in the Member State of the main establishment (column J). It is shared with ENISA at the same time (Art. 14(1), (7) and Art. 16).
  • A manufacturer with no EU main establishment uses, in order: the Member State of its authorised representative for most of its products, then of the importer placing most of its products on the market, then of the distributor making most of them available, then where most of its users are (Art. 14(7)).

Fines and the small-firm exception

Breaching Article 14 can lead to fines of up to EUR 15 million or 2.5% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher (Art. 64(2)), and authorities must take account of the size of micro, small and medium enterprises (Art. 64(5)(c)). Article 64(10)(a) says fines do not apply to microenterprises or small enterprises for failing to meet the 24-hour early-warning deadline. That exception does not cover the 72-hour notification, the final report or telling users. Member States set the penalty rules (Art. 64(1)), so check national law before advising.

Paragraph for the client letter

"You sell [product], which connects to a network and carries your name. Since 11 September 2026 the EU Cyber Resilience Act requires you to report any actively exploited vulnerability or severe security incident affecting it: early warning within 24 hours, notification within 72 hours, then a final report, and to tell your users. This applies to products you sold before that date too. Please name one person and a deputy who will file reports, and keep the attached event sheet. The wider CRA product requirements apply from 11 December 2027; we will review those with you next year."
