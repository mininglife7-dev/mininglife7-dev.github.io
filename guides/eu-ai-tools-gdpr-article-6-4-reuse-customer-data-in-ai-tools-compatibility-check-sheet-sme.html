Reusing customer emails, chats and call notes in AI tools: a GDPR Article 6(4) compatibility check sheet for small businesses

Updated 27 Oct 2026 · Disclosure: the EURO AI Register mentioned below is an Unadhi product with a free edition. This guide is for information only, not legal advice.

What you get: a finished Article 6(4) reuse check sheet in three tabs: a reuse log with one row for each time you want to put existing customer data into an AI tool for a new purpose, a five-factor tab with the five questions Article 6(4) GDPR lists and a formula that sorts each row into "stop", "get consent or rework", "review" or "likely compatible: tell people first", and a before-you-start tab that records the notice you must give before the new use, plus four made-up example rows.

Short answer: personal data must be "collected for specified, explicit and legitimate purposes and not further processed in a manner that is incompatible with those purposes" (Article 5(1)(b) GDPR). When a new use is not based on consent or on a law, Article 6(4) says you "shall" take into account, among other things: the link between the purposes, the context of collection and your relationship with the people, the nature of the data (in particular Article 9 and 10 data), the possible consequences for them, and safeguards such as encryption or pseudonymisation. And before the new use starts you must tell people about the other purpose (Articles 13(3) and 14(4)).

Why AI tools make this come up

A small business already holds years of support emails, chat logs, call notes and order history. New AI tools make it tempting to reuse them: paste old tickets into a chatbot's knowledge base, feed past sales emails to an AI writing tool, or let a vendor use your customer chats to improve its model. Each of these is a use for a purpose other than the one the data was collected for. The EDPB names Article 6(4) as a provision that "may be relevant in the context of the development and deployment of AI models" (Opinion 28/2024, para. 17), and says that the different stages of developing or deploying a model "may constitute the same or different processing activities" (para. 64).

For column J, write Yes or No; for H, I, K and L, write Low, Medium or High. A note from the EDPB on expectations: mentioning a use in your privacy policy "does not necessarily mean that the data subjects can reasonably expect it to happen" (Opinion 28/2024, para. 92), so a line in the notice does not turn a High in column I into a Low.

This sheet is a working aid, not a legal opinion or proof of compliance. References are to Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (Articles 5(1)(b), 6(4), 13(3), 14(4) and Recital 50, as published on gdpr-info.eu) and EDPB Opinion 28/2024 on certain data protection aspects related to the processing of personal data in the context of AI models, adopted 17 December 2024 (paras. 17, 64, 92 to 94). The EDPB opinion leaves the application of Article 6(4) to AI models for supervisory authorities to assess case by case. The questions, Low/Medium/High scale, result formula and thresholds are our own working rules. Article 6(4) also does not apply where the further use is based on a law that meets Article 23(1); check your data protection authority's guidance. Checked on 27 October 2026.
