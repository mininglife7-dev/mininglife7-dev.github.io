Short answer (checked 22 Oct 2026): AY 2026-27 is still governed by the Income-tax Act, 1961 (department's FAQs on interplay and transition, Q3.7–3.8). A client who missed the section 139(1) due date can file a belated return up to Thursday 31 December 2026, or before the assessment is completed if that is earlier, with a section 234F fee of ₹5,000, or ₹1,000 where total income does not exceed ₹5 lakh. A client who filed and found an omission or wrong statement can file a revised return: the Finance Act, 2026 substituted section 139(5) so revision is allowed up to the end of the assessment year (31 March 2027) or completion of assessment, whichever is earlier, subject to the new section 234-I fee (₹1,000 / ₹5,000 on the same ₹5 lakh test), which the department's return of income page applies to revised returns furnished more than nine months after the end of the financial year: for FY 2025-26, from 1 January 2027. So 31 December 2026 is the last day for every belated return and the last fee-free day for revisions. This is a starting point, not tax advice.

Only a return filed under section 139(1) or a belated return under section 139(4) can be revised; a return filed in response to a section 142(1) notice cannot be revised under section 139(5) (return of income page). An updated return cannot be a return of a loss (except where it reduces a loss), cannot reduce tax, and cannot create or increase a refund. That last rule is why a missed deduction or missed TDS credit has to be fixed through a revised return by 31 March 2027: after that, there is no route to claim it.

What a late return loses (put this in front of the client)

  • Losses: a loss other than a house property loss cannot be carried forward from a belated return (return of income page, consequences of delay).
  • Some deductions: deductions under Part C of Chapter VI-A are not available in a belated return (same page).
  • The old regime: a client without business income can choose the old regime only in a return filed by the section 139(1) due date; the AY 2026-27 ITR-1 validation rules block old-regime selection after that date and in a revised return whose original was belated (CBDT e-filing validation rules, AY 2026-27, rules 151, 189 and 190). A client with business income needed Form 10-IEA by the due date (Form 10-IEA FAQ, Q7 and Q15).
  • Money: the section 234F fee, and section 234A interest on any unpaid tax.

Where to find the rows

  • Your own AY 2026-27 filing tracker: every client marked "documents pending", "on hold" or "not responding" after their due date.
  • Clients who filed but whose AIS/TIS or Form 26AS now shows income, TDS or high-value transactions that are not in the return. Compare before you revise, so you fix everything in one revised return.
  • Clients who asked "why is my refund less?" or received a processing intimation that does not match the return.
  • Family members and new clients who say "I never filed last year".

Notes

  • Every route ends earlier if the assessment is completed first. Check the e-filing portal for pending proceedings before you promise a client a date.
  • From tax year 2026-27, returns move to section 263 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (belated within 9 months and revised within 12 months from the end of the tax year, per the transition FAQs, Q3.13). This sheet is only for AY 2026-27.
  • Section 234-I was inserted by section 16 of the Finance Act, 2026; read its text alongside the department's page before you quote a fee to a client.
  • The sheet holds client income data: share it only inside your firm.
