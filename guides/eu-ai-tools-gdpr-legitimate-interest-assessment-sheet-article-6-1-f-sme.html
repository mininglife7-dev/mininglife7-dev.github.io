Using customer data in AI tools on 'legitimate interests': a three-step GDPR assessment sheet for small EU businesses (Article 6(1)(f))

Updated 22 Oct 2026 · Disclosure: the EURO AI Register mentioned below is an Unadhi product with a free edition. This guide is for information only, not legal advice.

What you get: a finished assessment sheet to fill in once per AI use, with ten rows: the AI use, the data, the three EDPB steps (interest, necessity, balancing with reasonable expectations), extra safeguards, the decision, the privacy-notice line to copy and a review date. Plus five common AI uses with a first view on each.

The short answer

You can use customer or staff data in an AI tool on the basis of legitimate interests only if three cumulative conditions are met: a legitimate interest, processing that is necessary for it, and no overriding interests or rights of the people concerned (GDPR Article 6(1)(f)). The EDPB says to assess and document this before you start, involve your DPO if you have one, and tell people which interest you rely on. If the balance goes against you and no extra safeguard fixes it, you need another legal basis or you should not do it.

The assessment sheet (one per AI use)

RowWhat to writeYour answer
AI use and tool[e.g. AI summaries and priority tags on support tickets in our helpdesk tool]. One sheet per use, not per tool.[fill in]
Whose data and which data[customers / prospects / staff]; [names, e-mails, ticket text]. Note anything sensitive or about children.[fill in]
Step 1: the interestWrite it in one precise sentence. The EDPB test: lawful, clearly and precisely articulated, real and present (not speculative).[fill in]
Step 2: necessityCould you reach the same aim just as effectively with less data or a less intrusive method? Which fields can you drop or mask before they reach the tool? If a less intrusive way works, Article 6(1)(f) cannot be used for this processing.[fill in]
Step 3a: impactNature of the data, context, possible consequences for the person (wrong scores, exclusion, data reaching the supplier).[fill in]
Step 3b: reasonable expectationsExisting customer or not, where and how data was collected, the person's position. 'Common in our sector' is not the test.[fill in]
Step 3c: extra safeguardsOnly ones beyond GDPR duties, e.g. objection honoured without any balancing; outputs never used alone for decisions; shorter retention than needed. Then re-run 3a-3b.[fill in]
DecisionProceed / proceed with safeguards / stop or use another legal basis. Who decided, date, DPO involved if you have one.[fill in]
Privacy-notice lineThe interest in plain words plus the right to object (Articles 13(1)(d), 21(4)).[fill in]
Review dateWhen the tool, the data or the purpose changes, and at least once a year.[fill in]

Keep it short and honest. The EDPB says Article 6(1)(f) should not be chosen automatically because it looks less demanding than other legal bases, nor treated as a last resort. The duty is on you to show the balancing test was done properly, so a one-page sheet signed and dated beats a long template nobody reads.

Five common AI uses, a first view

AI useFirst view (still do the sheet)
AI triage and summaries of incoming support ticketsOften arguable: faster answers to existing customers. Keep sensitive details out, check the supplier's retention, let people ask for a human.
AI fraud or abuse checks on orders or sign-upsRecital 47 names fraud prevention as a legitimate interest, limited to what is strictly necessary. Note the logic and keep a human review path.
AI lead scoring of existing customers for offersPossible, but direct marketing objections are absolute (Article 21(2)-(3)); offer an easy opt-out in every message.
Uploading your whole customer list to an AI tool to 'find insights'Weak: the interest is vague and the data is not limited. Narrow the purpose and the fields first, or do not do it.
AI analysis of staff e-mails or productivityHigh risk: the employer-employee imbalance weighs heavily. Get legal advice and check national employment rules and the EU AI Act duties for AI at work.

Copy this privacy-notice line

We use [tool] to [purpose, e.g. summarise and prioritise your support requests] because we have a legitimate interest in [answering requests faster]. We only send [data] to it, and [supplier] keeps it for [period]. You can object to this at any time by writing to [address]; for direct marketing we will always stop.

The right to object must be brought to people's attention clearly and separately from other information, at the latest at the first communication (Article 21(4)).
