Can you prove consent for AI call recording and other AI features? A GDPR Article 7 consent record sheet for small businesses

Updated 24 Oct 2026 · Disclosure: the EURO AI Register mentioned below is an Unadhi product with a free edition. This guide is for information only, not legal advice.

What you get: a finished Article 7 consent record sheet: one row per AI purpose that relies on consent (for example recording and transcribing customer calls, using client photos in AI-edited marketing, AI chatbot follow-up messages), one column per consent check from Articles 7, 8 and 9, a result formula that counts failed checks, three made-up example rows, a per-person log layout and a withdrawal routine.

Short answer: if you rely on consent, Article 7(1) GDPR says you must be able to demonstrate it. That means keeping the exact wording you used, which version each person agreed to, when and how, and making withdrawal as easy as saying yes. A pre-ticked box or a line buried in your terms does not count.

Step 1: is consent the right basis for this AI use?

  • Consent is one of six lawful bases in Article 6(1). If the AI use is needed to deliver what the customer asked for, or you can pass a legitimate interest assessment, another basis may fit better. Do not ask for consent you would ignore if refused.
  • Typical consent cases for small businesses: recording calls for AI transcription, using a client's photo or voice in AI-made marketing, sending AI-personalised marketing messages, and using customer chats to train a tool.
  • Recording calls can also fall under national telecom or recording laws. Check your country's rules as well as the GDPR.

Put Y, Partial or N in columns C to K (Not relevant where it does not apply; the formula ignores it). Sort by column L so the rows to fix come first.

Step 3: the per-person log (second tab)

One row per person per purpose: person ID (customer number, not a copy of their file) · purpose (matches column A) · wording version (matches column B) · date and time · how given (ticked web form, said yes on recorded call at 00:12, signed form) · withdrawn on · data deleted or basis changed on. Keep only what you need to show consent; the EDPB says the record itself should not lead to excessive extra processing.

Withdrawal routine

  • Same channel: if consent was given on a call, a yes on a later call or a reply email withdraws it; if given by tick box, the untick must be just as easy (Article 7(3)).
  • Stop and log: stop the processing that relied on consent, note the date in the per-person log, and tell the AI supplier if it holds the data.
  • Delete or switch basis: where no other lawful basis applies, delete the data (Article 17(1)(b)). Use the erasure and objection request sheet to track it.
  • Earlier processing stays lawful: withdrawal does not undo processing done before it (Article 7(3)).
