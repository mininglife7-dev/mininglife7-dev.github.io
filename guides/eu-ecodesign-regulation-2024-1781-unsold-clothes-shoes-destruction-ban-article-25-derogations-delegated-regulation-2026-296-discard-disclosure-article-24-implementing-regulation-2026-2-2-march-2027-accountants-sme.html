What you get: a finished, tested unsold stock destruction and discard disclosure sheet for accountants, bookkeepers and tax advisers whose clients sell clothing, shoes or other consumer goods. One row per batch of unsold stock or returns that the client throws away or hands to a waste company. Formulas give the client's size class (micro, small, medium, large, with the two-period rule), whether the goods are on the EU ban list (apparel, clothing accessories, footwear), whether the treatment counts as destruction, whether one of the 10 derogations covers it, whether the client must publish a yearly disclosure, the CN category to use, the disclosure date and how long to keep the papers. Short answer: since 19 July 2026, large enterprises may not destroy unsold apparel, clothing accessories and footwear (Regulation (EU) 2024/1781, Article 25(1) and Annex VII), unless one of the derogations in Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/296 applies and the papers are kept. Medium-sized enterprises are covered from 19 July 2030; micro and small enterprises are exempt. Separately, large enterprises (medium-sized from 19 July 2030) that discard any unsold consumer products must publish each year the number and weight discarded, the reasons and the treatment (Article 24(1)). From 2 March 2027 the common format in Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2 applies, with a deadline of 12 months after the end of each financial year it covers (Articles 1 and 7).

What to tell a client in one minute

  • Check your size first. The rules use the EU SME definition: fewer than 250 staff and turnover up to EUR 50 million or balance sheet up to EUR 43 million is medium-sized at most; fewer than 50 staff and up to EUR 10 million is small (Recommendation 2003/361, Annex, Article 2).
  • Large companies: do not shred, burn, landfill or recycle unsold clothes, accessories or shoes. Recycling counts as destruction too. Handing goods over for preparing for reuse, refurbishment or remanufacturing does not (Article 2(34)).
  • Returns count. Goods sent back within the 14-day withdrawal period are "unsold consumer products" (Article 2(37)).
  • Exceptions need paper. Damaged, unsafe, counterfeit or defective goods may be destroyed, but only with the documents listed in Delegated Regulation 2026/296, Article 3, kept for 5 years.
  • Publish the numbers. Large companies must show each year, on an easily reachable web page, what they discarded, why and where it went. This covers all consumer products, not only clothes.
  • Medium-sized: you have until 19 July 2030, but in France national rules still apply to you until then.

Why AI tools matter here

Many shops now use AI tools to forecast stock, grade returns or decide which items are "unsellable". Those decisions feed straight into this sheet: if a returns-grading tool marks an item as damaged, the derogation in point (f) still needs an inspection record or a described quality procedure showing that repair is not technically feasible or cost-effective (Delegated Regulation 2026/296, Article 3(f)). What we tested

Twenty made-up rows in LibreOffice (xlsx → ods → csv), in two runs (today 10 Nov 2026, then 15 Jan 2030): a large client recycling unsold trousers (STOP: BANNED); damaged shoes with papers (DISCLOSE, and a reminder to give the waste operator a statement); T-shirts claimed as dangerous without papers (GET PAPERS FIRST); phones (no EU ban, still DISCLOSE, CN 8517 kept at 4 digits); knitwear handed over for preparing for reuse (not destruction, still DISCLOSE in category 61); donated stock (OK, not disclosed); a medium-sized client before 19 July 2030 (NOT YET) and after (STOP: BANNED); a small client (EXEMPT); a client whose size changed between years (CHECK SIZE) and the same client with the size entered (DISCLOSE under derogation H); toys from 2024, before the first full financial year (NO EU BAN); shoes discarded before 19 July 2026 (DISCLOSE); a non-consumer product (NOT IN SCOPE); unknown treatment (treated as destruction: STOP: BANNED); a missing financial year end (FIX FY END); and three bad inputs (FIX). 20 of 20 results as intended in both runs, the empty row stays blank, no errors. The 2028 phone row shows "DISCLOSE BY 2029-12-31 (IN 1147 DAYS)" on 10 Nov 2026 and "(OVERDUE)" on 15 Jan 2030. The Disclosure tab gave, for category 64 in 2026, 42 units, 34 kg, 71% disposal and 29% unknown.

Checklist before 2 March 2027

  • Fix each client's size class from the last two approved accounts, including partner and linked enterprises.
  • For large clients: list every batch of unsold stock and returns discarded since 19 July 2026 and check it in the sheet.
  • Stop any destruction of clothing, accessories or shoes that has no derogation and no papers.
  • Ask every waste company for statements on reception and treatment, so the percentages are not "unknown".
  • Publish the yearly disclosure on an easily accessible web page (or link to the sustainability report).
  • Set up the Annex I format now for the first financial year that starts on or after 2 March 2027.
  • List AI tools that grade returns or decide what is unsellable, and keep their output with the batch papers.

Limits and our reading

  • Not read, flagged: national penalty rules; national laws on unsold products other than the French provisions summarised in Decision 2026/1435; Commission guidance or FAQs; the combined nomenclature itself (Regulation (EEC) No 2658/87) beyond the descriptions in Annex VII and Annex II; the waste definitions in Directive 2008/98/EC; Commission proposals 52025PC0504 and 52026PC0590 that would amend Regulation 2024/1781 (not adopted when checked). The EU Publications Office lists two corrigenda to Regulation 2024/1781; we read both and neither changes Articles 23 to 26 or Annex VII.
  • Our reading: size is taken from the SME definition, and the sheet does not add partner and linked enterprises for you; when the two periods give different classes the sheet asks you to decide; "unknown" treatment is treated as destruction for the ban check; the medium-sized start date is applied to the discard date; "first full financial year after 2 March 2027" is read as a financial year starting on or after that date, and the sheet assumes 12-month financial years; financial years before the format applies get no EU deadline in the sheet because Article 24(1) itself only says "annual".

Questions

Is it now illegal to destroy unsold clothes in the EU?

For large enterprises, yes, since 19 July 2026: destroying unsold apparel, clothing accessories and footwear listed in Annex VII is prohibited (Regulation (EU) 2024/1781, Article 25(1)), unless a derogation in Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/296 applies. Medium-sized enterprises are covered from 19 July 2030; micro and small enterprises are exempt.

Does recycling unsold stock count as destruction?

Yes. Destruction means intentionally damaging or discarding a product as waste; only discarding to deliver the product for preparing for reuse, refurbishment or remanufacturing is excluded (Article 2(34)). Recycling, energy recovery and disposal all count.

Are customer returns covered?

Yes. Products returned under the right of withdrawal, or during a longer withdrawal period offered by the trader, are unsold consumer products (Article 2(37)).

What must a company disclose about discarded unsold products?

Each year: the number and weight discarded per product category, the reasons and any derogation, the share sent to preparing for reuse, recycling, other recovery and disposal, and the measures taken and planned (Article 24(1)). It applies to large enterprises, to medium-sized enterprises from 19 July 2030, and to all consumer products, not only clothes.

When does the common disclosure format apply?

Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2 applies from 2 March 2027. It covers each financial year from the first full financial year after that date, and the disclosure must be published within 12 months after the end of that financial year (Articles 1 and 7).

How long must the papers be kept?

Derogation papers for 5 years after the products are destroyed (Delegated Regulation 2026/296, Article 3), and delivery and treatment papers for 5 years after the disclosure (Implementing Regulation 2026/2, Article 4). Both must be provided within 30 days of a request.
