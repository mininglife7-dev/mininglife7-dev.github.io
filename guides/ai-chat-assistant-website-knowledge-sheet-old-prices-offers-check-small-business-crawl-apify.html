Website knowledge sheet for an AI chat assistant: check old prices, offers and short pages before your small business chatbot learns from your site

What you get: a website knowledge sheet for an AI chat assistant in one Google Sheet. It has one row per page of your website: the page address, title, word count and status, plus flags for pages that mention an old year, mention a price or are too short to help. You mark each page Yes or No, and a knowledge tab gathers the Yes pages as clean text blocks (title, source address, page text) that you upload to your chat tool. You check the old prices, hours and offers before the assistant repeats them to customers. Formulas are below. The example rows are made up.

The Diwali 2024 offer page is the usual trap: it is still on the site, the crawler finds it, and without this check the assistant can quote last year's discount as if it were current.

Who this is for

Small businesses (salons, clinics, coaching centres, shops, accountants, trades) that are adding an AI chat assistant to their website or messaging channel and want it to answer from their own pages, and the freelancers and small agencies who set these up. Most chat tools let you upload files as knowledge. What you upload is what the assistant repeats, so a sheet that shows every page and flags the risky ones is quicker than reading the site page by page.

Step 1: crawl the site into a tab named crawl

With the website crawler

Open the Website Content Crawler (Half Price) on the Apify Store and use this input with your site's address:

{ "startUrls": [{ "url": "https://your-site.example/" }],
  "maxPagesPerSite": 300, "maxDepth": 5, "useSitemap": true,
  "minWords": 0, "outputMarkdown": true }
  • outputMarkdown: keep it on. The markdown column is the page's main text with headings, lists and tables kept, and menus, footers and cookie banners removed. That is the text the assistant will learn from.
  • maxPagesPerSite: set it above the number of pages on the site (the default is 50).
  • minWords: set it to 0 so short pages such as Contact still appear; the sheet flags them instead of dropping them.
  • useSitemap adds pages listed in your sitemap, including old offer pages that are no longer linked from the menu. Those are the ones you most need to see.

The crawler follows robots.txt. Run it on your own site or a client's site that you look after.

In the run, export the dataset as CSV. In your Google Sheet use File → Import → Upload, "Insert new sheet", and name the tab crawl. Do not rename the headers.

What the columns contain

  • url, title, description, language, canonicalUrl, headings: the page address and its basic details.
  • wordCount: words in the main content.
  • markdown and text: the main content as Markdown and as plain text.
  • httpStatus, depth and fetchedAt: the server response, how many clicks from the start page, and when the page was read.
  • Pages whose main text exactly matches a page already delivered are skipped, so copies do not appear twice.

A Google Sheets cell holds at most 50,000 characters. A very long page (a big price list or terms page) can be cut short in the sheet; the sheet flags those so you can take their text from the JSON export instead.

Step 2: the settings tab

Add a tab named settings. In B1 type the pattern for years you treat as old: 20(1[0-9]|2[0-4]) matches 2010 to 2024. In B2 type 80 (fewer words than this counts as a short page). Next year, change 2[0-4] to 2[0-5].

Step 3: build the check tab

Add a tab named check. Row 1 holds the headers below; row 2 holds the formulas, which you copy down one row per row of crawl.

ColHeaderFormula (row 2)
APage=IFERROR(INDEX(crawl!$A2:$ZZ2, MATCH("url", crawl!$A$1:$ZZ$1, 0)), "")
BTitlesame pattern with "title"
CWordssame pattern with "wordCount"
DStatussame pattern with "httpStatus"
ETextsame pattern with "markdown" (hide this column once it works)
FOld year=IF(A2="", "", REGEXMATCH(E2, "\b(" & settings!$B$1 & ")\b"))
GPrice=IF(A2="", "", REGEXMATCH(E2, "(₹|Rs\.?|INR|€|£|\$)\s?\d"))
HIssues=IF(A2="", "", TEXTJOIN("; ", TRUE, IF(AND(D2<>"", N(D2)<>200), "Status " & D2, ""), IF(F2=TRUE, "Old year", ""), IF(G2=TRUE, "Has a price: check it", ""), IF(N(C2)<settings!$B$2, "Short page", ""), IF(LEN(E2)>=49000, "Text may be cut: use JSON export", "")))
IUse?you type Yes or No
JBlock=IF(I2="Yes", "## " & B2 & CHAR(10) & "Source: " & A2 & CHAR(10) & CHAR(10) & E2, "")

Notes on the formulas:

  • Old year catches "2024" anywhere in the text, including a copyright line or "since 2012". Read the page before marking it No; often only one sentence needs changing on the website.
  • Price only looks for a currency sign or code followed by a number. It is a prompt to check that the price on the page is today's price, not a judgement that the page is wrong.
  • Status other than 200 means the page did not load normally when it was read; check it in a browser.

Step 4: the knowledge tab and the upload

  1. Add a tab named knowledge and put =FILTER(check!J2:J, check!J2:J<>"") in A1. Each cell is one page: its title, its address and its text.
  2. Fix the website first where you can (old offers, old prices, old hours), then crawl again, so the site and the assistant say the same thing.
  3. Download the knowledge tab (File → Download → Comma-separated values, or Microsoft Excel) and upload it to your chat tool's knowledge or files section if it accepts that format. If it only takes documents, copy column A into a document and save it in a format the tool accepts. Check your tool's own help pages for file types and size limits.
  4. Add a short first line of your own, such as your opening hours, booking link and "if a price is not listed here, ask the customer to call", so the assistant has a safe answer when the pages do not cover a question.
  5. Re-run the crawl after any price or offer change and replace the upload. Note the date in the sheet.

If you serve customers in the EU

From 2 August 2026, Article 50 of the EU AI Act requires that people are told when they are talking to an AI system, unless it is obvious. Our chatbot disclosure checklist covers the wording.

Check before you act on it

  • The number of rows is close to the number of pages you expect. If it is much lower, raise maxPagesPerSite or maxDepth.
  • Open three Yes pages and compare the Text column with the live page: the main content should be there without menus or footers.
  • Ask the assistant five real customer questions (price, hours, location, a service you no longer offer, a refund) and check each answer against the site before you switch it on for customers.
