Running an AI chatbot or lead list together with another business? A GDPR Article 26 joint controller sheet for small businesses

Updated 26 Oct 2026 · Disclosure: the EURO AI Register mentioned below is an Unadhi product with a free edition. This guide is for information only, not legal advice.

What you get: a finished joint controller sheet in three tabs: a "who decides?" test with one row per shared activity and a result formula, an arrangement tab that names who does each GDPR duty (privacy notice, requests, breaches, security, records) with a status formula, and the "essence" text you show people, plus two made-up example rows.

Short answer: when you and another business together decide why and how personal data are used, for example a co-run AI chatbot, a shared lead list for a joint campaign or a franchise-wide CRM, Article 26 GDPR makes you joint controllers. You must agree in writing who handles which duty, make the essence of that agreement available to the people concerned, and accept that they can still go to either of you.

The formula is a first sort, not a legal conclusion. The EDPB's Guidelines 07/2020 explain that joint control can arise from a common decision or from converging decisions that complement each other, and that both parties do not need equal shares or access to all the data. If you are unsure, record the facts in columns A to F and ask.

Put the column letters in row 1 and the first duty in row 2; the formulas assume that layout. Article 26(1) says the arrangement must set out responsibilities "in particular" for people's rights and for the information in Articles 13 and 14, so make sure those two rows are never blank.

Step 3: tab 3, the essence for people

Article 26(2) says the essence of the arrangement must be made available to the people whose data you process. A short paragraph in each privacy notice is a practical way to do it. Example wording to adapt: "For [activity], [Business A] and [Business B] are joint controllers. [Business A] provides this notice and handles requests about your data at [contact]. You can also contact [Business B] at [contact]."

What people and courts can still do

  • Whatever you agree between yourselves, a person can exercise their rights against each of you (Art. 26(3)). Your contact point helps, but you cannot refuse a request because "the partner handles that".
  • Where more than one controller is responsible for damage, each can be held liable for the entire damage, then claim back the other's share (Art. 82(4) and (5)). Say in the contract how you share costs.
  • Joint control does not by itself give either party a lawful basis. Each activity still needs one (Art. 6).
