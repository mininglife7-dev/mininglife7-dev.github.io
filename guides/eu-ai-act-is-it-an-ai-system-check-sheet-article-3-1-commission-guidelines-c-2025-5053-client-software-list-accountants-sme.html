Short answer: the EU AI Act only applies to software that meets the definition of an "AI system" in Article 3(1) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. The key test is whether the system infers how to generate outputs (predictions, content, recommendations or decisions), using machine learning or logic- and knowledge-based techniques (Recital 12). Software that only follows rules written by people, basic data processing such as standard spreadsheets and sorting or filtering, descriptive statistics and charts, classical heuristics, and simple prediction such as "next month = the average" is outside the definition according to the Commission's guidelines (C(2025) 5053, paragraphs 40 to 51). The guidelines are not binding, give no automatic list and say each system must be assessed on its own features (paragraphs 6, 7 and 62).

Why clients ask their accountant this question

  • Everything is sold as "AI" now. Clients see the label on their ledger app, scanner, CRM and email tool and ask whether the AI Act now applies to them. The label is not the test; the way the feature works is.
  • The answer decides the rest of the AI Act. Prohibited practices (Article 5), high-risk rules (Article 6) and transparency duties (Article 50) only apply to AI systems. Even then, the Commission notes that the vast majority of AI systems will not be subject to any regulatory requirements under the AI Act (guidelines paragraph 63).
  • AI literacy applies to every AI system in use. Providers and deployers must take measures to support the development of AI literacy of staff who operate or use AI systems on their behalf (Article 4 as replaced by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, which also says no specific level has to be guaranteed for any individual). A business can only do that if it knows which tools are AI systems.
  • Using a tool at work makes the business a "deployer". Article 3(4): any person using an AI system under its authority, except in a personal non-professional activity.

The seven parts of the definition (Article 3(1))

"'AI system' means a machine-based system that is designed to operate with varying levels of autonomy and that may exhibit adaptiveness after deployment, and that, for explicit or implicit objectives, infers, from the input it receives, how to generate outputs such as predictions, content, recommendations, or decisions that can influence physical or virtual environments." The guidelines split this into seven elements (paragraph 9). For a client software list, three do the work:

ElementWhat it means in practiceSource
Infers how to generate outputsBuilt with machine learning (supervised, unsupervised, self-supervised, reinforcement, deep learning; language models are given as an example) or logic- and knowledge-based approaches that reason from encoded knowledge (expert systems, inference engines). This is the "key, indispensable condition".Recital 12; guidelines paras 26, 30-39
Some autonomyThe system produces an output without a person manually specifying it. A system that needs manual inputs but then produces the output itself still counts. Only systems with full manual human involvement are excluded.Recital 12; paras 17-20

Step 2: tab 2, questions for the software vendor (for every "ASK VENDOR" row)

  1. Does this feature use a machine learning model, including a large language model, trained on data? If so, whose model is it?
  2. If it is rule-based: were all the rules written by people, or does the system derive rules or conclusions itself from a knowledge base?
  3. What does it produce: predictions, generated text or images, recommendations, or decisions?
  4. Is any output applied automatically, without a person reviewing it?
  5. Does it change its behaviour while in use, for example by learning from our corrections?
  6. Do you see yourselves as the provider of an AI system under Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 for this feature? Where is that documented?

Paste the reply or link into column L and change D and E from ? to Y or N.

Checklist: what follows for each "AI SYSTEM" row

  • ☐ List it in the AI inventory with the business's role (usually deployer, Article 3(4)).
  • ☐ AI literacy: record the measures taken for the staff who use it (Article 4, as replaced by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744).
  • ☐ Check it is not used for a prohibited practice (Article 5: applies since 2 February 2025; two points added by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 apply from 2 December 2026).
  • ☐ Check whether the use falls in an Annex III area such as hiring or creditworthiness; high-risk rules for those systems apply from 2 December 2027 (Article 113, third paragraph, point (c), as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744).
  • ☐ Chatbots and generated content: check the transparency duties in Article 50 (the Regulation applies generally from 2 August 2026).
  • ☐ Re-check the row when the vendor adds a new feature. For "LIKELY NOT" rows, keep the reason in column K as your note of why the tool is outside the AI Act.

Limits and open points

The Commission's guidelines are not binding; only the Court of Justice of the European Union can give an authoritative interpretation (paragraph 7), and the Commission says no automatic determination or exhaustive list is possible (paragraph 62). The guidelines do not cover general-purpose AI models (paragraph 64). Article 3(1) itself was not changed by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, but several other articles were, so read the current text on EUR-Lex. This sheet covers the AI Act only: data protection, consumer and sector rules apply separately. When a row matters, for example hiring or credit decisions, get advice.

Frequently asked questions

Is an Excel spreadsheet an AI system under the AI Act?

A standard spreadsheet without AI-enabled features is given by the Commission as an example of basic data processing outside the definition (guidelines paragraph 46). AI features switched on inside a spreadsheet are a separate row to check.

Does a tool have to keep learning to be an AI system?

No. Article 3(1) says the system "may" exhibit adaptiveness after deployment; the Commission says this is not a decisive condition (paragraphs 22 and 23). The decisive condition is the capability to infer (paragraph 26).

Is a chatbot built on a language model an AI system?

The guidelines give language models that learn to predict the next token as an example of self-supervised machine learning, and content generated with GPT-type models as an AI output (paragraphs 36 and 56). Using one at work normally makes the business a deployer.
