AI images and videos in your marketing: a label sheet for small EU businesses (Article 50(4), 2026)

What you get: a finished AI content label sheet you can paste into Google Sheets or Excel: one row per image, video, voice clip or article you publish, with what the AI did, whether it could pass as real, the label you used, and who signed it off. Plus a 3-question test and ready-to-use label wording for posts, ads, video and audio. It is built for Article 50(4) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, which applies from 2 August 2026.

Does this post need a label? The 3-question test

The Commission's Article 50 FAQ explains that content is a deepfake (Article 3(60)) only when three things are all true. Ask them in order for every AI image, video or audio you are about to publish.

  1. Resemblance: does it closely resemble a person, object, place, entity or event?
  2. Existing or plausible: does that person, place or event exist, or could it plausibly exist or have existed?
  3. False appearance: would your audience, in this context, take it as authentic or truthful?

Three yeses mean a visible or audible label at the latest when people first see or hear it (Article 50(4) and 50(5)). If any answer is no, write the reason in the sheet so you can show your thinking later.

Typical small-business useLabel?Why
Photorealistic AI "customer" or "staff member" in an ad or testimonialYesResembles a plausible real person and is meant to look real. Also check consumer law: a made-up customer endorsing you can mislead buyers on its own.
AI-generated photo of your shop, product in use, or an event that never happenedYesLooks like a real place or event and would be taken as genuine
Cloned voice of a real person in a radio ad, reel or phone messageYesAudio resembling an existing person; get that person's written consent too
Product photo with background swapped or tidied by AIDependsFine-tuning or background clean-up may not create a false impression; if the setting implies something untrue (a store you don't have, a celebrity holding it), label it
Clearly cartoon, illustrated or stylised artworkUsually noUnlikely to "falsely appear authentic"; note the reason in the sheet
Satire, fiction or an obviously creative piece that uses realistic AILighter labelArticle 50(4) limits the duty to a disclosure that does not spoil the work, such as a credit line or end card
AI-drafted blog or newsletter text, reviewed and approved by a named person who takes editorial responsibilityUsually noThe text rule covers public-interest information without human review or editorial control; keep the reviewer's name and date
AI-written article on a public-interest topic (health, consumer safety, local politics) published with no human reviewYesFalls inside the Article 50(4) text rule

Content you published before 2 August 2026 does not have to be labelled after the fact, according to the Commission's transparency quick facts, though labelling it is encouraged.

Who does what

  • You, as deployer (the business that publishes the content): the human-visible label under Article 50(4).
  • Your AI tool's provider: machine-readable marking under Article 50(2). For generative tools already on the market before 2 August 2026, the Digital Omnibus gives providers until 2 December 2026. That mark does not replace your label: the Commission FAQ says deployers cannot rely on it.
  • The platform (Instagram, YouTube, TikTok and others) may have its own AI label or disclosure setting. Use it as well, but keep a visible label in the post itself so it travels when the content is shared.

Ready-to-use label wording

FormatShort labelWhere to put it
Image or ad"AI-generated image" or "Image created with AI"On the image itself, readable on a phone, not only in the caption
Edited photo"Photo edited with AI"On the image or the first line of the caption
Video"This video contains AI-generated scenes"On screen in the first seconds; for a long video, repeat it or keep a corner tag
Audio or voice"This voice is AI-generated"Spoken at the start, plus text where the audio is shown
Creative or satirical work"Made with AI" in the credits or end cardWhere it doesn't spoil the work (Article 50(4) second subparagraph)
Public-interest article with no human review"This article was generated with AI"At the top, before the text

Article 50(5) asks for the information to be clear and distinguishable and given at the latest at first exposure. A label hidden in hashtags or at the end of a long caption is weak.

Checklist before you publish AI content

  • Every AI tool you use for images, video, voice or text is in your AI inventory (SME AI inventory template)
  • Row added to the label sheet, with the 3-question answers or the text-review note
  • Real person's likeness or voice used only with their written consent; no AI testimonials presented as real customers
  • Label visible on the asset itself, plus the platform's AI setting where it has one
  • Staff who make content know the rule: the AI-literacy duty under Article 4 has applied since 2 February 2025 (AI literacy guide)
  • Chatbots and voice bots have their own "you are talking to AI" notice (Article 50 chatbot checklist)
  • Nothing sexual or intimate is generated of a real person without consent; the Digital Omnibus adds a ban on AI practices for generating non-consensual intimate content

This sheet is a starting point, not a legal opinion or proof of compliance. Advertising, consumer-protection, image-rights and data-protection rules in your country can add more. Fines for breaching Article 50 can reach €15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover, and for SMEs the lower of the two applies (Article 99(4) and 99(6)). Spot an error in this guide?
