EU AMLR client review and deletion date sheet for accountants and tax advisers: customer information updates at least every 1 year for higher-risk clients and every 5 years for others (Article 26), trigger events, and 5-year record retention then deletion (Article 77), from 10 July 2027

Updated 10 Nov 2026 · Disclosure: the EURO AI Register mentioned below is an Unadhi product with a free edition. Legal facts come from Regulation (EU) 2024/1624 (the Anti-Money Laundering Regulation, AMLR), Articles 3, 26, 33, 34, 77 and 90, read on EUR-Lex on 10 Nov 2026. This is a working sheet, not legal advice.

What you get: a finished, tested client review and deletion date sheet for accountants, bookkeepers, auditors and tax advisers. One row per client. Formulas give each active client's next customer information update date (at least every year for higher-risk clients, at most every 5 years for everyone else), flag clients where a trigger event means you must update now, and give each former, refused or one-off client the date their personal data must be deleted. A to-do column cites the Article for each step.

Short answer: from 10 July 2027 (Article 90), you must keep client documents, data and information up to date, and the gap between updates "shall not in any case exceed" 1 year for higher-risk clients under enhanced due diligence and 5 years for all other clients (Article 26(2)). You must also update when circumstances change, when you learn a relevant fact, or when you must contact the client that year about beneficial owners or under Directive 2011/16/EU (Article 26(3)). Records are kept for 5 years after the relationship ends, the one-off transaction, or your refusal, and then personal data is deleted (Article 77(3)).

What to tell a client in one minute

  • We will ask you to confirm your details regularly. The law sets a maximum gap: 1 year if you are a higher-risk client, 5 years otherwise (Article 26(2)).
  • Tell us when something changes. A new director, a new owner or a new line of business means we update your file straight away (Article 26(3)(a), (c)).
  • Low risk does not mean never. We may check less often for low-risk clients (Article 33(1)(b)), but never more than 5 years apart.
  • We keep your file for 5 years after you leave and then delete the personal data, unless another law or the authority needs it longer (Article 77(3)).

Why AI tools matter here

AI onboarding and identity-check tools often store client documents and scores without a review date or a deletion date. The AMLR needs both: a dated update cycle per client, and deletion after 5 years under GDPR rules. Checklist before 10 July 2027

  • Give every active client a risk level and a last-update date.
  • Book higher-risk client reviews at least yearly.
  • Pick a house rule of 5 years or less for everyone else.
  • Write down what counts as a trigger event in your procedures, and make staff log it.
  • List former and refused clients with their end dates, and diarise deletion 5 years later.
  • Check that your AI onboarding or ID-check tools can delete records on that date.

Questions

How often must an accountant update client due diligence information under the AMLR?

Based on the risk, and never less often than every 1 year for higher-risk clients under enhanced due diligence and every 5 years for all other clients (Article 26(2) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1624).

Can a lower-risk client go longer than 5 years between updates?

No. Simplified due diligence lets you reduce the frequency of updates (Article 33(1)(b)), but Article 26(2) says the period between updates shall not in any case exceed 5 years.

When must I update earlier than the schedule?

When the client's relevant circumstances change, when you become aware of a relevant fact about the client, or when you have a legal duty that calendar year to contact the client to review beneficial owner information or under Directive 2011/16/EU (Article 26(3)).

How long do I keep CDD records after a client leaves?

5 years from the end of the business relationship, the date of an occasional transaction, or the date you refused the client. Then personal data is deleted, unless other EU or national law needs it longer (Article 77(3)).

Can the authority make me keep records longer?

Yes. A competent authority may require further retention case by case for money laundering or terrorist financing cases, for up to another 5 years (Article 77(3), second subparagraph).

When does this apply?

From 10 July 2027 (Article 90). Until then, your national law implementing the earlier AML directives applies. Accountants, auditors and tax advisers are obliged entities (Article 3(3)(a)).
