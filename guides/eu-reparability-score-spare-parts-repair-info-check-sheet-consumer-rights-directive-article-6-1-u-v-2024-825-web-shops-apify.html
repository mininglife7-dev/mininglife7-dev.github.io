Short answer: from 27 September 2026 Member States must apply the national rules transposing Directive (EU) 2024/825 (Article 4(1)). That Directive amended the Consumer Rights Directive so that, before a consumer is bound by a distance or off-premises contract, Article 6(1)(u) requires, where applicable, the reparability score for the goods (a score based on harmonised Union requirements). Where point (u) is not applicable and the producer has made the information available to the trader, Article 6(1)(v) requires information about the availability and estimated cost of, and procedure for ordering, spare parts necessary to keep the goods in conformity, about the availability of repair and maintenance instructions, and about repair restrictions.

Why accountants, bookkeepers and web agencies should look at this now

  • Same deadline as the green-claims rules. Directive (EU) 2024/825 applies from 27 September 2026. Shops that already fixed “eco-friendly” wording still need a product-page pass for reparability and spare parts.
  • Score vs spare-parts is easy to mix up. If a Union reparability score applies, show it (Article 6(1)(u)). Only when (u) does not apply — and the producer gave the trader the data — do spare-parts, instructions and restrictions under Article 6(1)(v) kick in.
  • Producer-available is a real gate. Article 6(1)(v) does not invent data the producer never supplied. Column J forces that check before the sheet grades FIX.
  • It is a quick, billable catalogue check. Run the product scraper, open a few product pages, answer the columns, and the sheet writes the fix list with Article cites. Step 1: pull product title and description (scraper, free during launch)

  1. Put product URLs for each client shop into a list and run the E-commerce Product Data Scraper. Prefer Shopify products.json, WooCommerce Store API, then JSON-LD — you get title, brand, price, currency, availability, SKU/GTIN, variants, images and description.
  2. Copy title into column E. Paste a short product-, repair-, spare-parts- or score-related snippet into column F (the word-scan looks for repair/reparab/spare/score/pièce/ersatz words).
  3. Shops blocked by robots or terms are skipped for free: fill E–F by hand for those.
  4. The scraper does not decide whether a Union score applies. Whether a reparability score exists for the category, whether it is shown, and whether the producer made spare-parts data available is your own check (Step 3).

Step 3: the five-minute checklist per product

  1. Confirm the offer is a distance or off-premises B2C sale of goods (columns C–D–G). Digital downloads and pure services are out of scope on this sheet for (u)/(v).
  2. Does a Union reparability score based on harmonised requirements apply to this category? If yes, is the score on the product page before the consumer is bound? (H–I / Article 6(1)(u).)
  3. If no score applies: did the producer make spare-parts / repair information available to the trader? If not, Article 6(1)(v) is not triggered on these answers (J).
  4. If (v) is triggered: are availability, estimated cost and ordering procedure for necessary spare parts shown? (K)
  5. Is the availability of repair and maintenance instructions told? (L)
  6. Are repair restrictions disclosed (or clearly none)? (M)
  7. Take dated screenshots of the product page (Article 6(9) burden of proof on the trader).

Frequently asked questions

What is a reparability score under the Consumer Rights Directive?

Directive (EU) 2024/825 amended Directive 2011/83/EU. Article 6(1)(u) requires, where applicable, that before the consumer is bound by a distance or off-premises contract the trader provides 'the reparability score for the goods'. In the consolidated CRD text reused for this guide, 'reparability score' means a score expressing the capacity of a good to be repaired, based on harmonised requirements established at Union level. Where no such Union score applies, point (u) is not applicable.

When must a web shop show spare-parts and repair information?

Article 6(1)(v) applies where point (u) is not applicable and provided that the producer makes the information available to the trader. Then the trader must give information about the availability and estimated cost of, and procedure for ordering, spare parts that are necessary to keep the goods in conformity, about the availability of repair and maintenance instructions and about repair restrictions.

From when do Article 6(1)(u) and (v) apply?

Article 4(1) of Directive (EU) 2024/825 required Member States to adopt their national measures by 27 March 2026 and to apply them from 27 September 2026. Check your national transposition for the exact wording and penalties. This guide grades the EU Directive text, not every national law.

Does this apply to digital downloads or services?

Article 6(1)(u) and (v) refer to goods (and, for (u), the reparability score for the goods). On this sheet we mark Digital-only and Service rows as OUT OF SCOPE for (u)/(v). Separate CRD points cover digital content functionality and interoperability (Article 6(1)(r) and (s)).

Is this the same as the commercial durability guarantee or GPSR sheets?

No. The complaint-handling / commercial-guarantee sheet covers Article 6(1)(l)/(la)/(lb)/(m) legal and commercial guarantees (including the commercial guarantee of durability label under Article 6(1)(la) and Article 22a). The GPSR Article 19 sheet covers manufacturer, responsible person and safety warnings on product pages. This sheet grades only the reparability score (Article 6(1)(u)) and the spare-parts / repair-instructions / repair-restrictions information (Article 6(1)(v)).

Must every product show a reparability score?

No. Article 6(1)(u) applies 'where applicable' — when a Union reparability score based on harmonised requirements exists for that good. If no such score applies, use Article 6(1)(v) only when the producer has made the spare-parts and repair information available to the trader.
