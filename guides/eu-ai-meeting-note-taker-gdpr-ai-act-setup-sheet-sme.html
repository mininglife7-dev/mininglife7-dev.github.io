The short answer

An AI note-taker records or listens to a meeting and turns it into a transcript or summary. Voices, names, what people said and the call metadata are personal data, so the GDPR applies in full: a legal basis (Article 6), a notice before it starts (Article 13), processor terms with the supplier (Article 28), and keeping the material no longer than needed (Article 5(1)(e)). A DPIA is likely to be expected where you record staff regularly, record job interviews, or the meeting touches health or other special category data (Article 35).

The EU AI Act does not ban transcription or summaries. It adds one hard line that already applies: since 2 February 2025 it is prohibited to use AI to infer the emotions of people in the workplace, including job candidates, from biometric data such as voice or facial expressions, except for medical or safety reasons (Article 5(1)(f), read with the definition in Article 3(39)). The Commission's guidelines on prohibited practices give "AI systems monitoring the emotional tone in hybrid work teams by identifying and inferring emotions from voice and imagery of hybrid video calls" as a prohibited example, and say inferring emotions from written text alone is outside the ban. Fines for prohibited practices go up to EUR 35 million or 7% of worldwide turnover; for SMEs, the lower of the two applies (Article 99(3) and (6), see our fines guide).

7Emotion, mood, tone or 'engagement' featuresSwitch off any feature that reads emotions or intentions from voice, face or video for staff, contractors or job candidates. This is a prohibition, in force since 2 February 2025, not a setting preference. Text-only sentiment on a transcript is outside this ban but is still personal data; we suggest off.AI Act Art. 5(1)(f) and Art. 3(39); Commission guidelines on prohibited practices; dates Art. 113(a); fines Art. 99(3)
8Speaker scores used to judge staffTalk-time, interruption or 'performance' scores used to evaluate staff can turn a note-taker into a workplace monitoring tool. If you plan to use them for decisions about people, run the high-risk check first (duties apply from 2 December 2027). In Germany and some other countries a works council has a say before such tools are introduced.AI Act Annex III point 4(b); Art. 113(c)(i); for Germany § 87(1) no. 6 BetrVG
9Supplier termsSigned GDPR processor terms, the list of sub-processors, where recordings and transcripts are stored, and the legal basis for any transfer outside the EEA (for example the EU-US Data Privacy Framework adequacy decision for participating US companies, or standard contractual clauses). Ask the supplier to name it in writing.Processor contract Art. 28(3); transfers Art. 44-46; Implementing Decision (EU) 2023/1795
10Training on your meetingsIs the supplier allowed to use your recordings or transcripts to train or improve its models, and can people at the supplier listen to them? Turn this off in settings or get a written 'no'. The AEPD notes that a supplier who retrains on your data acts as a separate controller for that, with its own legal basis. This is a GDPR point, not an AI Act rule.GDPR Art. 5(1)(b), 28; AEPD blog, 14 Jan 2026
11RetentionWrite a number for each thing kept: audio/video [__] days, transcript [__] days, checked summary [kept with the project file]. Set auto-delete in the tool if it has one. The numbers are your choice; the law asks for no longer than necessary.Storage limitation Art. 5(1)(e)
12Who can see and shareLimit access to the organiser and named people. Turn off automatic sharing of full transcripts to every invitee, especially external ones; send a checked summary instead.Security Art. 32
13Human checkSomeone who was in the meeting reads the AI summary before it is sent or relied on. AI summaries can attribute words to the wrong person or miss a 'no'.Accuracy Art. 5(1)(d)
14Access and erasure requestsTranscripts and recordings that relate to a person are their personal data. Know how to search, export and delete one person's data in the tool before the first request arrives.Right of access Art. 15(3); erasure Art. 17
15Records and DPIA screeningAdd the tool to your record of processing activities and do a DPIA screening. The under-250-staff exemption from records does not apply to processing that is not occasional, which regular meeting recording usually is. Check your national authority's DPIA list.Art. 30(1) and (5); Art. 35(1), (4)
16Staff know how to use itShort briefing for anyone who switches it on: the 'never' list, the notice, the emotion-feature rule, how to delete. Keep a record of who was briefed.AI Act Art. 4 (AI literacy, as amended)

Check the official source for each country where participants sit.National law, for example § 201 StGB and § 87(1) no. 6 BetrVG (Germany)

Keep the filled sheet with the tool's entry in your AI register, together with the supplier's written answers. If the supplier refuses a point (for example, will not stop training on your data), record the refusal and decide which meetings, if any, the tool may still join.

Never-record list (starting point)

  • Disciplinary, grievance, performance and dismissal conversations
  • Health, sick leave, pregnancy, disability or family matters
  • Legal advice and dispute strategy
  • Works council, trade union or employee representative meetings
  • Job interviews, unless you have done the legal-basis and DPIA work and told the candidate in advance
  • Client or supplier meetings where the other side has not agreed, or has asked you not to
  • Any meeting where someone asks for it to be switched off

Paste-ready meeting notice

Calendar invite line

This meeting will use [tool name], an AI note-taker, to [record audio and] produce a [transcript and] summary for [purpose, e.g. action points for the project file]. [Business name] is responsible for this. The [recording/transcript] is deleted after [30] days; the checked summary is kept [with the project file]. If you would prefer not to be recorded or transcribed, tell [name] before or at the start and we will switch it off. More information: [link to privacy notice].

Spoken line at the start

"Before we start: [tool name] is taking notes for us [and recording the audio]. If anyone would rather it didn't, say so now or message me, and I'll switch it off."

Saying this line is about transparency and giving people an easy way to object. It does not by itself make consent your legal basis; record the basis you actually rely on in row 4.

Privacy-notice paragraph

AI meeting notes. When we use an AI note-taker in a meeting, [business name, address, contact email] is the controller. We use it to [produce accurate notes and action points]. Our legal basis is [our legitimate interest in keeping accurate records of meetings / your consent / performing our contract with you]. The tool is provided by [supplier], which acts as our processor[, and may store data in [country] under [transfer basis]]. We keep recordings and transcripts for [__] days and checked summaries for [__]. You can ask for access to, correction or deletion of your data, object to this processing, or ask us not to use the tool in a meeting, by writing to [contact]. You can also complain to your data protection authority.

These are the main Article 13 items (who, why, legal basis, recipients, transfers, retention, rights). Check the official source for the full list and adapt the wording to your situation.

30-second check before each call

  • Is this meeting on the never-record list? If yes, note-taker off.
  • Did the invite carry the notice line? If not, say the spoken line and offer to switch it off.
  • Are there people from outside the business, or in another country? Check row 17.
  • Emotion, tone or engagement features still off?
  • After the call: check the summary before sending it, and send the summary, not the full transcript.

Do this week

  • Find every AI note-taker in use, including ones staff switched on in their own video-call accounts (SME AI inventory template)
  • Turn off automatic joining and any emotion, mood or engagement feature
  • Fill the set-up sheet for each tool and send the supplier the open questions (AI vendor questions, supplier clause sheet)
  • Add the notice line to your calendar template and the paragraph to your privacy notice
  • Set retention and delete old recordings you no longer need
  • Tell staff the rules in your staff AI use policy and use the staff and applicant notice if AI is used about them
