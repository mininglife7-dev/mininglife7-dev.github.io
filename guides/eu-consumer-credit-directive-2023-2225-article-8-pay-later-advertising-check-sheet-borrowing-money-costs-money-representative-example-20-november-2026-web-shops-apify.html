Short answer: from 20 November 2026 (11 days from 9 November) national laws apply the new Consumer Credit Directive (Article 48(1)). Every advert concerning a credit agreement must carry “a clear and prominent warning to make consumers aware that borrowing costs money, using the wording ‘Caution! Borrowing money costs money’ or an equivalent wording” (Article 8(1)). If the message shows “an interest rate or any figures relating to any cost of credit”, it also needs a representative example with the borrowing rate, total amount of credit, APR and, where applicable, duration, cash price and advance payment, total amount payable and instalments (Article 8(2)–(4)). Black Friday is a week later, on 27 November, so check the pay-later messages before the deal banners go live.

Why accountants, bookkeepers and web agencies should look at this now

  • The pay-later message is usually added by an app or plug-in, not written by the shop. Nobody re-reads it when the law changes. A web agency can check every client shop in an afternoon.
  • Buy-now-pay-later from a third party is in scope of the new directive (recitals 16 and 17), and a shop that presents or offers the credit for remuneration can be a credit intermediary (Article 3(12)). Our pay-later scope sheet answers whether an offer is covered; this sheet checks what the adverts say.
  • Black Friday adds risk. “Extra 10% off if you pay later” is wording a Member State may ban (Article 8(8)(b)), and a banner that says “interest-free” while the instalments add up to more than the price can create “false expectations … regarding … the cost of credit”, which Article 7 prohibits.
  • It is a quick, billable check with a written fix list. It pairs with our total-price sheet and our bait-advertising sheet.

What we tested

Fourteen made-up pages in LibreOffice (xlsx → ods → csv), with today fixed at 9 Nov 2026 (11 days left): complete message (OK); “pay in 3, 0%” with nothing else (FIX: warning and all six items listed); instalments adding up to EUR 6 more than the price (CHECK: do not call it interest-free); “pay later” with no figures but a warning (OK: Article 8(2) not triggered); “credit boosts your budget” (FIX, Article 8(7)); “instant approval” on a EUR 150 item where the national option applies (CHECK national law, Article 8(8)); B2B only (OUT OF SCOPE); no message (NO CREDIT MESSAGE); pay by invoice in 30 days from the shop (CHECK SCOPE); option unclear (CHECK BY HAND); instalments adding up to EUR 30 less than the price (FIX: show the advance payment); and three half-filled rows (CHECK BY HAND). 14/14 gave the intended result, the empty row stayed blank, and no cell showed an error.

Launch checklist (before 20 November and again before Black Friday)

  • Every pay-later message, banner and newsletter has the borrowing-costs-money warning, clearly and prominently (Art 8(1)).
  • Any message with “0%”, “interest-free”, a fee or an instalment amount carries one representative example with all items that apply (Art 8(3)–(4)); only points (e) and (f) may sit behind a click, scroll or swipe, and only where the screen genuinely cannot show them (Art 8(6)).
  • Instalments × amount equals the cash price shown on the page, or the difference is explained as a cost or an advance payment.
  • No wording says pay later improves finances, that existing debts do not matter, or that it replaces savings (Art 8(7)).
  • No “extra discount if you pay later” or “instant approval” wording until you have checked the national law (Art 8(8)).
  • The provider's widget settings were updated after any Black Friday price change.
  • Dated screenshots of each message are kept with the sheet.

Limits and our reading

  • National law decides. A directive applies through national laws; we did not read any of them. Wording, the APR rule in Article 8(2) second subparagraph, the Article 2(8) and 8(8) options and who is responsible for a widget (shop, provider or both) may differ by country.
  • Our reading, flagged: that a pay-later widget on a product page is “advertising concerning credit agreements”; that “0%” or “interest-free” counts as indicating an interest rate under Article 8(2); the EUR 0.05 rounding tolerance; and treating an instalment sum above the cash price as a sign of a cost of credit (it could also be a wrong price in the widget).
  • Not covered: pre-contractual information (Articles 10–12), creditworthiness assessment, the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive, and agreements concluded before 20 November 2026 (Article 2(2)(l)).
  • The scraper gives dated price data only. It does not decide whether an advert complies.

FAQ

Does a 'Pay in 3' message on a product page count as credit advertising?

Our reading: very likely, where the pay-later offer is credit under Directive (EU) 2023/2225. Article 8 applies to 'advertising concerning credit agreements' and Article 7 to 'any advertising and marketing communications concerning credit agreements'. Buy-now-pay-later from a third party is in scope of the new directive. The directive does not define advertising, so check the national law that transposed it.

What must every credit advert show from 20 November 2026?

A clear and prominent warning that borrowing costs money, using the wording 'Caution! Borrowing money costs money' or an equivalent wording (Article 8(1)). National laws apply the directive from 20 November 2026 (Article 48(1)).

When is the full representative example needed?

When the advert indicates an interest rate or any figures relating to any cost of credit (Article 8(2)). It must then show, in a representative example: the borrowing rate and charges, the total amount of credit, the APR, and where applicable the duration, the cash price and advance payment, and the total amount payable and the instalments (Article 8(3) and (4)). Our reading: '0%' or 'interest-free' indicates an interest rate.

Can the representative example sit behind a click?

Only partly. Article 8(6) allows the cash price and advance payment and the total payable and instalment amount (points (e) and (f)) to be reached by clicking, scrolling or swiping, in specific and justified cases where the electronic medium cannot display them prominently and clearly. Recital 33 says consumers should still see all the standard information before accessing the credit offer.

Which wording is banned?

Article 8(7) bans adverts that encourage consumers to seek credit by suggesting it would improve their financial situation, that say outstanding credit or registered credit has little or no influence on the credit assessment, or that falsely suggest credit increases financial resources, replaces savings or raises living standards. Member States may also ban adverts that highlight ease or speed, make a discount conditional on taking credit, or offer grace periods over three months (Article 8(8)).

Is pay by invoice in 30 days from the shop itself covered?

Not if the shop itself gives time to pay, no third party offers credit, there is no interest or other charge (only limited late-payment charges) and payment is due in full within 50 days of delivery; for large online sellers that are not SMEs the limit is 14 days (Article 2(2)(h)). The sheet marks those rows CHECK SCOPE; our separate scope sheet covers them.
