India EPR for plastic packaging: who must register, which category, and how the tonnage is worked out

Last updated 30 Oct 2026 · India · Plastic packaging

Short answer. If your business puts plastic packaging on the Indian market you may be an obligated entity under the Plastic Waste Management Rules 2016 and must register on the CPCB centralised EPR portal. Four kinds of entity are covered: producer (including micro and small), importer, brand owner and plastic waste processor. Which office you register with depends on how many States you operate in. Your yearly obligation is a tonnage figure — the weight of plastic packaging you placed on the market — and it has to be built from your own specification sheets, product by product. This page sets out the structure, the categories and the dates, and links the official texts at the bottom.

Who is an obligated entity?

Clause 6.1 of the EPR Guidelines lists the entities required to register. Two points in the Guidelines are easy to miss and they pull in opposite directions, so read them together:

QuestionWhat the Guidelines say
Are micro and small producers required to register?Yes. Clause 6.1 includes micro and small enterprises within "producer".
Are micro and small brand owners required to meet the EPR target?No — clause 4 excludes micro and small brand owners, and export-oriented units, from the target.
If a micro or small producer is exempt from the target, does the obligation disappear?No. Clause 7.8 passes the target upstream to the raw-material supplier.

The practical effect: being small can remove the target from you, but it does not automatically remove the registration, and the target does not vanish — it moves to someone else in the chain.

Where do you register?

Your footprintRegister with
More than two States or Union TerritoriesCentral Pollution Control Board (CPCB), on the centralised portal
One or two States or Union TerritoriesThe State Pollution Control Board (SPCB) or Pollution Control Committee (PCC)

Registration is granted for one year when fresh and can be renewed for three years. CPCB's own service target for deciding a complete application is two weeks (clause 12.3), and if the authority does not decide within 30 days the registration is deemed granted under Rule 13(8). Those two figures are frequently confused: 12.3 is the internal target, 13(8) is your legal fallback if nothing happens.

The four packaging categories

CategoryWhat it covers
I — RigidRigid plastic packaging.
II — FlexibleFlexible plastic packaging of single layer or multilayer; plastic sheets and covers; carry bags; sachets and pouches.
III — MultilayeredMultilayered packaging with at least one layer of plastic and at least one layer of a material other than plastic.
IV — CompostablePlastic packaging and plastic sheets used for carrying, storing or packaging compostable plastic.

Category matters because the recycling and reuse obligations differ by category and by year. Under the 2026 amendment the mandatory recycled content percentages rise over four years — for example Category I at 30 / 40 / 50 / 60% and Category II at 10 / 10 / 20 / 20% for 2025-26 / 2026-27 / 2027-28 / 2028-29 onwards. Recycled plastic packaging must also conform to IS 14534:2023 and carry the specified marking, with FSSAI labelling where it touches food.

How the tonnage figure is actually built

This is where most first-time filers get stuck, and it is not a paperwork problem — it is a data problem. The obligation is driven by the weight of packaging you placed on the market, and that weight exists in your own records. The calculation is a sum of products:

tonnage (material m) = Σ over products ( units sold × packaging weight per unit )

Three rules keep that figure defensible if it is ever questioned:

  • A weight you do not have is flagged, never estimated. If a packaging weight is missing from the specification sheet, it is an open item to be obtained or measured — not a number to be guessed. An invented weight is the fastest way to turn a filing into a liability.
  • The arithmetic is done once, in a fixed tool, not by hand. A spreadsheet you retype each year drifts. A fixed calculation with a hash of its inputs can be reproduced and defended.
  • Only approved records count. Packaging data that has not been checked against a specification sheet should be shown separately as provisional, so the final figure is traceable to evidence.

A worked example: a snack maker sells two products. Product A: 400,000 units a year, printed flexible laminate pouch weighing 4.2 g each. Product B: 150,000 units, rigid PET jar weighing 28 g each. Product A is Category II: 400,000 × 4.2 g = 1,680 kg. Product B is Category I: 150,000 × 28 g = 4,200 kg. Total 5,880 kg, split across two categories and reported per category, not as one lump. If Product A's pouch weight was missing, it is flagged — the answer is "obtain the spec", not "assume 4 g".

Third-party verification — what changed in 2026

The Plastic Waste Management (Amendment) Rules 2026 (G.S.R. 237(E), published 31 March 2026 and in force on publication, with no grace period) insert the words "or Registered Environment Auditor" after "designated agency" in Rule 17(5) and in two places in Schedule II. A Registered Environment Auditor is one defined under the Environment Audit Rules 2025. In plain terms: third-party verification is now permitted in addition to the previously designated agency — it widens who may verify, it does not remove the requirement.

Frequently asked questions

Who has to register for EPR on plastic packaging in India?

Producers (including micro and small), importers, brand owners and plastic waste processors. Micro and small brand owners are exempt from the EPR target under clause 4, but that is separate from the registration duty, and clause 6.1 includes micro and small enterprises within "producer".

CPCB or the State Board?

More than two States or Union Territories: CPCB, on the centralised portal. One or two: your SPCB or PCC.

What are the four categories?

I Rigid, II Flexible, III Multilayered (plastic plus another material), IV Compostable.

When are the returns due?

Generally 30 June of the following financial year. For FY 2025-26, CPCB notices set 31 October 2026 for plastic waste processors and 31 December 2026 for producers, importers and brand owners.

How do I work out my tonnage if a packaging weight is missing?

You flag it and go and get it — from the supplier's specification sheet, or by weighing a sample. You do not estimate it. A missing weight is an open item, not a small number.

Is the fine automatic?

No. Under section 15 of the Environment (Protection) Act 1986 the penalty is imposed only after conviction by a court. It can be up to one lakh rupees plus five thousand rupees for each day the contravention continues, and where it continues beyond a year the imprisonment can extend to seven years. The figure to plan against is the compliance work, not the fine.
