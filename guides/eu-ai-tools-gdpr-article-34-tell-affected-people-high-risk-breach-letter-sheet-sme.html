AI tool breach put customers at risk? A GDPR Article 34 sheet for telling the people affected, for small businesses

Updated 26 Oct 2026 · Disclosure: the EURO AI Register mentioned below is an Unadhi product with a free edition. This guide is for information only, not legal advice.

What you get: a finished breach letter sheet in three tabs: a "must we tell people?" test with one row per breach and a result formula, a letter builder with the four things the message must contain, and a sent log with a status formula, plus AI tool examples and two made-up example rows.

Short answer: when a personal data breach is likely to result in a high risk to people, Article 34 GDPR says you must tell those people without undue delay, in clear and plain language. This is separate from the 72-hour report to the authority under Article 33. You can skip the letters only in three cases: the data was unreadable to outsiders (for example encrypted), later steps mean the high risk is no longer likely, or writing to everyone would take disproportionate effort, in which case you make a public notice instead.

Step 1: tab 1, must we tell the people affected? (one row per breach)

ColumnWhat to writeSource
A. Breach"chatbot transcripts visible to other users", "AI note-taker shared a client call link publicly", "staff pasted a customer list into a public AI tool"Art. 4(12)
B. We became aware onDate and timeArt. 33(1)
C. High risk to people likely?Yes or No, with one line on why: health data, ID or bank details, passwords, children, data that could lead to fraud, discrimination or embarrassmentArt. 34(1), Recital 86
D. Data unreadable to outsiders?Yes only if the affected data was protected, for example encrypted, and the key was not takenArt. 34(3)(a)
E. Later steps removed the high risk?Yes only if what you did afterwards means the high risk is no longer likely to happen. Write what you didArt. 34(3)(b)
F. Writing to each person is disproportionate?Yes if you cannot reasonably reach people one by one, for example no contact detailsArt. 34(3)(c)
G. Result=IF(C2<>"Yes","No letters needed: still log it and check the 72-hour report",IF(OR(D2="Yes",E2="Yes"),"Letters may not be needed: write down why",IF(F2="Yes","Public notice instead of letters","Tell each person now: fill tab 2")))Art. 34(1), (3); our working rule

The formula is a first sort, not a legal conclusion. Your supervisory authority can still require you to tell people, or decide that one of the three exceptions applies (Article 34(4)). Keep column C's reasons, because Article 33(5) asks you to document every breach, its effects and what you did.

Step 2: tab 2, the letter builder

Article 34(2) says the message must describe the breach in clear and plain language and contain at least the items in Article 33(3)(b), (c) and (d). Recital 86 adds recommendations so people can protect themselves.

RowWhat to writeSource
1. What happenedIn plain words: what data, when, how it was exposed. "On 20 October our website chatbot showed parts of other customers' chats to some visitors for about two hours."Art. 34(2)
2. Who to contactName and contact details of your data protection officer, or another contact pointArt. 33(3)(b)
3. Likely consequences"Someone may have read your order number and delivery address."Art. 33(3)(c)
4. What we did and will do"We switched the chatbot off, deleted the stored transcripts at the supplier and changed its settings."Art. 33(3)(d)
5. What you can do"Be careful with emails or calls that mention your order; we will never ask for your card details."Recital 86
6. Joined-up text="Dear customer, "&B1&" "&B3&" "&B4&" "&B5&" Questions: "&B2our working rule

Put the answers in column B of rows 1 to 5; row 6 joins them. Read the result aloud: if a customer would need a lawyer to understand it, rewrite it.

F. Status=IF(D2<>"","Sent: keep a copy",IF(E2>=3,"Late: send today and note the reason","Draft and send without undue delay"))Art. 34(1); the 3-day flag is our working rule

Name tab 1 Test; the formula in E assumes that layout. Article 34 sets no fixed number of days. "Without undue delay" is the test, and Recital 86 says an immediate risk of harm calls for prompt communication.

Where AI tools cause breaches small businesses must tell people about

  • A chatbot or AI help desk that shows one customer's conversation to another
  • An AI meeting note-taker that sends a transcript with health, money or HR details to the wrong people: see the note-taker setup sheet
  • Staff pasting customer lists into a public AI tool whose terms let the supplier reuse inputs: see the staff AI use policy
  • A shared AI tool account with a weak password that someone else gets into: see the AI account access sheet

Example 1, a physiotherapy clinic whose AI booking chatbot exposed appointment reasons: tab 1: C. Yes (health data) · D. No · E. No · F. No · G. Tell each person now: fill tab 2. Tab 3: group "38 patients who used the chatbot on 20 Oct" · email · sent 21 Oct · status Sent: keep a copy.
