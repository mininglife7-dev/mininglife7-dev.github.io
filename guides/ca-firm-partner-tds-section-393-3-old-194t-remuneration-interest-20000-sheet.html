Partner TDS sheet for CA firms: 10% on remuneration and interest to partners of firms and LLPs once the year crosses ₹20,000 (section 393(3), old 194T)

Updated 27 Oct 2026 · Disclosure: the white-label calendar linked below is an Unadhi product (₹999/year). This guide is for information only, not professional advice.

What you get: a finished partner TDS sheet in three tabs: a Partners tab that adds up each partner's remuneration, interest, commission, bonus and salary for the tax year and tells you whether the ₹20,000 line is crossed, a Credits tab that gives each entry its deduction month and deposit date, and a Not covered tab for items that stay outside, plus a made-up example and a client message.

Short answer (checked 27 Oct 2026): from 1 April 2026 the old section 194T of the Income-tax Act, 1961 sits in section 393(3), Table serial number 7, of the Income-tax Act, 2025. A firm (an LLP counts as a firm) must deduct 10% on any sum in the nature of salary, remuneration, commission, bonus or interest paid to a partner or credited to the partner's account, including the capital account. The threshold is ₹20,000 per partner for the year, counted on all five items together, and once it is crossed tax is deducted on the whole amount, not only the part above ₹20,000. Deduction is due at credit or payment, whichever is earlier.

Why firms miss it

Many partnership firms and LLPs only post partner remuneration and interest on capital on 31 March, as a book entry with no money moving. That book entry is the trigger. If the firm also pays monthly drawings against remuneration, the earlier of credit or payment decides the month. A short sheet kept from October gives you time to plan the year-end entries and the deposits before the March rush.

Tab "Not covered": keep these out of the total

  • Share of profit: not one of the five items.
  • Drawings and withdrawal of capital: money the partner takes out of their own balance.
  • Genuine reimbursement of expenses the partner spent for the firm, backed by bills.

A drawing paid "against remuneration" is different: it is an advance payment of remuneration, so it goes in the Credits tab.

Made-up example (not a real client)

Partner P1 of an LLP gets ₹1,500 a month interest on capital, credited monthly from April (₹18,000 for the year), and ₹1,00,000 remuneration credited on 31 March. By March the total is ₹1,18,000, well over ₹20,000, so TDS for the year is ₹11,800. Nothing was deducted on the monthly interest because the firm expected to stay under the line, so the sheet shows "Deduct ₹11,800 at next credit or payment" on 31 March, with deposit by 30 April. Partner P2 gets only ₹12,000 interest and no remuneration, so the sheet shows "Below line: keep watching".
