Short answer: since Delegated Directive (EU) 2023/2775, a company is micro if on its balance sheet date it does not exceed two of: balance sheet total EUR 450,000, net turnover EUR 900,000, 10 employees on average (Article 3(1)). Small: EUR 5,000,000, EUR 10,000,000, 50 employees, and a Member State may raise the two euro limits to at most EUR 7,500,000 and EUR 15,000,000 (Article 3(2)). Medium: EUR 25,000,000, EUR 50,000,000, 250 employees (Article 3(3)). Above two of those: large (Article 3(4)). A change of category counts "only if it occurs in two consecutive financial years" (Article 3(10)). The new figures apply for financial years beginning on or after 1 January 2024, and Member States could allow them from 1 January 2023 (Delegated Directive 2023/2775, Article 2(1)).

What to tell a client in one minute

  • Two out of three. The size depends on not exceeding at least two of the three limits on the balance sheet date. A company with a big balance sheet but small turnover and few staff can still be small.
  • One good or bad year does not change the category. Crossing a limit (up or down) matters only when it happens on two balance sheet dates in a row (Article 3(10)). The sheet shows WATCH in the first year and CHANGED in the second.
  • Medium and large means a statutory audit. The financial statements of medium-sized and large undertakings and of public-interest entities are audited (Article 34(1)). Small and micro companies have no audit duty under the Directive, but national law may add one.
  • Smaller means lighter accounts. Your Member State may let small companies draw up an abridged balance sheet and profit and loss account (Article 14) and skip publishing the profit and loss account and management report (Article 31(1)); micro companies may get more (Article 36). These are Member State options, so check national law.
  • Banks, insurers and listed companies are large anyway. A public-interest entity "shall be treated as a large undertaking regardless of" its figures (Article 40).
  • Groups have their own limits. Small groups are exempt from consolidated accounts unless a company in the group is a public-interest entity (Article 23(1)); Member States may exempt medium-sized groups (Article 23(2)).

Client checklist before the 31 December 2026 year-end

  • Get the balance sheet total (assets A to E), net turnover and average employees for the last two balance sheet dates.
  • Use your Member State's small limits, not only the EU defaults.
  • Apply the two consecutive years rule before telling a client it has moved up or down.
  • Moving to medium? Plan the statutory audit now (Article 34(1)).
  • Moving down? Check which abridged formats and publication exemptions national law offers (Articles 14, 31, 36).
  • Public-interest entity, investment undertaking or financial holding? Check the restrictions (Articles 36(7), 40).
  • Parent company? Run the group test on consolidated figures too (Articles 3(5)-(8), 23).

Limits and our reading

  • Member State law decides the details. Most size consequences in the Directive are options for Member States. The sheet says what the Directive allows or requires; your national accounting and company law says what applies.
  • Not read, flagged: national laws transposing Delegated Directive 2023/2775 (deadline 24 December 2024) and their chosen small limits and start dates; national audit thresholds; the sustainability reporting rules in Articles 19a and 29a and their recent amendments; Directive 2006/43/EC on statutory audits; Commission Recommendation 2003/361.
  • Our reading, flagged: the category moves only when this year and last year are both on the same side of last year's category, and then to the nearer of the two sizes; a client's first row uses the category you enter in column H; groups are never micro; a public-interest company in the group is shown as a note for groups, while a public-interest company is set to LARGE.

FAQ

What are the EU micro, small and medium company limits now?

Under Article 3 of Directive 2013/34/EU as amended by Delegated Directive (EU) 2023/2775: micro up to EUR 450,000 balance sheet total, EUR 900,000 net turnover and 10 employees; small up to EUR 5,000,000, EUR 10,000,000 and 50 employees (Member States may raise the euro limits to EUR 7,500,000 and EUR 15,000,000); medium up to EUR 25,000,000, EUR 50,000,000 and 250 employees. A company must not exceed at least two of the three limits.

From when do the higher limits apply?

Member States had to transpose Delegated Directive 2023/2775 by 24 December 2024 and apply it for financial years beginning on or after 1 January 2024; they could allow it for financial years beginning on or after 1 January 2023 (Article 2(1)).

Does one year above the limits change a company's size?

No. Exceeding or ceasing to exceed two of the three limits affects the derogations only if it occurs in two consecutive financial years (Article 3(10)).

Which companies need a statutory audit under the Accounting Directive?

Public-interest entities, medium-sized and large undertakings (Article 34(1)). National law may also require audits of smaller companies.

Is a small bank or insurer a small company?

No. A public-interest entity is treated as a large undertaking regardless of its net turnover, balance sheet total or average number of employees (Article 40).

Are the Accounting Directive limits the same as the SME definition used by the AI Act?

No. The AI Act and EU funding rules refer to Commission Recommendation 2003/361, which has its own definition. Use Article 3 of Directive 2013/34/EU for accounts, audit and publication.
