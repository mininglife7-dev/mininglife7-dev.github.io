Short answer: under Article 14(1)(a) of the EU Food Information Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011, when prepacked food is sold at a distance (a web shop, an online marketplace listing, a phone or catalogue order), the mandatory food information must be available before the purchase is concluded, on the selling material or through other clearly identified means, at no extra cost. The only exception is the date (best before or use by), which, like everything else, must be there at delivery (Article 14(1)(b)). The mandatory list is in Article 9(1): name, ingredients, allergens, quantity of certain ingredients, net quantity, date, storage conditions, the food business operator's name and address, origin where required, instructions where needed, alcohol strength for drinks over 1.2% vol, and the nutrition declaration.

Why accountants, bookkeepers and web agencies should look at this now

  • Small food shops often show only a photo of the pack. Many product pages have a title, a price and a nice description, but no ingredients list, no allergens and no nutrition table in text. Article 14(1)(a) asks for the information before purchase, and Article 9(2) says the particulars are given "with words and numbers".
  • Allergens are the one item that applies to almost every food. Article 9(1)(c) covers the 14 groups listed in Annex II. For loose or made-to-order food (non-prepacked), Article 44(1)(a) still makes allergen information mandatory, and Article 14(2) applies the before-purchase rule to it.
  • The shop is responsible for its own pages. Article 8(5) asks each food business operator to make sure the food information law that is relevant to its activities is met, and Article 8(4) says it must not change information in a way that misleads.
  • It is a quick, billable check. About 15 minutes per shop for ten product pages, with one fix list you can send to the client or their web designer before the Christmas season.

  1. Put your client shop homepages in a list. Run the E-commerce Product Data Scraper with maxProductsPerShop: 20 and includeDescription: true.   6. Soybeans

  8. Nuts: almonds, hazelnuts, walnuts, cashews, pecans, Brazil nuts, pistachios, macadamia or Queensland nuts
  9. Celery
  10. Mustard
  11. Sesame seeds
  12. Sulphur dioxide and sulphites over 10 mg/kg or 10 mg/litre (as total SO2)
  13. Lupin
  14. Molluscs

Article 21(1): the allergen name is shown in the list of ingredients and emphasised "through a typeset that clearly distinguishes it from the rest of the list", for example font, style or background colour. With no list of ingredients, write "Contains" followed by the Annex II name. No mention is needed where the name of the food clearly refers to the allergen. Online, use bold in the text list; we read Article 14 as asking for the same information on the page, and bold is our suggestion for showing the emphasis there.

Tab 3: who is exempt from the nutrition declaration (Annex V, selection)

Write Exempt in O only for foods in Annex V, for example: unprocessed single-ingredient products, waters, herbs and spices, salt, coffee beans, tea and herbal infusions without other added ingredients (except flavourings that do not change the nutritional value), vinegars, yeast, chewing gum, food in packs whose largest surface is under 25 cm², and food, including handcrafted food, "directly supplied by the manufacturer of small quantities of products to the final consumer or to local retail establishments directly supplying the final consumer" (point 19). Drinks over 1.2% vol need no ingredients list and no nutrition declaration under Article 16(4). The Regulation has applied since 13 December 2014; the nutrition declaration since 13 December 2016 (Article 55).

Delivery checklist (Article 14(1)(b))

  • Every pack delivered carries all mandatory particulars, including the best-before or use-by date (Article 9(1)(f)).
  • If the shop repacks or splits food, the new pack has its own label: ask who is the operator under Article 8(1).
  • Made-to-order or loose food: allergen information goes with the delivery; check national rules for the rest (Article 44).

Frequently asked questions

Does the best-before date have to be on the product page?

No. Article 14(1)(a) excludes the particulars in Article 9(1)(f), the date of minimum durability or use-by date, from the before-purchase rule. It must be on the food at delivery (Article 14(1)(b)).

Is a photo of the label enough?

The Regulation does not say "text only", but Article 9(2) asks for words and numbers, Article 13(1) asks for information that is easily visible and clearly legible, and Article 14(1)(a) asks for it before purchase. A readable text block is the safer choice; this is our reading, not a ruling.

Do bakeries selling made-to-order bread online need a nutrition table?

For non-prepacked food, Article 44(1) makes only allergen information mandatory under the Regulation; the rest depends on national measures. For prepacked food, check Annex V point 19 for small producers supplying final consumers directly.
