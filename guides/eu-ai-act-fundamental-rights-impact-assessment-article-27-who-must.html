Fundamental rights impact assessment (FRIA) under the EU AI Act: who must do one, and the six contents

Last updated 20 Oct 2026 · European Union

Short answer. Under Article 27 of the EU AI Act, a fundamental rights impact assessment must be done before first use of a high-risk AI system by: (1) public bodies and private entities providing public services that use Annex III high-risk systems (except critical infrastructure), and (2) any deployer using AI for creditworthiness or credit scoring of individuals or risk assessment and pricing in life and health insurance. The results are notified to the market surveillance authority. On our reading of the Digital Omnibus on AI, these Annex III duties apply from 2 December 2027.

Who is in scope

DeployerFRIA needed?
Public authority using an Annex III high-risk systemYes (except critical infrastructure, Annex III point 2)
Private company providing a public service (for example education, health or housing services)Yes, for Annex III systems
Lender, NBFC, credit union or BNPL using AI to score individuals' creditworthinessYes (Annex III 5(b); fraud detection excluded)
Insurer using AI for life or health insurance risk and pricingYes (Annex III 5(c))
SME using AI for marketing copy or a website chatbotNo (not high-risk; Article 50 transparency may apply)

The six contents (Art. 27(1))

  1. How and for what purpose the system is used in your processes
  2. How long and how often it will be used
  3. Which groups of people are likely to be affected
  4. Specific risks of harm to those groups (using the provider's instructions)
  5. How human oversight is carried out
  6. What you will do if the risks materialise, including internal governance and complaint handling

Practical points

  • You can rely on an earlier FRIA, or one from the provider, for similar cases, but update it when something changes.
  • If you already ran a GDPR DPIA, the FRIA complements it rather than repeating it (Art. 27(4)).
  • The AI Office is to provide a template questionnaire for notifying results.

Accountants and advisers: who to flag now

  • Clients in lending or credit scoring
  • Clients selling or pricing life or health insurance
  • Clients delivering public services under contract
  • Each one: record the AI system, provider and use in an inventory

Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 moved the high-risk dates; see what applies now. Read Article 27 in the current consolidated text on EUR-Lex.

Frequently asked questions

Who must carry out a fundamental rights impact assessment under the EU AI Act?

Public bodies and private entities providing public services that deploy Annex III high-risk AI (except critical infrastructure), and deployers using AI for credit scoring of individuals or for life and health insurance risk assessment and pricing.

When must the FRIA be done?

Before the high-risk AI system is first used, and it must be updated if relevant elements change.

Is a FRIA the same as a GDPR DPIA?

No. Article 27(4) says the FRIA complements an existing data protection impact assessment.

Does a small business using ChatGPT need a FRIA?

Normally no. General office or marketing use is not an Annex III high-risk use; transparency duties under Article 50 may apply instead.
