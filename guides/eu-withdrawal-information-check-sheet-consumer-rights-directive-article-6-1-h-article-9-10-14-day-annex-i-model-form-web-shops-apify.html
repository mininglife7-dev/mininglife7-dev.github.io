Short answer: before a consumer is bound by a distance or off-premises contract, Article 6(1)(h) requires the conditions, time limit and procedures for exercising the right of withdrawal and the model withdrawal form in Annex I(B). Article 6(4) lets the trader meet points (h), (i) and (j) by supplying correctly filled Annex I(A) model instructions. If that information is missing, Article 10 can extend the withdrawal period to 12 months after the end of the initial period. Article 9 sets the ordinary 14-day period and when it starts; Article 11 covers how the consumer exercises it; Article 14 covers return costs; Article 16 lists exceptions (including personalised goods and digital content under (c) and (m)). Step 3: the five-minute checklist per shop

  1. Open a typical product page and the returns/Widerruf page. Is the right of withdrawal explained before the consumer is bound? (Column E / Article 6(1)(h).)
  2. Are the Annex I Part A model instructions present and filled in (trader name, address, period start)? (F / Article 6(4).)
  3. Is the Annex I Part B model form on the site or linked for download/print? (G.)
  4. Does the text say when the 14 days start (goods: physical possession; services/digital not on a tangible medium: day of conclusion)? (H / Article 9(2).)
  5. Does it say how to exercise (unequivocal statement or the model form; sending before expiry is enough)? (I / Article 11.)
  6. For goods: does it say who pays return costs? If not told, the consumer shall not bear them (J / Articles 6(1)(i), 6(6), 14(1)).
  7. If the shop claims an Article 16 exception (personalised goods, sealed goods, digital after performance), is Article 6(1)(k) notice also given? For digital content under 16(m): prior express consent, acknowledgement of loss, and Article 8(7) confirmation? (K, L.)
  8. Does the order confirmation on a durable medium include the Article 6(1) withdrawal information (unless already given on a durable medium before the contract)? (M / Article 8(7).)
  9. Take dated screenshots of product, returns page, checkout and confirmation (Article 6(9) burden of proof on the trader).

Frequently asked questions

Must an EU web shop tell consumers about the 14-day right of withdrawal before they order?

Article 6(1)(h) of Directive 2011/83/EU requires, before the consumer is bound by a distance or off-premises contract, information on 'where a right of withdrawal exists, the conditions, time limit and procedures for exercising that right in accordance with Article 11(1), as well as the model withdrawal form set out in Annex I(B)'. Article 6(4) says the information in points (h), (i) and (j) of Article 6(1) may be provided by means of the Annex I(A) model instructions on withdrawal; if those instructions are correctly filled in and supplied, the trader has fulfilled those information requirements.

What happens if the shop never gives the withdrawal information?

Article 10(1) provides that if the trader has not provided the consumer with the information on the right of withdrawal as required by Article 6(1)(h), the withdrawal period shall expire 12 months from the end of the initial withdrawal period determined in accordance with Article 9(2). Article 10(2) adds that if the trader later provides that information within those 12 months, the period expires 14 days (or 30 days where Member States have used Article 9(1a)) after the day the consumer receives it.

When does the 14-day period start?

Article 9(2) sets the start: for services, and for digital content not supplied on a tangible medium, from the day of the conclusion of the contract; for goods, from the day the consumer (or a third party indicated by the consumer, other than the carrier) acquires physical possession of the goods, with special rules for multiple goods, lots or regular delivery. Annex I Part A note 1 restates those starts in the model instructions.

Is providing the model withdrawal form enough?

Article 6(1)(h) requires both the information on conditions, time limit and procedures and the model form in Annex I(B). Article 6(4) lets the trader meet points (h), (i) and (j) by supplying correctly filled Annex I(A) model instructions. The consumer can exercise the right by any unequivocal statement (Article 11(1)); using the model form is optional for the consumer (Article 11(1)). The trader may also offer an online withdrawal form or button in addition (Article 11(3) and, from 19 June 2026, Article 11a for the online withdrawal function — covered in our separate Art 11a guide).

Can digital downloads escape the right of withdrawal?

Point (m) of Article 16 excepts the supply of digital content which is not supplied on a tangible medium if the performance has begun with the consumer's prior express consent and the consumer's acknowledgement that he thereby loses his right of withdrawal, and (after Omnibus Directive (EU) 2019/2161) the trader has provided confirmation in accordance with Article 8(7) — where the contract places the consumer under an obligation to pay. If those conditions are missing, the right of withdrawal typically remains. Grades on the sheet for Art 16(m) are our reading of those conditions against your answers.
