Short answer (checked 21 Oct 2026): from 1 April 2025, a taxpayer with aggregate annual turnover (AATO) of ₹10 crore or more cannot report an e-invoice on the IRP more than 30 days after the document date. It covers invoices, credit notes and debit notes, and the IRP blocks IRN generation once the window closes. GSTN's own example: a document dated 1 April cannot be reported after 30 April. Below ₹10 crore there is no such limit "as of now". Sources: GSTN's advisory Time limit for reporting e-invoice on the IRP portal (threshold lowered from ₹100 crore, which applied from the advisory of 13 September 2023) and the IRIS IRP release note of 31 March 2025. Check einvoice1.gst.gov.in for later changes before you act. This is a starting point, not tax advice.

Why October is the month it bites

Festive-season billing, a credit note issued late for a return, an export or SEZ invoice someone billed from the old software: these are the documents that sit unreported. A credit note dated 25 September 2026 has its last reporting day on 25 October 2026, a Sunday. Once the window closes the portal will not take it, and the client is left with a B2B document that has no IRN.
