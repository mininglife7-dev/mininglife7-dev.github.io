You corrected or deleted someone's data, but your AI tools already shared it? A GDPR Article 19 recipient notification sheet for small businesses

Updated 25 Oct 2026 · Disclosure: the EURO AI Register mentioned below is an Unadhi product with a free edition. This guide is for information only, not legal advice.

What you get: a finished recipient notification sheet in two tabs: a recipients list (who received which personal data from each AI tool, and how to send them a change) and a notifications log with one row per recipient per change, a column for the "impossible or disproportionate effort" reason, a status formula, the reply to "who did you share my data with?" and two made-up example rows.

Short answer: fixing a wrong record, deleting it or restricting it in your own system is only half the job. If the data were already sent on, for example to an AI chatbot or transcription supplier, a CRM, a mailing tool or a partner, Article 19 GDPR says you must pass the correction, erasure or restriction on to each of them, unless that is impossible or involves disproportionate effort, and tell the person who they are if they ask.

Step 1: when Article 19 is triggered

  • You carried out a rectification under Article 16 (for example a wrong AI-generated note in a customer file), an erasure under Article 17(1) or a restriction under Article 18. Use your rectification, erasure or restriction sheet for the request itself; this sheet starts once the change is made.
  • The personal data had been disclosed to a recipient. Article 4(9) covers anyone the data are disclosed to, "whether a third party or not", so check your AI suppliers as well as other businesses. Public authorities receiving data in a particular inquiry are not recipients.
  • Data you made public (for example on a website) are a separate duty: under Article 17(2) you take reasonable steps to inform other controllers processing it about the erasure request. Note it in the same log, but it is a different test.

F. What you told themThe corrected value, "please erase all data on this person" or "please restrict: store only, do not use" (Art. 18(2)), with the minimum identifiers neededArt. 19; Art. 5(1)(c)

Put the column letters in row 1 and the first change in row 2; the formulas assume that layout. Filter column J for "Finish before reply due" every Monday, and only send the person your final reply once every row for their request is Notified or has a reason in column I.

Step 4: AI tools need a closer look

  • Check whether a correction in your main system actually reaches copies kept elsewhere: chatbot conversation history, call transcripts and AI summaries, exports a colleague made, and a supplier's own logs. A sync that only covers some fields means extra rows.
  • For a restriction, ask the recipient to store the data but not use it, which includes not feeding it into AI features or automations while the restriction lasts (Art. 18(2)).
  • If a supplier keeps prompts or files for its own purposes, it may be acting as a controller for that part; that is a recipient in its own right and gets its own row.
  • Keep the message short and send only what the recipient needs to find and change the record.

This sheet is a working aid, not a legal opinion or proof of compliance. References are to Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (Articles 4, 5, 12, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19, 28 and 30 and Recital 66, as published on gdpr-info.eu) and to the judgment of the Court of Justice of 12 January 2023 in case C-154/21, Österreichische Post.
