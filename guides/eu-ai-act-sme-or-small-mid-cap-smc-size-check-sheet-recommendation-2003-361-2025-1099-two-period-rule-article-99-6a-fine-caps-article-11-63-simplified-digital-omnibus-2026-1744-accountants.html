Short answer: the Digital Omnibus on AI, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, added two definitions to the AI Act. An SME is defined by Commission Recommendation 2003/361/EC (fewer than 250 staff and turnover up to EUR 50 million and/or balance sheet up to EUR 43 million). A small mid-cap enterprise (SMC) is defined by Commission Recommendation (EU) 2025/1099 (not an SME, fewer than 750 staff, turnover up to EUR 150 million or balance sheet up to EUR 129 million). The difference matters. SMEs get the lower fine cap for every AI Act fine (Article 99(6)). SMCs get it only for Article 99(4) and (5) fines (new Article 99(6a)), not for Article 5 bans, and new Article 5 bans apply from 2 December 2026. SMEs and SMCs can both use a simplified technical documentation form (Article 11(1)). The simplified quality management system in Article 63(1) is for SMEs only, and only for SMEs with no partner or linked enterprises.

Why this lands on the accountant's desk

  • 23 days left to 2 December 2026, when the new Article 5(1)(ba)/(bb) bans start to apply (Article 5(1)(ba) check sheet). Size decides the fine cap, and only the accountant has the approved figures.
  • 388 days left to 2 December 2027, when high-risk rules for Annex III systems apply (Article 113(c) as amended). Providers that are SMEs or SMCs can budget for the simplified technical documentation form.
  • Partner and linked enterprises, public-body shareholdings and the two-period rule are read from the ownership chart and accounts, which are bookkeeping records.

Step 3: checklist

  1. Take headcount (annual work units: employees, owner-managers, active partners; not apprentices, students in vocational training or maternity/parental leave), turnover excluding VAT and the balance sheet total from the latest approved accounts.
  2. Draw the ownership chart. Add partner enterprises (25% or more) pro rata and linked enterprises (control) at 100%, unless already in consolidated accounts.
  3. Check whether public bodies control 25% or more of capital or voting rights, and whether an investor exception applies (business angels, venture capital, universities, institutional investors, small autonomous local authorities).
  4. Apply the two-period rule: status changes only when the ceilings are crossed in two consecutive accounting periods.
  5. Find the client's AI Act role for each AI system (role check sheet) and whether any system is high-risk (Annex III check).
  6. Check every AI use against the Article 5 list before 2 December 2026 (Article 5 list). For SMCs the Article 5 fine cap is the higher-of amount.
  7. Record the figures used, who decided and the date.

Frequently asked questions

Why does company size matter under the AI Act?

Several AI Act rules depend on size. For SMEs, including start-ups, each fine is capped at the lower of the fixed amount or the turnover percentage (Article 99(6)), while other companies face the higher of the two. Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (Digital Omnibus on AI) added definitions of SME and small mid-cap enterprise (SMC) to Article 3 and extended some SME measures to SMCs.

What is an SME for the AI Act?

New Article 3(14a) points to Article 2 of the Annex to Commission Recommendation 2003/361/EC: fewer than 250 persons and annual turnover not exceeding EUR 50 million and/or annual balance sheet total not exceeding EUR 43 million. Small: fewer than 50 persons and turnover and/or balance sheet not over EUR 10 million. Micro: fewer than 10 persons and turnover and/or balance sheet not over EUR 2 million.

What is a small mid-cap enterprise (SMC)?

New Article 3(14b) points to point 2 of the Annex to Commission Recommendation (EU) 2025/1099: an enterprise that is not an SME, employs fewer than 750 persons and has an annual turnover not exceeding EUR 150 million or an annual balance sheet total not exceeding EUR 129 million. Partner and linked enterprises and public-body control are counted in a similar way to the SME definition.

Do SMCs get the lower fine cap for Article 5 breaches?

No, on our reading. New Article 99(6a) gives SMCs the lower-of cap only for the fines in paragraphs 4 and 5 of Article 99. Article 5 fines are in paragraph 3 (up to EUR 35 000 000 or 7% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher), and Article 99(6) gives the lower-of cap for paragraphs 3, 4 and 5 to SMEs only. New Article 5 bans apply from 2 December 2026.

What happens if a client grows past a ceiling in one year?

Both recommendations have a two-period rule: crossing a headcount or financial ceiling at the closure of the accounts does not change the status unless the ceilings are crossed over two consecutive accounting periods (Recommendation 2003/361 Annex Article 4(2); Recommendation 2025/1099 point 4.2). New businesses without approved accounts use a bona fide estimate made during the financial year.

Which simplifications are for SMEs only?

Article 63(1) as amended lets SMEs, including start-ups, comply with certain quality management system elements in a simplified way, but only if they do not have partner or linked enterprises within the meaning of Recommendation 2003/361/EC. SMCs are not named there. SMEs and SMCs can both use the Commission's simplified technical documentation form (Article 11(1) as amended), and the quality management system is proportionate to size (Article 17(2) as amended).
