Trade fair exhibitor contact sheet: one row per exhibitor with booth, website, public inbox and phone, ready before the fair

Updated 20 Oct 2026 · Disclosure: the contact scraper linked below is an Unadhi product on the Apify Store. This guide is free, and the sheet works with a CSV from any tool or by copying contacts by hand. Current Apify pricing is only on the Store page.

What you get: one Google Sheet per trade fair with one row per exhibitor: company, hall and booth, website, the shared inbox (info@, sales@, export@...) and phone published on the exhibitor's own website, a ready wa.me link where the site has one, the company LinkedIn page, and a flag that says contact before the fair or visit the booth. A second tab is your walking list: the exhibitors with no usable contact online, sorted by hall. You build it from the exhibitor list, one scraper run on the exhibitors' websites and a handful of formulas.

Who this is for

  • Visitors and buyers who want meetings booked before day one instead of walking every aisle.
  • Exhibitors who want to know their neighbours, suppliers and possible partners at the same fair.
  • Export promotion staff, agents and consultants who prepare a delegation and need one clean list for the group.

Step 1: copy the exhibitor list (tab exhibitors)

Columns: A company, B hall/booth, C country, D website. Take them from the organiser's exhibitor list, the fair app's export or the PDF catalogue.

Do not point a scraper at the organiser's directory. Check the fair's terms first; many exhibitor directories do not allow automated collection. Copy by hand or use an official export. The scraper in step 3 only visits each exhibitor's own company website, and it skips sites whose terms forbid scraping and pages that robots.txt disallows.

Step 2: websites to domains

In E2 of the exhibitors tab:

=ARRAYFORMULA(IF(D2:D="",,LOWER(REGEXREPLACE(REGEXREPLACE(TRIM(D2:D),"^(https?://)?(www\.)?",""),"[/?#].*$",""))))

That turns https://www.Example-Spices.in/en/ into example-spices.in. The scraper writes its domain field the same way (host name in lower case, without www.), so this is the key you join on in step 5. Rows with no website go straight to the walk tab.

Step 3: run the contact scraper on those domains

  1. Open Website Contact Scraper: Emails, Phones & WhatsApp and click Try for free.
  2. Paste column E into Websites or domains (plain list) (urls), one per line. Duplicates of the same domain are removed, so two exhibitors sharing one website are visited once.
  3. Use this input (JSON tab):
{
  "urls": ["example-spices.in", "sample-pack.de", "demo-textiles.com"],
  "maxPagesPerSite": 4,
  "defaultCountry": "IN",
  "checkEmailMx": true,
  "includePersonalEmails": false,
  "includePersonProfiles": false
}

Set defaultCountry to the country most exhibitors are from, so local numbers without a country code are read correctly. Keep includePersonalEmails and includePersonProfiles off: addresses and LinkedIn profiles that identify one person are dropped and only counted. Four pages per site (homepage plus the most contact-like pages such as contact, about, impressum) is the default and is usually enough. You pay only Apify platform usage for pages actually fetched; blocked and failed sites are not charged.

When the run finishes, open the dataset, choose Export → CSV and download it.

Step 4: import the CSV (tab scrape)

File → Import → Upload, "Insert new sheet", name it scrape. Do not rename headers. Apify flattens lists into numbered columns, so you will see domain, status, emails/0/email, emails/0/mxValid, phones/0/phone, socials/whatsapp/0/url, socials/linkedin/0/url and many more. Column order can change between runs; the formulas below find columns by header name.

Step 5: the sheet tab

A2 to C2 come from the exhibitors tab (=exhibitors!A2, =exhibitors!B2, =exhibitors!E2). For the other columns use this one formula pattern, changing only the header in quotes:

=IFERROR(INDEX(scrape!$A:$ZZ,
  MATCH($C2, INDEX(scrape!$A:$ZZ, 0, MATCH("domain", scrape!$1:$1, 0)), 0),
  MATCH("emails/0/email", scrape!$1:$1, 0)), "")

Step 6: the walk tab

=SORT(FILTER({sheet!B2:B, sheet!A2:A, sheet!K2:K}, LEFT(sheet!K2:K,5)="Visit", sheet!A2:A<>""), 1, TRUE)

Sorted by hall and booth, this is the list you print or keep on your phone for day one.

Before you write to anyone

  • One message per company, about the fair. Name the booth, say who you are and why this exhibitor, and ask for a time slot. Include a way to opt out.
  • Rules differ by country. In Germany, for example, UWG section 7(2) no. 2 treats advertising by email without the addressee's prior express consent as an unreasonable nuisance, and that applies to businesses as well as consumers. For German exhibitors the safer route is the booth, or a phone call where their consent can at least be presumed (section 7(2) no. 1). Elsewhere, check the local rules.
  • Shared inboxes can still reach a person. Work emails and numbers of identifiable people are personal data under the GDPR and India's DPDP Act 2023. Keep the sheet to what you need for the fair and delete it afterwards, or document why you keep it.
  • In India, unsolicited commercial calls and SMS are restricted under TRAI rules; WhatsApp has its own business messaging policies.

General information, not legal advice.

What not to do

  • Do not bulk-mail the whole list. A fair list is a reason for one relevant conversation, not a mailing list.
  • Do not turn on personal emails "to get more rows". The sheet is built on shared inboxes on purpose.
  • Do not paste the organiser's directory URL into the scraper.

Check before you act

  • Domains in column C match the exhibitors' real websites (spot-check five).
  • Every "Contact before the fair" row has status ok and an inbox or phone.
  • German (and other consent-first) exhibitors are on the booth or phone route, not email.
  • Your message names the booth and has an opt-out.
  • Delete or archive the sheet after the fair.
