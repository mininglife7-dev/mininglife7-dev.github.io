Short answer: since 2 August 2026, any business that offers an AI tool under its own name that generates text, images, audio or video must make sure the outputs are marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as AI-generated (AI Act Article 50(2)). This sits with the provider of the tool, which includes small firms that wrap a bought-in model in their own app (Article 3(3), 3(68)). The Digital Omnibus on AI, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, gave tools already on the market before 2 August 2026 until 2 December 2026 to comply (new Article 111(4)). That is 30 days from today. Tools launched since 2 August 2026 had no such transition. Features that only assist standard editing, or do not substantially alter the input or its meaning, are outside the duty. An open-source licence does not take a tool out of Article 50 (Article 2(12)).

Why this lands on the accountant's desk

  • Software houses, agencies and app makers are often clients of small accounting firms. Many added an "AI write", "AI image" or "AI voice" button in 2025 or 2026 and do not know they count as a provider.
  • Enterprise customers' supplier questionnaires now ask whether AI outputs are marked under Article 50. The client needs one clear answer, and the evidence behind it.
  • Fines for Article 50 breaches are in Article 99(4)(g): up to EUR 15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover, and for SMEs and start-ups whichever is lower (Article 99(6)). Whether this needs a mention in year-end risk notes is a judgement for the adviser (our reading, not a rule we read).

Step 3: the 10-question checklist

  1. Have we listed every feature in our products that generates text, images, audio or video, including features built on a bought-in model?
  2. Is each one offered under our own name or trademark, free or paid? If yes, we are its provider (Art 3(3)).
  3. Can people in the EU use it, or is its output used in the EU (Art 2(1)(a), (c))?
  4. When was it first made available on the EU market? Before 2 August 2026 means 2 December 2026; later means the duty applied from launch (Art 111(4)).
  5. Does it only assist standard editing or leave the input and its meaning substantially unchanged? If so, write down why (Art 50(2), last sentence).
  6. What marking do we use: metadata, watermark, cryptographic provenance, logging, fingerprints or a mix (recital 133)?
  7. If we rely on the model supplier's marking, do we have it in writing, and have we checked it on our own outputs?
  8. Does the mark survive download, export, resizing and our own post-processing?
  9. If the tool also chats with people, does it tell them they are dealing with AI at the first interaction, unless obvious (Art 50(1), 50(5))?
  10. Do our terms or help pages tell customers about their own duty to disclose deep fakes and public-interest text (Art 50(4))?

Frequently asked questions

What does Article 50(2) of the AI Act require?

Providers of AI systems, including general-purpose AI systems, generating synthetic audio, image, video or text content must ensure that the outputs are marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated or manipulated. The technical solutions must be effective, interoperable, robust and reliable as far as technically feasible. The duty does not apply to the extent the system performs an assistive function for standard editing or does not substantially alter the input data provided by the deployer or its semantics (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 50(2)).

Is there extra time for generative AI tools already on the market?

Yes, a short one. Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (the Digital Omnibus on AI) added Article 111(4): providers of AI systems generating synthetic audio, image, video or text content that were placed on the market before 2 August 2026 must take the necessary steps to comply with Article 50(2) by 2 December 2026. Systems placed on the market from 2 August 2026 get no transition under that paragraph.

We build on a bought-in AI model. Are we the provider?

If you develop an AI system, or have it developed, and place it on the market or put it into service under your own name or trademark, whether for payment or free of charge, you are a provider under Article 3(3). Article 3(68) calls a provider that integrates someone else's AI model a downstream provider. Recital 133 says marking can be implemented at the model level, which helps the downstream provider, but the Article 50(2) duty itself sits with the provider of the AI system.

Does an open-source licence exempt us?

No. Article 2(12) says the AI Act does not apply to AI systems released under free and open-source licences unless they are placed on the market or put into service as high-risk AI systems or as an AI system that falls under Article 5 or 50.
