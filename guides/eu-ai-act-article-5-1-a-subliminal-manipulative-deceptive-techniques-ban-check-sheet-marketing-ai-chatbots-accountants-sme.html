Short answer: from 2 February 2025 (AI Act Article 113), the Union prohibits placing on the market, putting into service or using an AI system that deploys subliminal techniques beyond a person's consciousness or purposefully manipulative or deceptive techniques, with the objective or the effect of materially distorting the behaviour of a person or a group of persons by appreciably impairing their ability to make an informed decision, thereby causing them to take a decision that they would not have otherwise taken in a manner that causes or is reasonably likely to cause significant harm (Article 5(1)(a); recital 29). Recital 29 also says common and legitimate commercial practices (for example advertising) that comply with applicable law should not, in themselves, be regarded as the ban, and preserves lawful medical treatment practices. The Digital Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744) added intimate-image and CSAM bans as Article 5(1)(ba)/(bb) from 2 December 2026; it did not amend Article 5(1)(a) (our Omnibus check). Fines for Article 5 breaches sit under Article 99(3): up to EUR 35 000 000 or 7% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher (for SMEs, the lower of those two caps — Article 99(6)).

Why this lands on the accountant's, tax adviser's or agency's desk

  • Marketing AI, sales chatbots and personalisation can cross into purposefully manipulative or deceptive techniques that distort a buyer's decision — Article 5(1)(a) targets exactly that path when significant harm is caused or reasonably likely.
  • Checkout dark patterns and false-scarcity upsells powered by AI are the classic client questions after a complaint or a regulator letter.
  • The ban has already applied since 2 February 2025. Anything still live that fits the test is overdue, not a future deadline.
  • Article 5 fines are the top tier (Article 99(3)). Accountants are asked "is our marketing AI illegal?" — one row per practice answers with the article behind it.

Step 3: checklist

  1. Is an AI system involved (Articles 3(1), 5(1)(a))?
  2. Is it placed, put into service or used in the EU, or is its output used in the EU (Article 2)?
  3. Does it deploy subliminal techniques beyond consciousness, or purposefully manipulative or deceptive techniques (Article 5(1)(a); recital 29)?
  4. Is there an objective or effect of materially distorting the behaviour of a person or group?
  5. Does that appreciably impair the ability to make an informed decision, causing a decision they would not otherwise have taken?
  6. Is significant harm (physical, psychological or financial — recital 29) caused or reasonably likely? Harm may accumulate over time.
  7. If a lawful medical treatment or common legitimate commercial practice is claimed, what applicable law does it comply with (recital 29)? Keep it in writing.
  8. If the row is STOP or IN SCOPE, has the practice been switched off? The ban has applied since 2 February 2025 (Article 113).
  9. Have we told the client that Article 5 fines are the top tier (Article 99(3)/(6))?
  10. Who decided, on what date, and when is the next review?

Frequently asked questions

What does Article 5(1)(a) of the AI Act ban?

It prohibits placing on the market, putting into service or using an AI system that deploys subliminal techniques beyond a person's consciousness or purposefully manipulative or deceptive techniques, with the objective or the effect of materially distorting the behaviour of a person or a group of persons by appreciably impairing their ability to make an informed decision, thereby causing them to take a decision that they would not have otherwise taken in a manner that causes or is reasonably likely to cause significant harm (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 5(1)(a)).

When did the manipulative / subliminal techniques ban start to apply?

Chapters I and II of the AI Act, which include Article 5, apply from 2 February 2025 (Article 113). Non-compliance with Article 5 can attract administrative fines of up to EUR 35 000 000 or 7% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher; for SMEs the lower of those two caps applies (Article 99(3) and 99(6)).

Are ordinary ads and personalisation automatically banned?

No. Recital 29 says common and legitimate commercial practices, for example in the field of advertising, that comply with the applicable law should not, in themselves, be regarded as constituting harmful manipulative AI-enabled practices. The ban needs subliminal or purposefully manipulative/deceptive techniques, material distortion of behaviour that appreciably impairs an informed decision, and significant harm (or a reasonable likelihood of it). The sheet marks a recital 29 claim as CHECK BY HAND so you keep the legal basis.

Did the Digital Omnibus change Article 5(1)(a)?

No. Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 amended Article 5 by inserting points (ba) and (bb) on non-consensual intimate material and child sexual abuse material (from 2 December 2026). We searched the Omnibus text: it does not amend Article 5(1)(a) subliminal or manipulative techniques.

How is this different from the Article 50(4) deepfake disclosure sheet?

Article 5(1)(a) is a prohibition that has applied since 2 February 2025 when the technique, distortion, informed-decision and significant-harm tests are met. Article 50(4) is a transparency/disclosure duty for certain artificially generated or manipulated content that applies from 2 August 2026. A practice can fail one sheet and pass the other; run both if the client uses synthetic media in marketing.
