The formulas return the user status (pre-existing or new user, or outside), the date the self-certification is due, days left to 1 January 2027, a form status that names each missing item (signature and date, name and address, TIN for each Member State, date of birth, entity criteria or controlling persons), a block-risk warning after two reminders and 60 days, a to-do list with the provision behind each line and a result (OK, FIX, URGENT, UPDATE, CHECK BY HAND or OUTSIDE). Short answer: DAC8 has applied since 1 January 2026 (Article 2(1)). Crypto service providers must collect a tax self-certification from every customer. For customers who already had an account on 31 December 2025, the deadline is 1 January 2027, 53 days from 9 November 2026 (Annex VI, Section III, A(1) and B(1)(a)). If a client ignores the request and two reminders, the provider must stop the client's reportable transactions once 60 days have passed (Section V, A(2)). The 2026 data, with the client's TIN, is reported in 2027 and reaches the client's own tax authority by 30 September 2027 (Article 8ad(6)).

Why accountants and tax advisers should look at this now

  • Clients are getting forms they do not understand. Exchanges and brokers are emailing "confirm your tax residence and TIN" requests. Many clients ignore them or answer with only one country when they have two residences.
  • A blocked account in December is a client problem you will hear about. After the first request, two reminders and 60 days, the provider must prevent reportable transactions, which covers purchases and sales against euros, crypto-to-crypto swaps and transfers (Section V, A(2); Section IV, C).
  • The tax office will match the data. The report includes per-asset totals for purchases and sales against euros, crypto-to-crypto exchanges and transfers (Section II, B(3)). If your client's 2026 return does not show disposals that the provider reported, expect questions after 30 September 2027.
  • It is a short, billable job. Ask each client which providers they use, fill one row per account and send the to-do list.

Step 1: list the accounts

  1. Ask each client: which exchanges, brokers, banks offering crypto, and custodial wallet apps have you used since 2025? When did you open each account? In which countries are you tax-resident?
  2. One row per account. Companies, partnerships and trusts that hold crypto get their own rows as Entity.
  3. Column D asks whether the provider is a Reporting Crypto-Asset Service Provider in the EU: authorised or notified under the MiCA Regulation (EU) 2023/1114, or tax-resident, incorporated, managed or with a regular place of business in a Member State (Annex VI, Section I, A). Providers of crypto-asset services that are not MiCA crypto-asset service providers ("Crypto-Asset Operators") must register in a single Member State (Article 8ad(7)). If you are not sure, enter Not sure and ask the provider.
  4. Pull the request emails from the client's inbox: the date of the first request (column O) and the number of reminders (column P).

Client checklist (before 1 January 2027)

  • List every exchange, broker, bank and custodial wallet app used since 2025.
  • Answer each provider's tax self-certification request; do not wait for the second reminder.
  • Give every country where you are tax-resident and the TIN for each one.
  • Check name, residence address and date of birth match your ID and tax records.
  • Companies: state whether you are an Active Entity, or give the details of each controlling person.
  • Moved country or gained a second residence? Send a new form.
  • Save a dated copy or screenshot of each submitted form and send it to your accountant.
  • Keep your own 2026 records of purchases, sales, crypto-to-crypto exchanges and transfers; the provider's totals will reach the tax office by 30 September 2027.

Limits and our reading

  • National law decides the details. DAC8 is a directive; Member States transpose it and set penalties (Article 25a). We did not read any national law, provider form or tax authority guidance.
  • Our reading, flagged: counting the 60 days in Section V, A(2) from the initial request; treating an entity form as complete if it gives either the Active Entity or Excluded Person criteria or the Controlling Persons' details; using the account opening date as the date the relationship was established; and treating an account opened on 31 December 2025 as pre-existing ("as of 31 December 2025").
  • Not covered: whether a crypto-asset is a Reportable Crypto-Asset (Section IV, A), Identification Services, the new e-money and CBDC rules for financial accounts (Annex I changes), the Crypto-Asset Operator registration procedure, and the client's own income tax treatment.
  • The sheet tracks forms and dates. It does not decide anyone's tax residence.

FAQ

What is the 1 January 2027 deadline in DAC8?

Under Annex VI, Section III of Directive 2011/16/EU as added by Council Directive (EU) 2023/2226, a Reporting Crypto-Asset Service Provider must obtain a self-certification from each Pre-existing Crypto-Asset User 'by 1 January 2027'. A pre-existing user is one that 'has established a relationship with the Reporting Crypto-Asset Service Provider as of 31 December 2025'. New users give it when the relationship starts.

What must a valid self-certification contain?

For an individual: first and last name, residence address, Member State(s) of residence for tax purposes, the TIN for each Member State and date of birth; it must be signed or otherwise positively affirmed and dated at the latest at the date of receipt (Annex VI, Section III, C(1)). For an entity: legal name, address, Member State(s) of residence, TIN for each Member State, and either the criteria for Active Entity or Excluded Person status or the details of each Controlling Person (C(2)).

Can the exchange block my client's account?

Yes, for reportable transactions. Annex VI, Section V, A(2): where a user does not provide the information 'after two reminders following the initial request ... but not prior to the expiration of 60 days', the provider 'shall prevent the Crypto-Asset User from performing Reportable Transactions'. Our reading counts the 60 days from the initial request.

When does the tax office get my client's 2026 crypto data?

Providers report 'annually in the calendar year following the year to which the information relates', starting with 2026 (Annex VI, Section II, D). The tax authority that receives it exchanges it with the client's Member State 'within nine months following the end of the calendar year' (Article 8ad(6)), so 2026 data reaches other Member States by 30 September 2027. The national deadline for the provider's own report is set by national law.

What gets reported?

Name, address, Member State(s) of residence, TIN(s) and, for individuals, date and place of birth (place of birth only where domestic law requires it), plus per crypto-asset type the aggregate amounts, units and number of transactions for purchases and sales against fiat, exchanges between crypto-assets, retail payments over USD 50 000, and transfers in and out (Annex VI, Section II, B and C).

Is a self-hosted wallet covered?

DAC8 puts the duties on Reporting Crypto-Asset Service Providers (Annex VI, Section I), not on the wallet itself. A wallet the client controls alone has no provider to report it, but transfers from a provider to addresses not known to belong to a service provider are reported in aggregate (Section II, B(3)(i)). The client's own tax return duties are national law and are not covered here.
