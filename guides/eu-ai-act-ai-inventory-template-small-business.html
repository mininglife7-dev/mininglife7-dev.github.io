EU AI Act AI inventory for small businesses: what to record for each AI system (free template)

Updated 13 Oct 2026 · Disclosure: the EURO AI Register mentioned below is an Unadhi product with a free edition.

What you can hold at the end: an Article 4 AI-use and AI-literacy record for your business, listing the AI tools you use. You can keep it in the EURO AI Register, which is free for up to 10 AI systems.

Short answer: list every AI system you build or use, one entry each, with who owns it, which vendor and model sit behind it, what you use it for, your role under the EU AI Act (provider, deployer, importer or distributor), the result of a quick risk screen, the duties that follow, and the evidence that you acted on them. The Act doesn't use the word "inventory", but you can't apply its rules to systems you haven't listed, so this is the usual first step.

These duties already apply. AI literacy for staff (Article 4) since 2 Feb 2025; chatbot and AI-content transparency (Article 50) since 2 Aug 2026 (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689).

Which EU AI Act rules already apply

Since / fromWhat appliesWhy it matters for your list
2 Feb 2025Banned practices (Article 5) and AI literacy for staff (Article 4)You need to know every system to rule out banned uses and to brief the people who use them
2 Aug 2026Transparency duties (Article 50), such as telling people they are talking to a chatbot or that content is AI-generatedFlag every customer-facing chatbot, voice bot or content generator
2 Dec 2027High-risk rules for Annex III uses such as recruitment and credit scoring, as set by the Digital Omnibus on AI (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744)Mark hiring, credit, education and biometric uses now so you have time to prepare
2 Aug 2028High-risk rules for AI in regulated products (Annex I)Relevant if AI is part of a product you make or import

Check the official text before relying on a date: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 on EUR-Lex and Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 on EUR-Lex.

A 30-minute way to start

  • Ask each team which AI tools they use, including free ones and features switched on inside tools you already pay for.
  • Add one row per system with the fields above. "Not sure" is a fine answer for now; mark it so you come back to it.
  • Run the banned-practice and high-risk screens. Anything that touches hiring, credit, education, biometrics or medical use goes to a qualified lawyer.
  • Write a short AI use policy and brief the staff who use these tools. Record the date as evidence.
  • Set a reminder to review the list every quarter and whenever you add a new tool.
