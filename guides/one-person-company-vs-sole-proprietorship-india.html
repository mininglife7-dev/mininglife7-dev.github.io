One Person Company vs sole proprietorship in India: which one fits a one-owner business

Last updated 13 Oct 2026 · India

Short answer. A sole proprietorship is you trading under a name: there is no separate legal person, so the business's debts are your debts, and there is no company registration to do. An OPC is a company with one member: it is a separate legal person, it is registered with MCA through SPICe+, it must name a nominee, and it carries company paperwork every year. Choose a proprietorship for a small, low-risk start; consider an OPC when contracts, clients or lenders want a company, or when you want the business's liabilities separated from your own.

Why people pick an OPC

  • Separation. The company, not you personally, signs contracts and owns assets.
  • Credibility. Some corporate clients and platforms prefer to contract with a company.
  • Room to grow. Since April 2021 there is no forced conversion at ₹50 lakh capital or ₹2 crore turnover, and conversion is allowed at any time (PIB).

Why people stay proprietors

  • Lower running cost. No audit or Registrar filings just because you exist as a company.
  • Simplicity. One tax return and the registrations you actually need.
  • Easy exit. Closing a proprietorship is simpler than winding up a company.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing OPC only for the word "company" on a visiting card, then missing the yearly filings.
  • Forgetting the nominee is a real role: pick someone who agrees and understands it.
  • Planning to bring in a co-founder soon. If that is likely within a year, ask your CA whether a private limited company or LLP from day one saves a conversion.
  • Comparing tax only. Company and individual tax work differently; get your CA to run your actual numbers before deciding.

Quick decision checklist

  • Do clients or lenders insist on a company? (OPC)
  • Is the business low-risk and small for now? (Proprietorship)
  • Do you have an Indian-citizen nominee willing to sign? (Needed for OPC)
  • Can you budget for yearly audit and Registrar filings? (Needed for OPC)
  • Will a second owner join soon? (Consider private limited or LLP)

Frequently asked questions

Is a sole proprietorship a separate legal entity in India?

No. The proprietor and the business are the same person in law, so the business's debts are the proprietor's own.

Who can start a One Person Company?

Only a natural person who is an Indian citizen, whether resident in India or not. A person can be a member of only one OPC, and the OPC must name an eligible nominee.

Can an OPC have more than one owner?

No. To add members it must convert into a private or public company by filing INC-6 within 30 days of the special resolution.

Is an OPC forced to convert after crossing ₹2 crore turnover?

No. The forced conversion at ₹50 lakh paid-up capital or ₹2 crore average annual turnover was removed from 1 April 2021.
