EU AI Act AI literacy (Article 4): “what do we actually have to provide?” A small-business checklist

Last updated 13 Oct 2026 · European Union

What you can hold at the end: an Article 4 AI-use and AI-literacy record for your business, listing the AI tools you use. You can keep it in the EURO AI Register, which is free for up to 10 AI systems.

Short answer. If your business uses AI systems (a deployer) or builds them (a provider), Article 4 of the EU AI Act asks you to take measures to support the AI literacy of staff and others who use AI on your behalf. It has applied since 2 February 2025. After the AI Omnibus amendments, the Commission says no specific or 'sufficient' level is mandated, there is no obligation to measure staff knowledge and no mandatory training or certificate. What it expects is a sensible, documented programme that fits your AI use and its risks (Commission AI literacy Q&A).

These duties already apply. AI literacy for staff (Article 4) since 2 Feb 2025; chatbot and AI-content transparency (Article 50) since 2 Aug 2026 (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689).

Why the rule exists

Most AI harm in small firms is not a rogue model; it is a person trusting an output they should have checked, or pasting data they should not have. Article 4 targets that: the people who touch the tool should know what it is, what it is for and where it goes wrong.

The 4 steps the Commission lists

  1. General understanding. What is AI, how does it work, what AI do we use, what are the opportunities and dangers?
  2. Your role. Do you build AI systems (provider) or only use systems made by others (deployer)?
  3. The risk of each system. What must staff know when using it, which risks should they watch for, and how do they reduce them?
  4. Tailor it. Build actions on that analysis, considering people's technical knowledge, experience and training, and the context and people the AI is used on.

Who counts: anyone in your organisation dealing directly with an AI system, and also people acting on your behalf such as contractors or service providers. The Commission also notes that simply asking staff to read the instructions for use is often ineffective.

Common mistakes

  • Buying a "certificate" course and stopping there. No certificate is required; what counts is training that fits your tools and risks.
  • Forgetting contractors and freelancers who use AI on your behalf.
  • One generic slide deck for everyone. A support agent using a chatbot needs different points from a developer fine-tuning a model.
  • No record. The law does not prescribe a form, but a short written note of what you did, for whom and when, is the easiest way to show you took measures.
  • Confusing deployer training for high-risk systems (Art. 26: staff must be trained for human oversight) with the general Article 4 duty. If you use a high-risk system, both apply.

One-page checklist

  • List every AI tool in use (including free chatbots staff use for work)
  • Mark your role per tool: provider or deployer
  • Note the main risk per tool (wrong output, personal data, bias, over-reliance)
  • Short training or briefing per group, with dos and don'ts
  • Include contractors who use AI for you
  • Keep a dated record and review it when tools change

Frequently asked questions

Does the EU AI Act require an AI literacy certificate?

No. The Commission's Q&A says no strict requirements or mandatory trainings are imposed. Organisations should take measures that fit their role, their AI systems and the risks.

When did the AI literacy obligation start?

2 February 2025, together with the prohibited-practices rules.

Do we have to test employees' AI knowledge?

No. Article 4 does not require measuring employees' AI knowledge, and after the AI Omnibus no specific 'sufficient' level is mandated.

Does Article 4 apply if we only use ChatGPT-style tools?

If you use AI systems in your business you are a deployer, and Article 4 asks deployers to support the AI literacy of staff and others using those systems on their behalf.

When do the high-risk rules apply?

Per the Commission, high-risk use cases in Annex III areas apply from 2 December 2027 and high-risk AI in regulated products (Annex I) from 2 August 2028, after the AI Omnibus.
