Short answer: Member States must have their Platform Work Directive laws in force by 2 December 2026 (Article 29(1)). From then, a digital labour platform may not feed banned data into its algorithms (Article 7), must treat this processing as high risk and do a DPIA that seeks workers' views (Article 8), must explain in writing what its systems monitor and decide (Article 9), must have a human take every suspension or deactivation decision (Article 10(5)), and must answer review requests within two weeks (Article 11(2)).

What to tell a client in one minute

"If your app or website organises paid work by individuals for customers and uses software to assign, rate, track or pay them, the new platform work rules probably apply to you from 2 December 2026, whether your people are employees or self-employed. List each automated system. Stop using emotion, private chat or off-duty data. Write a plain notice explaining what the system watches and decides. Make sure a person, not the system, suspends or deactivates anyone, and that someone answers review requests within two weeks. Your existing contracts also face a new presumption of employment from that date."

Why AI matters here

The Directive does not use the word AI; it covers automated monitoring systems (systems that monitor, supervise or evaluate work performance by electronic means, Article 2(1)(h)) and automated decision-making systems (systems that take or support decisions that significantly affect people, including recruitment, task allocation, earnings and pricing of tasks, working time and account suspension, Article 2(1)(i)). Any AI tool a platform uses for these jobs is included, and Articles 7(3), 9(1)(c) and 12(4) extend some duties to any automated system that affects the people "in any manner". A client that buys an AI dispatching or rating tool from a supplier still carries the duties as the platform.

The rules the sheet uses

  • Scope: all four Article 2(1)(a) tests must be met; Article 2(2) excludes asset-sharing and non-professional resale services. The Directive applies to platforms organising work in the Union wherever they are established (Article 1(3)); intermediaries' workers get the same protection (Article 3).
  • Banned data (Article 7(1)): emotional or psychological state; private conversations, including with other workers and representatives; data while the person is not offering or performing platform work; predicting the exercise of fundamental rights (association, collective bargaining, information and consultation); inferring racial or ethnic origin, migration status, political opinions, religious or philosophical beliefs, disability, health, emotional state, trade union membership, sex life or sexual orientation; biometric identification against a database. From the start of recruitment (Article 7(2)).
  • DPIA (Article 8): the processing is high risk under GDPR Article 35(1); the platform seeks the views of the people and their representatives, and gives the DPIA to workers' representatives.
  • Information (Article 9): a written, plain-language document on what is monitored, the aim, recipients of the data, the decisions taken or supported, the main parameters and their relative importance, and the grounds for restricting, suspending or terminating accounts or refusing payment. Concise version to each person at the latest on the first working day, before changes, and on request (Article 9(3)); to candidates before recruitment starts (Article 9(5)); free data portability tools, including ratings and reviews (Article 9(6)).
  • Human oversight (Article 10): an impact evaluation of individual automated decisions with workers' representatives regularly and at least every two years; enough trained staff with authority to override; a human takes every restriction, suspension or termination decision (Article 10(5)).
  • Human review (Article 11): explanation on request and access to a named contact person; written reasons for suspension, termination, refused payment or status decisions at the latest when they take effect; substantiated written reply to a review request within two weeks; rectify an infringing decision within two weeks of its adoption or offer adequate compensation. Not for business users under Regulation (EU) 2019/1150 (Article 11(5)).
  • Safety, consultation and information (Articles 12 to 14): risk evaluation of the systems and no undue pressure on platform workers; consultation of representatives before introducing or substantially changing the systems, with an expert paid by the platform if it has more than 250 workers in the Member State (Article 13(3)); without representatives, written information directly to workers (Article 14).
  • Employment presumption (Article 5): rebuttable presumption of employment where facts indicate direction and control under national law; not in tax, criminal or social security proceedings unless national law says so; for contracts running on 2 December 2026, only from that date.

Checklist before 2 December 2026

  • List every automated system that monitors, rates, allocates, prices, pays or deactivates.
  • Remove Article 7(1) data from every system, including in recruitment.
  • DPIA done with workers' views, given to representatives.
  • Plain-language information document ready; concise version for new starters on day one.
  • Deactivation and suspension decisions moved to a named person.
  • Contact person named; two-week review reply process in place.
  • Impact evaluation date set (at least every two years).
  • National transposing law of each country where work is done checked.

Limits and our reading

  • Read in full: Articles 1 to 5, 7 to 15, 27 to 30 of Directive (EU) 2024/2831. Read in part: Articles 6, 16 and 17. Not read: Articles 18 to 26, recitals in detail, national transposing laws, Commission guidance. We did not check which Member States have adopted their laws; obligations reach platforms only through national law, which may add rules (Article 28).
  • Our reading: the sheet treats the Article 10(1) evaluation as overdue two years after the last one; it does not test whether facts show direction and control (Article 5), whether a person is a business user under Regulation (EU) 2019/1150, or Article 12 safety duties in detail; columns B to P are your answers, not checked. Article 12 and 13 duties are written for platform workers, so the sheet shows them as notes.

Questions

When does the Platform Work Directive apply?

Member States must bring their national laws into force by 2 December 2026 (Article 29(1) of Directive (EU) 2024/2831). Platforms are bound through those national laws, so check the law of each Member State where the work is done.

Is my client a digital labour platform?

Only if the service meets all four tests in Article 2(1)(a): provided at least partly at a distance by website or app, at the request of a customer, organising paid work by individuals as a necessary and essential part, and using automated monitoring or decision-making systems. Services whose main purpose is to share assets, or to let non-professionals resell goods, are excluded (Article 2(2)).

Do the algorithmic management rules apply to self-employed people too?

Yes for the personal data rules. Article 1(2) applies the algorithmic management measures to persons performing platform work even without an employment contract. Some duties, such as Article 12 safety and health, are written for platform workers only.

What data can a platform no longer process with its algorithms?

Article 7(1) bans processing data on emotional or psychological state, private conversations, data collected while the person is not offering or doing platform work, data to predict the exercise of fundamental rights such as union activity, data to infer sensitive traits such as ethnic origin, health or sexual orientation, and biometric identification against a database. It applies from the start of recruitment (Article 7(2)).

Can an algorithm suspend or deactivate a worker's account?

No. Any decision to restrict, suspend or terminate the contractual relationship or account, or of equivalent detriment, must be taken by a human being (Article 10(5)), and the person gets a written statement of reasons at the latest on the day it takes effect (Article 11(1)).

How fast must a platform answer a review request?

With a sufficiently precise and substantiated written reply without undue delay and in any event within two weeks of receiving the request (Article 11(2)). A decision that infringed the person's rights must be rectified within two weeks of its adoption, or adequate compensation offered (Article 11(3)).

Does the employment presumption affect tax?

The legal presumption of employment in Article 5 does not apply to tax, criminal or social security proceedings, unless national law applies it there (Article 5(3)). For contracts that were already running on 2 December 2026 it applies only from that date (Article 5(6)).
