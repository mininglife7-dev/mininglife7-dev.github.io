Privacy by default for your AI tools: a GDPR Article 25 settings sheet for small businesses

Updated 23 Oct 2026 · Disclosure: the EURO AI Register mentioned below is an Unadhi product with a free edition. This guide is for information only, not legal advice.

What you get: a finished privacy-by-default settings sheet for your AI tools: 10 columns, one row per tool (the data the task needs, what actually goes in, training on your inputs, history period, share links, connected apps, who sees results, less identifying input, and the setting you chose with a date and reason), each tied to the GDPR rule behind it, plus an eight-line fix list. Made for small shops, agencies, clinics, trades and service businesses where staff use chat assistants, writing tools, transcription or email helpers with client data.

The short answer

Article 25(2) GDPR requires the controller to ensure that, by default, only personal data necessary for each specific purpose are processed, covering the amount collected, the extent of processing, the storage period and who can access the data. Article 25(1) asks for measures, such as pseudonymisation, that put principles like data minimisation into practice, both when you choose a tool and while you use it. GDPR does not list settings per product, so the practical step is one row per AI tool comparing what the task needs with what the tool takes by default, and recording the setting you chose and why.
