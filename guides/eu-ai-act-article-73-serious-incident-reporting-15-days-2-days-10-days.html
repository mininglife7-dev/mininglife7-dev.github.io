Serious incident reporting under Article 73 of the AI Act: who reports, to whom and how fast

Last updated 24 Oct 2026 · European Union

Short answer. Under Article 73 of the AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689), a provider of a high-risk AI system must report any serious incident to the market surveillance authority of the member state where it happened, immediately after establishing a causal link (or its reasonable likelihood) and no later than 15 days after becoming aware. The limit is 10 days where a person has died, and 2 days for a widespread infringement or a serious disruption of critical infrastructure. An incomplete first report may be followed by a complete one. A deployer that identifies a serious incident must inform the provider first and then the importer or distributor and the authorities (Article 26(5)).

What a small deployer should set up now

  • A list of the AI systems you use and whether any is high-risk
  • A named person who receives incident reports from staff
  • The provider's incident contact for each high-risk system
  • A log of incidents and complaints with dates of awareness

Common mistakes

  • Waiting for full facts. An initial incomplete report is allowed.
  • Not recording the awareness date, which starts the clock.
  • Assuming it only applies to providers. Deployers have a duty to inform under Article 26(5).

These duties apply with the high-risk rules; see our guide to the AI Act dates for when they take effect.

Frequently asked questions

What is the deadline for reporting a serious AI incident?

No later than 15 days after the provider becomes aware; 10 days where a death occurred; 2 days for widespread infringements or serious disruption of critical infrastructure (Article 73).

Who receives the report?

The market surveillance authority of the member state where the incident occurred.

What must a deployer do?

Inform the provider first, then the importer or distributor and the relevant market surveillance authorities (Article 26(5)).
