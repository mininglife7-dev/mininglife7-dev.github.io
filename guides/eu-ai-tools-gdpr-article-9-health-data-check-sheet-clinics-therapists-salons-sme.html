Health details in your AI tools: a GDPR Article 9 check sheet for small clinics, therapists, trainers and salons

Updated 22 Oct 2026 · Disclosure: the EURO AI Register mentioned below is an Unadhi product with a free edition. This guide is for information only, not legal or medical-regulatory advice.

What you get: a finished health-data check sheet for a small practice that uses AI tools: 12 columns, one row per tool (what health details it sees, the Article 9 condition you rely on, consent, supplier contract, supplier use of your inputs, records, DPIA screening and a keep-or-stop verdict), a do-not-paste list for staff, a line for your client notice, and a do-this-week list. Made for physiotherapists, psychotherapists and counsellors, dentists, osteopaths, personal trainers, nutritionists and beauty or aesthetic salons.

The short answer

Health details about an identifiable person are special category data (Article 4(15) and Article 9(1) GDPR). Processing them is prohibited unless an Article 9(2) condition applies: for care or treatment by a health professional bound by secrecy that is usually Article 9(2)(h) with 9(3); for many salons, gyms and coaches it is usually explicit consent under 9(2)(a). An AI note-taker, booking chatbot or writing assistant that sees those details is part of that processing, so each tool needs a row: condition, supplier contract (Article 28), a written record (Article 30, because the small-business exemption in 30(5) does not cover Article 9 data) and a DPIA screening (Article 35).

Do-not-paste list for staff

Do not paste into a general AI toolInstead
Full names with diagnoses, symptoms or medicationUse initials or a client number, or a tool covered by row G
Photos of skin, injuries or scansKeep in your clinical or client system
Mental health, sexual health or pregnancy detailsOnly in tools that pass rows E, G and H
Children's health detailsOnly in tools that pass rows E, G and H, with the parent's or guardian's involvement as your rules require

Line for your client or patient notice

We use [tool type, e.g. a transcription tool] to help us write [session notes / appointment summaries]. It is provided by [supplier], which processes the information only on our instructions under a written agreement. [It does / does not] keep recordings after [period]. [If consent: You can say no, and you can withdraw consent at any time by telling us; this will not affect your care.] Questions: [contact].

Do this week

  • List every AI tool staff use, including free ones on personal phones, and fill one row each
  • For each tool, read the supplier terms on training and retention and write the answer in column H
  • Get a processing agreement for every tool that sees health details, or stop using it for them
  • Put the do-not-paste list where staff write notes
  • Add the tools to your written record of processing and run the DPIA screening sheet
  • Check your professional body's guidance and national rules on health data (Article 9(4))
