Who can see what in your AI tools: a GDPR Article 32 access and security sheet for small businesses

Updated 23 Oct 2026 · Disclosure: the EURO AI Register mentioned below is an Unadhi product with a free edition. This guide is for information only, not legal advice.

What you get: a finished access and security sheet for your AI tools: 10 columns, one row per AI account (personal data that goes in, who has access, sign-in protection, owner and admins, leavers, saved chats, export and restore, staff instruction, review date), each tied to the GDPR rule behind it, plus an eight-line fix list you can work through in an afternoon. Made for small shops, agencies, clinics, trades and service businesses where staff use chat assistants, transcription tools or writing tools with client data.

The short answer

Article 5(1)(f) GDPR requires personal data to be processed with appropriate security, including protection against unauthorised processing and accidental loss, and Article 5(2) makes you responsible for being able to show it. Article 32 turns that into measures appropriate to the risk, with examples such as encryption, confidentiality of systems, the ability to restore access after an incident and regular testing. GDPR does not list settings per tool, so the practical step is one row per AI account showing who can get in, what they can see, how you get data back, what staff were told, and when you last checked.

Do this week

  • Take your list of AI tools and add one row per account, including free accounts staff opened themselves
  • Fill columns C to F from each tool's user or admin settings page
  • Check the supplier's help pages for history, sharing and export options and fill G and H
  • Write the two-line staff rule per tool (column I) and send it
  • If something does go wrong, use the 72-hour breach sheet: Article 33 sets the clock
