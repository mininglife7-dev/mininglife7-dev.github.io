EU AI Act Article 50: chatbot and AI-content disclosure checklist (from 2 Aug 2026)

Updated 13 Oct 2026 · Disclosure: the EURO AI Register mentioned below is an Unadhi product with a free edition.

Short answer: from 2 August 2026, Article 50 of the EU AI Act makes deployers and providers tell people when they are dealing with an AI system or with certain AI-generated content. If you run a customer chatbot, a voice bot, or publish AI images, audio or video that could mislead, you need a clear notice and a record of which systems trigger it.

What Article 50 covers

SituationWho actsWhat people must be told
People interact with an AI system (chatbot, voice assistant, support bot)Deployer (you, if you put it in front of customers or staff)That they are interacting with an AI system, unless it is obvious from the context
AI-generated or manipulated images, audio or video that resembles real people, places or events (deepfake-style)Deployer who publishes or uses itThat the content is artificially generated or manipulated, in a way that is clear and distinguishable
AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interestDeployer, with limited exceptions for editorial control under human reviewThat the text was generated or manipulated by AI
Emotion recognition or biometric categorisation in publicly accessible spacesDeployerPeople in those spaces must be informed

Read the official text before you rely on a paraphrase: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 50, on EUR-Lex. High-risk duties for many Annex III uses were postponed by the Digital Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744); the transparency clock for Article 50 still runs from 2 August 2026 unless your counsel says otherwise for your case.

A practical checklist

  • List every customer-facing or staff-facing chatbot, voice bot and AI writing or media tool.
  • Mark which ones talk to people without a human in the loop for the first reply.
  • Write the notice wording (for example: "You are chatting with an automated assistant. Ask for a human at any time.") and where it appears: first message, footer, voice prompt.
  • For marketing or social media, mark any AI images, audio or video that could be mistaken for a real recording, and add a visible label before publish.
  • Keep a dated note of who approved the wording and which systems it covers.
  • Re-check the list when you add a new tool or change a vendor model.

Where to keep the record

A spreadsheet is enough. If you want the transparency flags, duties and dates next to each system, the EURO AI Register free edition runs in the browser with no account and no upload (up to 3 AI systems and 10 evidence records). It can generate a short AI use policy from your entries. A paid membership removes the limits; you do not need it to follow this checklist.
