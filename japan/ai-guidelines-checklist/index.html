AI Guidelines for Business Ver1.2 (METI/MIC): a free English checklist

General information, not legal advice. The Guidelines are voluntary (soft law). This checklist is our own summary; it does not replace the official checklist and worksheet (Appendix 7), and using it does not show that your organisation meets the Guidelines.

Source

Japan's Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications (MIC) and Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry (METI) published the AI Guidelines for Business Ver1.2 on 31 March 2026. Headings below follow the official English provisional translation; the Japanese text prevails.

  • MIC: AI Guidelines for Business page (all versions and appendices)
  • AI Guidelines for Business Ver1.2 (English provisional translation, PDF)
  • Official checklist, Appendix 7, Ver1.2 (Japanese, PDF)

Step 0: know your role

The Guidelines name three kinds of AI business actor: AI developer, AI provider and AI business user. One company can hold more than one role depending on how it uses AI.

  • For each AI system, write down which role(s) you hold. Register field: Screening → "Your role for this system"; "Built by".

Part 5: items for AI business users

Most SMEs are AI business users (they use AI tools in their work). Table 1 of the Guidelines lists these items for them, in addition to the common principles:

  • U-2 i. Proper use of AI that considers safety. Register: intended purpose, status (active/planned/retired).
  • U-3 i. Consideration for bias in input data or prompts. Register: Human review, Evidence log.
  • U-4 i. Countermeasures against inappropriate input of personal data and privacy violation. Register: Data classes, Policy.
  • U-5 i. Implementation of security measures. Register: Evidence log.
  • U-6 i. Providing relevant stakeholders with information. Register: Transparency triggers, Report.
  • U-7 i. Explanation to relevant stakeholders. Register: Internal owner, Report.
  • U-7 ii. Effective use of provided documents and conformity to agreements. Register: Vendor; vendor documents in the Evidence log.

For AI providers (Part 4), in short

Table 1 adds for providers, among others: actions against risks to lives, bodies, properties, minds and the environment; bias in configurations and data; privacy-protection mechanisms; security mechanisms and handling of vulnerabilities; documentation of system architectures; explanation to AI business users of conformity to the common principles; and documentation of service agreements.
