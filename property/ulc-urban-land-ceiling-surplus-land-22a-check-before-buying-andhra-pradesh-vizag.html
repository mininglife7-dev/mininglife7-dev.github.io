ULC land and the 22A list in Andhra Pradesh: G.O.Ms.444 (2026) rules for buyers in Vizag

Official pages read on 23 Oct 2026 · Not legal advice · Government websites change, so re-check before you rely on a step.

Short answer: in Andhra Pradesh, G.O.Ms.No.444 dated 22.07.2026 says ULC land shall not go on the section 22A prohibited list unless the specific extent taken over by the Government under section 10(6) of the Urban Land (Ceiling and Regulation) Act, 1976 has been separately demarcated and given its own survey sub-division number. An entire survey number is not prohibited just because part of it vested. ULC land regularised by the Competent Authority is to be removed from the list. For a buyer in Vizag or another city, the question is narrow: is your exact sub-division the taken-over extent, and if so, is there a regularisation order?

Background: when the repeal came to Andhra Pradesh

A Government order record (G.O.Ms.No.1049, Revenue (UC-II), 21-08-2008, from the AP G.O. portal as mirrored on archive.org) describes the Urban Land (Ceiling and Regulation) Repeal Act, 1999 as adopted in the State of Andhra Pradesh "with effect from 27.03.2008", and deals with surplus land "taken possession u/s.10(6)". That is why the 2026 G.O. focuses on the extent actually taken over: land the Government had not taken possession of is treated differently from land it had. How the repeal applies to a particular plot is a legal question; we did not read the Repeal Act text itself on an official site, so its details are to verify with your lawyer.

Seven ULC checks before you buy in Vizag or elsewhere in AP

  1. Fix the sub-division: get the survey or LP number with sub-division and extent from the 1-B, Adangal and the seller's deed.
  2. Search IGRS: run the prohibited property search and save a dated screenshot.
  3. Find the category: if the number is listed, confirm whether it is a ULC entry or some other 22A class.
  4. Locate the taken-over extent: ask whether the s.10(6) extent has its own sub-division, and whether it overlaps your plot.
  5. Ask for regularisation papers: if your plot is within that extent, ask for the Competent Authority's regularisation order and the deed registered on it.
  6. Write to the Joint Collector: send a written query on ULC status for the sub-division and keep the reply.
  7. Tie payment to paper: have your lawyer make the balance payable only after the list is corrected or the regularisation order is produced.

Common mistake: walking away from a whole layout because "the survey number is ULC". Under para 4.4.B only the demarcated taken-over extent should be listed. The reverse mistake is worse: buying the taken-over extent itself without a regularisation order.

FAQ

Can a whole survey number be blocked because part is ULC land?

Not under para 4.4.B(ii) of G.O.Ms.No.444 of 22.07.2026. Only the extent taken over under section 10(6), demarcated with its own sub-division number, should be listed.

Who is the ULC authority in AP now?

The G.O. says the Joint Collector is presently designated as Special Officer and Competent Authority under the ULC Act, 1976.

What happens after ULC land is regularised?

Para 5.2 says it is removed from the prohibited list forthwith, and deeds registered on regularisation orders are admitted subject to checking earlier registered documents.

When did AP adopt the ULC Repeal Act?

A G.O. record of 21-08-2008 (G.O.Ms.No.1049) says with effect from 27.03.2008. What that means for a specific plot is for your lawyer.
