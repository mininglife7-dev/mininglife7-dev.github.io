Waqf and endowment (temple) land check before buying property in Telangana and Hyderabad

Official pages read on 23 Oct 2026 · Not legal advice · Government websites change, so re-check before you rely on a step.

Short answer: waqf land and temple (endowment) land are two separate categories, and both can sit inside survey numbers that look like ordinary patta or layout land. A transfer of waqf property is void under section 51(1A) of the Waqf Act, so a buyer cannot cure it later with a registration. In Telangana, run three checks before paying: the Registration department's Prohibited Properties page (on 23 Oct 2026 it said "Prohibition data is under review. it will be published soon..."), the land class on Bhu Bharati, and a written enquiry with the Telangana State Waqf Board and the Endowments Department for the exact survey number.

Why waqf land is different from an ordinary title problem

Section 51(1A) of the Waqf Act, now titled the Unified Waqf Management, Empowerment, Efficiency and Development Act, 1995, says: "Any sale, gift, exchange, mortgage or transfer of waqf property shall be void ab initio". Section 51(1) adds that a lease of waqf property "shall be void unless such lease is effected with the prior sanction of the Board", and that no mosque, dargah, khanqah, graveyard or imambara shall be leased. Section 52 deals with recovery of waqf property transferred in breach of section 51. In plain words, if a plot really is waqf property, a registered sale deed in your name may not give you title, however clean the rest of the papers look.

Seven waqf and endowment checks before you buy in Telangana

  1. Get the full description: survey number, sub-division, village, mandal, Bhudhaar and land class from the seller's passbook or ROR-1B, and the door number for a city property.
  2. Look at the ground: note any mosque, dargah, graveyard, ashurkhana or temple on or beside the land, and ask neighbours about old religious use.
  3. Read the old papers: ask for link documents and old revenue entries going back as far as possible. Words like inam, wakf or devasthanam in any of them need an explanation.
  4. Check the prohibited list: use the prohibited property check. If the data is still "under review", keep a dated screenshot and do not treat a blank result as clearance.
  5. Write to the authorities: send a written enquiry with the survey number to the Telangana State Waqf Board and the Endowments Department, and keep the reply with your file.
  6. Watch for notices: under the 2025 amendment, mutation of waqf property needs a 90-day newspaper notice (Bill text). Search recent local papers for the village name.
  7. Put it in the agreement: have your lawyer add a seller declaration that the land is not waqf or endowment land, with refund of advance if a claim appears before registration.

Common mistake: treating a clean encumbrance certificate as proof. An EC shows registered transactions; a waqf or endowment claim may come from a gazette list, a survey or long religious use that never appears in an EC.

FAQ

Can I buy waqf land in Telangana?

Section 51(1A) of the Waqf Act says any sale, gift, exchange, mortgage or transfer of waqf property is void ab initio. If the land is waqf property, a sale deed may not give you title. Check with the Telangana State Waqf Board before paying.

The prohibited property search shows nothing. Am I safe?

Not necessarily. On 23 Oct 2026 the Telangana Registration page said prohibition data is under review. Keep a dated screenshot and make written enquiries with the Waqf Board and Endowments Department too.

What is the UMEED portal?

The central portal launched on 06.06.2025 under the Waqf (Amendment) Act, 2025, through which waqf registration applications are made and waqf details are uploaded. Whether the public can search it by survey number is to verify.

How is a wrong prohibited entry removed in Telangana?

The Bhu Bharati FAQ says the owner applies under Grievance relating to prohibited properties list for removal. The seller should finish this before you pay.
