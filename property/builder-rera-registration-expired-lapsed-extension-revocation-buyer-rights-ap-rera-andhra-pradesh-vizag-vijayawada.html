RERA registration expired or lapsed in Andhra Pradesh: extension, revocation and buyer rights under AP-RERA

Official texts read on 27 Oct 2026 · Not legal advice · Government websites change, so re-check before you rely on a step.

Short answer: AP-RERA's FAQ explains it in four answers. A registration can be extended for force majeure, or in reasonable circumstances without the builder's fault for at most one year in total (FAQ Q48). The builder applies in Form-E before expiry with a fee of twice the registration fee, which AP-RERA may waive for force majeure (Q49). AP-RERA can also revoke a registration (Q50), and section 8 of the Act then provides ways to get the project finished (Q51). Your section 18 rights for late possession stay.

Who this is for: buyers in Visakhapatnam, Vijayawada, Guntur, Tirupati and the rest of Andhra Pradesh whose project has run past the completion date shown on AP-RERA.

What to do in AP when the date has passed

  1. Find the project on AP-RERA (our AP-RERA project check guide) and note the completion date and any extension shown.
  2. Ask the builder for the extension order in writing, with the new date and the reason given to AP-RERA.
  3. Check the possession date in your agreement for sale. See our AP agreement of sale checklist. Delay is counted against that date under s.18.
  4. Decide: wait with interest, or withdraw with a refund and interest. Both are in s.18. Our AP-RERA complaint guide covers the forms.
  5. If there is talk of revocation, organise the buyers. Our AP apartment association guide shows how an association is formed.

Red flag: "RERA is only paperwork, the extension is automatic". On the FAQ it is an application, with a fee and reasons, that the Authority decides. Ask to see the order.

FAQ

Can AP-RERA extend a project's registration?

Yes, for force majeure, or in reasonable circumstances without the promoter's fault for at most one year (FAQ Q48).

What is the fee for extension in Andhra Pradesh?

Twice the registration fee under rule 3(3) of the AP Rules, which the Authority may waive for force majeure (FAQ Q49).

What counts as force majeure?

War, flood, drought, fire, cyclone, earthquake or any other calamity caused by nature affecting the project (s.6, FAQ Q48).

Who completes a project whose registration is revoked?

Section 8 mechanisms (FAQ Q51): for example the competent authority or the association of allottees, which gets the first right of refusal.
