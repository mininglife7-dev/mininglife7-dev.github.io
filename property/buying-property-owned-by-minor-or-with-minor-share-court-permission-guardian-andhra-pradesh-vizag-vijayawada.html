Buying property with a minor's share in Andhra Pradesh: court permission, ratification deed (Vizag, Vijayawada)

Official pages read on 24 Oct 2026 · Not legal advice · Government websites change, so re-check before you rely on a step.

Short answer: in Andhra Pradesh, a guardian needs the court's permission before selling land, a flat or a house site that belongs (fully or partly) to a child. The IGRS Property Purchase FAQ (read 24 Oct 2026) warns that without that approval the sale is open to challenge by the child after they turn 18, unless the grown-up child later signs a registered ratification deed. The IGRS duty table prices that deed at Rs.5 stamp duty and Rs.1,000 registration fee (row 55). The rule comes from section 8 of the Hindu Minority and Guardianship Act, 1956.

Who this is for: buyers in Visakhapatnam, Vijayawada, Guntur and Tirupati looking at ancestral or inherited plots, family flats being sold after a death, or land where a grandchild was given a share in a settlement.

Buyer: five checks before you pay in Vizag or Vijayawada

  1. List every owner and heir with age. Get the death certificate, the family member certificate and the Meebhoomi / adangal entries; a child heir is easy to miss when only the adults come to the meeting.
  2. Look at how the seller got the property. A partition or settlement deed may give a share to a child by name. If so, that share needs the court's permission to sell.
  3. Get the court permission order (court-issued copy) before the balance is paid, and check the survey number, extent and guardian's name match the deed.
  4. Older sales in the chain: if a past deed was signed for a minor, ask whether that person has since signed a ratification deed (Rs.5 + Rs.1,000 on IGRS). Pull an EC to find it.
  5. Publish a public notice in a newspaper calling for claims, as the IGRS FAQ recommends, and keep the clipping with your papers.

Common mistake: thinking an uncle or grandmother who has raised the child can sign for them. Section 11 of the Act says no one can dispose of a Hindu minor's property just because they are looking after the child. Only the natural guardian, and only with the court's permission.

FAQ

Can a guardian sell a child's land in Andhra Pradesh without court permission?

No. The IGRS Property Purchase FAQ says court approval is required, and section 8(2) of the Hindu Minority and Guardianship Act bars a sale, gift, exchange or mortgage of a minor's immovable property without the court's previous permission.

How much does a ratification deed cost in AP?

The IGRS duty table (row 55) lists stamp duty Rs.5, registration fee Rs.1,000 and user charges Rs.100 for ratification and other supplemental deeds.

On what grounds does the court allow the sale?

Only for necessity or an evident advantage to the minor (section 8(4)). The guardian applies to the district court where the property is.

Can a grandfather sign for a minor grandchild?

Not merely because he looks after the child: section 11 bars a de facto guardian from disposing of a Hindu minor's property. Under section 6 the natural guardian is the father, and after him the mother.

What if a minor has a share in joint family (HUF) property?

Section 12 says no guardian is appointed for that undivided interest while an adult member manages it. Whether the sale is safe depends on the facts, so take a lawyer's view (to verify).
