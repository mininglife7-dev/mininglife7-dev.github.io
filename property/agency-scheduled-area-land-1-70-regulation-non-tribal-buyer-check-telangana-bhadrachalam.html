Agency (Scheduled Area) land in Telangana: can a non-tribal buy? 1/70 rule check near Bhadrachalam

Official texts read on 24 Oct 2026 · Not legal advice · Government websites change, so re-check before you rely on a step.

Short answer: if the land is in a notified Scheduled Area (agency area) of Telangana, such as parts of Bhadradri Kothagudem around Bhadrachalam, a non-tribal generally cannot buy it. The A.P. Scheduled Areas Land Transfer Regulation, 1959, as amended in 1970 (the "1/70" rule), makes a transfer by a tribal to a non-tribal void, presumes that land held by a non-tribal came from a tribal, and lets officials order eviction. A registered deed does not cure this. Before you pay, confirm whether the village is in a Scheduled Area.

Who this is for: Hyderabad buyers and NRIs offered "cheap farm land" or a weekend plot near Bhadrachalam, Kothagudem, Mulugu, Utnoor or other forest-belt villages, and anyone whose seller is not a tribal but holds land in an agency village.

Five checks before you buy land in a Telangana agency area

  1. Ask the Tahsildar or ITDA in writing whether the village and survey number fall in a Scheduled Area. Do not rely on the broker's word or on the land being near a town.
  2. Trace the title back in the Bhu Bharati record and the EC. If any owner in the chain was a tribal and a later owner is not, treat the land as high risk.
  3. Check the prohibited list on our prohibited property page, and look for an LTR or court case on the land.
  4. Be wary of workarounds: a deal through a tribal "front" buyer is a benami transfer under s.3(4), and long leases or agreements also count as transfers under s.2(g).
  5. Get a lawyer's written opinion on the specific survey number before any advance. Land assigned by the government has its own bar; see our assigned land page.

Common mistake: believing that a registered sale deed, a pattadar passbook or decades of possession make the purchase safe. Under s.3(1)(b) the non-tribal holder must prove the land did not come from a tribal, and an ejectment order can follow even years later.

FAQ

Can a non-tribal buy land in Bhadrachalam agency area?

Generally no, where the land is in a notified Scheduled Area. Under s.3(1)(a) of the 1959 Regulation (as amended in 1970), a transfer by a tribal to a non-tribal is void, and s.3(1)(b) presumes land held by a non-tribal there came from a tribal.

What is the 1/70 Act in Telangana?

It is the common name for Regulation 1 of 1970, which substituted section 3 of the A.P. Scheduled Areas Land Transfer Regulation, 1959. Telangana's Tribal Welfare Department (ITDA Bhadrachalam) reports LTR cases under it.

The seller is not a tribal and has a registered deed. Is that enough?

Not by itself. The law presumes the land came from a tribal unless proved otherwise, so trace the full chain and get the Scheduled Area status in writing.

Can I take the land on a long lease instead?

A lease is a transfer under s.2(g). A lease by a tribal to a non-tribal in agency tracts falls under the same bar.

What happens if I buy anyway?

Officials can order ejectment and restore the land (s.3(2)), and s.6-A provides up to one year's rigorous imprisonment, a fine up to Rs 2,000, or both.
