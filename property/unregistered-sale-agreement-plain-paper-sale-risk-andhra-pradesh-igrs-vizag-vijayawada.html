Unregistered sale agreement or plain-paper sale (sada bainama) in Andhra Pradesh: risks and what to do

Official pages read on 23 Oct 2026 · Not legal advice · Government websites change, so re-check before you rely on a step.

Short answer: in Andhra Pradesh, the Registration Department's own FAQ says title passes only on a registered deed: "right, title or interest can be acquired only if the deed is registered". An unregistered sale paper "will not be admissible in evidence (Sec.49 of Registration Act 1908)". On the official pages we read today we did not find an AP scheme like Telangana's section 6 regularisation, so treat a plain-paper sale (sada bainama) as a risk to fix before you pay, not after.

Seven checks before you pay for land or a plot in Vizag, Vijayawada, Guntur or Tirupati districts

  1. Trace every link in the seller's title. The IGRS FAQ's source-of-title list expects a sale deed for a purchase and revenue entries plus the predecessor's title for an inheritance.
  2. Mark any plain-paper link (sada bainama, notarised agreement, unregistered receipt). Each one is a gap in title.
  3. For farm land, ask for the adangal or pahani, khata, pattadar passbook and title deed, as the FAQ suggests, and match them on Meebhoomi.
  4. Get a Sub-Registrar search and EC, and remember a Nil EC does not show unregistered deals.
  5. Check the s.22-A list: the FAQ lists documents barred from registration, including government, endowment, wakf and ceiling-surplus land.
  6. Place a public notice in a newspaper. The FAQ says it "helps in proving that you purchased the property bona fide and in good faith".
  7. Pay against a registered sale deed only, and have a lawyer check the chain. The FAQ itself advises: "Engage a competent lawyer".

Common mistake: accepting "it is an old family sada bainama, everyone knows it is ours" and paying an advance. A Nil EC will not show that paper, and the paper cannot be used as evidence of transfer in court (s.49). Fix the title first.

FAQ

Is an unregistered sale agreement valid in Andhra Pradesh?

The IGRS AP FAQ says title passes only on a registered deed, and a compulsorily registrable deed that is not registered is not admissible in evidence (s.49 Registration Act). An unregistered agreement has no effect for the purposes of s.53A of the Transfer of Property Act.

Does a Nil EC show a sada bainama?

No. The IGRS FAQ says unregistered transactions are not included in an encumbrance certificate.

How long do I have to register a signed sale deed in AP?

Four months from signing, plus another four months with a penalty if the District Registrar permits. After that it cannot be accepted, per the IGRS FAQ.

Is there an AP regularisation scheme for plain-paper sales?

We did not find one on the official pages we read on 23 Oct 2026. Check with the Tahsildar office and a lawyer.
