Home loan interest tax deduction for joint owners: Hyderabad flat buyers (co-borrowers, old vs new regime)

Official pages read on 23 Oct 2026 · Not legal advice · Government websites change, so re-check before you rely on a step.

Short answer: if two or more people buy a flat or house in Hyderabad, Secunderabad, Gachibowli, Kokapet or anywhere in Telangana together and take a joint home loan, the Income Tax Department's pages say each co-owner can claim home-loan interest separately, in line with their share and what they actually pay towards the interest, as long as the shares are clear. For a home you live in, the limit is Rs. 2,00,000 (or Rs. 30,000 if the conditions are not met), and it is only in the old tax regime: the default new regime does not allow it. Income tax is a central law, so the rule is the same in every state; the local part is how your sale deed and loan papers are written.

Before you sign: six checks for a couple or family buying together

  1. Write each owner's share in the sale deed (for example 50:50 or 60:40). The department's rule on separate computation depends on shares being "definite and ascertainable".
  2. Make each owner who wants the deduction a co-borrower on the loan, and pay the EMI from their own account in line with their share. The claim follows each owner's contribution to the interest.
  3. Ask the bank for an interest certificate in each borrower's name, or one that shows the split. The Rs. 2,00,000 limit needs a certificate from the lender.
  4. Under-construction flat? Check the builder's RERA completion date. The higher limit needs completion within 5 years from the end of the financial year you borrowed in; interest paid before that is claimed later in 5 instalments.
  5. Compare old vs new regime each year with the department's Income and Tax Calculator. A salaried owner with no business income can switch every year in the return (FAQ Q11); the self-occupied interest claim is only available in the old regime.
  6. Keep the file: sale deed with shares, loan sanction letter naming all borrowers, yearly interest certificates, EMI bank statements, and the completion or occupancy certificate date.

Common mistake: one spouse pays the whole EMI but both claim the deduction, or a co-owner who is not on the loan claims interest. The department's text ties each claim to the owner's share and contribution. If your case is unusual, ask a chartered accountant before you file.

Not quoted here (to verify): principal repayment and stamp duty under section 80C, and any first-time-buyer deduction. We did not read these on an official page today, so we don't give limits for them.

Telangana: getting the papers right at registration

The share split is written in the sale deed you register at the Sub-Registrar office, and the stamp duty is the same whether one person or two buy (see our stamp duty guide below). If you are buying a flat that is still being built in Hyderabad, check the TG RERA completion date before you rely on the higher limit, and keep the bank's legal opinion and the mortgage (deposit of title deeds) papers in the same file as your interest certificates.

FAQ

Can both husband and wife claim home loan interest on a Hyderabad flat?

Yes, if both are co-owners with clear shares and both repay the loan. The Income Tax Department's page says each co-owner claims interest according to their contribution in repayment of interest. For a flat you live in, it is allowed only in the old tax regime.

Is the Rs. 2 lakh limit per person or per flat?

The department's page writes the Rs. 2,00,000 limit as the deduction an assessee (a taxpayer) can claim in aggregate for up to two self-occupied houses. How it applies to your joint loan is best confirmed with a chartered accountant.

Can I claim home loan interest in the new tax regime?

Not for a self-occupied house. The e-filing FAQ (Q7) says this interest is not allowed in the new regime; to claim it you choose the old regime in your return.

I pay EMI for an under-construction flat. When can I claim?

Interest paid before the purchase or construction is complete is claimed in 5 equal yearly instalments from the year it is completed, per the department's page.
